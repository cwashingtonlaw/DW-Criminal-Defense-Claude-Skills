# Lab Accreditation

This reference is loaded by `dw-crime-lab-auditor-crim` Module D. It covers the current accreditation regime for U.S. forensic laboratories, the legacy systems being absorbed into it, the role of audit reports and corrective-action plans, complaint mechanisms, and the consequences of lost or restricted accreditation for trial admissibility.

---

## 1. The Current U.S. Accreditation Landscape

### ANAB — ANSI National Accreditation Board

**The dominant U.S. accrediting body** for forensic laboratories. ANAB (an institute of the American National Standards Institute) accredits crime labs under **ISO/IEC 17025:2017** ("General requirements for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories") with additional **forensic-specific supplementary requirements** documented in ANAB's accreditation manual.

ANAB succeeded and absorbed the **ASCLD/LAB-International** accreditation program (administered by the American Society of Crime Laboratory Directors / Laboratory Accreditation Board) during a multi-year transition culminating around 2018.

**Verify current — accreditation bodies and program names evolve.**

### ISO/IEC 17025:2017

The international standard for testing and calibration laboratories. Key requirements include:
- Documented management system (quality manual, procedures)
- Personnel competence (qualifications, training, authorization)
- Methods (validated, documented in SOPs, version-controlled)
- Measurement traceability (calibration, reference materials)
- Equipment (maintained, calibrated, performance-checked)
- Sampling procedures
- Handling of test items (chain of custody)
- Technical records
- Reporting of results
- Complaints management
- Nonconforming work / corrective action
- Internal audits and management review

ISO 17025 is not forensic-specific by itself; ANAB's supplementary requirements add forensic-specific provisions (e.g., handling of evidence, qualifications for forensic analysts, technical-review requirements).

### Legacy: ASCLD/LAB-International

The legacy ASCLD/LAB-International program was a forensic-specific accreditation widely used in U.S. crime labs prior to ANAB consolidation. Older accreditation certificates may reference ASCLD/LAB. The substantive standards have been carried forward (with revisions) into ANAB's forensic supplemental.

When auditing older case work, check whether the lab held ASCLD/LAB accreditation at the time of analysis and whether the scope of that accreditation covered the specific method.

### Other Bodies

- **A2LA** — American Association for Laboratory Accreditation, also accredits some forensic labs under ISO 17025
- **NELAC / TNI** — environmental testing accreditation, generally not forensic but may appear in environmental-crime cases

## 2. Regulatory and Standards-Setting Bodies (Beyond Accreditation)

Accreditation is competency certification; these bodies set the technical standards the labs are accredited against.

### NIST OSAC — Organization of Scientific Area Committees

The **NIST OSAC** (Organization of Scientific Area Committees for Forensic Science) is a NIST-administered framework that drafts and recommends standards across forensic disciplines, including seized drugs, toxicology, fire debris, trace evidence, firearms, and others. OSAC standards are increasingly being adopted as the technical baseline for accreditation scope.

**Audit point:** is the lab's method consistent with the current OSAC recommendation for the discipline?

### SWGDRUG / SWGTOX

Pre-OSAC and overlapping with current OSAC work, the **Scientific Working Group for the Analysis of Seized Drugs** (SWGDRUG) and **Scientific Working Group for Forensic Toxicology** (SWGTOX) developed widely-cited methodology recommendations. SWGDRUG's Category A/B/C identification framework is still in active use.

### State Forensic Science Commissions

- **Texas Forensic Science Commission** — a state regulator created after several Texas lab scandals; investigates complaints, has authority over forensic-analyst licensure
- **New York State Commission on Forensic Science** — sets accreditation requirements for NY labs
- Other states have similar or weaker oversight; Louisiana's structure should be verified at the time of audit

### Standards in Specific Disciplines

- **AAFS Standards Board (ASB)** — drafts consensus standards, some published by ASTM and ANSI
- **ANSI/ASB** standards for drug analysis, toxicology, etc. — increasingly cited as the validated technical baseline

## 3. Reading an Accreditation Certificate

An accreditation certificate is not a blanket endorsement of every method the lab performs. The certificate has a **scope** — typically a published "scope of accreditation" document — listing every method the lab is accredited to perform.

**Audit points:**
- [ ] Who is the accrediting body? (ANAB, A2LA, other)
- [ ] What is the accreditation number?
- [ ] What is the effective date and expiration date?
- [ ] Is the certificate **current** as of the date of the lab work in this case?
- [ ] Does the scope cover the **specific method** used in this case? (E.g., "Identification of cocaine and cocaine analogues by GC/MS" — if the lab tested an emerging synthetic and the scope does not list it, the accreditation does not cover this work)
- [ ] Has there been any **suspension, restriction, or withdrawal** of accreditation between the test date and trial?

## 4. Audit Reports and Corrective-Action Plans

ANAB performs **on-site assessments** typically every 2 years (verify current cycle) plus annual surveillance reviews. The on-site assessment produces an **assessment report** identifying:

- **Nonconformities** — findings where the lab fails to meet a requirement
- **Opportunities for improvement** — non-findings, observations
- **Corrective-action plans** — the lab's documented response and timeline

These documents are typically **available on request** and often discoverable in criminal cases through specific Brady/Giglio demand.

**Critical audit points:**
- [ ] Has the lab had any nonconformities in the section relevant to this case (drug chemistry, toxicology) during the case period?
- [ ] Have prior nonconformities been closed out, or are any open?
- [ ] Was a corrective-action plan implemented before the analysis in this case, and was it verified effective?
- [ ] Are there any **major nonconformities** (vs. minor) — major typically affects competence or core methodology

### How to obtain audit reports

- Direct request to ANAB (some materials public)
- Public-records request to the lab's parent agency (often a state or local government — subject to public-records law)
- Brady/Giglio demand to the State in the case
- Subpoena duces tecum to the lab

## 5. Complaint Mechanisms

Defense bar can file **complaints** against accredited labs, which triggers ANAB review.

- **ANAB complaint procedure:** documented on the ANAB website; written complaints with specifics trigger investigation; the complainant is typically notified of the outcome
- **State commissions** (Texas FSC, NY State Commission) have their own complaint procedures

**Strategic consideration:** filing an ANAB complaint creates a paper trail that can be referenced in future cases involving the same lab and analyst. Even if the immediate result is limited, the documentation accumulates.

## 6. Louisiana Labs — Practical Notes

Louisiana criminal cases commonly involve work from:

- **Louisiana State Police Crime Laboratory (LSP Crime Lab)** — Baton Rouge, with regional facilities
- **Southwest Louisiana Crime Lab (SPCL)** — Lake Charles; serves the 14th JDC and surrounding parishes
- **Acadiana Criminalistics Laboratory (ACL)** — Lafayette
- **North Louisiana Criminalistics Laboratory (NLCL)** — Shreveport
- **New Orleans Police Department Crime Lab (NOPD)** — historically problematic; check current accreditation status
- **Jefferson Parish Sheriff's Office Crime Lab**
- **East Baton Rouge Parish Sheriff's Office Crime Lab**
- **St. Tammany Parish Coroner's Office** (toxicology in some cases)

**Audit point in every Louisiana case:** confirm the testing lab's identity, the current accreditation body and number, and the scope of accreditation for the specific method used. **Verify currency — accreditation status can change between case events.**

For DWI cases, the state's Office of Alcohol and Tobacco Control / Department of Health certifications for breath instruments and blood-alcohol testing are separate from forensic-lab accreditation; see `dw-dwi-specialist-crim` for breath-instrument issues.

## 7. Loss of Accreditation and Admissibility

What happens to admissibility when a lab loses accreditation?

### Suspension during the case

If the lab's accreditation is suspended between the date of analysis and trial, several issues arise:

- **Foundation challenge:** the State must lay a foundation for the reliability of the analysis. Suspended accreditation undermines that foundation.
- **Daubert/Foret:** under La. C.E. Art. 702 and *State v. Foret*, expert testimony based on methodology that was accredited at the time may still be admissible — but **suspended-during-the-case** accreditation strongly supports a Daubert challenge.
- **Cross-examination:** even if admitted, the suspension is fully fair game for cross.

### Suspension or withdrawal **before** the analysis

- If the lab was not accredited (or accredited only in a different scope) at the time of analysis, the State's foundation is even weaker.
- Move to exclude or restrict the analyst's testimony.

### Sanctions short of full withdrawal

- **Restriction of scope** — the accreditation is narrowed; the specific method may or may not still be covered
- **Conditional accreditation pending corrective action** — accredited but on notice
- **Public discipline** — letter of admonishment, etc.

Each of these is fair-game cross material and supports motion practice depending on severity.

## 8. Discovery Demand Checklist — Accreditation

- [ ] Current accreditation certificate, with scope
- [ ] Accreditation certificate in effect on the date of analysis (if different)
- [ ] ANAB (or other) on-site assessment reports for the past 3-5 years
- [ ] Nonconformities and corrective-action plans during the case period
- [ ] Any suspension, restriction, or withdrawal notices
- [ ] Surveillance review records
- [ ] Internal-audit records (the lab's own audits required by ISO 17025)
- [ ] Management-review records
- [ ] Method validation documentation for the specific method
- [ ] SOP version history showing the version in effect on the date of analysis

---

## 9. Audit Findings Checklist

When auditing the lab's accreditation, mark each item as confirmed, missing, or problematic.

- [ ] Lab name confirmed and accredited?
- [ ] Accrediting body identified?
- [ ] Accreditation number cross-checked against the body's public registry?
- [ ] Effective date covers the analysis date?
- [ ] Scope covers the specific method?
- [ ] Recent (past 3 years) assessment reports reviewed?
- [ ] Any nonconformities affecting the analyst or method?
- [ ] Any open corrective actions at the time of analysis?
- [ ] Any suspension, restriction, or withdrawal events?
- [ ] Lab management-review documents reviewed?
- [ ] Internal-audit records reviewed?

A "no" or "missing" on any of items 4-10 escalates to a **CRITICAL or SIGNIFICANT** audit finding and supports motion practice.
