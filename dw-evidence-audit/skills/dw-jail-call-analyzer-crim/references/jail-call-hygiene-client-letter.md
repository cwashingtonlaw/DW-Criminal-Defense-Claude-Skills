# Jail-Call Hygiene Client Letter — Template

Used by `dw-jail-call-analyzer-crim` Module H. Delivered to the client at the next attorney visit or through `dw-client-communication-drafter-crim`. Pairs with `dw-client-intake-interview-crim` for new-client deployment.

## Delivery Notes

- **Hand-delivered or in-person reading is strongly preferred.** Do NOT send through the jail messaging system — those messages are recorded and produced.
- The attorney should read the letter aloud with the client and have the client initial each numbered rule, then sign and date the acknowledgment at the end.
- The signed original is retained in the case file under Trial Notebook / Client Communications. A second signed copy goes to the client to retain in their cell.
- Re-deliver after any major case event (indictment, superseding indictment, plea offer, bond ruling, new co-defendant, new witness identification).

## Variables

The template uses the same variables as the rest of the D&W shared-protocols system, resolved from `dw-case-brain-crim` before the letter is finalized:

- `{{CLIENT_NAME}}` — full legal name as filed
- `{{CLIENT_NICKNAME}}` — short address form preferred by the client
- `{{ATTORNEY_NAME}}` — assigned D&W attorney
- `{{CASE_CAPTION}}` — short caption (State v. Last Name)
- `{{DOCKET}}` — docket / case number
- `{{FACILITY}}` — current housing facility
- `{{DATE}}` — date of letter

## Template

**[FIRM LETTERHEAD — apply per dw-shared-protocols-crim/references/letterhead.md]**

**Attorney Work Product / Privileged & Confidential**

**TO:** {{CLIENT_NAME}}, c/o {{FACILITY}}
**FROM:** {{ATTORNEY_NAME}}
**RE:** {{CASE_CAPTION}}, Docket No. {{DOCKET}} — Jail Call Hygiene
**DATE:** {{DATE}}

Dear {{CLIENT_NICKNAME}},

This letter is a set of rules about how you should use the phones at {{FACILITY}} between now and the resolution of your case. Read it carefully. We are going to read it together at our next visit, and I am going to ask you to initial each rule and sign at the bottom. Keep your copy and re-read it once a week.

I am not writing this letter because I think you have done anything wrong. I am writing it because every prosecutor in this parish, and every prosecutor in every parish around it, knows how to pull jail call recordings, and they know how to use them at trial. The State has already produced your calls in our case, and we have reviewed them. What you say tomorrow will be in the next batch the State produces. We need to fix that pattern starting today.

### The Single Rule You Have to Remember

**Every call you make from any phone in any jail is recorded, transcribed, stored, and producible to the prosecutor. That includes calls to your mother, your girlfriend, your wife, your children, your pastor, and your best friend. The only calls that are not recorded are calls to a phone number that has been registered as an attorney line in the facility's privileged-call system.**

If you remember nothing else from this letter, remember that one rule.

### The Specific Rules

Initial next to each rule when we go through this together.

___ **1. Do not discuss the case.** Not the facts. Not the charges. Not the evidence. Not the witnesses. Not what you remember. Not what you think happened. Not what the police are saying. Not what your co-defendants are saying. Not what the news is reporting. Nothing about the case, on any call, ever. Case discussion happens in person with me, in writing on properly marked attorney-client material, or on a registered attorney call.

___ **2. Do not contact witnesses, victims, or victims' families — directly or through anyone else.** This includes calls placed by you, messages relayed through a family member or friend, three-way calls, conference calls, and social media messages anyone sends on your behalf. If there is a witness you believe I need to talk to, tell me at our next visit and I will route it through our investigator. **Witness contact from inside the jail can result in additional charges, bond revocation, and the loss of any plea leverage we currently have. There is no exception to this rule.**

___ **3. Do not coordinate stories.** Do not tell anyone — a co-defendant, a witness, a family member — what to say if they are asked about the case, what to remember, what to forget, or "how to keep our story straight." Even saying "just tell them you don't remember" is enough to support an obstruction-of-justice charge. The State will use any coordination call as both substantive evidence against you and as a separate basis for new charges.

___ **4. Do not use codes or nicknames to talk around the rules.** Codes do not work. Prosecutors hear hundreds of jail calls a month. Codes are easy to decode, and the use of a code is itself evidence that you knew you were saying something you should not have been saying. If you would not say it plainly, do not say it in code.

___ **5. Do not make jokes about the case, the charges, the victim, the witnesses, or the system.** Jurors hear jail calls. Tone of voice matters. Laughter at the wrong moment, sarcasm, or casual references to the offense will be played back to a jury and will be remembered.

___ **6. Do not talk about prior bad acts, prior arrests, prior charges, or anything you have done that the State does not already know about.** Even if you think it does not relate to this case, the prosecutor may try to use it under La. C.E. Art. 404(B). If it comes up, change the subject.

___ **7. Do not talk about co-defendants — by name, by nickname, or by reference.** If someone on the call brings up a co-defendant, change the subject. Do not say what they did, what they said, what you think of them, whether you are angry at them, whether they cooperated, or what you would say to them if you could.

___ **8. Do not threaten anyone, joke about threatening anyone, or make any statement that could be heard as a threat — toward witnesses, victims, victims' families, prosecutors, judges, or jurors.** Statements you mean as venting will be played back to a jury as intimidation evidence.

___ **9. Do not talk about disposing of, hiding, getting rid of, or "taking care of" anything related to the case** — weapons, vehicles, phones, clothing, money, drugs, or any other item the State might want to collect. Even talking about something that has already been disposed of is admissible.

___ **10. Three-way calls and call forwarding are detected and logged.** Even when the system blocks the third party from connecting, it logs that you tried. Do not try.

___ **11. If you call me and there is anyone in the room or on the line at the other end besides the person who answered the phone in my office, hang up and call back when they are alone.** Privilege is fragile. Once a third party hears me give you advice, that advice is no longer privileged.

___ **12. The jail messaging system (kiosks, tablets, email-style messaging) is recorded too.** Do not write what you would not say.

### What You Can Talk About

You can call your family. You can talk about how you are doing, what you need from commissary, who is visiting and when, your kids, your health, plans for after release, plans for trial logistics that do not involve facts of the case (dates, who is taking off work, who is sitting in court). You can ask your family to call my office to schedule a visit. You can tell your family you love them. You can listen — to their day, their problems, their lives. Most calls are fine. The rules above are about what to NOT say; everything else is open.

### What to Do If You Slip

You will slip at some point. Everyone does. If you say something on a call that breaks one of these rules, do not panic, do not try to "take it back" on the same call (that just makes it worse), and do not call me on the regular jail phone to discuss it. Just write it down, and we will discuss at our next visit. Tell me what you said and when. We will figure out together how to address it.

### Acknowledgment

I have read this letter or had it read to me. I understand each of the rules above. I understand that {{ATTORNEY_NAME}} has explained why these rules matter for my case. I understand that following these rules is an important part of my own defense.

Client signature: ____________________________
Date: ____________________________

Attorney signature: ____________________________
Date: ____________________________

The first-contact version omits the "we have reviewed your calls and found a pattern" framing in paragraph 2 and replaces it with:

## Variant: Post-Slip Version

When a Module B audit identifies one or more Severity-3-or-higher admissions on already-recorded calls, the hygiene memo includes a specific addendum acknowledging that the prior calls already exist and re-emphasizing the rules going forward:

> The State already has calls from before today in which you said things that may hurt our case at trial. We are working on how to address those calls — through evidentiary motions, through trial strategy, and through your own testimony decision if it comes to that. I will explain those decisions at our next visit. **What I need from you starting today is no more of these calls.** From this moment forward, the rules below apply without exception.

## Operational Reminder

This letter coaches the client to **stop discussing the case on calls** — not to discuss the case in undetectable ways. The skill does not provide, and the firm does not provide, instruction on how to evade lawful recording. If a client asks for help working around the recording system, decline and re-direct to the rules above.

## Reinforcement Schedule

The hygiene memo is not a one-time delivery. Build the following touchpoints into the case calendar:

- **Initial delivery.** At the first attorney visit after the audit is complete, or at intake for new clients. Read aloud, initialed, signed, retained.
- **Weekly re-read.** The client retains a copy in their cell and is asked to re-read it once a week. Confirm at every visit.
- **Trigger events.** Re-deliver and re-read after any of: indictment, superseding indictment, addition or removal of co-defendants, identification of a new witness, plea offer presentation, bond hearing, suppression-motion ruling, trial-date setting, or any incident in which the client reports having been on a call where one of the rules came up.
- **Family delivery.** With client permission, the assigned attorney may also deliver an abbreviated version of Rules 1-3 and Rule 10 to the client's regular call partners — the goal is to get the relay people to redirect the client back to the rules when the client begins to drift on a call. Family delivery is at the attorney's discretion and depends on the family-witness overlap profile from Module A.

## Coordination with `dw-client-intake-interview-crim`

For all new clients, the hygiene memo template is paired with `dw-client-intake-interview-crim` so that hygiene coaching happens at first contact, not after damaging calls already exist. The intake skill includes a hygiene-checkpoint step that confirms:

1. The client has been read this letter
2. The client has signed the acknowledgment
3. The signed acknowledgment is in the case file
4. The client has the second copy in their cell
5. The next re-read date is calendared

If any of those five items is missing at intake completion, the intake skill loops back through this template before closing the intake.

## Coordination with `dw-court-jail-tracker-crim`

Whenever the client transfers between facilities, the prior facility's PIN is retired and a new PIN issues. The new facility's call recording starts immediately. `dw-court-jail-tracker-crim` will surface every facility transfer; on each transfer, this letter is re-delivered along with a fresh acknowledgment so that the client cannot later argue that the rules applied only at the prior facility.

## File-Retention Notes

The signed acknowledgment is internal work product and follows the standard D&W work-product marking. It is retained in:

```
{{CASE_ROOT}}/01 - Trial Notebook/02 - Client/Hygiene Acknowledgments/Jail Call Hygiene - {{DATE}} - signed.pdf
```

Successive re-deliveries append the new date and are stored side-by-side; do not overwrite prior signed copies. The chain of acknowledgments is itself useful: if the client later violates a rule, the prior acknowledgment evidences notice and may matter for any later argument about voluntariness, knowledge, or coaching.

## What This Letter Is NOT

- It is not legal advice for the recipient (the family member, romantic partner, or other relay person). If a relay person has independent exposure (e.g., they have been receiving relay messages and may themselves be at risk), the assigned attorney evaluates whether to refer them to independent counsel under firm conflict policy.
- It is not a substitute for in-person attorney-client conferences. The letter coaches conduct on the phones; substantive case discussion happens in person.
- It is not a defense to obstruction-of-justice charges. If the client violates the rules and the State charges obstruction, the existence of this letter does not insulate the client; if anything, it documents the client's awareness of the recording and the rules. The defensive value of the letter is preventive, not retroactive.

## Module H Procedure (carried over from SKILL.md) — H.1 Why This Module Matters, H.2 Hygiene Memo Components, H.3 Delivery Mechanism, H.4 Cross-Feed to Intake

### H.1 Why This Module Matters

Calls produced in discovery are the calls that already exist. Calls the client makes tomorrow will be in the next discovery production. The audit is incomplete if the defense team does not actively shape the client's call behavior going forward.

### H.2 Hygiene Memo Components

The memo (built from the template) should cover:

- The fundamental rule: **assume every call is recorded, transcribed, and will be played to the jury**. Even calls that "feel" private — to a parent, to a romantic partner, to a child — are recorded.
- No discussion of the case facts, the charges, the evidence, the witnesses, or the defense theory on any call. Period. Case discussion happens with counsel, in person, or on registered attorney lines.
- No witness contact, direct or relayed. If the client has a need to communicate with a witness (rare and almost always inadvisable), it goes through counsel.
- No coded language. Codes do not work — jurors and prosecutors are not stupid, and the use of codes is itself evidence of consciousness of guilt.
- No bragging, joking, or downplaying. Even calls about unrelated topics can include tone and word choice that the State will replay in opening or closing.
- No commentary on co-defendants, victims, or law enforcement. Anything said about a witness or a victim is admissible character / consciousness-of-guilt material.
- Three-way calls and call-forwarding workarounds are detected and flagged by the vendor; do not attempt them.
- If counsel calls and there is a third party in the room or on the line at the recipient end, hang up and call back when alone.
- Explicit acknowledgment that the client has received the memo, signed and dated, retained in the case file.

### H.3 Delivery Mechanism

The memo is delivered through `dw-client-communication-drafter-crim` or hand-delivered at the next jail visit. It is NOT emailed through the jail messaging system — those messages are also recorded and produced.

### H.4 Cross-Feed to Intake

For new clients, the hygiene memo template is paired with `dw-client-intake-interview-crim` so that hygiene coaching happens at first contact, not after damaging calls already exist.
