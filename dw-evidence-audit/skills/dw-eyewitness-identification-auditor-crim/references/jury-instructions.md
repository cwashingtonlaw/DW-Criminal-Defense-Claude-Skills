## STEP 4 -- Jury Instruction Proposals

### Standard Eyewitness Identification Instruction
Louisiana does not have a mandatory expanded eyewitness identification jury instruction comparable to the Henderson instruction in New Jersey. However, defense counsel should request a special instruction addressing the factors that affect identification reliability:

**Proposed Instruction Framework:**

> Members of the jury, you have heard testimony that a witness identified the defendant as the person who committed the charged offense. Identification testimony is an expression of belief or impression by the witness. The value of identification testimony depends on the opportunity the witness had to observe the offender at the time of the offense and to make a reliable identification later.
>
> In evaluating identification testimony, you should consider the following factors:
>
> 1. Whether the witness had an adequate opportunity to observe the person during the commission of the offense, including the lighting conditions, the distance between the witness and the person, the duration of the observation, and whether the witness's view was obstructed;
>
> 2. Whether the witness was under stress or fear at the time of the observation, and the effect that stress may have had on the witness's ability to perceive and remember;
>
> 3. Whether a weapon was present and visible during the commission of the offense, and whether the presence of the weapon may have affected the witness's ability to observe the person's face;
>
> 4. Whether the witness and the person identified are of different races or ethnic groups, and that research has shown that people may have greater difficulty accurately identifying members of a different race or ethnic group;
>
> 5. The length of time between the witness's original observation and the identification procedure;
>
> 6. Whether the identification procedure was conducted in a manner that minimized the risk of misidentification, including whether the witness was instructed that the perpetrator may or may not be present, whether the administrator knew which person was the suspect, and whether the witness's confidence was recorded immediately;
>
> 7. The witness's level of certainty at the time of the identification and whether that level of certainty has changed over time;
>
> 8. Whether the witness was exposed to any information about the suspect between the time of the crime and the identification, including photographs, media coverage, or statements by others.
>
> You are not required to accept identification testimony even if it is uncontradicted. Identification testimony carries the risk of error, and misidentification has been shown to be a leading cause of wrongful convictions.

### Case-Specific Instruction Add-Ons

Depending on the facts, request additional instructions addressing:

| Condition | Proposed Instruction Topic |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| Cross-racial identification | Own-race bias instruction |
| Weapon present | Weapon focus instruction |
| Showup used | Inherent suggestiveness of single-suspect confrontations |
| Multiple procedures | Commitment effect / memory contamination |
| Confirming feedback given | Post-identification feedback and confidence inflation |
| Long delay | Memory decay instruction |
| Co-witness discussion | Memory contamination from co-witness information |
