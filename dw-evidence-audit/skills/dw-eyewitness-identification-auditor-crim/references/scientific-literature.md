## Quick Reference -- Key Scientific Literature

| Citation | Finding | Application |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| NAS Report, *Identifying the Culprit* (2014) | Comprehensive review of eyewitness science; recommended double-blind administration, unbiased instructions, confidence statements, video recording | Foundation for all system variable arguments |
| Innocence Project data (ongoing) | Mistaken eyewitness ID in ~69% of DNA exonerations | Establishes real-world prevalence of misidentification |
| Wells & Bradfield (1998) | Post-identification feedback inflates confidence, self-reported attention, and viewing conditions | Challenges inflated trial confidence |
| Steblay (1992); Fawcett et al. (2013) | Weapon focus reduces identification accuracy | Weapon-present cases |
| Meissner & Brigham (2001) | Own-race bias: 1.4x increase in false IDs cross-racially | Cross-racial identification cases |
| Deffenbacher et al. (2004) | High stress negatively affects identification accuracy | Violent crime / threatening situations |
| Clark (2005) | Biased instructions increase false identification rate 15-20% | Instruction failure cases |
| Gabbert et al. (2003) | Co-witness discussion contaminates individual memory | Multiple witness cases |
| Dysart et al. (2001) | Mugshot exposure creates commitment to previously viewed faces | Prior mugshot viewing cases |
| Loftus & Harley (2005) | Visual acuity limits reliable face perception at distance | Distance-at-identification cases |
| Steblay, Wells, & Douglass (2014) | Meta-analysis confirming post-identification feedback effect | Confirming feedback present |
| Hagsand et al. (2013) | Alcohol impairs identification accuracy | Intoxicated witness cases |
| Deffenbacher et al. (2006) | Forgetting curve for faces; most rapid in first 24 hours | Delayed identification procedures |
| Valentine et al. (2003) | Sequential presentation reduces false IDs | Presentation method arguments |
| Wells et al. (1998) | Seminal framework for system variable reform | Foundation for best-practice arguments |
| PCAST Report (2016) | Review of forensic science validity including eyewitness identification | Supplementary authority for science-based arguments |
| Wixted & Wells (2017) | Initial confidence-accuracy relationship under pristine conditions | Nuanced confidence discussion |
