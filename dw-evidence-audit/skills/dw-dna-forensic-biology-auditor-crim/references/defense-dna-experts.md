# Defense DNA Experts — Reference

Non-exhaustive reference of **defense DNA expert categories** and sourcing channels. Used by **Module B–F** audits to recommend retention of qualified defense experts.

---

## CRITICAL NOTE TO ATTORNEY

This reference identifies **expert categories** and **sourcing organizations**, not specific individuals. Specific individual names are deliberately omitted because:

- Expert availability, current employment, and rates change frequently.
- A "qualified" expert today may have a recent professional issue tomorrow.
- Conflicts of interest (prior work for the same lab, prior involvement in the case, prior adverse testimony) must be independently checked.
- Some experts are no longer accepting defense work; some have moved to lab employment that creates conflicts.

**Before engaging any defense DNA expert:**

1. **Verify current availability and rates.**
2. **Conflict-check** against the case, the State's lab, the State's analyst, and the defendant.
3. **Review the expert's recent CV** — publications, testimony list (often via NACDL or *Daubert Tracker*), proficiency record if applicable.
4. **Check for adverse appellate decisions** that have excluded or limited the expert's testimony.
5. **Confirm the expert's specialty fits the case** — DNA expertise is highly subspecialized; a probabilistic genotyping critic is not the same as a touch-DNA transfer specialist.
6. **Budget approval** — DNA expert engagements can range from $5,000 to $50,000+ depending on scope.

Mark in the audit report: `[EXPERT REQUIRED — retain defense [category] expert; see references/defense-dna-experts.md]`.

## Expert Categories

### 1. Probabilistic Genotyping Critics (STRmix / TrueAllele)

**Typical engagement:** Review of the State's STRmix or TrueAllele run files, internal validation studies, MCMC convergence diagnostics, NOC sensitivity, propositions tested, LR verbal-scale translation. May provide testimony on the methodology's reliability and on the as-applied analysis in the specific case.

**What they provide:**
- Independent re-analysis using alternative software (e.g., EuroForMix) for cross-system comparison.
- Identification of validation gaps for the operating point of the case sample.
- Source-code review (if disclosure obtained under protective order).
- Critique of NOC determination and sensitivity testing.
- Testimony at *Daubert/Foret* hearing and at trial.

**Sourcing channels:**
- NACDL DNA Resource Center / DNA Task Force.
- Forensic Science Standards Board listings.
- University forensic-science programs (academic experts, often available for defense).
- *Daubert Tracker* / Westlaw expert databases — search for prior PG-related testimony.
- Forensic-genetics academic publication authors with critical or nuanced views on PG.

### 2. Mixture Interpretation Specialists

**Typical engagement:** Independent review of EPGs, NOC determination, major/minor designation, application of lab SOP and SWGDAM 2017 guidelines. May testify on whether the mixture was interpretable and on the analyst's conformance to standards.

**What they provide:**
- Independent NOC determination and sensitivity analysis.
- EPG-level review of every called peak.
- Critique of mixture interpretation against SWGDAM 2017 and ISFG recommendations.
- Testimony on the limits of mixture interpretation and the operating range of the lab's method.

**Sourcing channels:**
- Innocence Project DNA Network (cold-case and post-conviction work).
- Academic forensic-genetics programs.
- Former crime-lab analysts who have left law-enforcement employment.
- NACDL DNA Resource Center.

### 3. Touch / Transfer DNA Specialists

**Typical engagement:** Review of evidence-collection methodology, transfer/persistence/shedder issues, secondary-transfer literature, activity-level inference limits. May testify on the limits of touch-DNA interpretation and on alternative explanations.

**What they provide:**
- Literature review on transfer and persistence studies relevant to the case.
- Analysis of whether the lab's conclusion overreaches into activity-level inference.
- Testimony on shedder variability and the lack of empirical support for activity claims based on touch DNA alone.

**Sourcing channels:**
- Academic forensic-genetics researchers publishing in this area (e.g., investigators associated with the ISFG DNA Commission on transfer/persistence).
- Innocence Project DNA Network.
- NACDL.

### 4. IGG / Genetic Genealogy Specialists

**Typical engagement:** Review of the IGG workflow — SNP profile generation, database queries, family-tree construction, candidate identification. May testify on the methodology's validation status, the privacy/4th-Amendment scope, and the genealogist's methodology in the specific case.

**What they provide:**
- Independent review of the SNP profile generation lab's methodology and accreditation.
- Critique of the genealogist's working notes, draft trees, and candidate-list filtering.
- Testimony at *Daubert/Foret* hearing on IGG methodology.
- Evaluation of database opt-in compliance and TOS-violation issues.

**Sourcing channels:**
- Limited pool — IGG is a young field; defense-side experts are few.
- Academic experts with genetic-genealogy or population-genetics backgrounds.
- Some former private-lab employees.
- Privacy / civil-liberties organization referrals (ACLU, EFF) for the 4th-Amendment side.
- NACDL emerging-technology committees.

### 5. Lab QA / Accreditation Specialists

**Typical engagement:** Review of lab accreditation status, internal validation studies, SOPs, contamination event logs, proficiency-test history, corrective-action records. May testify on whether the lab is operating within its accreditation scope and SWGDAM standards.

**What they provide:**
- Audit of lab QA documentation against ANAB/ASCLD-LAB and FBI QAS standards.
- Identification of contamination events, equipment failures, or SOP deviations relevant to the case.
- Testimony on the lab's QA posture as relevant to the reliability of the specific result.

**Sourcing channels:**
- Former ANAB / ASCLD-LAB assessors.
- Former lab directors and QA managers (post-employment).
- Academic forensic-science programs with QA emphasis.

### 6. Population Genetics / Statistics Specialists

**Typical engagement:** Review of statistical computation, population databases, theta application, verbal-scale translation, relative-as-alternative analysis. May testify on statistical overstatement and appropriate framing of the DNA statistic.

**What they provide:**
- Independent recomputation of the statistic with alternative parameters (theta, population, NOC).
- Critique of the lab's population database selection.
- Testimony on the prosecutor's-fallacy risk and proper Bayesian framing.

**Sourcing channels:**
- Academic population-genetics programs.
- Authors of foundational forensic-statistics literature.
- Academic statisticians with forensic engagement.

## Sourcing Organizations Table

| Organization | Type | What They Provide |
|---|---|---|
| **NACDL DNA Resource Center** | National defense bar organization | Expert referrals, training, motion templates, amicus support |
| **NACDL DNA Task Force** | NACDL subgroup | Specialized referrals for PG, mixture, IGG cases |
| **Innocence Project DNA Network** | Non-profit / academic collaboration | Expert referrals for post-conviction and difficult-case DNA work; some pre-conviction support |
| **National Institute of Justice — Forensic Technology Center of Excellence** | Federal | Standards and validation studies; not direct experts but useful resource |
| **ISFG (International Society for Forensic Genetics)** | International scientific society | Member directory; published standards |
| **AAFS (American Academy of Forensic Sciences)** | U.S. forensic-science society | Member directory; specialized sections |
| **ABC (American Board of Criminalistics)** | Certification body | List of board-certified DNA analysts (cross-reference for State experts; some defense work) |
| **University Forensic Science Programs** | Academic | Faculty with research-based expertise; often available for defense work |
| ***Daubert Tracker*** / Westlaw / Lexis expert databases | Commercial | Prior testimony, exclusion decisions, expert lists |
| **ACLU / EFF** | Civil-liberties | Privacy- and constitutional-side expertise on IGG, database searches |
| **State public-defender training programs** | State | Local expert referrals; reduced-rate engagements for indigent defense |

## Practical Engagement Checklist

- [ ] Define the scope of the engagement (review only, written report, testimony at *Daubert*, testimony at trial)
- [ ] Verify current availability and rates
- [ ] Conflict-check against case, lab, analyst, defendant
- [ ] Review CV and recent testimony list
- [ ] Check for adverse appellate decisions
- [ ] Confirm specialty match
- [ ] Obtain budget approval (court-appointed: ex parte motion for funds under *Ake v. Oklahoma*, 470 U.S. 68 (1985); private retainer: client approval)
- [ ] Provide the expert: complete lab file, validation studies, SOPs, analyst CV, EPGs/raw data, software run files, chain of custody, charging documents
- [ ] Establish protective-order needs (source code, proprietary methods)
- [ ] Set deadlines aligned with motion schedule

## Ex Parte Motion for Defense DNA Expert Funds

For indigent defendants, *Ake v. Oklahoma*, 470 U.S. 68 (1985), and progeny establish a due-process right to defense expert assistance when expert testimony is reasonably necessary to a meaningful defense. Louisiana courts apply the standard via La. C.Cr.P. and indigent-defense funding mechanisms.

**Practice points:**
- File ex parte to preserve work-product privilege.
- Specify the expert category and estimated cost.
- Identify the specific DNA issues that make expert assistance necessary.
- Cite *Ake* and Louisiana case law applying it to DNA experts.
