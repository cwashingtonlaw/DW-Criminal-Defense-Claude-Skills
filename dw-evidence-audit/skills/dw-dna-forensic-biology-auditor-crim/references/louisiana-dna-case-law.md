# Louisiana DNA Case Law — Reference

Legal-standards reference for **DNA admissibility and challenge** under Louisiana law and 5th Circuit federal practice. Always confirm currency before relying on case law in motions — annotate `[VERIFY CURRENT]` where appropriate.

---

## 1. Core Expert Reliability Standards

### *State v. Foret*, 628 So. 2d 1116 (La. 1993)
Louisiana's adopted *Daubert* standard. Held that **La. C.E. Art. 702** requires the trial court to act as gatekeeper, assessing whether proffered expert scientific testimony rests on a reliable foundation and is relevant to the task at hand.

The *Foret* factors (parallel to *Daubert*):
1. Whether the theory or technique can be (and has been) tested.
2. Whether it has been subjected to peer review and publication.
3. The known or potential rate of error and the existence and maintenance of standards controlling the technique's operation.
4. General acceptance in the relevant scientific community.

The Louisiana Supreme Court has emphasized that the trial court has broad discretion in the gatekeeping role, and an appellate court reviews for abuse of discretion.

**Application to DNA:** *Foret* is the operative state-law vehicle for excluding unreliable DNA methodology — probabilistic genotyping outside validated range, IGG SNP work by an unaccredited methodology, low-template / LCN DNA without proper validation, etc.

### *Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993)
The federal Rule 702 reliability standard. Controls in 5th Circuit federal practice; cited persuasively in Louisiana state courts pre- and post-*Foret*.

### *General Electric Co. v. Joiner*, 522 U.S. 136 (1997)
Standard of review for *Daubert* gatekeeping decisions: abuse of discretion. Trial court is given substantial deference.

### *Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael*, 526 U.S. 137 (1999)
Extended *Daubert* gatekeeping to all expert testimony (technical, specialized), not just scientific. Relevant to DNA-analyst testimony that crosses into technical and applied disciplines (e.g., probabilistic genotyping software operation).

### La. C.E. Art. 702 — Testimony by Experts
> A witness who is qualified as an expert by knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education may testify in the form of an opinion or otherwise if: (1) The expert's scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge will help the trier of fact to understand the evidence or to determine a fact in issue; (2) The testimony is based on sufficient facts or data; (3) The testimony is the product of reliable principles and methods; and (4) The expert has reliably applied the principles and methods to the facts of the case.

The four-prong text mirrors federal Rule 702 (as amended 2000 and 2023).

### La. C.E. Art. 703 — Bases of Expert Opinion
Permits an expert to base an opinion on facts or data that the expert has been made aware of or has reasonably relied on in the field. Relevant where the State's analyst relies on lab reports, validation studies, or SOPs not personally created by the analyst.

### La. C.E. Art. 704 — Opinion on Ultimate Issue
Permits opinion on ultimate issue (with limits in criminal cases on opinion regarding mental state). For DNA, the analyst's opinion on whether the defendant is the source is permitted but constrained by foundational reliability.

### La. C.E. Art. 705 — Disclosure of Underlying Facts
The State must disclose the underlying facts and data on cross-examination if not on direct. Relevant to compelling production of the analyst's bench notes and raw data.

## 2. Brady, Giglio, and Disclosure

### *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83 (1963)
Suppression of evidence favorable to the accused violates due process when the evidence is material to guilt or punishment. **Applied to DNA:** the State must disclose:
- Lab reports, both draft and final.
- Bench notes.
- Validation studies.
- Proficiency-test history of the analyst.
- Lab corrective-action reports and contamination event logs.
- The analyst's CV and personnel file.
- Internal QA audit findings.
- Source code of probabilistic genotyping software when accessible.
- IGG genealogist's full workfile, candidate list, and family-tree drafts.

### *Giglio v. United States*, 405 U.S. 150 (1972)
Impeachment evidence material to witness credibility falls within *Brady*. **Applied to DNA analysts:** prior disciplinary action, prior testimony where the analyst's conclusions were rejected by a court, history of proficiency-test failures, prior false statements.

### *Kyles v. Whitley*, 514 U.S. 419 (1995)
Brady obligation extends to evidence in the possession of any agent of the government, including the lab — even if the prosecutor was not personally aware.

### La. C.Cr.P. Art. 718, 721, 722, 723 — Discovery
Louisiana's discovery statute. Article 718 requires disclosure of scientific reports and test results. Article 723 requires disclosure of expert reports.

### *Arizona v. Youngblood*, 488 U.S. 51 (1988)
Bad-faith destruction of potentially exculpatory evidence violates due process. **Applied to DNA:** consumption of evidence sample without preservation for defense re-test may trigger *Youngblood*. La. R.S. 15:621 and 15:622 govern DNA preservation in Louisiana.

### La. R.S. 15:621 et seq. — DNA Preservation
Louisiana statutory framework for DNA evidence preservation post-conviction. Mandates preservation of biological evidence and procedures for defense access.

## 3. Confrontation and Hearsay

### *Crawford v. Washington*, 541 U.S. 36 (2004)
Testimonial statements are inadmissible unless the declarant testifies and is subject to confrontation. Established the modern framework.

### *Melendez-Diaz v. Massachusetts*, 557 U.S. 305 (2009)
Held that a lab certificate of analysis is testimonial; the analyst must testify. **Applied to DNA:** the analyst who performed and signed the DNA report must testify; affidavit-only admission violates confrontation.

### *Bullcoming v. New Mexico*, 564 U.S. 647 (2011)
Surrogate testimony by a non-performing analyst is insufficient. The actual analyst who performed the test must testify, or a sufficient substitute who can speak to the methodology.

### *Williams v. Illinois*, 567 U.S. 50 (2012)
Fractured opinion on whether an expert may rely on a non-testifying analyst's report in forming an opinion. Continues to be litigated. **Applied to DNA:** the State may attempt to put a technical leader on the stand to testify to a profile generated by a different analyst; the defense should object under *Bullcoming / Williams*.

### La. C.E. Arts. 801–807 — Hearsay
Lab reports are hearsay unless within an exception. La. C.E. Art. 803(6) (business records) is sometimes invoked, but confrontation overrides business-records admission for testimonial lab reports per *Melendez-Diaz*.

## 4. Suppression and Search Authority

### La. C.Cr.P. Art. 162 — Search Warrants
Louisiana statutory framework for search warrants. Particularity required.

### La. C.Cr.P. Art. 703 — Motion to Suppress
Vehicle for challenging evidence obtained in violation of the Fourth Amendment, the Louisiana Constitution Art. I § 5, or other rights.

### *Carpenter v. United States*, 585 U.S. 296 (2018)
Held that the third-party doctrine does not extend to historical CSLI. Implications for DNA-database searches (IGG) are still being litigated; the third-party-doctrine reasoning applies analogically to DTC genetic databases.

### *Maryland v. King*, 569 U.S. 435 (2013)
Held that arrestee DNA collection at booking for serious offenses is a reasonable booking procedure. Limited; does not authorize DTC database searches.

### *Mincey v. Arizona*, 437 U.S. 385 (1978)
Crime-scene exception to warrant requirement is narrow; warrant required for most evidence collection.

### Consent searches — *Schneckloth v. Bustamonte*, 412 U.S. 218 (1973)
Voluntariness assessed under totality of circumstances. Relevant where a reference sample was obtained by consent.

## 6. Expert Qualification

### Cases on DNA Analyst Qualification
A DNA analyst's qualification under La. C.E. Art. 702 generally requires:
- Bachelor's degree in biology, biochemistry, forensic science, or related field.
- Training in molecular biology, genetics, statistics, population genetics.
- Lab-specific training on the specific kit and methodology used.
- Successful completion of proficiency tests.
- Ongoing continuing education.

Specific deficiencies that can support a challenge:
- No training on the specific software at issue (e.g., analyst trained on STRmix v2.5 but lab uses v2.8 with different algorithm).
- No proficiency-test record on the specific kit used.
- Recent transition from a different forensic discipline (e.g., trace evidence) with limited DNA-section seasoning.
- Failed proficiency tests not adequately resolved.
- Prior testimony excluded by another court.

### Voir Dire of the DNA Analyst
Conducted on motion before the analyst testifies as expert. Defense should:
- Probe specific kit / software / sample-type training.
- Probe proficiency-test history on the specific operations relevant to the case.
- Probe prior testimony — has the analyst testified in similar cases? Has the analyst's testimony been excluded?
- Probe continuing education and currency of training.
