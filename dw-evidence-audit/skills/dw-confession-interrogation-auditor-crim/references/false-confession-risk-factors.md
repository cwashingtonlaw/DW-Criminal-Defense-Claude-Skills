# False Confession Risk Factors Reference

## False Confession Taxonomy

| Type | Definition | Mechanism | Key Indicators |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Voluntary False Confession** | Defendant confesses without coercion, for reasons unrelated to police pressure | Desire for attention, mental illness, protecting another person, belief that confessing will end an overwhelming situation | No significant interrogation pressure; defendant initiated the confession; confession contains obvious impossibilities; defendant has history of false reporting |
| **Compliant False Confession** | Defendant confesses to escape the interrogation, knowing the confession is false | Exhaustion, fear, implied or express promises, learned helplessness, desire to end the interrogation | Extended interrogation; significant coercion factors; defendant recants shortly after interrogation; confession is thin on details |
| **Persuaded (Internalized) False Confession** | Defendant comes to believe they committed the crime, despite having no genuine memory of it | Memory distrust, suggestibility, sleep deprivation, psychological manipulation, mental illness | Defendant expresses genuine uncertainty about their own memory; "I must have done it if you say so"; post-confession behavior consistent with genuine belief of guilt |

## False Confession Risk Factor Scoring

| Risk Factor | Present? | Weight | Cumulative Risk |
|------------|---------|--------|----------------|
| **Youth (under 18)** | Y/N | +3 | Research: juveniles are 2-3x more likely to give false confessions (Drizin & Leo, 2004) |
| **Intellectual disability (IQ < 70)** | Y/N | +4 | Research: individuals with ID are significantly overrepresented in false confession cases; compliance tendency; suggestibility; desire to please authority |
| **Mental illness (active at time of interrogation)** | Y/N | +3 | Psychosis, severe depression, anxiety, PTSD may impair reality testing, increase compliance, distort memory |
| **Substance influence at time of interrogation** | Y/N | +2 | Impairs judgment, increases suggestibility, may cause memory gaps that interrogators exploit |
| **Sleep deprivation (> 24 hours awake)** | Y/N | +3 | Research: sleep deprivation significantly increases false confession risk (Frenda et al., 2016); impairs executive function, decision-making, and resistance to pressure |
| **Lengthy interrogation (> 6 hours)** | Y/N | +2 | Mean interrogation length in proven false confession cases: 16.3 hours (Drizin & Leo, 2004); duration alone is a major risk factor |
| **False evidence ploy used** | Y/N | +3 | Research: false evidence ploys significantly increase false confession rates, especially with vulnerable suspects (Kassin & Kiechel, 1996; Perillo & Kassin, 2011) |
| **Minimization used (implied leniency)** | Y/N | +2 | Research: minimization functions as an implied promise and increases confession rates in both guilty and innocent subjects (Kassin & McNall, 1991) |
| **Alternative question used** | Y/N | +2 | The Reid alternative question ("Was it planned or spur of the moment?") forces a choice between two guilty scenarios; innocent suspects may choose the "lesser" option |
| **No prior criminal justice experience** | Y/N | +1 | First-time suspects are more vulnerable to interrogation pressure |
| **Threats to family / third parties** | Y/N | +3 | Creates overwhelming motivation to end the interrogation at any cost |
| **Isolation from support (> 8 hours)** | Y/N | +2 | Extended isolation increases despair and compliance |

### Risk Assessment Scale:
- **Score 0-5:** LOW false confession risk — standard interrogation without significant vulnerability factors
- **Score 6-12:** MODERATE risk — multiple vulnerability or coercion factors present; warrant careful scrutiny
- **Score 13-20:** HIGH risk — significant combination of vulnerability and coercion factors; strong basis for false confession argument
- **Score 21+:** CRITICAL risk — extreme false confession risk; multiple vulnerability factors combined with coercive techniques; expert testimony on false confessions should be strongly considered

## Contamination Analysis — The Critical Distinction

The most important question in false confession analysis: **Did the defendant provide details that only the true perpetrator would know, or were those details fed by the interrogators?**

| Indicator | Suggests True Confession | Suggests Contaminated/False Confession |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **"Inside" details** | Defendant provides specific crime details that were not publicly available and were not provided by interrogators | Defendant's "inside" details match only information that was disclosed by interrogators during the interrogation |
| **Detail accuracy** | Defendant's account is accurate on verifiable details (location, sequence, physical evidence) | Defendant's account contains errors on verifiable details, or details are vague/generic |
| **Post-admission narrative** | Defendant provides a rich, detailed, coherent narrative after the initial admission | Defendant's post-admission narrative is thin, inconsistent, or relies on interrogator prompts and corrections |
| **Crime scene knowledge** | Defendant describes the crime scene accurately, including details not visible in photographs or news reports | Defendant's crime scene description matches only publicly available information or information the interrogators provided |
| **Spontaneous corrections** | Defendant spontaneously corrects interrogator's incorrect assumptions | Defendant agrees with interrogator's incorrect assumptions — a hallmark of compliance |
| **Motive / emotional detail** | Defendant provides personal motive and emotional context that rings true | Defendant's stated motive is vague, inconsistent, or mirrors the interrogator's suggested theme |
| **Leading vs. independent** | Key details emerge from defendant's unprompted narrative | Key details emerge only after interrogator provides them — "Did you use a knife?" "Yes" (rather than defendant independently identifying the weapon) |
| **Fit with evidence** | Confession is consistent with physical evidence, timeline, and witness accounts | Confession contradicts physical evidence, timeline, or witness accounts on key points |

**For each "inside" detail the State claims proves the confession is genuine, audit whether the detail could have been:**
1. Fed by the interrogator (explicitly or through leading questions)
2. Inferred from publicly available information (news, social media)
3. Obtained from co-defendant statements shared with the defendant
4. Guessed (generic details that would be true in many similar crimes)
5. Genuinely known only to the perpetrator
