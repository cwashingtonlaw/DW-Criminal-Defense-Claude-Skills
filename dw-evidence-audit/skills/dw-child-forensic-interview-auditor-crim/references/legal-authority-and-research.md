# Louisiana, Federal & Research Authorities

This reference compiles the legal and scientific authorities used in the Forensic Interview Audit Report's Legal Analysis section and to support expert testimony challenges.

## Louisiana Evidence Code

**La. C.E. Art. 702 — Testimony by Experts**
- Requires expert testimony on reliability of interview methodology
- Your report can serve as basis for expert's opinion
- Expert can testify that NICHD or Cognitive Interview protocols are scientifically sound; this interview deviated

**La. C.E. Art. 801-803 — Hearsay Definition & Exceptions**
- Art. 801: Hearsay definition (out-of-court statement offered for truth)
- Art. 803: Exceptions (excited utterance, present sense impression, statements against interest, child abuse statements)
- Child's forensic interview statements may qualify for exception, BUT suggestive interviewing undermines reliability guarantee

**La. C.E. Art. 705 — Basis of Opinion Testimony**
- Expert can base opinion on interview transcript/recording, protocols, research
- Does not need to personally interview child

## Louisiana Criminal Code & Procedure

**La. Ch.C. Art. 329 — Child Hearsay in Child in Need of Care (CINC)**
- In child protection cases (not criminal cases), broader hearsay admissibility
- Note: If criminal case, federal Confrontation Clause applies instead

**State v. Foret, 628 So.2d 1116 (La. 1993)**
- Louisiana adoption of Daubert standard for expert testimony reliability
- Interview methodology must satisfy Daubert: error rates, peer review, testing, acceptance in field

**State v. Langley — Louisiana Child Competency Standard**
- Child witness competency requires capacity to perceive, remember, narrate, and understand duty to tell truth
- Competency hearing may be necessary
- Your report assesses competency issues

## U.S. Supreme Court

**Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36 (2004)**
- Confrontation Clause: Testimonial hearsay requires live testimony subject to cross-examination
- Child's forensic interview may be "testimonial" if primary purpose is to establish/prove abuse for prosecution

**Davis v. Washington, 547 U.S. 813 (2006)**
- Refines testimonial vs. non-testimonial distinction
- Objective reasonable view: Would reasonable person perceive statement as intending to create evidence for prosecution?
- Child's statement to forensic interviewer is likely **testimonial**

**Idaho v. Wright, 497 U.S. 805 (1990)**
- Where child hearsay is excluded by Confrontation Clause, reliability may be established by "particularized guarantees of trustworthiness"
- Suggestive interview **undermines** such guarantees
- Your report documents failures of trustworthiness

## Research Authorities (for expert testimony)

**Ceci, Stephen J. & Bruck, Maggie (1995) — "Jeopardy in the Courtroom"**
- Landmark empirical research on false memory implantation in children
- Repeated suggestive interviews cause false memory development
- Younger children (3-6) more vulnerable; even 9-year-olds show 30% false memory rate after repeated suggestion
- Citation: American Psychological Association; peer-reviewed literature

**Lamb, Michael E. et al. — NICHD Protocol Research**
- 20+ studies validating NICHD protocol's effectiveness
- Open-ended questions yield more accurate information, fewer false details
- Citation: Multiple journals (Law and Human Behavior, Child Development, etc.)

**Lyon, Thomas D. — Child Witness Research**
- Research on child credibility, competency, and developmental vulnerabilities
- Expert on cross-examination effectiveness with child witnesses
- Citation: Numerous peer-reviewed articles; frequently called as expert witness

## Legal Analysis Section (for the Audit Report)

When drafting the audit report's Legal Analysis section, use the following framework:

**Daubert Reliability Issues (State v. Foret, 628 So.2d 1116 (La. 1993)):**
- Does the interview methodology satisfy Daubert standards?
- Are there recognized error rates or publication in peer-reviewed literature?
- Is there general acceptance in the scientific community?
- **Conclusion:** Interview methodology is [RELIABLE/UNRELIABLE] under Daubert.

**Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36 (2004) — Confrontation Clause:**
- Are the child's statements "testimonial"?
- If so, child hearsay may violate Sixth Amendment right to confront witnesses
- **Conclusion:** Statements are [TESTIMONIAL/NON-TESTIMONIAL]; therefore [ADMISSIBLE/INADMISSIBLE].

**Davis v. Washington, 547 U.S. 813 (2006) — Testimonial vs. Non-Testimonial:**
- Does the statement reflect objective reasonable view of its primary purpose?
- Was it to establish/prove past events potentially relevant to criminal prosecution?
- **Conclusion:** Statements are [TESTIMONIAL/NON-TESTIMONIAL].

**Idaho v. Wright, 497 U.S. 805 (1990) — Child Hearsay Reliability:**
- Are there circumstances rendering statement reliable despite lack of cross-examination?
- Particularized guarantees of trustworthiness?
- **Conclusion:** Statements have [ADEQUATE/INADEQUATE] reliability guarantees.

**La. C.E. Art. 801-803 — Hearsay Exceptions:**
- Excited utterance (Art. 803(2)): Contemporaneous emotional state?
- Present sense impression (Art. 803(1)): Immediate perception?
- Child abuse exception (Art. 803(8)): Corroboration requirement?
- **Conclusion:** Statements may qualify for hearsay exception: [YES/NO]; however, reliability issues identified above render exception problematic.

**La. C.E. Art. 702 — Expert Testimony on Interview Reliability:**
- Can expert testify that interview was suggestive?
- Can expert testify about false memory research (Ceci & Bruck, Lamb, Lyon)?
- **Conclusion:** [EXPERT TESTIMONY ADMISSIBLE/INADMISSIBLE on interview methodology].

**La. Ch.C. Art. 329 — Child Hearsay in CINC Cases:**
- In child protection cases (not criminal), relaxed hearsay rules apply
- If applicable: Does child's forensic interview qualify?
- **Conclusion:** [Art. 329 applies/does not apply]; hearsay may be [ADMISSIBLE/INADMISSIBLE].

**State v. Langley — Louisiana Child Witness Competency:**
- Does child have capacity to perceive, remember, and communicate?
- **Conclusion:** Competency is [ESTABLISHED/QUESTIONABLE].
