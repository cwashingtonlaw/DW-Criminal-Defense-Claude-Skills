# Scientific Reports — Forensic Reliability Standards

## Quick Reference — Key Scientific Reports on Forensic Reliability

| Report | Year | Issuing Body | Key Findings |
|--------|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Strengthening Forensic Science in the United States** | 2009 | National Academy of Sciences (NAS) | Found that most forensic disciplines (except nuclear DNA) lack rigorous scientific validation; recommended creation of independent forensic science entity |
| **Forensic Science in Criminal Courts** | 2016 | President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST) | Evaluated foundational validity of six forensic feature-comparison disciplines; found DNA mixtures and firearms analysis have limited validity; recommended empirical testing |
| **DOJ Uniform Language for Testimony and Reports** | 2018+ | Department of Justice | Established boundaries on what forensic examiners may claim in testimony; prohibits "to the exclusion of all others" and similar absolute language |
| **FBI Hair Microscopy Review** | 2015 | FBI / Innocence Project / NACDL | Found FBI examiners gave erroneous hair comparison testimony in over 90% of cases reviewed |
| **FBI/Noblis Black Box Study** | 2011 | FBI / Noblis | Established latent print false positive error rate of approximately 0.1% (1 in 1,000) |
| **Ames Laboratory Firearms Study** | 2014 | Ames National Laboratory | Found firearms comparison false positive rate of approximately 1.01% |
| **Miami-Dade Firearms Study** | 2014 | Miami-Dade Crime Lab | Found firearms comparison false positive rate of approximately 2.2% |

## Error Rate Reference by Discipline

Use these established error rates when challenging methodology reliability (Daubert Factor 3):

### Latent Fingerprints
- **FBI/Noblis Black Box Study (2011)**: ~0.1% false positive rate (1 in 1,000)
- **Note**: Error rate higher for partial prints, distorted prints, or prints on difficult substrates
- **Context**: Black Box study involved full prints under laboratory conditions; real-world error rates likely higher

### Firearms / Toolmarks
- **Ames Laboratory Study (2014)**: ~1.01% false positive rate
- **Miami-Dade Study (2014)**: ~2.2% false positive rate
- **PCAST Finding**: Limited foundational validity; noted methodological concerns with error rate studies
- **DOJ Uniform Language**: Prohibits claims of "exclusion of all others"

### DNA Analysis
- **Single-source profiles**: Extremely low error rate; well-established through validation studies
- **Complex mixtures (3+ contributors)**: PCAST found foundational validity lacking for some probabilistic genotyping approaches
- **Low template DNA**: Error rates not well-established; stochastic effects significant
- **Touch DNA**: Secondary transfer possible; cannot determine when deposited or biological source from profile alone

### Bloodstain Pattern Analysis
- **Osborne et al. (2014)**: High inter-analyst disagreement on pattern classification and origin
- **NAS Report (2009)**: Characterized BPA opinions as "more subjective than scientific"
- **No established error rate**: Formal false positive rate data unavailable

### Hair Microscopy
- **FBI Review (2015)**: >90% of reviewed cases contained erroneous testimony
- **Status**: FBI and DOJ no longer support microscopic hair comparison; no longer used in federal cases

### Digital Forensics
- **No universal error rate**: Varies by tool, device, operating system, and methodology
- **Validation concerns**: Tools must be validated against known datasets
- **Timestamp reliability**: Timestamps can be manipulated by users or altered by system processes

### Toxicology
- **Error rate**: Depends on methodology (immunoassay vs. GC-MS vs. LC-MS/MS)
- **Back-calculation**: Post-mortem redistribution can make ante-mortem concentration estimation unreliable
- **Impairment determination**: Blood concentration alone does not establish impairment

### Forensic Pathology
- **Cause/manner of death**: Opinion-based classification, not scientific determination
- **Inter-examiner disagreement**: Qualified pathologists may reach different conclusions on same case
- **Contextual bias**: Knowledge of case circumstances before autopsy can influence interpretation

### Accident Reconstruction
- **Error rate**: Varies by methodology and assumptions
- **Coefficient of friction**: Assumptions about this parameter produce different results
- **No universal standard**: Varies by ACTAR accreditation and methodology used

### Cell Site / Geolocation
- **Limited accuracy**: Does not establish precise location, only connection to cell sector
- **Coverage variation**: Sector coverage ranges from hundreds of meters to several miles
- **No standardized methodology**: Vendor-specific tools with varying validation standards
