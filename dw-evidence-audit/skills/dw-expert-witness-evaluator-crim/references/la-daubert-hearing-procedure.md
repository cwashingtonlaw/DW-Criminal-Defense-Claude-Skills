# Louisiana Daubert/Foret Hearing Procedure

This reference explains the procedural mechanics of a Daubert/Foret hearing in Louisiana criminal court — distinct from the doctrinal reliability framework in `daubert-foret-framework.md`, which governs the substantive analysis.

**Use this file when:** preparing for a hearing, advising on strategic timing of a motion, anticipating burden allocation, or structuring the appellate record.

---

## 1. Statutory and Jurisprudential Framework

### Governing authorities

- **La. C.E. Art. 702** — the substantive admissibility standard (relevance + reliability + reliable application)
- **La. C.E. Art. 104(A)** — preliminary questions of admissibility decided by the court
- **La. C.E. Art. 705** — disclosure of facts or data underlying expert opinion
- **La. C.Cr.P. Art. 719 / 723** — discovery obligations for expert testimony in criminal cases
- ***State v. Foret*, 628 So.2d 1116 (La. 1993)** — adopting the Daubert framework
- ***Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc.*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993)**
- ***General Electric Co. v. Joiner*, 522 U.S. 136 (1997)** — analytical gap; abuse-of-discretion review
- ***Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael*, 526 U.S. 137 (1999)** — extension to non-scientific expertise

### What Foret adopted, what it modified

Louisiana adopted the Daubert reliability framework but did not abandon the *Frye* "general acceptance" inquiry — it preserved general acceptance as **one factor among several**. The Five-Factor analysis (testability, peer review, error rate, standards, general acceptance) is the working framework Louisiana courts apply.

## 2. Timing of the Motion

### When to file

| Case Posture | Filing Recommendation |
|--------------|----------------------|
| **Discovery received; trial date set** | File no later than the deadline for pretrial motions; many courts set a separate Daubert deadline |
| **Discovery incomplete** | File a placeholder motion to preserve the issue; reserve right to supplement |
| **Late-disclosed expert** | Move first to exclude under Art. 723 disclosure violation; alternatively or additionally file Daubert motion |
| **Mid-trial discovery of new methodology issue** | Object contemporaneously; request mid-trial Daubert hearing outside jury presence under Art. 104(A) |

### Pretrial vs. mid-trial hearings

The strongly preferred posture is **pretrial**, before jury selection, because:

- Mid-trial hearings disrupt jury management
- Mid-trial exclusion creates mistrial risk if the State has previewed the testimony in opening
- Pretrial rulings allow both parties to plan trial strategy around the ruling
- The court can hold a more thorough hearing without jury management pressure

If the court sets the Daubert motion for hearing on the day of trial or as part of trial, **object on the record** and request a separate pretrial setting. Preserve for appeal the prejudice of forcing the issue into trial proceedings.

### Deadlines to calendar

- Local rule deadline for pretrial motions
- Court-set Daubert deadline (if any)
- 30 days before trial (default rule of thumb absent local rule)
- Discovery deadline for State's expert disclosures (Art. 723)

## 3. Burden of Proof and Persuasion

### Who bears the burden

**The proponent of the testimony bears the burden of establishing admissibility.** In a criminal case where the State seeks to introduce expert testimony, the State bears the burden. *See Daubert*, 509 U.S. at 592 n.10.

This is **threshold-significant**: at the hearing, the defense's role is not to "prove unreliability" but to test whether the State has carried its burden.

### Standard of proof

**Preponderance of the evidence.** *Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171, 175-76 (1987) (Art. 104(a) preliminary questions); applied in Louisiana criminal practice.

The State must show by a preponderance that the testimony is:
1. Based on sufficient facts or data (Art. 702(2))
2. The product of reliable principles and methods (Art. 702(3))
3. Reliably applied to the facts of this case (Art. 702(4))

### What the State must produce

- The expert's CV
- The expert's report
- The methodology used (often through expert testimony)
- Foundation that methodology meets reliability standards (factor-by-factor showing or stipulation)

If the State does not produce sufficient foundation testimony, **move for exclusion at the close of the State's evidence** before defense puts on a rebuttal expert. This forces the court to rule on whether the State carried its burden.

## 4. Evidentiary Standards at the Hearing

### Rules of evidence apply, but loosely

La. C.E. Art. 104(A) governs preliminary questions: "the court is not bound by the rules of evidence except those with respect to privileges." This means:

- Hearsay objections: **generally overruled** for foundation purposes
- Authentication: **relaxed** — courts accept reasonable indicia
- Best evidence: **relaxed**
- Privileges: **fully enforced**

### What this means strategically

- **Defense may use prior testimony transcripts** without strict Art. 613 prior-inconsistent-statement foundation
- **Defense may use scientific literature directly** without strict Art. 803(18) treatise authentication, though authentication strengthens record for appeal
- **Defense may rely on lab records, accreditation documents, and similar foundation documents** even if not strictly authenticated, as long as authenticity is not seriously contested

### Building the record for appeal

Although the rules of evidence are relaxed at the hearing, the appellate court reviews on the record made. Therefore:

- **Authenticate exhibits anyway** when feasible — this protects the record
- **Make every objection on the record** even if denied — preserves issue for appeal
- **Request specific findings** at the close of the hearing (see Section 6)
- **Order the hearing transcript** promptly — needed for any post-hearing brief, supervisory writ, or appeal

## 5. The Hearing Itself

### Who attends

- Judge (no jury — this is a court determination under Art. 104(A))
- Court reporter
- Counsel for both sides
- Witness(es) — sequestration on motion
- Defendant — has the right to be present (La. C.Cr.P. Art. 831 / 832); confirm before hearing

### Sequencing within the hearing

1. **Preliminary matters** — exhibit pre-marking, sequestration, stipulations
2. **Proponent's case** — State calls expert; State puts on foundation testimony
3. **Opponent's cross** — defense cross of State's expert (centerpiece)
4. **Opponent's case** — defense calls rebuttal expert (if any); offers literature
5. **Proponent's rebuttal** — State may recall expert or respond
6. **Argument** — typically defense first (as movant), State responds, defense reply
7. **Ruling** — bench ruling, ruling under advisement, or written ruling later

### Common procedural moves

| Move | Purpose | Citation |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Sequestration of witnesses** | Prevent State expert from tailoring testimony | La. C.E. Art. 615 |
| **Motion to limit scope** | Cabin the State's foundation testimony | Art. 104; Art. 611 |
| **Offer of proof** | Preserve excluded testimony for appellate review | La. C.E. Art. 103(A)(2) |
| **Continuing objection** | Cover repeated foundation testimony without standing each time | Art. 103 |
| **Motion to strike** | Remove improperly admitted foundation testimony | Art. 103 |
| **Renewed motion at close** | Force ruling that State did not carry burden | Art. 702 |

## 6. Ruling Format and Findings

### Three forms of ruling

1. **Bench ruling** — court rules from the bench at the close of argument
2. **Ruling under advisement** — court takes the matter under advisement; defers ruling
3. **Written ruling** — court issues a written decision, typically with FOF/COL

### Requesting specific findings

Whenever the court rules, **defense should request on the record that the court make specific findings as to each Daubert factor** the defense raised. Sample on-record request:

> *"Your Honor, for the record, would the court make specific findings as to each of the five Daubert factors and the analytical gap analysis defense raised? This is for the appellate record."*

Specific factor findings make the ruling reviewable. A general ruling ("motion denied") makes the appellate court defer to the trial court under abuse-of-discretion review.

### Asking for written reasons if denied

If the court denies the motion from the bench without specific findings, request:

> *"Your Honor, defense respectfully requests written reasons for the ruling for purposes of appellate review."*

Most Louisiana trial courts will provide written reasons on request, particularly where the issue is methodologically substantial.

## 7. Standards of Review on Appeal / Writ

### Direct appeal after conviction

- **Standard:** Abuse of discretion. *Joiner*, 522 U.S. at 142; *State v. Foret*, 628 So.2d at 1123
- **What "abuse of discretion" means in practice:** the appellate court will reverse only if the trial court applied the wrong legal standard or made findings unsupported by the record
- **Implication:** the hearing record is decisive — if defense did not develop the record, the appellate court has nothing to review

### Pretrial supervisory writ

- **Available:** A defendant who loses a Daubert motion before trial may seek supervisory review of the ruling. La. C.Cr.P. Art. 912.1; La. Sup. Ct. Rule X
- **Standard:** Abuse of discretion at this stage as well, but the court of appeal can stay trial pending review
- **Strategic considerations:** writs are discouraged absent exceptional circumstances; reserve writ application for cases where the expert testimony is essentially case-dispositive (e.g., DNA in a case with no other inculpatory evidence)
- **Timing:** writs must be filed promptly after the ruling; typical 30-day deadline absent court-set deadline

### Federal habeas

- **Standard:** AEDPA deference (28 U.S.C. § 2254(d)) — federal court will not disturb state court's evidentiary ruling unless contrary to clearly established federal law or unreasonable application thereof
- **Practical reality:** Daubert claims rarely survive AEDPA review unless the admission was so prejudicial as to render the trial fundamentally unfair (due process)
- **Implication:** preserve the constitutional dimension at trial — argue the admission violates due process, not merely state evidentiary law

## 8. Discipline-Specific Procedural Notes

Different disciplines tend to draw different judicial responses at Louisiana Daubert hearings. Calibrate strategy accordingly.

| Discipline | Typical Judicial Posture | Strategic Implication |
|------------|--------------------------|------------------------|
| **DNA / forensic biology** | Generally admitted; courts familiar with framework | Focus on this case's specific application (mixture interpretation, low template, software validation), not the methodology in general |
| **Latent fingerprints (ACE-V)** | Increasingly contested; PCAST findings are widely cited | Worth a serious challenge; lock in error rate concessions |
| **Firearms / toolmarks** | Increasingly contested post-PCAST | Strong basis for at least scope limitation (no "to the exclusion of all others" language) |
| **Bloodstain pattern analysis** | Inconsistent; many courts skeptical | Worth a full challenge; subjective methodology with limited validation |
| **Cell site / geolocation** | Mixed; depends on whether opinion is granular ("the phone was in this room") or general ("the phone was in this cell sector") | Granular opinions are vulnerable; general opinions usually admitted |
| **Digital forensics** | Generally admitted for tool-based analyses; vulnerable when interpretation is offered as fact | Focus on interpretation vs. extraction |
| **Forensic pathology** | Generally admitted; cause/manner findings deferred to | Challenge specific opinions exceeding pathology expertise (e.g., shaken baby, drug pharmacology) |
| **Mental health (sanity / competency)** | Generally admitted; methodology rarely scrutinized | Focus on application — was the evaluation adequate? |
| **Accident reconstruction** | Mixed | Vulnerable when conclusions exceed physics-based analysis |
| **Toxicology** | Generally admitted for testing; vulnerable on impairment back-calculation | Focus on extrapolation and error rate |

## 9. Common Pitfalls

### Defense-side pitfalls

- **Failing to develop the record.** A Daubert motion that relies on argument alone, without hearing testimony, gives the trial court nothing to find — and the appellate court nothing to review.
- **Conceding qualification to attack methodology only.** If qualification is also weak, attack both. Qualification is the cleanest exclusion path.
- **Failing to request specific findings.** A general denial is harder to appeal than a denial with stated factor analysis.
- **Burning trial cross at the hearing.** Save discipline-specific cross seeds (`cross-exam-seeds.md`) for trial; the hearing requires factor-anchored cross only.
- **Not calendaring the post-hearing brief or writ deadlines.** Deadlines are short and unforgiving.

### Substantive pitfalls

- **Overstating PCAST.** Quote the report accurately — it does not say all forensic evidence is unreliable; it identifies specific disciplines lacking foundational validity.
- **Using outdated case law.** Some pre-Foret cases admitted methodologies that would not survive modern Daubert/Foret review; do not rely on those admissions.
- **Asking the court to retry the science.** Frame the motion as a gatekeeping question, not a verdict on whether the science is "good."
