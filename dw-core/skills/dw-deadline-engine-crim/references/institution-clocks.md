# Institution-of-Prosecution Clocks — La. C.Cr.P. Arts. 571–576

**MODULE B working reference.** These clocks limit how long the State has to *institute* prosecution (grand jury indictment or bill of information) after the offense. They are distinct from the trial-commencement clocks (Arts. 578–583) and from Art. 701 — never conflate them in the Deadline Clock Table.

> **Offense-date discipline (applies to every article on this page):** These articles have been amended repeatedly — offenses added to the no-limit list, the Art. 571.1 victim-age threshold raised, discovery-rule categories expanded. The version *in effect on the date of the offense* generally controls (an expansion enacted after the prior period already expired cannot revive it). Confirm the offense date FIRST, then confirm the article text as of that date before computing. Never assume the current text applies to an older offense.

---

## Art. 571 — Crimes with NO time limitation

No time limit on institution of prosecution for:

- Any crime for which the punishment **may be death or life imprisonment**
- **Forcible or second degree rape** (La. R.S. 14:42.1) — added by amendment; check effective date against offense date
- **Molestation of a juvenile or a person with a physical or mental disability** (La. R.S. 14:81.2) — added by amendment; check effective date against offense date

Clock table treatment: `Status: SATISFIED` is never available here — render the clock as **NO-LIMIT** in the Computed Expiry column with the statutory basis, so the attorney sees the analysis was run, not skipped.

## Art. 571.1 — Certain sex offenses (delayed-start 30-year clock)

For the enumerated sex offenses (defined by cross-reference to the sex-offense lists; includes sexual battery offenses, felony carnal knowledge, indecent behavior with juveniles, pornography involving juveniles, molestation, and others) involving an **underage victim**:

- Limitation period: **thirty (30) years**
- Start event: the period **does not begin until the victim attains the age of majority threshold in the applicable version** (current text: age eighteen; earlier versions used seventeen)
- This article has been amended multiple times (victim-age threshold, offense list). The applicable version is the one in effect on the offense date — pull the historical text before computing. Mark the start date `NEEDS-DATA` until the victim's date of birth is confirmed from a sourced document.

## Art. 572 — Noncapital offenses (the general tiers)

Prosecution must be instituted within the following periods after commission of the offense:

| Offense class | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Felony **necessarily** punishable by imprisonment at hard labor | **6 years** |
| Felony **not necessarily** punishable at hard labor | **4 years** |
| Misdemeanor punishable by fine or imprisonment, or both | **2 years** |
| Misdemeanor punishable only by fine or forfeiture | **6 months** |

**Classification step (mandatory):** pull the charged statute's penalty clause as of the offense date to determine "necessarily punishable at hard labor" (penalty says "shall be imprisoned at hard labor") vs. "not necessarily" (penalty says "with or without hard labor"). Cite the penalty clause in the table.

**DNA / new-evidence exception:** Art. 572 also permits prosecution of sex offenses beyond the limitation period where the offender's identity is established after expiration through a **DNA profile** (or newly discovered photographic/video evidence, per later amendment). If the State relies on this, the defense audit shifts to whether the statutory conditions for the exception were met — flag for attorney analysis rather than computing a date.

## Art. 573 — Delayed-running (discovery-rule) offenses

For designated offenses, the Art. 572 period **does not commence** until the predicate relationship or status ceases (or the offense is discoverable), including:

- Misappropriation by one entrusted by **office, employment, or fiduciary relationship** — clock runs from cessation of the relationship
- **Extortion or false accounting by a public officer or employee** in official capacity — clock runs from termination of the office/employment
- The article's category list has been amended over time (additional victim-vulnerability and abuse categories appear in later versions; companion Arts. 573.1 and 573.2 add further delayed-start rules `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm current text and categories of Arts. 573, 573.1, 573.2 on Westlaw before relying]`)

If the charge plausibly falls in an Art. 573 category, mark the Start Date `NEEDS-DATA` until the cessation/discovery date is sourced.

## Art. 574 — Lesser and included offenses

The time limitation applicable to the offense **charged** governs conviction or punishment for a **lesser and included offense**. Practical effect: the State cannot rescue an untimely greater charge by pointing to a shorter-limitation lesser offense, and a timely greater charge carries its limitation down to the lesser verdict.

## Art. 575 — Interruption of institution clocks

The Arts. 571.1–573 periods are **interrupted** (clock resets; runs anew from cessation of the cause) when the defendant:

1. For the purpose of avoiding detection, apprehension, or prosecution, **flees from the state, is outside the state, or is absent from his usual place of abode** within the state; or
2. **Lacks mental capacity to proceed** at trial and is committed under Art. 648.

**Interruption vs. suspension:** interruption restarts the full period anew; suspension pauses it and the balance resumes. Label which one applies in the Interruptions/Suspensions column — the arithmetic differs.

The **State bears the burden** of proving facts supporting an interruption once the defense shows the period has facially run. Record the State's claimed interruption facts and their source documents in the table.

## Art. 576 — New prosecution after dismissal

When a timely prosecution in a court of proper jurisdiction is dismissed (by the DA with defendant's consent, or before the first witness is sworn at trial on the merits, or by a court for error/defect/irregularity/deficiency), a new prosecution for the **same offense or a lesser offense based on the same facts** may be instituted within:

- the time remaining under this Chapter, **or**
- **six months from the date of dismissal**,

**whichever is longer.**

**Defense pressure point:** a new prosecution is NOT permitted after a DA dismissal unless the State shows the dismissal was **not for the purpose of avoiding the Art. 578 trial-commencement limitation**. When the audit shows a dismissal-and-refile close to an Art. 578 expiry, flag it as a motion-to-quash candidate and record the dismissal and refile dates with sources.

## Table rendering notes for this module

- One row per charged count when counts carry different classifications (a hard-labor felony count and a misdemeanor count get separate rows).
- Start Event for the general case: "Offense committed" + the sourced offense date. For Art. 571.1: "Victim attained threshold age." For Art. 573: "Cessation of [relationship/status]."
- End event that SATISFIES the clock: institution of prosecution — record the bill/indictment **filing date and instrument type** with source citation.
- If the offense date itself is unsourced or disputed, the entire module's rows are `NEEDS-DATA` — never compute from an assumed offense date.
