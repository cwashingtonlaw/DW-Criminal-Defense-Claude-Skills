# Louisiana Sentencing Guidelines Quick-Reference

**Last Verified:** April 6, 2026

> **DISCLAIMER:** This reference is for quick lookup during case analysis. Always verify current statutory text — Louisiana sentencing laws are frequently amended. This document provides ranges based on Louisiana Revised Statutes and case law as of the verification date. Consult current La. R.S. § 14 and § 15 provisions for authoritative sentencing requirements.

## 1. HOMICIDE OFFENSES

| Charge | Statute | Sentencing Range | Notes |
|--------|---------|------------------|-------|
| **First Degree Murder** | La. R.S. 14:30 | Mandatory Life Without Parole (LWOP); Death Penalty if aggravating circumstances | Aggravating circumstances required for capital punishment under La. R.S. 14:30(F) |
| **Second Degree Murder** | La. R.S. 14:30.1 | Mandatory Life Without Parole (LWOP) | No parole eligibility; applicable to felony murder rule |
| **Manslaughter** | La. R.S. 14:31 | 0-40 years hard labor | No mandatory minimum unless circumstances specify |
| **Vehicular Homicide** | La. R.S. 14:32.1 | 5-30 years hard labor | DWI enhancement: 5 years minimum; 15-30 years if DWI aggravation |
| **Negligent Homicide** | La. R.S. 14:32 | 0-5 years with or without hard labor | Non-violent driving offense; may result in fine or probation |

### Homicide Enhancements
- **DWI-Related Homicide (La. R.S. 14:32.1(C)):** Prior DWI conviction(s) mandate minimum 5 years
- **Habitual Traffic Offender:** Additional penalties apply if status established

| Charge | Statute | Sentencing Range | Notes |
|--------|---------|------------------|-------|
| **Aggravated Rape** | La. R.S. 14:42 | Mandatory Life Without Parole (LWOP) if victim under 13; Otherwise 5-99 years hard labor | Victim age critically determinative; LWOP is non-negotiable for victims under 13 |
| **Forcible Rape** | La. R.S. 14:42.1 | 5-40 years hard labor | Minimum 2 years without parole eligibility if victim under 13 |
| **Sexual Battery** | La. R.S. 14:43.1 | 0-10 years hard labor | Enhanced to 2-10 years if victim under 13 or 65+ |
| **Indecent Behavior with Juveniles** | La. R.S. 14:81 | 2-25 years hard labor | Aggravated (repeat/victim under 13): minimum 10 years without parole |
| **Molestation of a Juvenile** | La. R.S. 14:81.2 | Varies by victim age: Under 13: 10-40 years (minimum 10 years no parole); 13-17: 5-40 years | Mandatory registration as sex offender; enhanced penalties for repetitive acts |
| **Criminal Sexual Abuse** | La. R.S. 14:43.2 | 0-20 years hard labor | Enhanced if victim under 13 (2-20 years, minimum 2 no parole) |

### Sex Offense Enhancements
- **Victim Under 13 (Child):** Mandatory minimum parole ineligibility; LWOP possible for Agg. Rape
- **Repeat Offender:** Enhanced mandatory minimums; potential habitual sex offender classification
- **Sex Offender Registration (La. R.S. 15:540-566):** Mandatory for all felony sex offenders; tiered by offense severity

## 3. DRUG OFFENSES

### Controlled Dangerous Substance Distribution & Possession with Intent (La. R.S. 40:966-970)

| Schedule | Simple Possession | Possession with Intent | Distribution | Notes |
|----------|-------------------|------------------------|--------------|-------|
| **Schedule I** (e.g., heroin, cocaine base, fentanyl) | 0-1 year, may be suspended | 2-20 years hard labor | 5-40 years hard labor (first offense); 10-40 if prior felony | First offense simple possession may receive probation/deferred sentence |
| **Schedule II** (e.g., cocaine powder, methamphetamine) | 0-1 year, may be suspended | 2-20 years hard labor | 5-40 years hard labor | Same structure as Schedule I; cocaine base treated more seriously |
| **Schedule III** (e.g., LSD analogs, certain barbiturates) | 0-6 months | 1-10 years hard labor | 2-20 years hard labor | Lower penalties than Schedules I-II |
| **Marijuana (First Offense)** | 0-15 days jail or $100-$300 fine (under 2.5 oz); 0-6 months and/or $200-$500 fine (2.5-30 oz) | 0-1 year, may be suspended (under 1 lb); 1-10 years (1-100 lb); 10-20 years (100+ lb) | [VERIFY] Distribution: 1-10 years (under 1 lb); varies by weight | Decriminalization ongoing; verify current statutory treatment |

### Drug-Free Zone Enhancement (La. R.S. 40:981.3)
- **Mandatory Minimum Doubling:** If offense within 1,000 feet of school, public housing, bus stop, or recreational facility
- **Example:** Possession with intent to distribute Schedule I normally 2-20 years becomes mandatory **4-40 years** minimum
- **Applies to:** All CDS offenses within zone; strict application; no discretion

### Fentanyl-Specific Provisions (La. R.S. 14:39.1)
- **Fentanyl Distribution Resulting in Death:** [VERIFY] 10 years to life hard labor; enhanced if victim is minor or addict

## 7. HABITUAL OFFENDER ENHANCEMENTS (La. R.S. 15:529.1)

Louisiana's habitual offender statute dramatically increases sentencing. **Critical:** Predicate offenses must be correctly classified.

### Cleansing Periods
- **Non-violent/Non-sex Felonies:** 10-year cleansing period (offender-friendly lookback)
- **Sex Offenses, Crimes of Violence:** 5-year lookback from current offense date (state-favorable)
- **Violent Crime:** Specific definition under La. R.S. 14:2(B) — includes robbery, burglary of occupied dwelling, rape, etc.

### Habitual Offender Sentencing

| Prior Felony Count | Sentencing Requirement | Notes |
|-------------------|----------------------|-------|
| **2nd Felony** | Not less than **one-half the maximum** of current sentence; **no suspension** | E.g., 10-year max crime becomes minimum 5 years; no probation/suspension |
| **3rd Felony** | Not less than **two-thirds the maximum**; **no suspension** | E.g., 10-year max crime becomes minimum ~6.7 years |
| **4th or Subsequent Felony** | **20 years to Life** (depends on predicate offenses) | If fourth violent felony: potential LWOP or life sentence depending on current charge |

### Habitual Status Defenses/Procedural Issues
- **Article 215.1 Procedure:** Habitual status must be pleaded and proven as separate allegation
- **Burden of Proof:** Preponderance of evidence standard (not BARD)
- **Predicate Offense Issues:** Must be final conviction; federal offenses may qualify; timing critical
- **Charging Discretion:** Prosecutor's decision; substantial variation across parishes

## 8. KEY SENTENCING ARTICLES & STATUTES

### General Sentencing Framework
- **La. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 894.1:** Sentencing guidelines factors; consideration of aggravating/mitigating circumstances (non-binding but influential)
- **La. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 893:** Suspension of sentence and probation eligibility; mandatory vs. discretionary suspension
- **La. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 890.1:** Minimum mandatory sentences; enumerated offenses that prohibit suspension or probation
- **La. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 878.1:** Jury sentencing in capital cases (bifurcated trial); jury determination of aggravating/mitigating factors

### Post-Conviction/Release
- **La. R.S. 15:571.3:** Good time credit system; diminution of sentence rate tables
  - Standard: 1 day earned time per 2 days served (potentially higher for well-behaved inmates)
  - Administrative segregation: Limited or forfeited good time
  - First offenders: May earn increased good time credit (varies by institution)

- **La. R.S. 15:574-574.4:** Parole eligibility and release procedures; Board of Parole guidelines
  - Risk assessment instruments (LA/STATIC/LSI-R); parole hearing procedures

- **La. R.S. 14:22:** Accessory After the Fact — distinct offense; sentencing not to exceed principal offense maximum

## 9. SUSPENDED SENTENCE & PROBATION ELIGIBILITY QUICK GUIDE

**"Suspension" = Option to impose prison sentence but suspend execution (probation alternative)**

### MANDATORY Suspension (Judge Must Suspend):
- First-degree murder (but mandatory LWOP applies)
- Armed robbery (no suspension available — fixed 10-99 years)
- Manslaughter (suspension available, judge discretion)

### NO Suspension Allowed (La. R.S. 15:571.2 & specific statutes):
- Armed robbery (mandatory 10-99 years hard labor)
- Felon in possession of firearm (mandatory 5-20 years)
- Habitual offender sentences (no suspension beyond fixed term)
- Certain sex offenses against children (victim age-based)
- DWI with prior conviction (after 2nd DWI)

### Discretionary Suspension:
- Most felonies allow judge discretion to suspend; probation terms typically 2-5 years
- Exception: Statutes specifically prohibiting suspension override judge discretion

## 10. SPECIAL SENTENCING CONSIDERATIONS

### Mitigating/Aggravating Circumstances (La. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 894.1)

**Mitigating Factors (argue for reduction):**
- Minimal prior criminal history
- Age (youth or advanced age)
- Remorse/restitution
- Family ties/employment
- Substance abuse treatment (addressable issues)

**Aggravating Factors (support higher sentence):**
- Prior felony convictions (especially violent)
- Victim vulnerability (elderly, child, disabled)
- Abuse of trust/position
- Cruelty, torture, or unnecessary suffering
- Crime motivated by prejudice

### Consecutive vs. Concurrent Sentencing
- **Louisiana Default:** Sentences run consecutively unless judge specifically orders concurrent
- **Multiple Victims:** Likely concurrent sentences, though not mandatory
- **Violent Crimes:** Prosecutors often request consecutive sentences; judge may impose if crime merits multiple distinct sentences

### Death Penalty Eligibility (First Degree Murder)
- **Required Aggravating Circumstances (La. R.S. 14:30(F)):**
  - Victim under 12 years old
  - Victim over 65 years old
  - Peace officer killed in line of duty
  - Defendant previously convicted of capital crime, violent felony, or second-degree murder
  - Defendant killed victim for remuneration
  - Defendant killed during commission of another felony (armed robbery, burglary, rape, etc.)
  - Multiple victims
  - Particularly heinous, atrocious, or cruel manner

- **Jury Sentencing Required:** Jury must find aggravating circumstance beyond reasonable doubt; separate sentencing phase (Art. 878.1)

## 13. CRITICAL CASE LAW NOTES

- **Mandatory Minimums (State v. Johnson, et al.):** Louisiana strictly enforces statutory minimums; no judicial discretion below mandatory floor
- **Habitual Offender Pleading (State v. Mussall, et al.):** Separate charge; clear notice required; procedural defects may negate enhancement
- **Parole Ineligibility (State v. Tate, et al.):** "Without parole" language is mandatory; board has NO discretion to release within ineligibility period
- **Capital Sentencing (Furman/Gregg precedent, La. application):** Aggravating factors must be proven to jury BARD; jury sentencing non-negotiable for death penalty
