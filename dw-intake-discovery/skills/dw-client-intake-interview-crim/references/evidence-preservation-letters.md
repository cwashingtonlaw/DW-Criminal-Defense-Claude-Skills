# Evidence Preservation Letters — Templates

Time-sensitive preservation letters drafted at intake under Module D.2. Every template here is a draft for attorney signature. Cowork drafts; the attorney reviews, signs, and dispatches.

**Why preservation letters matter at intake:**
- Body-worn camera footage in many Louisiana agencies is auto-purged on 90- to 180-day cycles unless flagged
- Business surveillance commonly overwrites on a 30/60/90-day rolling window depending on chain
- Cellular and platform records can be permanently lost if not preserved before normal retention expiration
- A preservation letter establishes a paper trail for any later spoliation argument

**Authority for social-media preservation:** Stored Communications Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2703(f) — a governmental entity or other person may request a provider to take all necessary steps to preserve records. The provider shall retain such records for 90 days, extendable by an additional 90 days upon a renewed request. The defense uses this section to preserve content while the formal subpoena/search-warrant process plays out.

## Letter 1 — Law Enforcement Preservation

Recipient: lead investigating agency (parish sheriff, municipal PD, state police troop, federal agency). Send certified mail or e-mail with delivery receipt. Copy the District Attorney's office.

```
[FIRM LETTERHEAD — apply per dw-shared-protocols-crim/references/letterhead.md]
[Date]

[Chief / Sheriff / Agency Head]
[Agency Name]
[Address]

Re:  PRESERVATION DEMAND
     [Client Name] — [Date of Incident] — [Agency Case Number, if known]
     [Charge(s)]

Dear [Title]:

Our firm represents [Client Name] in connection with the above-referenced
matter. This letter constitutes a formal demand that your agency preserve
all records, recordings, and tangible items relating to this matter,
including but not limited to:

1. Body-worn camera (BWC) footage, in its original native format with
   metadata intact, from every officer who was present at, responded to,
   or had any contact with this incident — including any pre-event,
   post-event, and station-house recordings within the same shift.

2. In-car (dashcam) video and audio from every responding unit, in its
   original native format with metadata intact.

3. Station-house and booking-area video and audio capturing [Client
   Name] from arrival through release or transfer.

4. All 911 audio and CAD (Computer-Aided Dispatch) records, including
   incoming calls, radio traffic, and dispatch entries from [start time]
   through [end time] on [date].

5. All officer notes (handwritten and electronic), incident reports,
   supplemental reports, narrative addenda, and any drafts.

6. Photographs, scene diagrams, measurements, and crime-scene technician
   logs.

7. All physical and biological evidence collected, including chain-of-
   custody documentation.

8. All digital evidence, including any phones, computers, or storage
   devices seized; all forensic extractions and reports; and the original
   media.

9. All communications between officers, supervisors, and prosecutors
   relating to this matter, including emails, text messages, and
   instant-messaging communications.

10. Any internal-affairs, use-of-force, or critical-incident review
    files relating to this matter or to officers involved.

11. The personnel files (Brady/Giglio material) for every officer
    expected to be a witness.

12. Any informant or cooperator records relating to this matter.

This demand applies regardless of any agency retention schedule or
auto-purge policy. We request that you suspend any automated deletion
or overwrite that would affect any of the above. Failure to preserve
this evidence will be addressed by motion, including but not limited
to motions for adverse-inference instructions and dismissal sanctions
where appropriate.

If any of the above material has already been destroyed, deleted, or
overwritten, please identify it specifically, by date and method, and
identify the person responsible.

Please confirm receipt of this letter and your agency's compliance in
writing within 10 days. Direct any inquiries to the undersigned.

Sincerely,

[Attorney Name]
[Bar Number]
[Firm Name]
[Address / Phone / Email]

cc:  [District Attorney — Parish]
     [Client File]
```

## Letter 2 — Business Surveillance Preservation

Recipient: business with potentially relevant surveillance — gas stations, convenience stores, restaurants, hotels, bars/clubs, parking garages, ATM/bank cameras, residential complex management, retail stores. Send the same day intake closes; many systems overwrite within 7-30 days.

```
[FIRM LETTERHEAD — apply per dw-shared-protocols-crim/references/letterhead.md]
[Date]

[Business Name]
ATTN: General Manager / Custodian of Records / Loss Prevention
[Address]

Re:  URGENT — Surveillance Video Preservation Demand
     [Date and approximate time of incident]
     [Specific location — address, intersection, parking lot]

Dear Sir/Madam:

Our firm represents [Client Name] in a pending criminal matter. We
have reason to believe your premises captured video and/or audio of
events relevant to that matter on [date] between approximately
[start time] and [end time].

We respectfully demand that you preserve, in original native format
with all metadata intact, all video and audio recordings from every
camera in or on your premises (including parking areas, entrances,
exits, drive-thru lanes, ATM cameras, and any external-facing cameras)
covering the period from [date and time, beginning at least one hour
before the incident] through [date and time, ending at least one hour
after the incident].

Many surveillance systems automatically overwrite recordings within
days or weeks. We are sending this demand immediately to ensure
preservation. Please confirm in writing within 5 business days that
the recordings have been pulled and preserved, and identify how the
recordings can be obtained — by attorney pickup, subpoena, or other
means.

If your system has already overwritten any portion of the requested
period, please identify what was overwritten and when.

Any costs of duplication will be paid by our firm. Please contact the
undersigned to coordinate.

Sincerely,

[Attorney Name]
[Bar Number]
[Firm Name]
[Phone / Email]

cc:  [Client File]
```

**Cowork notes for the business preservation letter:**
- Move within 24-48 hours of intake. Do not wait for retention.
- For each location identified in Module C, dispatch one letter.
- Common retention windows by chain (verify with the specific business — these are starting estimates only):
  - Convenience-store chains: typically 7-30 days
  - Gas stations: typically 14-30 days
  - National hotel chains: typically 30 days at the property level; longer if escalated
  - National restaurant chains: typically 30-60 days
  - ATM and bank exterior cameras: typically 30-90 days
  - Parking garages: typically 30-90 days
  - Residential complexes: highly variable; some 7 days, some 90+ days
  - Doorbell / Ring / Nest cameras: depends on subscription tier; some no cloud storage, some 30-60 days
- Mark each retention window in the intake memo as `[VERIFY WITH BUSINESS]` — never assert as fact

## Letter 3 — Social Media Preservation Request (18 U.S.C. § 2703(f))

Recipient: social media or communications platform via the platform's law enforcement / legal-process portal. Each platform has its own intake address and form — Cowork captures the platform identity; the attorney handles the actual submission via the platform's portal.

```
[FIRM LETTERHEAD — apply per dw-shared-protocols-crim/references/letterhead.md]
[Date]

[Platform Name — Legal / Law Enforcement Response Team]
[Address as listed on the platform's law-enforcement guidelines page]

Re:  PRESERVATION REQUEST UNDER 18 U.S.C. § 2703(f)
     Account: [username, account ID, email, phone — whatever identifiers
     are known]
     User: [Client Name]
     Matter: Criminal investigation [reference number if any]

Dear [Title]:

Pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 2703(f), this letter requests that
[Platform Name] preserve, for an initial period of 90 days,
all records and other information in its possession pertaining to
the following account(s):

[List every identifier known — username, handle, account ID,
associated email, associated phone number. List multiple accounts
on the same platform if applicable.]

The records to be preserved include, without limitation:

1. Subscriber information (name, registration data, IP logs, payment
   records, devices used).

2. All content stored or transmitted through the account — posts,
   messages, photos, videos, voice notes, location data, draft items,
   archived items, deleted-but-recoverable items.

3. All communications between this account and any other accounts.

4. All metadata associated with the above, including timestamps,
   geolocation, and device identifiers.

5. Any account-action logs (logins, password changes, security-event
   logs).

This is a preservation request only. We are not seeking content
disclosure under this letter; content will be sought by formal
subpoena, court order, or warrant as the investigation and any
litigation proceeds.

If the requested 90-day preservation period is insufficient to
complete formal process, we will submit a renewal request prior to
expiration as authorized by § 2703(f)(2).

Please confirm preservation in writing.

Sincerely,

[Attorney Name]
[Bar Number]
[Firm Name]
[Phone / Email]
```

**Cowork notes for the § 2703(f) request:**
- 18 U.S.C. § 2703(f) authorizes 90-day preservation, extendable for an additional 90 days upon renewed request. Cowork tracks the 90-day expiration on the case calendar so the attorney can renew before lapse.
- Most platforms accept § 2703(f) requests from defense counsel as well as government, but each has its own portal and verification requirement. The attorney sends through the platform's law-enforcement portal, not through this template alone.
- Some platforms (Snapchat in particular) have very short retention even with preservation; preservation must be sent immediately.
- Encrypted-messaging services (Signal, encrypted WhatsApp, ProtonMail) often retain little or no message content even with preservation. Capture the existence of the account and metadata; manage expectations on content recovery.
- Save the sent-letter copy and any platform acknowledgment in `00 - Client File/01 - Intake/Preservation Letters/`.

### Platform-specific dispatch notes (verify current portal addresses)

| Platform | Where to send | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Meta (Facebook / Instagram / WhatsApp / Messenger) | Meta Law Enforcement Online Request System (LEORS) | Defense counsel may also use; verification required |
| X (formerly Twitter) | X Legal Request portal | Capture account ID — usernames change |
| Snapchat | Snap Inc. Law Enforcement Portal | Very short native retention; speed critical |
| TikTok | TikTok Law Enforcement Portal | Some content may be retained at parent-company servers |
| Google (Gmail / YouTube / Photos / Drive / Maps Timeline) | Google Law Enforcement Request System (LERS) | Account-wide preservation possible |
| Apple (iCloud / iMessage) | Apple Government & Legal Affairs | iMessage content is typically end-to-end encrypted; metadata available |
| Microsoft (Outlook / OneDrive / Teams) | Microsoft Digital Crimes Unit / Law Enforcement Portal | — |
| Discord | Discord Trust & Safety / Legal Request portal | — |
| Reddit | Reddit Legal Operations | — |
| Dating apps (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Grindr, Match) | Each platform's dedicated legal team | Capture account email + phone |

Mark all portal addresses in dispatched letters as `[VERIFY CURRENT PORTAL ADDRESS]` — these change.

## Letter 4 — Witness Preservation Contact

Recipient: a known third-party witness who may have photos, videos, texts, or social posts relevant to the case. The attorney decides whether to send a letter or place a call. A letter creates a paper trail; a call is faster and may be appropriate when the relationship is delicate.

```
[FIRM LETTERHEAD — apply per dw-shared-protocols-crim/references/letterhead.md]
[Date]

[Witness Name]
[Address]

Re:  Request to Preserve Materials — [Date of Incident]

Dear [Mr./Ms. Name]:

Our firm represents [Client Name] in a pending matter. We understand
you may have been present, or in communication with someone present,
on or around [date]. We are not asking you to discuss what you saw
or know, and we recognize you may also be in contact with law
enforcement or the prosecution. This letter is solely to ask you to
preserve materials in your possession.

We respectfully ask you to preserve, and not delete or modify:

1. Any photographs or videos you took on [date], on any device.

2. Any text messages, group chats, or messaging-app conversations
   relating to [date] or to [Client Name].

3. Any social-media posts, comments, direct messages, or stories
   relating to [date] or to [Client Name].

4. Any voice recordings or voicemails relating to [date].

5. Your contact information so we can reach you if needed.

Preservation does not require you to provide the materials to us —
it only asks that you do not delete or destroy them. If law
enforcement, the prosecutor, or our defense investigator contacts
you, you may decide whom to speak with and what to share. We will
not press you and you should not feel pressured.

If you are willing to speak with our defense investigator, please
call [phone] or email [email]. If you would prefer not to be
contacted, please let us know in writing.

Sincerely,

[Attorney Name]
[Bar Number]
[Firm Name]
[Phone / Email]
```

**Cowork notes for witness contact:**
- Tone matters. The witness is not the defendant's adversary — yet — and may become a defense witness, a prosecution witness, or both. Aggressive or confrontational language risks driving the witness into the prosecution's arms.
- Do not include factual claims about the case in the letter. Keep it preservation-focused.
- If the witness is the alleged victim or is represented by victim's-rights counsel, the attorney must verify whether direct contact is permissible under any active no-contact order or under La. ethics rules concerning contact with represented persons before any letter or call.
- Do not contact a co-defendant directly. Co-defendant contact runs through co-defendant's counsel.
- Save the sent letter to `00 - Client File/01 - Intake/Preservation Letters/`.

## Letter 5 — Phone Carrier / ISP Preservation (when client device is in police custody)

When the client's phone has been seized but discovery has not yet revealed which carrier records the State has obtained, send a preservation letter to the carrier directly.

```
[FIRM LETTERHEAD — apply per dw-shared-protocols-crim/references/letterhead.md]
[Date]

[Carrier Legal Department]
[Address]

Re:  PRESERVATION REQUEST UNDER 18 U.S.C. § 2703(f)
     Subscriber: [Client Name]
     Account / phone number: [number]

Dear [Title]:

Pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 2703(f), please preserve, for 90 days, all
records pertaining to the following account(s):

[Phone number(s), account ID, IMEI/MEID if known]

The records to be preserved include, without limitation:

1. Call detail records (CDRs) — incoming, outgoing, attempted, and
   received calls — for [date range, typically 30 days before through
   30 days after the alleged incident, or as relevant].

2. Text and multimedia message logs (timestamps, parties, sizes —
   content where retained).

3. Cell-site location information (CSLI), per-call measurement data,
   and any precision-location records (timing-advance, RTT, NELOS).

4. Subscriber information, billing records, payment records, and
   device-identifier records.

5. Any data-session logs (timestamps, endpoints, byte counts).

6. Any account-event logs (activations, swaps, SIM changes,
   password changes).

This is a preservation request only. Content and records will be
sought by appropriate process.

Sincerely,

[Attorney Name]
[Bar Number]
[Firm Name]
[Phone / Email]
```

**Cowork notes for carrier preservation:**
- The defense ordinarily cannot obtain CSLI without a court order under *Carpenter v. United States* and Louisiana implementing law. The preservation letter locks the data; the order or subpoena obtains it later.
- Verify the carrier's preferred legal-intake address (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile/Sprint, US Cellular) — each has a distinct portal.
- If the State has already obtained these records by warrant, the defense will obtain them in discovery; preservation here is a backstop.
