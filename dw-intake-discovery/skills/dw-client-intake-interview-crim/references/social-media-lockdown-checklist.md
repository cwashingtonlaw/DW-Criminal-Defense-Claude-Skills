# Social Media Lockdown Checklist

Platform-by-platform lockdown protocol executed at intake under Module D.3. Pairs with the Social Media Lockdown Worksheet that the client signs and the firm files in `00 - Client File/01 - Intake/`.

---

## The Three Rules — Apply to Every Platform

### Rule 1 — Lock down. Do NOT delete.

Deletion is potential spoliation of evidence. Once a person knows or should reasonably know that litigation is anticipated, the duty to preserve attaches. Deletion of social-media content during a pending criminal investigation can:

- Support an obstruction-of-justice charge
- Support a tampering-with-evidence charge
- Support a spoliation argument against the defense at trial
- Be revealed anyway through forensic recovery, platform records, or third-party preservation by other parties — leaving the defense worse off than if the post had been left alone

The default is **lock, do not delete.** Deletion is an attorney decision in narrow circumstances and is never made by Cowork or by the client unilaterally.

### Rule 2 — Deactivate is reversible; delete is often not.

Most platforms distinguish:

- **Deactivation** — account hidden from public view; data retained on platform servers; reactivable
- **Deletion** — account scheduled for permanent removal after a grace period (typically 14-30 days); data eventually scrubbed from platform servers

For lockdown purposes, deactivation is acceptable on platforms that offer it. Deletion is not. If the client insists on deletion, the attorney decides — and documents the decision and the legal-advice given.

### Rule 3 — Inventory everything before changing anything.

Before any password is changed or any privacy setting is touched:

1. Capture the full list of platforms where the client has accounts (active, dormant, forgotten)
2. Capture the username, account email, and account phone for each
3. Capture every device that is signed into each account
4. Confirm cloud backup settings
5. Document this state in the Social Media Lockdown Worksheet

This pre-change snapshot is the baseline. Without it, the firm cannot prove what was on the account before any changes were made.

## The Lockdown Sequence (every platform)

For each platform identified, walk through:

1. **Inventory** — username, email, phone, devices signed in, last login date if visible
2. **Backup / data download** — most platforms offer a "download your data" archive. Initiate the download before changing privacy settings; save the archive to `00 - Client File/01 - Intake/Social Media Archives/`
3. **Privacy settings to maximum restriction** — posts, photos, friend list, tagging, story visibility, message-receipt settings
4. **Password change** — to a strong password the client can record in writing for the firm
5. **Two-factor authentication enabled** — using a phone or authenticator app the client controls
6. **Tagging and mention permissions** — restrict so others cannot tag or mention the client without approval
7. **Search visibility** — restrict the account from appearing in search engine results where the platform allows
8. **Friend-of-friends and connection visibility** — restrict
9. **Active sessions audit** — sign out of any device the client doesn't recognize
10. **Connected apps audit** — revoke third-party app access where unnecessary

Document each step in the worksheet, with the date completed.

## Platform-by-Platform Notes

### Meta — Facebook

- Privacy Center → Audience and visibility → set Posts, Stories, Friends List, Profile Information to "Friends" or "Only me"
- Profile and Tagging → "Who can post on your timeline" → Friends; "Review tags people add" → On
- Search → "Do you want search engines outside of Facebook to link to your profile?" → No
- Messenger separately — message-request settings, story sharing
- **Do NOT delete posts, photos, the account, Messenger conversations, or Marketplace history.**
- Download a copy of your information (Settings → Your Information → Download your information) — request the full archive in HTML and save the export ZIP to the firm

### Meta — Instagram

- Settings → Privacy → set Account to Private
- Tags → Allow tags from "People you follow" only; Manually approve tags → On
- Story → Hide from list (add anyone the client wants to exclude); Allow Sharing → Off
- Comments → restrict commenters
- Messages → restrict who can message
- **Do NOT delete posts, Stories archived, Reels, DMs, or the account.**
- Download data (Settings → Security → Download Data) — both account and connected accounts (Threads if linked)

### Meta — WhatsApp / Messenger

- Privacy → Last Seen, Profile Photo, About, Status → My Contacts or Nobody
- Disappearing messages — if previously enabled, **do NOT enable now if not already on** — adding disappearing messages after a charge can itself be spoliation. Capture the current setting; do not alter retroactively.
- Backups — if iCloud or Google Drive backup is on, leave on (preserves messages); if off, attorney decides whether to enable
- Two-step verification — On

### X (formerly Twitter)

- Privacy and safety → Audience and tagging → Protect your posts → On
- Photo tagging → Off, or restricted to people you follow
- Messages → Allow message requests from → Off
- Discoverability → Off (email, phone)
- Active sessions audit
- **Do NOT delete tweets, replies, retweets, likes, or DMs.**
- Request your archive (Settings → Your account → Download an archive of your data); save the ZIP to the firm

### TikTok

- Settings → Privacy → Private account → On
- Suggest your account to others → Off (all options)
- Comments, Duet, Stitch → Friends or Off
- Direct messages → Friends or No One
- Downloads → Off (so others cannot save your videos)
- **Do NOT delete videos, drafts, or the account.**
- Settings → Account → Download your data

### Snapchat

- Privacy → Contact Me, View My Story → My Friends
- See Me in Quick Add → Off
- Snap Map → Ghost Mode (so location is not shared)
- Memories — backups: confirm Memories backup is on (this preserves saved snaps and stories)
- **Critical: Snapchat is a high-deletion-risk platform.** Snaps and chats often disappear by default. The client should not "save to chat" or "save to Memories" anything new about the case, but should NOT delete what's already in Memories. The firm sends a § 2703(f) preservation letter to Snap (per `evidence-preservation-letters.md`) immediately at intake.
- Settings → My Data → request download

### Google — Gmail / YouTube / Photos / Drive / Maps Timeline

- Google Account → Security → review devices, sign out unrecognized
- 2-Step Verification — On
- Maps Timeline — **leave on** if currently on; this is potential alibi evidence. **Do NOT turn off, do NOT delete history, do NOT pause Location History.** Capture the current setting.
- YouTube — set channel to private if relevant; do NOT delete videos or comments
- Gmail — change password; review filter rules; review "less secure app access" and connected apps
- Photos — confirm Backup & Sync is on (preserves images)
- Google Takeout — request full account export; save the ZIP to the firm

### Apple — iCloud / iMessage

- Settings → Apple ID → review devices signed in
- Two-Factor Authentication — On (already mandatory for most accounts)
- iCloud Backup — leave on; this preserves the device state including iMessages
- Find My — leave on (location history)
- iMessage — **do NOT delete messages, threads, or attachments**
- Photos — iCloud Photos: leave on (preserves images and the deleted-recently folder)
- Settings → Apple ID → Privacy & Security → Get Copy of Your Data — request full export

### Microsoft — Outlook / OneDrive / Teams / Xbox

- Microsoft account → Security → review sign-in activity
- Two-step verification — On
- Outlook — change password; review forwarding rules; do NOT delete emails
- OneDrive — review shared links; revoke unfamiliar shares; do NOT delete files
- Teams — if used for personal communications about the case, do NOT delete chats

### Discord

- User Settings → Privacy & Safety → Direct Message Privacy and Server Privacy
- Two-Factor Authentication — On
- Sessions audit
- **Do NOT delete servers, messages, or the account.**
- Request data package (User Settings → Privacy & Safety → Request all of my data)

### Reddit

- Account → Privacy → Hide karma, hide active communities, hide profile from search engines
- Two-Factor Authentication — On
- **Do NOT delete posts, comments, or the account.**
- Request data export

### LinkedIn

- Account → Visibility → restrict profile visibility; turn off public profile if relevant
- Activity broadcast → off
- **Do NOT delete posts, the account, or connections.**

### Dating apps (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Grindr, Match, OkCupid, etc.)

- For each app the client has used:
  - Identify the account email and phone
  - Capture username/handle
  - Identify whether the account is currently active or dormant
- **Do NOT delete the account.** Several dating apps purge data quickly upon account deletion. Most allow "pause" or "hide" functions — use those.
- For sex-offense cases especially, dating-app communications are central evidence. Send a § 2703(f) preservation letter to each platform immediately.
- Request data download where the platform offers it.

### Encrypted-messaging apps (Signal, Telegram, Wire)

- These platforms typically retain little or no server-side content. Preservation letters yield minimal content but capture metadata.
- Confirm the client's local message retention setting; **do NOT enable disappearing messages now if not already enabled** (potential spoliation). Capture current setting.
- Do not delete chat threads.
- Some platforms (Signal in particular) allow Sealed Sender — capture the account state.

### Gaming and adjacent platforms

- Xbox Live, PlayStation Network, Steam, Twitch, Discord-as-game-chat: capture username and whether case-relevant communications occurred there
- Twitch — do NOT delete VODs (video-on-demand archives) or chat logs
- Game-specific chat (Fortnite, Call of Duty, etc.): capture in-game name; some games purge chat quickly, so § 2703(f) preservation may be warranted

### Cloud-storage and productivity

- Dropbox, Box, iDrive, pCloud — confirm content is preserved; do NOT delete
- Notion, Evernote, Apple Notes (cloud-synced), Microsoft OneNote — do NOT delete; especially if the client used these for journaling or task-tracking around the time of the alleged offense

## Family-Account Hygiene

Family members are the most common vector for evidence problems after intake. Even well-meaning family members:

- Post about the case publicly ("free my brother" / "innocent man")
- Tag the client in old photos that surface unrelated facts (drug use, weapons, prior incidents)
- Speculate about the alleged victim or witnesses
- Send messages to the client's accounts that the prosecutor will read
- DM with co-defendants or witnesses
- Post photos of jail visits, courthouse appearances, or family gatherings around the case

Cowork drafts a one-page **Family Communication Notice** that the client signs (if appropriate) and that the firm sends to immediate family. The letter:

- Asks family not to post about the case on any platform
- Asks family not to tag the client
- Asks family not to discuss the case in any group chat
- Asks family not to contact the alleged victim, witnesses, or co-defendants
- Asks family not to speak to media
- Provides the firm's contact for media inquiries

The notice does not invoke the privilege (family is not within it) and does not bind the family legally; it is a request grounded in the client's defense. Save the sent letter to `00 - Client File/01 - Intake/`.

## The Lockdown Worksheet (signed by the client)

The worksheet includes a signed acknowledgment by the client that:

- The client understands deletion of social-media content during a pending matter creates spoliation, obstruction, and tampering risk
- The client agrees not to delete any post, message, photo, video, or account during the representation without the attorney's express prior written approval
- The client has provided current passwords for every account listed (or has confirmed the firm has them in sealed storage)
- The client has reviewed each platform with the firm and confirmed the privacy settings are at maximum restriction
- The client understands that family members posting about the case may harm the defense

The signed worksheet is filed in `00 - Client File/01 - Intake/`. If the client is in custody and cannot sign in person at intake, the worksheet is presented at the first jail visit.
