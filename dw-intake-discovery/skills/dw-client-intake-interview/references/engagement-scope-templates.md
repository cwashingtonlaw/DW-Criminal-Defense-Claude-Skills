# Engagement Scope Templates

Drafts of engagement-scope language used in Module F of the SKILL. The scope language is part of the firm's engagement letter; this reference provides the modular building blocks the attorney assembles for each matter.

**Cowork drafts; attorney signs.** Engagement letters are the contract between client and firm. Cowork produces the draft scope language and routes to the attorney for finalization. The attorney delivers the final letter to the client and obtains the signature.

---

## Scope-Language Modules

The engagement letter typically includes:

1. Identification of client and firm
2. Scope of representation — what the firm will do, what it will not
3. Fee structure (flat fee, hourly, hybrid)
4. Trust-account and billing mechanics
5. Termination / withdrawal language
6. File-handling on closure
7. Conflict and waiver disclosures (where applicable)
8. Third-party payor disclosure (where applicable)
9. Communication expectations
10. Signature blocks

This reference covers Modules 2 through 8. The firm's standard engagement letter handles 1, 9, and 10.

## Scope of Representation — Templates

### Template A — Pre-Trial Only (Flat Fee)

```
SCOPE OF REPRESENTATION

The firm agrees to represent [Client Name] in connection with the
above-captioned matter through pre-trial proceedings only. The
representation includes, without further charge:

1. Initial intake, conflict clearance, and case-opening tasks;

2. Preliminary appearances, arraignment, and bond/release motions
   (initial bond motion plus one reduction motion if warranted by
   case developments);

3. Review of all discovery as produced by the State, including
   audio/video, written reports, and physical-evidence inventories;

4. Filing and litigating pre-trial motions ordinarily warranted in
   this type of case, including motions for discovery, bills of
   particulars, suppression motions, motions in limine, and motions
   for production;

5. Investigation as authorized by the firm's investigation budget for
   this engagement;

6. Plea negotiations with the State, including conveying offers to
   the client and counter-offers to the State;

7. Resolution of the matter by plea, dismissal, or other pre-trial
   disposition.

The representation under this agreement does NOT include:

A. Trial. If the matter does not resolve pre-trial and proceeds to
   trial, the firm and client will execute a separate engagement
   letter covering trial representation. Trial is not pre-paid by
   this agreement.

B. Appeal. Any post-conviction proceeding, including direct appeal,
   application for post-conviction relief, or federal habeas, is
   outside the scope.

C. Collateral proceedings (immigration, professional license,
   parole-board hearings, civil collateral matters, family-court
   matters) are outside the scope.

D. Federal companion proceedings are outside the scope; federal
   representation requires separate engagement.

E. Co-defendant representation is excluded; the firm represents
   [Client Name] only.

If the matter proceeds to trial and the client declines or is unable
to retain trial counsel from this firm, the firm will, on motion
filed with the court, request leave to withdraw with sufficient
notice to allow the client to retain successor counsel.
```

### Template B — Through Trial (Flat Fee)

```
SCOPE OF REPRESENTATION

The firm agrees to represent [Client Name] in connection with the
above-captioned matter from initial appearance through final
disposition at the trial-court level, including jury or bench trial
if required. The representation includes:

1. All matters listed in the firm's pre-trial scope;

2. Trial preparation, including witness preparation, exhibit
   preparation, and pre-trial trial-management filings;

3. Trial advocacy through verdict;

4. Sentencing, including pre-sentencing investigation review and
   sentencing memorandum where appropriate;

5. Post-trial motions filed within the trial court (motion for new
   trial, motion in arrest of judgment, motion for reconsideration of
   sentence) where supported by the record.

The representation under this agreement does NOT include:

A. Direct appeal to a court of appeal or the Louisiana Supreme Court.
   Direct appeal is a separate engagement.

B. Application for post-conviction relief.

C. Federal habeas corpus.

D. Federal companion or successor prosecution.

E. Collateral proceedings (immigration, professional license,
   parole-board hearings, civil collateral matters, family-court
   matters).

F. Co-defendant representation.

G. Re-trial in the event of mistrial or remand. Re-trial requires a
   new engagement and a re-quoted fee.
```

### Template C — Appellate-Only

```
SCOPE OF REPRESENTATION

The firm agrees to represent [Client Name] in connection with the
direct appeal of the [date] judgment of the [trial court] in the
above-captioned matter. The representation includes:

1. Review of the trial-court record, including transcripts, exhibits,
   and lower-court rulings;

2. Identification and preservation of appellate issues;

3. Drafting and filing the appellant's brief;

4. Drafting and filing any reply brief;

5. Oral argument before the court of appeal;

6. Filing for rehearing where appropriate;

7. Filing an application for supervisory writs to the Louisiana
   Supreme Court if the court of appeal denies relief, where the
   firm and client agree that further review is warranted.

The representation under this agreement does NOT include:

A. Application for post-conviction relief in the trial court (PCR is
   collateral and follows direct appeal).

B. Federal habeas corpus.

C. Re-trial proceedings on remand. Remand requires a new engagement.

D. Collateral matters (sentencing reconsideration, re-sentencing,
   probation/parole proceedings).

E. Continued representation if the client retains successor appellate
   counsel or if the firm is granted leave to withdraw.

The firm makes no guarantee of any particular outcome on appeal.
Appellate practice is governed by the trial-court record; the firm's
ability to raise issues is constrained by what was preserved at trial.
```

### Template D — Hourly Engagement (Investigation Phase)

```
SCOPE OF REPRESENTATION

The firm agrees to provide pre-charge representation and investigation
services to [Client Name] on an hourly basis. The representation
includes:

1. Initial intake and conflict clearance;

2. Review of any documents in the client's possession;

3. Engagement of a defense investigator and direction of investigative
   tasks;

4. Communications with the investigating agency or prosecutor's office
   on the client's behalf, where appropriate;

5. Negotiation of voluntary surrender or pre-charge resolution where
   feasible;

6. Counsel during any voluntary interview or proffer;

7. Strategic planning for the matter as it develops.

The hourly representation continues until either:

(a) Charges are filed, at which point the parties will execute a new
    engagement letter for trial-level representation (this may be a
    flat-fee engagement under Template A or B); OR

(b) The client and firm mutually agree the pre-charge engagement has
    concluded; OR

(c) The firm or client terminates the engagement under the
    Termination and Withdrawal provisions of this agreement.

Hourly rates: [List rates by attorney/paralegal/investigator]

The firm bills monthly. A retainer of $[amount] is due at engagement
and held in trust; fees and costs are drawn against the retainer with
monthly statements. The retainer is replenished as it is depleted.
```

### Template E — Hourly Engagement (Full Representation)

```
SCOPE OF REPRESENTATION

The firm agrees to represent [Client Name] in connection with the
above-captioned matter on an hourly basis. The scope of services
includes pre-trial, trial, and post-trial proceedings at the
trial-court level [or describe alternative scope].

Hourly rates: [List rates by attorney/paralegal/investigator]

A retainer of $[amount] is due at engagement and held in trust.
Fees and costs are drawn against the retainer with monthly
statements. The retainer is replenished as it is depleted.

The firm will provide reasonable advance notice of expected fee
exposure before significant work is undertaken (e.g., retention of
expert witnesses, extended trial preparation, contested evidentiary
hearings). The client retains the right to direct the firm to limit
hours expended on any task.

The firm tracks time in [tenth-of-an-hour / quarter-of-an-hour]
increments, with descriptions tied to specific tasks (see Billing
section).

Excluded matters:

A. Appeal and post-conviction relief — separate engagement.

B. Federal companion or successor prosecution — separate engagement.

C. Collateral proceedings (immigration, professional license,
   parole-board hearings, civil collateral matters, family-court
   matters) — separate engagement.

D. Co-defendant representation.
```

### Template F — Limited-Scope Consultation

For prospective clients who consult but do not retain — for circumstances where the firm provides advice on a discrete question without taking the matter:

```
LIMITED-SCOPE CONSULTATION

The firm will provide a single-meeting consultation with [Client
Name] regarding [topic], for a flat fee of $[amount]. The consultation
includes:

1. A meeting (in-person or remote) of up to [duration];

2. The firm's advice on the discrete question presented;

3. A brief written summary of the advice (one to two pages).

The consultation does NOT include:

A. Continued representation;

B. Court appearances;

C. Document drafting beyond the summary;

D. Any contact with third parties (police, prosecutor, witnesses) on
   the client's behalf.

If the client wishes to engage the firm for further representation
following the consultation, a separate engagement letter will be
executed.

This consultation is subject to the same confidentiality protections
as a full representation. La. Rule of Professional Conduct 1.18
governs the firm's duties to the client even if no further
representation results.
```

## Fee-Structure Modules

### Flat fee — non-refundable retainer language

```
FEES AND PAYMENT

The flat fee for the scope of representation described above is
$[amount]. The flat fee is earned on a benchmark basis as the firm
performs the work, with benchmarks tied to the milestones described
below. The fee schedule is:

  $[amount] — at engagement (covers intake, conflict clearance, case
              opening, initial investigation, and bond/release motions)

  $[amount] — at filing of pre-trial motions

  $[amount] — at receipt of substantive discovery and case analysis

  $[amount] — at trial readiness / final pre-trial conference

  $[amount] — at trial commencement (if applicable to scope)

The flat fee is the total fee for the scope of representation. The
firm does not bill additionally on an hourly basis for work within
the scope.

If the representation terminates before all benchmarks are reached
(by the client's discharge of the firm, by the firm's withdrawal for
good cause, or by the matter's earlier resolution), the firm will
account for fees earned to the date of termination based on the
benchmark structure above. Any unearned portion of the flat fee will
be refunded.
```

### Hourly billing language

```
HOURLY FEES AND PAYMENT

The firm's hourly rates for this engagement are:

  [Senior Partner]:    $[rate]
  [Associate]:         $[rate]
  [Paralegal]:         $[rate]
  [Defense Investigator (in-house or contracted at firm rate)]:
                       $[rate]

The firm bills time in [tenth-of-an-hour / quarter-of-an-hour]
increments. Time entries describe the task, the matter, and the
attorney/staff who performed the work.

A retainer of $[amount] is due at engagement and held in trust at
[bank/IOLTA]. Time and costs are drawn against the retainer with
monthly statements. When the retainer is reduced to [threshold],
the firm will request replenishment in the amount of $[amount].

If the retainer is not replenished within [days] of request, the
firm may pause work until replenishment is received and may, on
motion to the court with sufficient notice, seek leave to
withdraw.
```

### Hybrid (flat + hourly contingency) language

```
HYBRID FEE STRUCTURE

The firm and client agree to the following hybrid fee structure:

  Phase 1 (Pre-Trial): Flat fee of $[amount], earned on benchmark
  basis as described above.

  Phase 2 (Trial): If the matter proceeds to trial, the parties will
  execute a supplemental engagement letter covering trial
  representation. The supplemental fee is [flat / hourly / specified
  amount].

  Investigation costs above $[amount] in Phase 1 are billed
  separately on an hourly basis as contracted with the firm's
  investigator(s) or as billed by retained experts.

The firm provides the client a budget estimate before engaging an
expert witness or other major cost item. The client retains the
right to direct the firm to limit specific costs.
```

## Withdrawal and Termination Modules

### Withdrawal grounds (firm)

```
WITHDRAWAL BY THE FIRM

The firm may seek leave of court to withdraw from the representation
under the circumstances permitted by La. Rule of Professional Conduct
1.16, including without limitation:

1. The client's failure to pay fees and costs as agreed, after
   reasonable notice and opportunity to cure;

2. The client's insistence on a course of action the firm
   reasonably believes is criminal, fraudulent, or otherwise
   prohibited by the Rules of Professional Conduct;

3. The client's discharge of the firm;

4. A conflict of interest that develops during the representation
   that cannot be cured by waiver;

5. Other circumstances making continued representation unreasonably
   difficult or contrary to the Rules of Professional Conduct.

If the firm withdraws after a court appearance has been scheduled or
after charges have been filed, the firm will provide reasonable
notice to the client and will seek leave of court with sufficient
notice to permit the client to obtain successor counsel without
prejudice.
```

### Discharge by the client

```
DISCHARGE BY THE CLIENT

The client may discharge the firm at any time, with or without cause.
On discharge:

1. The firm will, on motion to the court, request leave to withdraw
   with notice to permit successor counsel to be retained.

2. The firm will return the client file to the client or to successor
   counsel as the client directs, subject to any retaining-lien
   considerations and applicable law.

3. The firm will account for any unearned fees and refund any unearned
   portion to the client (or to the third-party payor where
   applicable) within a reasonable time.

4. The firm's duty of confidentiality to the client survives the
   termination.
```

## File-Handling on Case Closure

```
FILE HANDLING

On termination of the representation (whether by case resolution,
client discharge, or firm withdrawal), the firm will provide the
client a complete copy of the client file, including:

1. All pleadings filed in the matter;

2. All discovery received from the State (subject to any protective
   order limitations);

3. All correspondence between the firm and the client, the
   prosecution, and the court;

4. All investigation reports and witness statements obtained by the
   firm;

5. All transcripts of court proceedings;

6. All retained-expert reports and communications.

Attorney work product (internal strategy memoranda, internal case
analyses, internal email between firm staff) is the firm's property
and is not included in the file return, except to the extent the
client's interests require disclosure.

The firm retains a copy of the client file for [retention period —
typically 10 years for criminal matters] and may destroy the file
after that period without further notice. The client may request the
file be destroyed earlier or that originals be returned to the
client.

If the matter is on appeal or in post-conviction proceedings at the
time of file return, the firm coordinates with successor counsel to
ensure continuity.
```

## Special-Circumstance Modules

### Third-Party Payor (Rule 1.8(f))

When fees are paid by a family member or other third party, include the consent paragraph from `conflict-check-protocol.md` as an addendum to the engagement letter, signed by the client. The engagement letter itself notes:

```
THIRD-PARTY PAYMENT

The legal fees for this engagement are being paid by [Third Party
Name]. The client (defendant) has executed the Informed Consent to
Third-Party Payment attached as Schedule A. As described in that
schedule, the firm's duty of loyalty and confidentiality runs to the
client alone; the third-party payor does not direct strategy, does
not receive privileged information, and does not control the
representation.

The firm's communications with [Third Party] are limited to
administrative billing matters unless the client expressly authorizes
specific substantive disclosures in writing.
```

### Conflict-Waiver Disclosure

When the engagement is conditional on a Rule 1.7 or 1.9 waiver, attach the executed waiver as a schedule to the engagement letter and note in the body:

```
CONFLICT WAIVER

The firm has identified [a current-client / former-client /
prospective-client] conflict that is waivable under La. Rule of
Professional Conduct [1.7 / 1.9 / 1.18]. The affected [client(s) /
former client(s) / prospective client(s)] have executed the waiver
attached as Schedule [B], and the client (defendant) has executed
the corresponding consent. The representation proceeds in reliance
on those waivers. If circumstances change such that the conflict
becomes non-consentable or the waiver is revoked, the firm may be
required to withdraw.
```

### Limited-English-Proficiency / Interpreter

```
INTERPRETER

The client and firm have engaged [Interpreter Name] of [Agency] to
provide [language] interpretation for all attorney-client
communications. The interpreter is bound by the firm's
confidentiality protocols. The interpreter's fee is a cost of the
representation and is billed [as included in the flat fee / on an
hourly basis at $[rate]].
```

### Client in Custody — Communication Expectations

```
COMMUNICATION

The client is currently held at [facility]. The firm will:

1. Visit the client at [facility] within [timeframe — typically 7
   days] of engagement and at intervals appropriate to the case
   posture thereafter;

2. Set up confidential attorney-client mail and, where the facility
   permits, secured video visitation;

3. Advise the client not to discuss the case on jail telephones, in
   visitation, in writing to non-attorney parties, or with cellmates
   (see Jail Call Hygiene Letter);

4. Communicate with the client's designated family contact
   ([Name]) on procedural matters only — substantive case
   information remains between attorney and client;

5. Calendar all court appearances and ensure the client is
   transported as required.
```

## Cowork Output Conventions

For every engagement-scope draft:

1. Save to `{{CASE_ROOT}}/00 - Client File/01 - Intake/Engagement Scope - [Client Last Name] - [Date].docx`
2. Apply attorney work product marking (drafts only)
3. Cowork drafts the modular blocks; the attorney assembles the final letter on firm letterhead
4. The signed engagement letter (a separate document on firm letterhead) is filed by the attorney
5. Cowork updates the Conflict Check Record with the engagement-letter signature date once received
