# Follow-Up Interview — Post-Discovery Review

**Use when:** Phase 1 discovery is in. State has produced BWC, witness statements, lab reports, photos, 911 calls, etc. Time to confront the client with what the State has.
**Target length:** 2–3 pages.
**Timing:** Within 2 weeks of substantial discovery production.

## PRE-INTERVIEW ATTORNEY PREP

- Pull `dw-discovery-compliance-monitor-crim` ledger — list what arrived, dates, what is still pending
- Identify the 3–5 most damaging pieces of State evidence
- Identify the 3–5 pieces of evidence that help the defense
- Bring copies (or laptop) for the items you intend to confront client with — do NOT leave with client
- Re-confirm privilege; remind client jail mail and calls are not privileged

## SECTION 1 — What Discovery Has Shown

**Guidance:** Walk through each major item the client has not seen. Watch reaction — note for cross-prep.

### 1.1 Body-worn camera / dash cam / surveillance video

- Does what they show match what you remember?
- Anything the video shows that you did not tell me before?
- Anything the video missed that you saw or heard?
- Identify the officers / civilians visible — do you know any of them?
- Audio captures any statements you made? Did you understand you were on camera?

### 1.2 Witness statements

For each statement:
- Do you know this person? How? Any history?
- Are they telling the truth about what they say they saw?
- Where would they have been to see what they describe?
- Anyone they would have spoken to before talking to police? Why might they say what they said?
- Reason to lie — bias, beef, pressure, payment, cooperation deal?

### 1.3 Lab / forensic reports

- DNA / prints / ballistics — does the result surprise you? Explain.
- If your DNA is on item X — when and how could it have gotten there?
- Lab analyst name — do you know them or anyone at the lab?
- Field test vs. lab result — discrepancies?

### 1.4 Photographs / scene reconstruction

- Look at this photo. What am I looking at?
- Anything in the scene photos that's different than how you remember?
- Anything missing from the scene that should be there?

### 1.5 911 calls / CAD reports / dispatch logs

- Recognize any voices on the 911 call?
- Timing of dispatch vs. arrest — does it match your memory?

### 1.6 Phone / digital evidence

- Cell site records — does the location data match where you were?
- Messages, calls, social media — anything to explain or contextualize?
- Anything missing that should be there (deleted, missing date range)?

---

## SECTION 2 — Changes to Client's Account

**Guidance:** After confronting client with discovery, ask:

- Anything you want to add, correct, or clarify from our first interview?
- Anything you remembered seeing/hearing/saying that you did not mention before?
- Anything you now realize you were mistaken about?

Do NOT pressure for changes — note discrepancies, not coercion.

---

## SECTION 3 — Defense Theory Refinement

**Guidance:** Without disclosing strategy, probe leads.

- Names of anyone we should locate as a defense witness?
- Records you think we should subpoena (medical, employment, phone, financial)?
- Surveillance we should preserve (store cameras, doorbell, traffic) — locations and approximate dates?
- Experts you think we should consult (medical, mental health, forensic)?

---

## SECTION 4 — Outstanding Discovery

**Guidance:** Use the `dw-discovery-compliance-monitor-crim` ledger. Tell client only that materials are still being requested.

- What materials are we still waiting for? (Generic — no strategy disclosure)
- Any concerns you have about evidence that has not been produced?
