## Speedy Trial / Art. 701 (Module 1)

### Statutory framework

- La. C.Cr.P. art. 701 — Right to a speedy trial; statutory delays (felonies: 120 days in custody / 180 days on bond following filing of motion and affidavit; misdemeanors: 30 / 60 days; capital: 2 years)
- La. C.Cr.P. art. 701(D)(1) — Motion-and-affidavit requirement; counsel must certify defense is prepared to proceed within statutory delays
- La. C.Cr.P. art. 701(B) — Excludable time (continuances on defendant's motion or with consent, defendant's absence/unavailability, mental incompetency proceedings, joint charges with co-defendant not yet apprehended, statutory suspensions)
- La. Const. Art. I, § 16 — speedy trial right
- U.S. Const. amend. VI — speedy trial right

### Louisiana authority

- *State v. Reaves*, 569 So.2d 650 (La. App. 2d Cir. 1990) — speedy trial analysis under Art. 701
- *State v. Rome*, 93-1221 (La. 1/14/94), 630 So.2d 1284 — speedy trial framework

### Drafting note

The bundled `motion_for_speedy_trial_701.docx` (Harrison, 14th JDC Calcasieu) cites ONLY the statute. The case citations above come from the in-skill Module 1 Key Authority. When filing a 701 motion contested by the State, expect to brief the *Reaves* / *Rome* framework in a supporting memorandum — the bare-bones Harrison form is appropriate only for routine, uncontested speedy trial demands. For prolonged-detention or constitutional-speedy-trial arguments (which are distinct from the Art. 701 statutory framework — those use the *Barker v. Wingo*, 407 U.S. 514 (1972), four-factor test), draft a full supporting memorandum.

## Citation hygiene reminders

1. **Always confirm the year and reporter** before filing. Several templates show year drift (e.g., Mincey cited as 1997, correct 1978).
2. **Cite parallel reporters for Louisiana cases** where applicable: *State v. Jones*, 252 La. 903, 215 So.2d 108 (La. 1968) — both the Louisiana Reports and the Southern Reporter pin cites.
3. **Check renumbering** — La. C.Cr.P. articles have been renumbered multiple times. Art. 334 → Art. 316 is one example. Always verify current article numbers via the West code or the official Louisiana Legislature site before incorporating older template language.
4. **Pin cites** — many template citations omit pin cites. When the proposition is specific (e.g., *Stack v. Boyle* on bail-as-non-punishment), include the page number (here, 4-5).
5. **Spelling** — *Lindsey v. King* (not Lindsay); *State v. Nogess* (not Noizess); *Williams v. Whitley* (not William v. Whitely). Several templates contain these typos — do not propagate them.
6. **Unpublished opinions** — flag any unpublished citation (e.g., *State v. Collins* La. App. 5th Cir. 2019). Louisiana Uniform Rules of the Courts of Appeal Rule 2-16.3 restricts citation of unpublished opinions in many contexts.
