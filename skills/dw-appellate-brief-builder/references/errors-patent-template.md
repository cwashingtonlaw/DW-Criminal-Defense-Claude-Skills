# Errors Patent — Template & Reference

**La. C.Cr.P. Art. 920** — controlling authority for errors-patent review on Louisiana criminal direct appeal.

> "The following matters and no others shall be considered on appeal:
> (1) An error designated in the assignment of errors; and
> (2) An error that is discoverable by a mere inspection of the pleadings and proceedings and without inspection of the evidence."

Errors patent are reviewable **without** a contemporaneous objection at trial. Art. 920(2) is the narrow exception to the contemporaneous-objection rule of Art. 841 and to the requirement that issues be raised in the assignment of errors. Every Louisiana court of appeal conducts an errors-patent review in every criminal direct appeal — counsel does NOT need to raise errors patent to trigger the review, but counsel SHOULD raise them affirmatively to ensure the panel addresses them.

This reference catalogs the categories of error that qualify as errors patent, and supplies template language for raising them in the appellate brief.

---

## What is "the face of the record"?

Errors patent are limited to the face of the record. The court does NOT review the trial transcript or the evidence for errors-patent purposes. The "face of the record" includes:

1. **Charging instrument** — bill of information or indictment, with all amendments
2. **Arraignment minute entry** — entry of plea
3. **All minute entries** — from arraignment through sentencing
4. **Jury composition records** — jury venire, voir dire results, verdict form
5. **Verdict** — written verdict form
6. **Sentence** — pronouncement, commitment order, sentencing minute entry
7. **Court rulings of record** — written rulings, orders, denials of post-trial motions
8. **Post-trial motions of record** — Art. 851, 858, 881.1 motions and rulings

Things that are NOT on the face of the record (and thus not errors-patent reviewable):
- Trial testimony (requires inspection of evidence)
- Witness credibility
- Sufficiency of evidence (requires inspection of evidence — but is reviewable as a separate assignment under *Jackson v. Virginia*)
- Pretrial-motion factual disputes
- Evidentiary rulings during trial

**Key Louisiana authority:**
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 920(2) — errors-patent review of "any error that is discoverable by a mere inspection of the pleadings and proceedings and without inspection of the evidence"
- *State v. Oliveaux*, 312 So.2d 337 (La. 1975) — defines the scope of the "record" reviewable for errors patent
- *State v. Augustine*, 555 So.2d 1331 (La. 1990) — Art. 873 sentencing-delay violation is errors patent
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 882 — illegal sentences may be corrected at any time
- *State v. Williams*, 2000-1725 (La. 11/28/01), 800 So.2d 790, 798-99 — La. R.S. 15:301.1 self-activation framework for statutory restrictions on parole/probation/suspension

## Errors-Patent Categories (with templates)

### 1. Illegal Sentence

A sentence is "illegal" when it (a) exceeds the statutory maximum, (b) falls below a mandatory minimum, (c) imposes unauthorized conditions, or (d) restricts benefits (good time, probation, parole) where the statute does not authorize the restriction.

**Common scenarios:**
- Court imposes 60 years on an offense with a 50-year statutory maximum
- Court imposes 5 years where the statute requires a mandatory minimum of 10
- Court orders sentence served "without benefit of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence" where the statute authorizes denial of only one or two of those benefits
- Court orders consecutive sentences in violation of La. C.Cr.P. Art. 883's concurrent-sentence presumption
- Court fails to impose mandatory fine, restitution, or special condition (e.g., sex-offender registration)

**Authority:**
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 882 — illegal sentences may be corrected at any time, by the court that imposed it or by an appellate court on review
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 920(2) — illegal sentence reviewable as errors patent without contemporaneous objection
- *State v. Williams*, 2000-1725 (La. 11/28/01), 800 So.2d 790, 798-99 — La. R.S. 15:301.1 self-activates statutory benefit-restrictions, eliminating need for remand on illegally lenient sentences in many cases (note: Williams is more accurately read as the self-activation framework, not as authority for "improper restriction of benefits" per se — confirm framing before relying)

**Template paragraph:**

```
Errors Patent — Illegal Sentence

Pursuant to La. C.Cr.P. Art. 920(2), this Court conducts an errors-patent
review on the face of the record. The trial court imposed a sentence of
[X years] on Count [Y], to be served "without benefit of parole, probation,
or suspension of sentence." (Sentencing Tr., p. [X], ll. [X-X]; Commitment
Order, R. Vol. [X], p. [X].) However, La. R.S. [statute citation] authorizes
restriction of [only specific benefit(s)], not all three. The sentence is
therefore illegal under Art. 882 and must be vacated and the case remanded
for resentencing within the statutory limits. See State v. Williams,
2000-1725 (La. 11/28/01), 800 So.2d 790, 798-99 (framework for statutory
restrictions on parole/probation/suspension under La. R.S. 15:301.1).
```

### 2. Boykin Deficiency (Guilty Pleas Only)

A guilty plea must reflect on the record a knowing and voluntary waiver of three federal constitutional rights:
1. Right to jury trial
2. Right to confront accusers
3. Right against compelled self-incrimination

*Boykin v. Alabama*, 395 U.S. 238 (1969). Louisiana applies the *Boykin* requirement strictly. The plea colloquy or written waiver must show, on the face of the record, that the defendant was advised of and waived each of the three rights.

**Common deficiencies:**
- Plea colloquy fails to mention one or more of the three rights
- Written waiver is not signed or initialed by the defendant
- Trial court does not personally address the defendant
- Defendant's responses do not affirmatively waive

**Template paragraph:**

```
Errors Patent — Boykin Deficiency

The record of the guilty-plea colloquy on [date] (R. Vol. [X], p. [X]) does
not reflect that [DEFENDANT] was advised of and waived [identify which of
the three Boykin rights is missing — jury trial / confrontation /
self-incrimination]. Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238 (1969), requires that
the record affirmatively show waiver of each of the three rights. The
record here does not. Accordingly, the guilty plea is constitutionally
infirm, the conviction and sentence must be vacated, and the matter remanded
to allow [DEFENDANT] to plead anew.
```

### 3. Art. 873 Sentencing-Delay Violation

La. C.Cr.P. Art. 873 requires:
- At least 24 hours between conviction (verdict) and sentence in felony cases (the article references "three days" but Louisiana jurisprudence applies it as 24 hours; see *State v. Augustine*, 555 So.2d 1331 (La. 1990))
- At least 24 hours between denial of a motion for new trial / motion in arrest of judgment and sentencing
- The defendant may waive the delay, but the waiver must appear in the record

**Common scenarios:**
- Sentence imposed immediately after verdict with no waiver
- Motion for new trial denied; sentence imposed same day with no waiver
- Sentencing minute entry shows no waiver, and the transcript shows no inquiry

**Authority:**
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 873
- *State v. Augustine*, 555 So.2d 1331 (La. 1990) — delay violation is errors patent; waiver must be in record

**Template paragraph:**

```
Errors Patent — Art. 873 Sentencing Delay

The verdict was returned on [date] (Verdict Form, R. Vol. [X], p. [X]).
The trial court denied the motion for new trial on [date] (Minute Entry,
R. Vol. [X], p. [X]) and imposed sentence the same day (Sentencing Tr.,
p. [X]). La. C.Cr.P. Art. 873 requires at least 24 hours between denial of
a motion for new trial and sentencing. The record contains no express waiver
of the delay by [DEFENDANT]. Failure to observe the Art. 873 delay without
a record waiver is errors patent. State v. Augustine, 555 So.2d 1331 (La.
1990). The sentence must be vacated and the matter remanded for resentencing
after the proper delay.
```

### 4. Defective Charging Instrument

The bill of information or indictment must charge an offense punishable under a valid statute. A charging instrument that fails to charge an offense, that charges an offense under an inapplicable statute, or that omits an essential element of the offense is fatally defective.

**Note:** Defective-charging-instrument challenges are also reviewable through a motion in arrest of judgment under Art. 858. But where the defect appears on the face of the record, errors-patent review applies even without a motion.

**Common scenarios:**
- Bill of information charges a non-existent offense
- Bill omits an essential element (e.g., specific intent for first degree murder)
- Bill cites a repealed or unconstitutional statute
- Bill describes facts that, if proved, do not constitute the charged offense

**Template paragraph:**

```
Errors Patent — Defective Charging Instrument

The Bill of Information filed [date] (R. Vol. [X], p. [X]) charges
[DEFENDANT] with [offense], citing La. R.S. [X]. The bill omits the
essential element of [identify element]. A charging instrument that fails
to charge an essential element of the offense is fatally defective on its
face. La. C.Cr.P. Art. 464; Art. 920(2). The conviction must be vacated.
```

### 5. Unauthorized Multiple Punishment (Double Jeopardy on Face of Record)

Where the record shows conviction and sentence on both the greater offense and a necessarily included lesser offense, double jeopardy bars the dual punishment. *Brown v. Ohio*, 432 U.S. 161 (1977); *Blockburger v. United States*, 284 U.S. 299 (1932).

**Common scenarios:**
- Conviction on first degree murder AND on the underlying felony (where the same conduct is the basis)
- Conviction on attempted murder AND aggravated battery on same victim/same act
- Conviction on possession with intent to distribute AND simple possession of the same controlled substance

**Template paragraph:**

```
Errors Patent — Multiple Punishment for Same Offense

The verdict (R. Vol. [X], p. [X]) and sentence (Sentencing Tr., p. [X])
reflect convictions and sentences on both Count [X] (first degree murder)
and Count [Y] (the underlying felony, [identify]). Where the underlying
felony is a necessary element of the greater offense, conviction and
sentence on both violates the Double Jeopardy Clause and Art. I, Section
15 of the Louisiana Constitution. The conviction on Count [Y] must be
vacated.
```

### 6. Improper Restriction of Benefits

Where the trial court imposes a restriction (denial of good time, probation, parole) that the sentencing statute does not authorize, the restriction is errors patent. This is a subset of "illegal sentence" but appears frequently enough to warrant separate treatment.

**Common scenarios:**
- Statute authorizes denial of probation only — court denies all three (probation, parole, suspension)
- Statute authorizes denial of benefits for a specified portion of the sentence — court denies for the entire sentence
- Statute permits good time — court orders sentence "without good time"

**Template paragraph:**

```
Errors Patent — Improper Restriction of Benefits

The trial court ordered the sentence on Count [X] served "without benefit
of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence" (Sentencing Tr., p. [X]).
La. R.S. [statute] authorizes only [identify which benefits may be denied].
The denial of [identify the unauthorized restriction] is therefore
unauthorized. The sentence must be amended to delete the unauthorized
restriction. La. C.Cr.P. Art. 882.
```

### 7. Failure to Impose Mandatory Sentencing Component

Where a sentencing statute mandates a fine, restitution, sex-offender registration, supervision condition, or other component, and the trial court omits it, the omission is errors patent and the sentence is "illegal" under Art. 882.

**Common scenarios:**
- Statute mandates fine of $1,000 to $5,000; court imposes no fine
- Statute mandates restitution to identified victim; court does not address restitution
- Statute mandates sex-offender registration as a special condition; court does not impose

**Template paragraph:**

```
Errors Patent — Failure to Impose Mandatory Sentencing Component

La. R.S. [X] requires that any sentence for [offense] include [mandatory
component — fine / restitution / registration / etc.]. The sentence imposed
on Count [X] (Sentencing Tr., p. [X]) does not include the mandatory
[component]. The omission is errors patent and the sentence is illegal
under Art. 882. The sentence must be vacated and the matter remanded for
resentencing including the mandatory component.
```

## Combined Errors-Patent Section Template

When the brief raises multiple errors patent, use a combined section heading.

```
ASSIGNMENT OF ERROR NO. [X] (ERRORS PATENT)

Pursuant to La. C.Cr.P. Art. 920(2), this Court conducts an errors-patent
review on the face of the record. The following errors patent require
correction:

A. Illegal Sentence — Improper Restriction of Benefits
   [Template paragraph from Section 1 / 6]

B. Art. 873 Sentencing-Delay Violation
   [Template paragraph from Section 3]

C. Failure to Impose Mandatory Restitution
   [Template paragraph from Section 7]

For each of the foregoing errors patent, the sentence must be vacated and
the matter remanded for resentencing in accordance with law.
```

## Drafting Notes

1. **Errors patent need not be preserved by objection.** That is the entire point of Art. 920(2). Do not state in the Preservation subsection that an errors-patent issue requires preservation — instead state: "Errors patent are reviewable under Art. 920(2) without contemporaneous objection. *State v. Augustine*, 555 So.2d 1331 (La. 1990)."

2. **Always cite the face-of-record source.** Every errors-patent argument must cite the minute entry, charging instrument, sentencing transcript, or commitment order from which the error is "discoverable by mere inspection."

3. **Errors patent and sufficiency are different.** Sufficiency under *Jackson v. Virginia* requires inspection of the evidence — it is NOT an errors-patent issue, but it IS reviewable as a separate assignment of error.

4. **The State will conduct its own errors-patent review.** The State's brief and the panel's per-curiam opinion both address errors patent independently. The defense brief should still raise them — counsel cannot rely on the State or the panel to catch every issue.

5. **Relief is typically vacatur-and-remand.** Most errors patent result in vacatur of the sentence (or, for Boykin issues, vacatur of the conviction) and remand for proceedings consistent with the corrected error. Sufficiency (not errors patent) is the only path to outright reversal without remand under *Burks v. United States*, 437 U.S. 1 (1978).
