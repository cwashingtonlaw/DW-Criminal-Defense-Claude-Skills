The **Bickham template** (`motion_for_bond_reduction.docx`, c. 2014) cites **La. C.Cr.P. Art. 334** for the factors. **The 2017 revisions renumbered the factors to La. C.Cr.P. Art. 316** with related provisions at Art. 318 and Art. 341 (modification). Any draft from the Bickham template **must** be updated to the current article numbers — every reference to "Art. 334" in that template is stale.

The **Garrison memorandum** (March 2026) correctly cites Art. 316 and Art. 341 — use it as the modern reference for current article numbering.

## Statutory framework

- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 316 — Factors for fixing bail (current numbering; formerly Art. 334):
  - (1) Seriousness of the offense charged
  - (2) Weight of the evidence against the defendant
  - (3) Previous criminal record of the defendant
  - (4) Ability of the defendant to give bail
  - (5) Nature and seriousness of danger to any other person or the community
  - (6) Risk of the defendant fleeing
  - (7) Defendant's past history of failure to appear for scheduled court appearances
  - (8) Any other circumstances affecting the probability of the defendant's appearance
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 318 — Bail amount; security for appearance
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 341 — Modification of bail conditions and amount at any time
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 313 — Capital offenses; State's burden on detention
- La. Const. Art. I, § 18 — Excessive bail prohibited
- La. Const. Art. I, § 16 — Right to speedy trial
- La. Const. Art. I, § 13 — Right to assist in defense
- U.S. Const. amend. VIII — Excessive bail clause

## Federal authority

- *Stack v. Boyle*, 342 U.S. 1 (1951), at 4-5 — bail excessive if higher than reasonably calculated to ensure appearance; "should not be a device for keeping persons in jail" (Jackson concurrence)
- *United States v. Salerno*, 481 U.S. 739 (1987) — bail must be reasonably calculated to assure appearance; due process limits on pretrial detention
- *Hernandez v. Heyd*, 307 F.Supp. 826 (E.D. La. 1970) — excessiveness measured by whether reasonably calculated to assure presence
- *Pugh v. Rainwater*, 572 F.2d 1053 (5th Cir. 1978) — money bail constitutes excessive restraint for indigent defendants when alternative conditions can assure appearance
- *United States v. McConnell*, 842 F.2d 105 (5th Cir. 1988) — financial inability as factor in bail determination

## Louisiana authority

- *State v. Jones*, 252 La. 903, 215 So.2d 108 (La. 1968) — fundamental purpose of bail is appearance, not pretrial punishment; cited the Jackson concurrence in *Stack v. Boyle* for the proposition that bail "should not be a device for keeping persons in jail"
- *State v. Mistich*, 182 La. 43 (La. 1935) — broad but not unlimited trial court discretion in fixing bail; identified seriousness of offense, danger to the public, and probable punishment as primary factors
- *State v. Wertheimer*, 183 La. 388 (La. 1935) — applications for bail reduction not premature at any stage; appellate courts retain supervisory jurisdiction
- *Foster v. Louisiana Dept. of Corrections*, 382 So.2d 986 (La. App. 1st Cir. 1980) — ability to pay as bail factor
- *State v. Collins* (La. App. 5th Cir. 2019, unpublished) — prior convictions not dispositive under Art. 316. **[VERIFY — unpublished opinion; Louisiana Uniform Rules of the Courts of Appeal Rule 2-16.3 restricts citation of unpublished opinions. Confirm citation, panel, and continuing validity before relying on it in a filed memorandum]**

## Drafting note — argument hierarchy

The Garrison memorandum demonstrates a strong four-pronged argument structure for prolonged-detention cases:

1. **Length of pretrial detention** — passage of time alone weighs heavily; pair with the speedy-trial constitutional provisions even when not asserting a speedy-trial violation
2. **Inability to post** — a bond the defendant cannot make is presumptively excessive (*Stack v. Boyle*); financial inability is a constitutional factor (*Pugh*, *McConnell*, *Foster*)
3. **Community ties / low flight risk** — Art. 316 factor (6) and (7); compliance history at every court date is the cleanest factual hook
4. **Proposed conditions of release** — GPS, curfew, pretrial services, no-contact, passport surrender; *Pugh v. Rainwater* recognizes that alternative conditions can adequately protect the community without money bail

For shorter or routine bond reductions, the Bickham short-form structure (numbered paragraphs addressing each Art. 316 factor) is sufficient. Reserve the full four-pronged memorandum approach for contested or prolonged cases.

## Citation hygiene reminders

1. **Confirm article numbering before filing.** Art. 334 → Art. 316 is the principal renumbering, but the bail provisions have been amended multiple times. Always check the current code.
2. **Parallel reporters for Louisiana cases.** *State v. Jones*, 252 La. 903, 215 So.2d 108 (La. 1968) cites both the Louisiana Reports and the Southern Reporter.
3. **Pin cites for specific propositions.** *Stack v. Boyle* on bail-as-non-punishment requires "342 U.S. 1, 4-5" — not just the first-page cite.
4. **Unpublished opinions.** Flag any unpublished citation (e.g., *State v. Collins*). La. Unif. R. Ct. App. 2-16.3 restricts citation in many contexts.
