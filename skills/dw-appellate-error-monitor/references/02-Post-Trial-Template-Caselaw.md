## Motion for New Trial — Art. 851

### Statutory framework

- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 851 — Grounds for new trial
  - (1) Verdict is contrary to the law and the evidence
  - (2) Prejudicial error in ruling on a written motion or an objection made during the proceedings
  - (3) New and material evidence not available at trial despite due diligence
  - (4) Defendant was deprived of constitutional rights
  - (5) Ends of justice would be served (court's discretion)
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 853 — Timing: "a motion for a new trial must be filed and disposed of before sentence"
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 854 — New trial procedure
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 855 — Motion based on newly discovered evidence
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 858 — Bills of exception not required when motion for new trial is filed

### Substantive theory citations (from Ross template — self-defense Art. 851(1))

- La. R.S. 14:20(A)(1) — Justifiable homicide; reasonable belief of imminent danger of losing life or receiving great bodily harm
- La. R.S. 14:20(A)(2) — Justifiable homicide; preventing a violent or forcible felony involving danger to life or great bodily harm; reasonable-person standard

### Drafting note — distinguishing Art. 851(1) from Jackson sufficiency

Art. 851(1) — "verdict contrary to the law and the evidence" — is a **weight of the evidence** challenge. This is distinct from a *Jackson v. Virginia*, 443 U.S. 307 (1979), sufficiency-of-the-evidence challenge raised on appeal. The trial court reviewing an Art. 851(1) motion sits as a "thirteenth juror" and weighs the evidence; the appellate court reviewing sufficiency views the evidence in the light most favorable to the prosecution. Counsel should not conflate these standards in the motion.

### Self-defense weight argument — Ross template structure

The Ross template uses a clean four-step weight argument that is portable to other self-defense cases:

1. Recite La. R.S. 14:20(A)'s "reasonable belief" standard
2. Identify physical evidence supporting self-defense (in Ross: officer's observation of the decedent holding a firearm in firing position at the scene)
3. Recite the defendant's own statement of the self-defense theory (in Ross: interview with detective)
4. State that "the State failed to produce any evidence to rebut" the self-defense claim — therefore the verdict is contrary to the evidence under Art. 851(1)

This is the cleanest weight-of-the-evidence framing for self-defense cases. Adapt the specific evidence items to the case at bar.

## Motion for Appeal — Art. 914

### Statutory framework

- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 912 — When appeal may be taken; 30 days after order denying post-trial motion, or 30 days from sentence if no post-trial motion is filed
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 914 — Motion for appeal procedure
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 914.1(A) — Designation of record on appeal
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 916 — Return date

### Louisiana Appeals and Writ Service (LAWS)

The State Public Defender's Office operates LAWS, which handles indigent criminal appeals statewide. The Jefferson Parish template appoints LAWS through:

- Remy Voisin Starns, LA # 26522, State Public Defender, LAWS, 301 Main St, Suite 700, Baton Rouge, LA 70825
- Michael A. Mitchell, LA Bar # 09623, Deputy State Public Defender

For indigent appellants, the Motion for Appeal should appoint LAWS and seek withdrawal of trial counsel. For retained appellants, the motion should designate the actual appellate counsel arrangement (retained appellate counsel, continuation by trial counsel, or other).

### Designation of record best practice

Art. 914.1(A) permits designation of "the entire record" — this is generally the safer designation for criminal appellants because any portion of the record not designated cannot be reviewed by the appellate court. The Jefferson Parish template uses this approach: "the defendant respectfully designates the entire record including transcripts of each hearing herein and all of the pleadings for inclusion in the appellate record."

For complex multi-defendant cases or unusually long records, partial designation may be appropriate to control transcript costs — but always discuss with appellate counsel before partial designation.

## Citation hygiene reminders

1. **Art. 851 vs. Jackson sufficiency.** These are different standards reviewed by different courts. Do not conflate.
2. **Filing deadlines drive everything.** Motion for New Trial → before sentence (Art. 853). Motion for Appeal → within 30 days of the triggering event (Art. 912). Miss either deadline and the corresponding right is waived.
3. **Caption integrity.** The Ross template has an internal caption inconsistency (32nd JDC, Calcasieu in the caption, Terrebonne in the body and order). Always verify the caption matches the actual filing parish and judicial district before filing — the 32nd JDC is Terrebonne, not Calcasieu (Calcasieu is 14th JDC).
