# Objection Cheat Sheet — Louisiana Evidentiary Objections

**Quick reference for the courtroom. Use it while standing up.**

This sheet pairs every common objection with (1) the controlling article, (2) the form of the objection (the words you say to the court), (3) the most likely ruling, and (4) the preservation pitfall — what trips trial counsel up and waives the issue on appeal.

Authority: Louisiana Code of Evidence (La. C.E.) and Louisiana Code of Criminal Procedure (La. C.Cr.P.). The contemporaneous-objection rule of La. C.Cr.P. Art. 841 governs every entry on this sheet — a general "objection, your honor" preserves nothing. State the specific ground.

---

## 1. Hearsay (La. C.E. Art. 801-806)

**Form:** *"Objection — hearsay, your honor; out-of-court statement offered for the truth of the matter asserted, and no exception under Art. 803 or 804 applies."*

- **Definition:** Out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted (Art. 801(C)).
- **Common exceptions to anticipate:** present sense impression (803(1)), excited utterance (803(2)), then-existing mental/emotional/physical condition (803(3)), business records (803(6)), public records (803(8)), prior testimony (804(B)(1)), statement against interest (804(B)(3)), dying declaration (804(B)(2)).
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained when the proponent cannot identify a specific exception; overruled when the proponent identifies an exception and lays foundation; sustained as to truth, overruled as to non-hearsay use (state of mind, effect on listener) — request a limiting instruction.
- **Preservation pitfall:** A general hearsay objection may not preserve a Confrontation Clause challenge. If the statement is testimonial (formal police interview, lab report by analyst not in court), object on BOTH hearsay AND Crawford grounds. *Crawford v. Washington*, 541 U.S. 36 (2004).

## 2. Relevance (La. C.E. Art. 401-403)

**Form (Art. 401):** *"Objection — relevance, your honor. The evidence has no tendency to make a fact of consequence more or less probable."*

**Form (Art. 403):** *"Objection — even if marginally relevant, the probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice / confusion of the issues / misleading the jury / undue delay / waste of time / cumulative evidence."*

- **Art. 401 — definition of relevant evidence.** Tendency to make a consequential fact more or less probable.
- **Art. 402 — relevant evidence is admissible unless excluded by another rule.**
- **Art. 403 — exclusion of relevant evidence on grounds of prejudice, confusion, or waste of time.** This is the workhorse exclusion provision.
- **Most-common rulings:** Art. 401 alone is a weak objection — the bar is low. Art. 403 is much stronger when the evidence is graphic, cumulative, or only marginally probative.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Bare "relevance" rarely wins. Pair with Art. 403 balancing language. State the specific prejudice (e.g., "graphic autopsy photos already established by stipulation") so the appellate court has a record.

## 3. Foundation (La. C.E. Art. 901, 602)

**Form (Art. 901 — authentication):** *"Objection — lack of foundation; the proponent has not authenticated this exhibit under La. C.E. Art. 901."*

**Form (Art. 602 — personal knowledge):** *"Objection — the witness has not been shown to have personal knowledge of the matter under Art. 602."*

- **Art. 901 — authentication.** Requirement: evidence sufficient to support a finding that the matter is what its proponent claims.
- **Art. 602 — lack of personal knowledge.** Witness may not testify to a matter unless evidence is introduced sufficient to support a finding of personal knowledge.
- **Common authentication issues:** chain of custody (physical evidence), business-records foundation (Art. 803(6) custodian testimony), photo/video authentication (witness familiar with scene), digital evidence (text messages, social media — increasingly contested).
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained when the proponent skips foundation; usually curable by laying foundation and re-offering.
- **Preservation pitfall:** If the court conditionally admits "subject to connection," renew the objection at end of proponent's case if foundation never materializes — otherwise waived.

## 4. Expert Testimony (La. C.E. Art. 702-705)

**Form:** *"Objection under Art. 702 / Daubert / Foret — the proponent has not established that this witness is qualified, that the testimony is the product of reliable principles and methods, or that those methods have been reliably applied to the facts of this case. We request a Daubert hearing."*

- **Art. 702 — qualifications and reliability.** The Louisiana Supreme Court adopted *Daubert v. Merrell Dow*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993), in *State v. Foret*, 628 So.2d 1116 (La. 1993).
- **Art. 703 — bases of expert opinion.** Expert may rely on facts/data made known at or before the hearing; if of a type reasonably relied upon in the field, need not be admissible.
- **Art. 704 — opinion on ultimate issue.** Generally permitted, BUT not on whether defendant did or did not have the mental state constituting an element of the crime.
- **Art. 705 — disclosure of underlying facts/data.** Expert may testify in opinion form without prior disclosure of underlying facts; on cross, the underlying facts may be required.
- **Most-common rulings:** Daubert/Foret challenges to forensic methodology (cell-site analysis, bite-mark, ballistics comparison, drug-recognition expert) increasingly granted in part. Pure qualifications challenges usually overruled.
- **Preservation pitfall:** A pre-trial motion in limine alone may not preserve. Renew at trial when the witness is offered as an expert. Lodge specific objection to each opinion offered.

## 5. Character Evidence (La. C.E. Art. 404, 405)

**Form (Art. 404(A) — character of accused / victim):** *"Objection — improper character evidence under Art. 404(A). The State has not opened the door."*

**Form (Art. 404(B) / Prieur):** *"Objection — Art. 404(B), prior bad act. The State has not provided proper Prieur notice and the evidence is not admissible for any non-propensity purpose."*

- **Art. 404(A) — character of accused.** State may not initiate character evidence against the accused; defendant may open the door, then State may rebut.
- **Art. 404(B) — other crimes / wrongs / acts.** Not admissible to prove character / propensity. Admissible for motive, opportunity, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity, absence of mistake or accident.
- **Prieur notice — *State v. Prieur*, 277 So.2d 126 (La. 1973).** State must give pre-trial notice and a hearing on admissibility before introducing 404(B) evidence.
- **Art. 405 — methods of proving character.** Reputation or opinion; specific instances only on cross.
- **Most-common rulings:** Prieur notice failure → exclusion. Notice given + non-propensity purpose articulated → usually admitted with limiting instruction.
- **Preservation pitfall:** When 404(B) evidence is admitted with a limiting instruction, REQUEST the limiting instruction on the record (don't just nod when offered). Object to scope creep — if the State exceeds the noticed purpose during questioning, object again. See `dw-404b-opposition` skill for full briefing.

## 6. Leading Questions (La. C.E. Art. 611)

**Form:** *"Objection — leading. The State is testifying."*

- **Art. 611(C) — leading questions.** Should not be used on direct, except as necessary to develop testimony.
- **Permitted:** preliminary matters, hostile witnesses, witnesses identified with adverse party, on cross-examination.
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained as to substantive matters; overruled as to preliminary, on cross, or with hostile witness.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Habitual leading is rarely a strong appellate issue absent a pattern showing the witness's testimony was the prosecutor's, not the witness's. Log the pattern in the objection log.

## 7. Narrative

**Form:** *"Objection — calls for a narrative response. The witness should answer specific questions."*

- **Basis:** trial court's control of mode and order of interrogation under Art. 611(A).
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained when narrative invites hearsay or unfocused testimony; overruled when court tolerates open-ended direct.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Object EARLY in the narrative, before objectionable content lands.

## 8. Asked and Answered

**Form:** *"Objection — asked and answered."*

- **Basis:** Art. 611(A) — court's control to avoid waste of time.
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained when the question is verbatim repetition; overruled when the rephrasing seeks clarification or impeachment.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Strongest when paired with cumulative-evidence objection under Art. 403.

## 9. Argumentative

**Form:** *"Objection — argumentative. Counsel is arguing with the witness rather than asking a question."*

- **Basis:** Art. 611(A); summation belongs in closing argument.
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained when the question is a speech with a question mark at the end; overruled in vigorous cross.
- **Preservation pitfall:** None significant — but flag for pattern in closing-argument objections.

## 10. Vague

**Form:** *"Objection — vague. The witness cannot reasonably be expected to know what counsel is asking."*

- **Most-common rulings:** sustained when the question is genuinely ambiguous; usually rephrased and re-asked.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Weak appellate issue; use to interrupt witness rhythm.

## 11. Compound

**Form:** *"Objection — compound. The question contains two questions."*

- **Most-common rulings:** sustained; counsel rephrases.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Weak appellate issue. Useful for cross discipline.

## 12. Calls for Speculation

**Form:** *"Objection — calls for speculation. The witness has not been shown to have knowledge of [X]."*

- **Basis:** Art. 602 — personal knowledge requirement.
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained when the question asks the witness to guess at someone else's state of mind, future events, or matters outside personal knowledge.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Lay-opinion shading — if the question seeks an opinion, also object under Art. 701 (improper lay opinion).

## 13. Lack of Personal Knowledge

**Form:** *"Objection — lack of personal knowledge under Art. 602."*

- See foundation entry above. Useful when a witness testifies to events at which the witness was not present.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Pair with hearsay objection — usually the witness is repeating what someone else said.

## 14. Lay vs. Expert Opinion (Art. 701, 702)

**Form (lay opinion exceeding 701):** *"Objection — improper lay opinion. This testimony requires expert qualification under Art. 702."*

- **Art. 701 — lay opinion.** Limited to opinions rationally based on the witness's perception, helpful to a clear understanding, and not based on scientific, technical, or specialized knowledge.
- **Art. 702 — expert opinion.** Specialized knowledge required.
- **Common Art. 701 abuses:** officer testifying that a substance is cocaine without lab confirmation; officer testifying to "common practice of drug dealers" without expert qualification; lay witness identifying defendant from surveillance footage.
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained when the opinion crosses the line into specialized knowledge; overruled when the opinion is grounded in direct perception.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Object early — if the witness gives the opinion before objection, request strike + limiting instruction.

## 15. Confrontation Clause / Crawford

**Form:** *"Objection — Sixth Amendment Confrontation Clause; under Crawford v. Washington, this is a testimonial statement and the declarant is not subject to cross-examination."*

- **Authority:** *Crawford v. Washington*, 541 U.S. 36 (2004); *Davis v. Washington*, 547 U.S. 813 (2006); *Melendez-Diaz v. Massachusetts*, 557 U.S. 305 (2009); *Bullcoming v. New Mexico*, 564 U.S. 647 (2011).
- **Testimonial vs. non-testimonial:** Statements with the primary purpose of establishing past facts for use in a criminal prosecution are testimonial. 911 calls during ongoing emergency are usually non-testimonial; police interviews are testimonial.
- **Forensic reports:** Lab analysts must testify in person. The State cannot substitute a surrogate analyst (`Bullcoming`).
- **Most-common rulings:** Confrontation objections to lab reports, autopsy reports, and police-interview hearsay are increasingly granted.
- **Preservation pitfall:** PAIR Crawford with hearsay. A hearsay objection alone may not preserve the Confrontation Clause issue; an Art. 802 objection is a state-evidence-rule objection, while Crawford is constitutional. State both grounds.

## 16. Improper Closing Argument

**Form:** *"Objection — improper argument. We move for an admonition under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 771; alternatively, we move for a mistrial under Art. 770."*

- **Art. 770 mandatory triggers** (race, religion, prior crimes inadmissible, comment on failure to testify): SEE `mistrial-trigger-checklist.md`.
- **Art. 771 discretionary triggers:** vouching, appealing to passion, misstating evidence, golden-rule arguments, comment on facts not in evidence.
- **Preservation pitfall:** OBJECT IMMEDIATELY. Do not wait for the prosecutor to finish the sentence. Restate the motion if the court rules quickly. Record the exact words the prosecutor used (verbatim quote in the objection log).

## 17. Bolstering / Vouching

**Form:** *"Objection — improper bolstering. Counsel is vouching for the witness's credibility."*

- **Basis:** Art. 608(A) — character of witness for truthfulness may be supported only after attack.
- **Most-common rulings:** sustained on direct before any impeachment; overruled after impeachment opens the door.
- **Preservation pitfall:** Note the moment in time-stamp; pattern bolstering in closing is a stronger Art. 771 issue than mid-trial.

## 18. Improper Impeachment

**Form (extrinsic evidence on collateral matter):** *"Objection — collateral matter; counsel is bound by the witness's answer under Art. 607(D)."*

**Form (prior inconsistent statement — no foundation):** *"Objection — Art. 613; counsel has not laid foundation by directing the witness to the time, place, and substance of the prior statement."*

- **Art. 607 — attacking and supporting credibility.**
- **Art. 608 — character for truthfulness.**
- **Art. 609 — impeachment by evidence of conviction (criminal cases — special rules).**
- **Art. 613 — foundation for prior inconsistent statement.**
- **Preservation pitfall:** Foundation objections to prior inconsistent statements must be made before extrinsic evidence is offered.

## 19. Cumulative Evidence

**Form:** *"Objection — cumulative under Art. 403."*

- Pair with Art. 403 prejudice when the State piles on graphic photos, repetitive 911 calls, multiple witnesses to the same point.
- **Most-common rulings:** Court grants discretion to State; useful to build a record for Art. 403 appellate issue.

## 20. Best Evidence (Art. 1002)

**Form:** *"Objection — best evidence rule. To prove the contents of a writing / recording / photograph, the original is required under Art. 1002 unless an exception under Art. 1003 or 1004 applies."*

- **Most-common rulings:** Usually overruled if duplicates are reliable; useful when the original was destroyed or never produced.

## Preservation Quick-Check (run for every objection)

1. **Was the objection contemporaneous?** (Before / immediately after the answer — not five minutes later.) La. C.Cr.P. Art. 841.
2. **Was a specific ground stated?** ("Hearsay" / "Art. 802 — no exception" / "Crawford" — not just "objection.")
3. **Did the court rule?** If not, press for a ruling. "May we have a ruling, your honor?"
4. **If evidence was excluded, was a proffer made?** La. C.E. Art. 103(A)(2). Without a proffer, the issue is waived on appeal.
5. **Was the objection logged?** Module B objection log entry, time-stamped.
6. **Is a continuing objection in place?** Capture the exact stated scope. Continuing objections do NOT cover content outside their stated scope.
7. **For Crawford / 404(B) / Confrontation issues — are BOTH grounds stated?** State-rule objections do not always preserve constitutional grounds.

## Standard Form for the Record

When in doubt, use this template:

> *"Objection — [ground]. Specifically, [legal basis: rule / case / article]. We request [admonition / strike / limiting instruction / curative instruction / mistrial under Art. 770 / 771]. May we have a ruling on the record?"*

If the ruling excludes defense evidence:

> *"Your Honor, we proffer the excluded evidence under La. C.E. Art. 103(A)(2). The substance would be [X]. Alternatively, we request leave to present the proffer outside the presence of the jury."*
