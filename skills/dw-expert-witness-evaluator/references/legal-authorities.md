## Case Law Summary

### Daubert Foundation
**Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc.**, 509 U.S. 579 (1993) — Established the flexible reliability standard for federal expert testimony, replacing the rigid Frye test. The trial court acts as a gatekeeper to ensure expert testimony is both relevant and reliable.

### Louisiana Adoption
**State v. Foret**, 628 So.2d 1116 (La. 1993) — Louisiana adopted the Daubert framework while maintaining state court procedural independence. Established that La. C.E. Art. 702 requires reliability assessment on four prongs: (1) relevance, (2) sufficient basis, (3) reliable principles and methods, (4) reliable application to the facts.

### Universal Application
**Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael**, 526 U.S. 137 (1999) — Clarified that Daubert reliability assessment applies not only to scientific testimony but to all expert testimony, including technical and experience-based opinions.

### Analytical Gap
**General Electric Co. v. Joiner**, 522 U.S. 136 (1997) — Established that courts may exclude expert testimony where a logical gap exists between the data presented and the opinion rendered -- the "analytical gap" doctrine.

## Additional Authority References

- *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83 (1963) — Prosecution must disclose exculpatory evidence, including evidence affecting expert credibility and reliability
- *Giglio v. United States*, 405 U.S. 150 (1972) — Extends Brady obligations to impeachment material regarding witness credibility
- La. C.E. Art. 401, 402, 403 — Relevance and balancing of probative value against prejudicial effect
- La. C.E. Art. 901 — Authentication requirements for expert evidence
- *Frye v. United States*, 293 F. 1013 (D.C. Cir. 1923) — "General acceptance" test (no longer controlling in Louisiana but remains a Daubert factor)
