# Louisiana Code of Criminal Procedure Quick-Reference

**Disclaimer:** This is a quick reference only and not a substitute for the current statutory text. Louisiana Criminal Code of Procedure articles are frequently amended by the Louisiana Legislature. Always verify the current statutory text before relying on this reference. Last verified: April 6, 2026.

---

## 1. PRE-TRIAL / CHARGING

### Art. 61 — Prosecution by District Attorney
**Summary:** The district attorney has the duty and authority to prosecute all crimes alleged to have been committed within their jurisdiction. Defines prosecutorial authority and responsibility.

**Common Use Case:** Foundation for understanding who initiates criminal prosecution; used in D&W case dashboards to establish who controls charging decisions and what leverage D&W may have with DA office.

**Critical Deadlines:** None specifically, but feeds into Art. 521 speedy trial timelines.

---

### Art. 382 — Grand Jury Indictment Requirements
**Summary:** Establishes that felonies must be prosecuted by indictment or information; requires grand jury presentation for capital crimes and certain serious felonies unless waived. Specifies grand jury composition and voting requirements (12 jurors, majority vote for true bill).

**Common Use Case:** D&W pretrial motion library uses this to challenge grand jury composition, number of jurors, or voting procedures; used when preparing motions to quash indictments on structural grounds.

**Critical Deadlines:** Motion to quash must typically be filed before entering plea or trial (Art. 485).

---

### Art. 383 — Indictment Form
**Summary:** Prescribes the required form and content of indictments, including caption, charges, essential elements, and requirement that grand jury foreperson sign. Establishes what constitutes a sufficient indictment.

**Common Use Case:** D&W suppression motion and pretrial motion skills examine indictment form defects; used to identify grounds for quashing defective indictments or challenging prosecution on technical grounds.

**Critical Deadlines:** Same as Art. 485 (motion to quash).

---

### Art. 384 — Information Form
**Summary:** Establishes the form and content requirements for informations (charges filed by DA without grand jury). Specifies caption, charges, essential elements, and DA signature requirement.

**Common Use Case:** D&W discovers cases charged by information rather than indictment in misdemeanor and lower felony cases; used to verify proper charging procedures.

**Critical Deadlines:** Same as Art. 485 (motion to quash).

---

### Art. 461-470 — Joinder and Severance of Offenses/Defendants
**Summary:** Articles 461-467 permit joinder of multiple offenses and defendants if arising from same act, episode, or transaction, or if connected in their commission. Articles 468-470 allow severance if joinder would be prejudicial or unfair. Court may sever on motion or on its own motion.

**Common Use Case:** D&W pretrial motion library drafts severance motions when client faces joint trial with co-defendants or multiple unrelated charges; critical in strategy decisions (separate jury for each charge vs. single jury).

**Critical Deadlines:** Severance motions should be filed with other pretrial motions under Art. 536 (before trial).

---

### Art. 485 — Quashing Defective Indictment/Information
**Summary:** Allows motion to quash an indictment or information that is defective in substance or form, including defects in grand jury proceedings, form defects, failure to charge essential elements, or lack of jurisdiction. Motion must be filed before entering plea or proceeding to trial.

**Common Use Case:** D&W pretrial motion library uses this as the primary vehicle to challenge charging documents; grounds include grand jury defects, technical form violations, insufficient essential elements, or jurisdictional defects.

**Critical Deadlines:** Must be filed before plea or trial; constitutes a waiver if not timely raised.

---

### Art. 521 — Time Limitations for Commencing Trial (Speedy Trial)
**Summary:** Establishes deadlines for commencing trial: within 60 days for misdemeanor if defendant is in custody; within 90 days if in custody for felony; within 6 months if defendant is free on bail or recognizance (any charge). Exceptions apply for continuances granted for good cause, mental competency proceedings, and post-conviction relief pending.

**Common Use Case:** D&W case dashboard and pretrial motion library track speedy trial deadlines; used to file motions to quash if prosecution violates these time limits. Critical litigation tool when case is moving slowly.

**Critical Deadlines:** **Misdemeanor in custody: 60 days from arrest. Felony in custody: 90 days. Bail/OR release: 6 months from first appearance. Failure to try within these periods requires dismissal.**

---

## 2. BAIL AND PRETRIAL RELEASE

### Art. 311-342 — Bail Provisions (General Framework)
**Summary:** Establishes the comprehensive scheme for bail and pretrial release in Louisiana, including authority to set bail, conditions, and procedures. Articles 311-342 cover the full spectrum of bail provisions and release conditions.

**Common Use Case:** D&W bond and release motion skill references this framework when building arguments for release or bail reduction.

**Critical Deadlines:** Initial bail hearing typically within 72 hours of arrest (Art. 330).

---

### Art. 316 — Factors in Setting Bail
**Summary:** Specifies the factors a court must consider when setting bail: nature and circumstances of the offense, weight of evidence, ties to community, employment, family status, criminal record, mental/physical condition, previous failure to appear, and defendant's financial resources.

**Common Use Case:** D&W bond and release motion skill structures mitigation arguments around these factors; used to demonstrate defendant's lack of flight risk and community ties.

**Critical Deadlines:** None specifically, but relevant at first appearance and any bail reduction hearing.

---

### Art. 330 — Bail Hearing Procedure
**Summary:** Requires the court to conduct a bail hearing within 72 hours of arrest (or without unreasonable delay if weekend/holiday). Defendant has right to counsel and opportunity to be heard; prosecution and defense present evidence and arguments.

**Common Use Case:** D&W bond motion skill prepares for bail hearings; identifies arguments for lower bail or release on own recognizance.

**Critical Deadlines:** **Bail hearing must occur within 72 hours of arrest or without unreasonable delay.**

---

### Art. 334-335 — Reduction and Increase of Bail
**Summary:** Permits defendant to petition for bail reduction (Art. 334) if circumstances have changed; also permits prosecution to petition for bail increase if circumstances warrant. Court may increase, decrease, or terminate bail conditions.

**Common Use Case:** D&W bond and release motion skill files bail reduction motions when client's initial bail is excessive or circumstances improve (employment, housing stability, treatment compliance).

**Critical Deadlines:** No fixed deadline; can be filed at any time before trial when changed circumstances justify relief.

---

### Art. 341 — Release on Own Recognizance
**Summary:** Court may release defendant on own recognizance without bail if satisfied that defendant will appear and comply with conditions; factors include Art. 316 considerations and any requirement for surety.

**Common Use Case:** D&W bond motion skill argues for OR release as alternative to bail; frequently successful for lower-level offenses or defendants with strong community ties.

**Critical Deadlines:** Can be requested at first appearance.

## 3. DISCOVERY

### Art. 716 — Disclosure of Evidence by State
**Summary:** Prosecution must disclose to defense all evidence favorable to defendant and material either to guilt or punishment, including exculpatory evidence and evidence affecting credibility of prosecution witnesses. Applies whether or not defense requests.

**Common Use Case:** D&W discovery orchestrator and Brady/Giglio auditor use this as the foundation for Brady obligations; tracks which evidence State must produce (witness statements, exculpatory lab results, impeachment material).

**Critical Deadlines:** Continuing duty under Art. 722; Brady material must be disclosed timely and before trial.

---

### Art. 717 — Disclosure of Statement of Defendant
**Summary:** Prosecution must disclose all statements of defendant (oral, written, or recorded) made to law enforcement or prosecution; applies whether or not defense requests. Defense is entitled to copies of all statements.

**Common Use Case:** D&W interrogation auditor and transcript pipeline use this to obtain all custodial statements, interviews, and recordings; critical for identifying coercive interrogation and false confession indicators.

**Critical Deadlines:** Must be disclosed timely; part of Brady/Giglio obligations.

---

### Art. 718 — Disclosure of Scientific Reports
**Summary:** Prosecution must disclose all scientific or forensic reports and test results, including laboratory reports, DNA analysis, toxicology, ballistics, fingerprint analysis, and expert reports. Defense is entitled to copies and to conduct independent testing.

**Common Use Case:** D&W forensic auditors (crime scene, mobile forensics, cell site geolocation) use this to obtain all lab results and reports; critical for building expert rebuttals.

**Critical Deadlines:** Must be disclosed timely; expert reports must be disclosed with sufficient time for defense expert preparation.

---

### Art. 719 — Disclosure of Statements of Witnesses
**Summary:** Prosecution must disclose all statements of witnesses (oral, written, or recorded) made to law enforcement or prosecution. Applies to both inculpatory and exculpatory witness statements.

**Common Use Case:** D&W discovery compliance monitor tracks all witness statements; critical for cross-examination preparation and impeachment material.

**Critical Deadlines:** Must be disclosed timely; witness statements in advance of trial necessary for adequate defense preparation.

---

### Art. 720 — Disclosure of Evidence by Defendant
**Summary:** Defendant has corresponding obligation to disclose certain evidence to State in reciprocal discovery, including: alibi witnesses (with identifying information), expert witnesses, and expert reports. Must be disclosed within reasonable time before trial.

**Common Use Case:** D&W case dashboard and pretrial motion library track defense disclosure obligations; important timing consideration for strategic surprises vs. reciprocal compliance.

**Critical Deadlines:** Must disclose alibi witnesses and expert information within reasonable time before trial (typically 10-14 days in local rules).

---

### Art. 721 — Regulation of Discovery by Court
**Summary:** Court has authority to regulate discovery by order, including imposing conditions, limitations, or protective orders. May limit discovery if parties dispute scope or if protective order is justified (confidential information, work product, etc.).

**Common Use Case:** D&W discovery compliance monitor addresses discovery disputes; used when prosecution attempts to limit reasonable discovery or when defense seeks protective order for sensitive witness information.

**Critical Deadlines:** Discovery disputes typically resolved at status conferences or pretrial hearings.

---

### Art. 722 — Continuing Duty to Disclose
**Summary:** Both prosecution and defense have continuing obligation to disclose additional evidence as it becomes available throughout prosecution, including after trial begins. Any newly discovered evidence favorable to defendant must be disclosed immediately.

**Common Use Case:** D&W case dashboard and discovery orchestrator track emerging evidence; important for preserving Brady/Giglio claims and preserving error if State fails to disclose late evidence.

**Critical Deadlines:** Ongoing obligation; Brady material must be disclosed before trial or sufficiently before verdict to allow adequate defense preparation.

---

### Art. 723 — Sanctions for Failure to Comply
**Summary:** Court may impose sanctions on party failing to comply with discovery obligations, including continuance, mistrial (if timely noticed), adverse inference, reduction of charges, or dismissal. Failure to disclose Brady material may result in suppression of conviction.

**Common Use Case:** D&W discovery compliance monitor documents compliance failures and files motions for sanctions; preservation tool for appellate record.

**Critical Deadlines:** Sanctions motion must typically be timely filed after discovery violation is discovered.

---

## 4. PRETRIAL MOTIONS

### Art. 521 — Motion to Quash (Speedy Trial)
**Summary:** (Covered above in pre-trial section.) Defendant may move to quash charges if trial is not commenced within statutory speedy trial period. Motion requires dismissal if successful.

**Common Use Case:** D&W pretrial motion library files when prosecution is dilatory; powerful dismissal mechanism when speedy trial time runs.

**Critical Deadlines:** Must be timely raised; failure to object waives the right.

---

### Art. 531-535 — Pleas (Jurisdiction, Prescription, Double Jeopardy, etc.)
**Summary:** Permits defendant to plead to defects in jurisdiction, lack of jurisdiction over subject matter or person, bars to prosecution (double jeopardy, statute of limitations/prescription), former jeopardy, and other threshold defects. Defendant may plead any of these issues without conceding guilt. Successful plea requires dismissal.

**Common Use Case:** D&W plea negotiation analyzer and pretrial motion library identify bar to prosecution (prescription/statute of limitations) or jeopardy-based defenses; low-risk tactical pleadings that preserve trial rights while asserting threshold defenses.

**Critical Deadlines:** Should be filed as pretrial plea before trial; some defects (jurisdiction) may be raised at trial if not earlier waived.

---

### Art. 536 — Time for Filing Pretrial Motions
**Summary:** All pretrial motions (except those specifically allowed to be filed later) must be filed within the time fixed by the court, or within 10 days before trial if no earlier deadline set. Court may extend this deadline for good cause.

**Common Use Case:** D&W pretrial motion library establishes motion filing deadlines; tracks all planned motions to ensure they are timely filed or waived.

**Critical Deadlines:** **Pretrial motions due 10 days before trial unless court sets earlier deadline. Failure to timely file results in waiver unless good cause shown.**

---

### Art. 691-703 — Change of Venue
**Summary:** Allows defendant to move for change of venue if defendant cannot receive a fair trial in the venue due to prejudice, publicity, or other circumstances preventing impartial jury. Standard requires showing of substantial prejudice. New venue is typically another parish in the district.

**Common Use Case:** D&W case dashboard and pretrial motion library file change of venue motions in high-publicity cases or when local prejudice is evident; requires detailed factual support and often witnesses (voir dire, newspaper archives, social media evidence).

**Critical Deadlines:** Should be filed within pretrial motion period (Art. 536).

---

### Art. 671-674 — Recusal of Judge
**Summary:** Judge must recuse themselves if legally disqualified (prior involvement, bias, family relationship, financial interest, etc.). Defendant may move for recusal by affidavit stating grounds. If judge denies motion or recuses, case proceeds before substitute judge.

**Common Use Case:** D&W pretrial motion library files recusal motions when judge has prior involvement with case, represented one party previously, or has demonstrated bias in preliminary matters (excessive bail, denial of reasonable continuances).

**Critical Deadlines:** Should be filed early to avoid appearance of tactical abuse; must be timely.

---

## 5. SUPPRESSION AND EVIDENCE

### Art. 703 — Suppression of Evidence
**Summary:** Evidence obtained in violation of defendant's constitutional rights (search and seizure, confession coercion, statements in violation of Miranda, etc.) must be suppressed and is inadmissible at trial. Defense bears burden of proving violation; burden then shifts to prosecution to show admissibility (if applicable).

**Common Use Case:** D&W suppression motion skill and trial notebook builder prepare suppression motions and evidentiary hearings; critical in cases involving police searches, arrests, interrogations, or statements.

**Critical Deadlines:** Suppression motions typically filed within pretrial motion period; suppression hearing held before trial or as part of trial.

---

### Art. 768 — Opening Statement Limitations
**Summary:** Prosecution's opening statement is limited to facts that prosecution expects to prove; prosecution cannot argue its theory of case beyond evidence expected to be presented. Prosecution must disclose evidence of inculpatory statements by defendant or other evidence the State intends to use. Defense has right to respond to any improper reference to defendant's silence or inculpatory statements.

**Common Use Case:** D&W trial notebook builder and jury instructions specialist flag potential opening statement violations; preservation tool for appellate review.

**Critical Deadlines:** Objection to improper opening must be timely raised during trial; preserved for appeal if proper record made.

---

### Art. 770 — Mandatory Mistrial
**Summary:** Defines circumstances requiring mandatory mistrial, including references to defendant's failure to testify or silence (unless defendant has opened door), references to defendant's prior criminal convictions (unless defendant opened door), comments on defendant's right to counsel, or attorney misconduct. Trial must be declared mistrial upon proper objection and showing that curative instruction is insufficient.

**Common Use Case:** D&W trial notebook builder and jury instructions specialist monitor for mistrial-triggering statements; critical preservation tool for appellate review and potential conviction reversal.

**Critical Deadlines:** Mistrial objection must be raised immediately when improper reference is made; failure to timely object may result in waiver.

---

### Art. 771 — Admonition to Jury
**Summary:** Court must admonish jury regarding jurors' duties, law applicable to case, presumption of innocence, burden of proof (beyond reasonable doubt), and specific instructions on charges and defenses. Failure to properly admonish may result in reversal.

**Common Use Case:** D&W jury instructions builder ensures proper jury admonitions; drafts proposed jury charges addressing required admonitions and case-specific instructions.

**Critical Deadlines:** Jury admonitions given at beginning of trial and reiterated in final charge.

---

### Art. 772 — Scope of Argument
**Summary:** Parties may argue facts proven or fairly inferred from evidence, but may not argue matters outside the record, personal opinions, or inflammatory matters not supported by evidence. Court has discretion to limit argument; improper argument may be grounds for mistrial or reversal.

**Common Use Case:** D&W trial notebook builder flags potential argument violations; preservation tool for appellate review.

**Critical Deadlines:** Objection to improper argument must be timely raised; failure to object may result in waiver.

---

## 6. TRIAL

### Art. 774 — Jury Charges (Scope)
**Summary:** Court has authority to instruct jury on law applicable to case, including definitions of charges, essential elements, defenses, burden of proof, credibility of witnesses, and other legal questions. Jury charges are prepared by court in consultation with parties; either party may propose jury instructions.

**Common Use Case:** D&W jury instructions builder drafts proposed jury charges addressing all defenses; critical for ensuring jury understands not-guilty predicates and burden of proof.

**Critical Deadlines:** Proposed jury instructions typically due before trial or at reasonable time before jury charge is given.

---

### Art. 780 — Polling the Jury
**Summary:** After jury returns verdict, either party may request that jury be polled (each juror asked individually if verdict represents their true vote). If polled juror indicates disagreement, verdict may be set aside and jury sent back for further deliberation.

**Common Use Case:** D&W trial notebook builder includes polling request in verdict contingencies; used when conviction appears unanimous may indicate coercion or lack of true agreement.

**Critical Deadlines:** Poll request typically made immediately after verdict is announced.

---

### Art. 782 — Jury Unanimity
**Summary:** Following Ramos v. Louisiana, 140 S.Ct. 1390 (2020), jury verdicts in all felony cases (not just capital cases) must be unanimous. Non-unanimous verdicts are unconstitutional. This applies retroactively to cases on direct appeal and to collateral review in some circumstances.

**Common Use Case:** D&W appellate error monitor and LWOP populator track non-unanimous verdicts as grounds for reversal or post-conviction relief; critical issue for older convictions.

**Critical Deadlines:** Ramos applies retroactively; cases with non-unanimous verdicts may be subject to post-conviction relief under Art. 924-930.10.

---

### Art. 795 — Challenges for Cause
**Summary:** Permits unlimited challenges for cause to remove potential jurors who are biased, related to parties, have prior knowledge of case, have interest in outcome, or have demonstrated inability to be impartial. Trial court has broad discretion in determining cause; standard is whether juror can set aside bias and apply law fairly.

**Common Use Case:** D&W voir dire assistant and jury focus group prepare for juror challenges; identifies likely-biased jurors based on voir dire responses and demographic factors.

**Critical Deadlines:** Challenges for cause raised during voir dire; must be exercised before peremptory challenges exhausted.

---

### Art. 799 — Peremptory Challenges
**Summary:** Parties have limited number of peremptory challenges (typically 6-12 per side depending on charge severity; capital cases have more). Peremptory challenges allow removal of jurors without stating reason, subject to Batson challenge if pattern suggests discrimination by race, gender, or other protected characteristic.

**Common Use Case:** D&W voir dire assistant structures peremptory challenge strategy based on juror profiles and case themes; works in conjunction with Batson challenge preparation.

**Critical Deadlines:** Peremptory challenges exercised during voir dire; once jury is sworn, challenges exhausted.

---

### Art. 800 — Batson Challenges
**Summary:** Either party may challenge opposing party's use of peremptory challenge if pattern suggests intentional discrimination based on juror's membership in cognizable group (race, gender, etc.). Three-step process: (1) opponent makes prima facie showing of pattern; (2) prosecutor states race-neutral reason; (3) trial court determines if discrimination proven.

**Common Use Case:** D&W jury focus group and voir dire assistant prepare Batson objections when prosecution appears to strike jurors based on protected characteristics; documentation critical for appellate preservation.

**Critical Deadlines:** Batson challenge must be raised immediately when discriminatory pattern is apparent; failure to timely object may waive issue.

---

### Art. 801-807 — Jury Instructions
**Summary:** Comprehensive articles governing jury charges, jury instructions, pattern instructions, and jury deliberation. Courts must instruct on all material facts and law; both parties may propose instructions; court selects final instructions given to jury.

**Common Use Case:** D&W jury instructions builder drafts all proposed jury instructions addressing statutory elements, defenses, burden of proof, and case-specific issues.

**Critical Deadlines:** Proposed instructions due before jury charge.

---

## 7. RESPONSIVE VERDICTS

### Art. 814 — Responsive Verdicts in Enumerated Felonies
**Summary:** For specified felonies (murder, manslaughter, rape, burglary, robbery, aggravated assault, simple assault, etc.), jury must be allowed to return responsive verdict on included lesser offenses even if not explicitly requested by defense. Jury may convict on lesser-included offense without convicting on charged offense.

**Common Use Case:** D&W jury instructions builder includes responsive verdict instructions for enumerated felonies; strategy consideration in whether to request lesser-included instructions or allow jury to find its natural level.

**Critical Deadlines:** Responsive verdict instructions given in final charge; failure to give may result in reversal if error was preserved.

---

### Art. 815 — Responsive Verdicts in Non-Enumerated Felonies
**Summary:** For felonies not enumerated in Art. 814, jury may return responsive verdict on lesser-included offense only if the lesser offense is necessarily included in the charged offense (i.e., all elements of lesser are included in greater charge).

**Common Use Case:** D&W jury instructions builder analyzes whether lesser-included offenses qualify for responsive verdict instruction; jurisdictional issue in parishes with particular charging practices.

**Critical Deadlines:** Same as Art. 814.

---

## 8. SENTENCING

### Art. 871 — Sentence on Plea of Guilty
**Summary:** Upon guilty plea, court may accept or reject plea; if accepted, court must proceed to sentencing. Court must advise defendant of sentence consequences and ensure plea is knowing and voluntary. Sentencing typically follows presentence investigation.

**Common Use Case:** D&W plea negotiation analyzer and sentencing mitigation specialist prepare for guilty plea proceedings; ensure all required admonitions are given and sentencing range is discussed.

**Critical Deadlines:** Sentencing typically within 30-60 days of guilty plea; defense and prosecution submit sentencing recommendations.

---

### Art. 875-878 — Presentence Investigation
**Summary:** Court typically orders presentence investigation (PSI) report before sentencing; report contains defendant's background, criminal history, employment, family situation, substance abuse history, and other factors relevant to sentencing. Defendant has right to access and challenge accuracy of PSI report.

**Common Use Case:** D&W sentencing mitigation specialist reviews PSI for inaccuracies and prepares mitigation package to supplement or correct PSI; critical for sentencing hearing preparation.

**Critical Deadlines:** PSI typically completed within 30-45 days of guilty plea or conviction; defense given opportunity to review and supplement before sentencing.

---

### Art. 881.1 — Motion to Reconsider Sentence
**Summary:** Allows defendant to file motion to reconsider sentence within 30 days of sentencing if circumstances support reconsideration. Court has broad discretion to grant or deny; grounds include newly discovered mitigating evidence, errors in sentencing calculation, or substantial change in circumstances.

**Common Use Case:** D&W sentencing mitigation specialist files motion to reconsider if significant mitigating evidence becomes available post-sentencing or if sentencing calculation contains errors.

**Critical Deadlines:** **Motion must be filed within 30 days of sentencing; failure to timely file results in waiver. Exception: manifest injustice standard may apply in unusual circumstances.**

---

### Art. 883 — Concurrent vs. Consecutive Sentences
**Summary:** Court has discretion to impose concurrent sentences (running together) or consecutive sentences (one after another) when defendant is convicted of multiple crimes. Court must articulate reasons for choosing consecutive sentences; considerations include seriousness of offenses, prior record, and rehabilitation potential.

**Common Use Case:** D&W sentencing mitigation specialist argues for concurrent sentences; preservation tool for appellate review of consecutive sentence decisions.

**Critical Deadlines:** Sentencing decision made at sentencing hearing.

---

### Art. 890.1 — Minimum Mandatory Sentence Departure
**Summary:** For crimes with minimum mandatory sentences (certain drug offenses, violent felonies, habitual offender cases), court may depart from minimum mandatory only if it finds substantial and compelling reasons. Standard is narrow and appellate courts strictly review departures.

**Common Use Case:** D&W sentencing mitigation specialist prepares departure arguments when client faces minimum mandatory; common in drug trafficking and habitual offender cases.

**Critical Deadlines:** Departure arguments presented at sentencing hearing; court's reasoning must be clearly articulated for appellate review.

---

### Art. 894.1 — Sentencing Guidelines Factors
**Summary:** Court must consider statutory factors in sentencing: nature and seriousness of offense, prior criminal record, opportunity to rehabilitate, threat to public safety, victim impact, deterrence, protection of public, and other relevant factors. Factors are not dispositive but provide framework for sentencing discretion.

**Common Use Case:** D&W sentencing mitigation specialist addresses each factor in mitigation packet; structures sentencing hearing around these statutory guideposts.

**Critical Deadlines:** Sentencing factors addressed at sentencing hearing.

---

### Art. 893 — Suspension of Sentence / Probation
**Summary:** Court may suspend sentence and place defendant on probation for fixed period; defendant serves no prison time but is subject to probation conditions (reporting, drug testing, restitution, etc.). Probation may be straight probation (no prison) or split sentence (some prison then probation).

**Common Use Case:** D&W sentencing mitigation specialist advocates for probation or suspended sentence; common alternative to incarceration in non-violent felonies and first-time offenders.

**Critical Deadlines:** Probation term set at sentencing; duration typically ranges from 1-5 years depending on offense.

---

### Art. 895 — Conditions of Probation
**Summary:** Court may impose various conditions on probation including reporting requirements, substance abuse treatment, counseling, restitution, community service, and other conditions reasonably designed to ensure compliance with law and rehabilitation. Defendant's violation of probation conditions may result in revocation and imposition of suspended sentence.

**Common Use Case:** D&W case dashboard and sentencing specialist address probation conditions at sentencing; structure conditions to be achievable and realistic for client.

**Critical Deadlines:** Probation conditions imposed at sentencing; violations may trigger probation revocation hearing.

---

## 9. POST-TRIAL AND APPEAL

### Art. 851 — Motion for New Trial
**Summary:** Defendant may move for new trial within 30 days of verdict on grounds including: newly discovered evidence (unavailable at trial), jury misconduct, trial error affecting substantial rights, inconsistent verdicts, or other grounds justifying new trial. Standard is whether verdict was influenced by passion/prejudice or is unjust.

**Common Use Case:** D&W appellate error monitor and post-conviction specialist file motion for new trial when newly discovered evidence emerges or substantial trial error is apparent; timing is critical (30-day deadline).

**Critical Deadlines:** **Motion must be filed within 30 days of verdict; failure to timely file results in waiver. Exception: newly discovered evidence may extend deadline under certain circumstances.**

---

### Art. 858 — Motion in Arrest of Judgment
**Summary:** Allows defendant to move to arrest judgment (set aside conviction) before or immediately after sentencing on grounds including: lack of jurisdiction, failure to charge essential element, insufficient evidence to support conviction, or other defects going to legality of conviction.

**Common Use Case:** D&W appellate error monitor files motion in arrest of judgment as alternative to motion for new trial when fundamental conviction defects exist.

**Critical Deadlines:** Typically filed before or immediately after sentencing; some jurisdiction for post-sentencing filing depending on circumstances.

---

### Art. 912-930 — Appeal Provisions
**Summary:** Comprehensive articles governing appeal procedures, including right to appeal, designation of record on appeal, appellate briefs, oral arguments, scope of review, and standards for reversal. Articles 912-920 cover procedural aspects; Arts. 924-930.10 cover post-conviction relief.

**Common Use Case:** D&W appellate error monitor manages entire appellate process; tracks error preservation, designates appellate record, and manages appellate briefing.

**Critical Deadlines:** Notice of appeal typically due within 30-45 days of sentencing; record designation within 20 days; briefing schedule set by appellate court.

---

### Art. 914 — Designation of Record on Appeal
**Summary:** Defendant (through counsel or on motion) must designate which portions of trial record will be included in appellate record. Record includes trial transcript, pleadings, motions, rulings, evidence designations, and verdict. Appellate court may require full record if necessary to decision.

**Common Use Case:** D&W appellate error monitor designates record to include all evidence, testimony, and rulings necessary to preserve appellate challenges.

**Critical Deadlines:** Designation typically due within 10-20 days of notice of appeal; failure to timely designate may limit appellate record available for review.

---

### Art. 920 — Scope of Appellate Review
**Summary:** Appellate court reviews questions of law de novo (independent review); questions of fact reviewed for manifest error (conviction reversed only if reviewing court is left with reasonable doubt as to guilt or if verdict is manifestly unreasonable). Trial court's credibility determinations and evidentiary rulings reviewed for abuse of discretion.

**Common Use Case:** D&W appellate error monitor structures appellate brief around applicable standard of review; focuses on legal questions and manifest error fact questions where appellate relief is available.

**Critical Deadlines:** Appellate briefs due per appellate court schedule.

---

### Art. 924-930.10 — Post-Conviction Relief
**Summary:** Defendant may challenge conviction through post-conviction relief procedures on grounds including: ineffective assistance of counsel (Strickland standard), newly discovered evidence, illegal sentence, or other extraordinary circumstances. Articles 924 (original post-conviction) and 927-930.10 (supplemental and second/successive post-conviction) establish procedures and standards.

**Common Use Case:** D&W appellate error monitor and LWOP populator file post-conviction petitions attacking convictions on IAC and other grounds; critical vehicle for challenging non-unanimous convictions (Ramos claims) and other appellate errors.

**Critical Deadlines:** Original post-conviction petition typically due within one year of final conviction unless good cause for delay; successive petitions subject to strict time limits and gatekeeping requirements.

---

## 10. COMPETENCY AND CAPACITY

### Art. 641-649 — Mental Incapacity to Proceed
**Summary:** Defendant must be mentally competent to stand trial; competency requires ability to understand charges, potential punishment, and nature of trial proceedings, and ability to consult with attorney in preparation of defense. If competency is questioned, trial court may order competency evaluation; incompetent defendants cannot proceed to trial until competency is restored.

**Common Use Case:** D&W case dashboard tracks competency issues; defendant may request competency evaluation or prosecution may raise issue if mental illness is apparent. Mental health expert evaluation required.

**Critical Deadlines:** Competency proceedings typically stay criminal proceedings until competency is restored or defendant is hospitalized.

---

### Art. 650-651 — Commitment and Status Hearings
**Summary:** If defendant is found incompetent, court may commit defendant to secure mental health facility for evaluation and treatment aimed at restoration. Periodic status hearings required to determine if defendant has regained competency; defendant has right to legal representation at status hearings.

**Common Use Case:** D&W case dashboard monitors commitment status; participates in status hearings to advocate for restoration of competency or, if restoration is unlikely, may argue for conditional release or other alternatives.

**Critical Deadlines:** Status hearings typically required every 6 months; defendant may petition for competency re-evaluation at any time if circumstances suggest restoration.
