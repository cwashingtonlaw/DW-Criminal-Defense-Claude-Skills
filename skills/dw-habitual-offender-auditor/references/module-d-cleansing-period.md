# MODULE D — Cleansing Period Calculator

Louisiana's Habitual Offender Law provides a "cleansing period" — a window during which, if the defendant remains conviction-free, prior convictions may no longer be used as predicates. La. R.S. 15:529.1(C).

## The Statutory Cleansing Period

La. R.S. 15:529.1(C) provides:

> "The current offense shall not be counted as a second offense, a third offense, or a fourth offense, if more than **ten years** have elapsed between the date of the completion of sentence, probation, parole, or suspension of sentence for the previous conviction and the commission of the current offense, **unless the prior offense was a crime of violence** as defined in R.S. 14:2(B), **a sex offense** as defined in R.S. 15:541, or the offense of production, manufacturing, distribution, or dispensing or possession with intent to produce, manufacture, distribute, or dispense a controlled dangerous substance as provided in R.S. 40:966(A) and (B), R.S. 40:967(A) and (B), and R.S. 40:968(A) and (B)."

**Critical exceptions where no cleansing period applies:**
- Crimes of violence (La. R.S. 14:2(B))
- Sex offenses (La. R.S. 15:541)
- Certain drug distribution/manufacturing offenses (La. R.S. 40:966-968, subsections (A) and (B))

For these excepted offenses, the predicate can be used regardless of how much time has elapsed.

## Cleansing Period Calculation Method

**Step 1:** Identify the completion date for the predicate sentence:
- If incarcerated: date of release/discharge from DOC custody
- If placed on probation: date probation was completed (not the date probation was imposed)
- If placed on parole: date parole supervision terminated
- If sentence was suspended: date the suspended sentence period ended
- If probation/parole was revoked and the defendant was incarcerated: date of release after the revocation

**Step 2:** Identify the commission date of the current (or subsequent) offense.

**Step 3:** Calculate the elapsed time between the completion date (Step 1) and the commission date (Step 2).

**Step 4:** If 10 or more years have elapsed AND the predicate is not an excepted offense (crime of violence, sex offense, or qualifying drug offense), the predicate is cleansed and cannot be used.

*State v. Shaw*, 2006-2467 (La. 11/27/07), 969 So.2d 1233 — discussed computation of the cleansing period and what constitutes "completion of sentence."

## Common Cleansing Period Issues

| Issue | Analysis | Impact |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Completion date unknown or undocumented | The State bears the burden of establishing when the sentence was completed; if the State cannot prove the completion date, it cannot prove the cleansing period has not run | Defense argues presumption favors the defendant — if completion date is unknown, the cleansing period may have run |
| Probation revoked — which completion date? | If probation was revoked and the defendant was incarcerated, the completion date is the release from incarceration after revocation, not the original probation completion date | This can significantly affect the cleansing period calculation — revocation restarts the clock |
| Offense reclassified — is it still a "crime of violence"? | If the predicate offense has been reclassified and is no longer a crime of violence, the cleansing period may now apply | Research the version of La. R.S. 14:2(B) in effect at the time of the predicate offense and the current version |
| Multiple sentences running concurrently or consecutively | Concurrent sentences: completion date is the latest release date. Consecutive sentences: completion date is the end of the last sentence | Calculate carefully — concurrent vs. consecutive affects the cleansing period start date |
| Parole vs. discharge | Completion of parole (not just release from incarceration) is the relevant date; if the defendant was on parole supervision, the cleansing period starts when parole ended | Request DOC records to determine exact parole termination date |
