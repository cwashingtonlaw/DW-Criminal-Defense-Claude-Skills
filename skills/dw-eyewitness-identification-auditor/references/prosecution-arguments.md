## Quick Reference -- Common Prosecution Arguments & Defense Responses

| Prosecution Argument | Defense Response |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| "The witness is very confident" | Confidence at trial is meaningless after feedback, repeated exposure, and passage of time. Only initial confidence (if properly recorded) is probative. Wells & Bradfield (1998). |
| "The witness picked him out of a six-pack" | Lineup size is nominal. The functional size may be far smaller if fillers do not match the description. Evaluate composition independently. |
| "The witness had a good opportunity to view" | Opportunity to view must be evaluated objectively: lighting, distance, duration, stress, weapon presence, obstructions. Witness's subjective claim of good viewing is unreliable -- especially after confirming feedback inflated that belief. |
| "The identification was corroborated by other evidence" | The Manson/Brathwaite test evaluates the identification independently. Other evidence does not cure a suggestive identification procedure -- it may explain why the wrong person was charged. |
| "No one told the witness who to pick" | Single-blind administration allows unconscious cues. The administrator does not need to verbally direct the witness -- body language, attention patterns, and post-selection feedback are sufficient. |
| "The witness described the perpetrator before the identification" | Compare the description to the defendant. If the description is generic ("tall Black male in dark clothing"), it does not corroborate the identification -- it describes thousands of people. |
| "Perry v. New Hampshire means there's no due process issue" | Perry applies only to suggestive circumstances not arranged by police. If police arranged the identification procedure (photo array, lineup, showup), the full Manson/Brathwaite framework applies. |
| "The witness identified the defendant, not a filler" | Correct identification of a suspect does not validate the procedure. In studies where the real perpetrator is absent from the lineup, witnesses still choose someone 30-50% of the time. The choosing rate, not just the choice, matters. |
