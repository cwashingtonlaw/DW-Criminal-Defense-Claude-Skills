## MODULE A -- Photo Array / Lineup Procedure Audit

This module audits formal photo array (photographic lineup / "six-pack") and live lineup procedures for compliance with best practices established by the scientific literature, the NAS Report (2014), and Louisiana procedural requirements.

### A.1 -- Administration Protocol

**Double-Blind Administration (Gold Standard):**
The lineup administrator should not know which photograph or person in the lineup is the suspect. If the administrator knows, conscious or unconscious cues can influence the witness's selection.

| Factor | Best Practice | Deficiency if... | Severity |
|--------|-------------|------------------|----------|
| **Administrator knowledge** | Double-blind: administrator does not know suspect position | Single-blind: administrator knows suspect position -- risk of inadvertent or deliberate cueing | CRITICAL |
| **Administrator training** | Trained in identification procedure best practices | No documented training; no certification | SIGNIFICANT |
| **Administrator identity** | Someone other than the lead case detective | Lead detective or investigating officer administered the procedure | CRITICAL |
| **Recording** | Entire procedure video/audio recorded | No recording; only written documentation | SIGNIFICANT |

**The Problem with Single-Blind Administration:**
Research demonstrates that lineup administrators who know the suspect's position unintentionally influence witness behavior through verbal cues ("Are you sure?", "Take your time"), nonverbal cues (body posture changes, eye movements toward the suspect's position), and differential attention to the witness's response when viewing the suspect vs. fillers. The NAS Report (2014) at 106 recommended double-blind administration as the single most important procedural reform: "The single most important characteristic of a lineup procedure is that it should be conducted by an administrator who does not know which person in the lineup is the suspect."

**If double-blind administration was not used, this is a CRITICAL deficiency in every case.**

### A.2 -- Lineup Composition & Filler Selection

The lineup must be constructed so that the suspect does not stand out from the fillers. Fillers should be selected based on the witness's prior description of the perpetrator -- not based on the suspect's appearance.

**Description-Based vs. Suspect-Matched Filler Selection:**

| Method | Description | Assessment |
|--------|------------|------------|
| **Description-based** (recommended) | Fillers match the witness's prior description of the perpetrator | Reduces the risk that the suspect stands out; tests the witness's actual memory of the perpetrator |
| **Suspect-matched** (common but inferior) | Fillers match the suspect's appearance | Can result in fillers so similar that legitimate identification is difficult, or so dissimilar that the suspect stands out on dimensions the witness did not describe |

**Composition Audit Checklist:**

- [ ] How many photographs/persons were in the array? (Minimum: six for photo arrays; six for live lineups under most standards; Louisiana requires no fewer than six under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163)
- [ ] Were fillers selected based on the witness's prior description or matched to the suspect's appearance?
- [ ] Does the suspect stand out from the fillers on any dimension -- clothing, background, photo quality, facial expression, age range, build, hairstyle, skin tone, photo size, color vs. black-and-white?
- [ ] Were fillers known-innocent (not other suspects in the case)?
- [ ] Was the same set of fillers used for multiple witnesses? (If so, a filler selection by one witness could cause that filler to be removed or noted, altering the effective lineup for subsequent witnesses)
- [ ] Was the suspect's position in the array rotated for different witnesses?
- [ ] Were any fillers reused from a prior photo array shown to the same witness?

**Functional Size Analysis:**
The *nominal size* of a lineup is the total number of photos or persons (typically six). The *functional size* is the number of lineup members who are plausible selections based on the witness's description. If the witness described a young Black male with short hair and three of the six fillers are middle-aged, white, or have long hair, the functional size is three -- not six. A functional size below four is a CRITICAL deficiency.

> **Formula:** Functional Size = Number of lineup members matching the witness's prior description of the perpetrator. A functional size analysis should be performed for every lineup. Report both nominal and functional size.

### A.3 -- Instructions to the Witness

The instructions given to the witness before the identification procedure are critically important. Research demonstrates that biased instructions dramatically increase the false identification rate.

**Unbiased Instruction (Required):**
The witness must be told that the perpetrator may or may not be in the lineup, and that it is as important to exclude innocent persons as to identify the perpetrator. Without this instruction, witnesses feel implicit pressure to select someone.

| Instruction Element | Best Practice | Deficiency if... | Severity |
|--------------------|-------------|------------------|----------|
| "The perpetrator may or may not be present" | Explicitly stated and documented | Not given; witness assumed perpetrator is present | CRITICAL |
| "You should not feel you must make an identification" | Explicitly stated | Not given; implicit pressure to select someone | CRITICAL |
| "Take as much time as you need" | Explicitly stated | Witness rushed; time pressure imposed | SIGNIFICANT |
| "If you make an identification, I will continue to investigate" | Explicitly stated (reduces commitment effect) | Not given; witness believes identification ends the investigation | SIGNIFICANT |
| "The administrator does not know who the suspect is" | Stated in double-blind procedures | Not applicable in single-blind (separate deficiency) | -- |
| Instructions documented in writing or on video | Written instruction form signed by witness; or video recording | Instructions were verbal only with no documentation | SIGNIFICANT |

**Clark (2005) meta-analysis:** Biased instructions (omitting "may or may not be present") increased the choosing rate by approximately 15-20%, with a disproportionate increase in false identifications. This is one of the most robust and well-replicated findings in the eyewitness science literature.

### A.4 -- Presentation Method: Sequential vs. Simultaneous

**Simultaneous Presentation (Traditional):**
All photos or lineup members are presented at the same time. The witness can compare across photos. This encourages *relative judgment* -- selecting the person who looks most like the perpetrator relative to the others, even if no one in the lineup is the actual perpetrator.

**Sequential Presentation (Recommended by NAS):**
Photos or lineup members are presented one at a time. The witness must make a yes/no decision about each before seeing the next. The witness does not know how many photos remain. This encourages *absolute judgment* -- comparing each photo to the witness's memory rather than to other photos.

| Factor | Sequential (Recommended) | Simultaneous (Traditional) | Significance |
|--------|------------------------|--------------------------|--------------|
| Judgment type | Absolute | Relative | Relative judgment increases false IDs |
| Choosing rate | Lower | Higher | Lower choosing rate = fewer false IDs |
| Correct ID rate | Slightly lower in some studies | Slightly higher | Trade-off: simultaneous produces more correct IDs but also more false IDs |
| NAS recommendation | Recommended | Not recommended | NAS Report (2014) at 106 |
| Louisiana requirement | Not mandated by statute | Permissible | La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163 does not require sequential presentation |

**If simultaneous presentation was used, note this as a SIGNIFICANT deficiency and cite the NAS recommendation.** Louisiana does not mandate sequential presentation, so this is a best-practice critique, not a statutory violation.

### A.5 -- Confidence Statement

The witness's level of confidence at the time of the identification is a critical data point -- but only if recorded immediately and before any feedback.

**Timing Requirements:**
- Confidence must be recorded *immediately* after the identification, before the administrator provides any feedback (verbal or nonverbal)
- The statement should be in the witness's own words, not a numerical scale imposed by the administrator
- The confidence statement must be documented verbatim -- paraphrasing by the administrator is insufficient

**Confidence Inflation:**
Research demonstrates that post-identification feedback ("Good, you picked the suspect" or even "OK, we'll continue our investigation") inflates the witness's retrospective confidence. *Wells & Bradfield (1998)* showed that confirming feedback caused witnesses to report dramatically higher confidence, better viewing conditions, and greater attention during the crime than witnesses who received no feedback. This is the *post-identification feedback effect* and it is one of the most dangerous sources of evidence corruption.

| Factor | Best Practice | Deficiency if... | Severity |
|--------|-------------|------------------|----------|
| Confidence recorded immediately | Before any feedback or conversation | Delayed; recorded in report hours or days later | CRITICAL |
| Confidence in witness's own words | Verbatim quotation documented | Paraphrased by administrator; numerical scale imposed | SIGNIFICANT |
| No feedback before confidence statement | Administrator gives no response to selection | Administrator confirmed selection, thanked witness, or provided any evaluative response | CRITICAL |
| Confidence documented on video | Recording captures statement in real time | No recording; written documentation only | SIGNIFICANT |

### A.6 -- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163 Compliance

Louisiana Code of Criminal Procedure Article 163 governs identification procedures. Audit for compliance with each requirement:

> **Art. 163. Identification procedure:**
> A. An identification procedure shall be conducted in a manner that promotes the reliability and accuracy of the identification and minimizes the risk of misidentification.

Specific Louisiana requirements and best practices under Art. 163 and related jurisprudence:

| Requirement | Source | Audit Point |
|-------------|--------|-------------|
| Procedure must promote reliability | La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163(A) | Overall assessment of procedure fairness |
| Procedure must minimize misidentification risk | La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163(A) | Were best practices followed? |
| Photo array composition standards | La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163 | Minimum six photos; fillers must be appropriate |
| Pre-procedure instructions | La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163; State v. Higgins | Was the witness instructed perpetrator may not be present? |
| Confidence statement | La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163 | Was confidence documented at time of identification? |
| Documentation requirements | La. C.Cr.P. Art. 163 | Was the procedure documented in writing and/or recorded? |
| Attorney presence at post-indictment lineup | U.S. v. Wade; La. C.Cr.P. Art. 164 | If post-indictment live lineup, was counsel present or waived? |
