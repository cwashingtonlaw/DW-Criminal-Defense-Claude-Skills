## CRITICAL CASE ORIENTATION

These matters involve allegations of extraordinary gravity. The analysis framework below maintains **zealous advocacy for the constitutional rights of the accused** while acknowledging the serious harm of sexual violence. Both wrongful convictions and sexual violence inflict profound damage. Our duty is to audit evidence rigorously, protect constitutional protections, and ensure the State meets its burden of proof beyond a reasonable doubt.

**This skill is defense-focused and intellectually honest.** We do not blame alleged victims. We audit evidence, process, and constitutional compliance.

## MODULE A — SANE EXAM AUDIT

### Critical Role & Scope

The Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner (SANE) is often the first professional to document alleged assault. The SANE report becomes central to prosecution theory. Defense audit requires understanding:

1. **Examiner Qualifications**
   - SANE-A (adult) vs. SANE-P (pediatric) certification?
   - Hours of specialized training completed?
   - Supervised clinical experience documented?
   - Current certification status and renewal record?
   - **Challenge Point:** Protocols vary by hospital. Examiner may not be fully trained despite performing exam.

2. **Timeline Analysis**
   - When was exam performed relative to alleged assault?
   - If >72 hours: acute findings increasingly unlikely
   - If >5–7 days: injury healing obscures initial trauma
   - **Challenge Point:** Delayed exams are presented as normal but limit evidentiary value.

| Time Window | Expected Findings | Significance |
|-------------|-------------------|--------------|
| 0–24 hours | Acute injuries most visible; semen recovery possible | Highest diagnostic window |
| 24–72 hours | Injuries may be healing; semen becomes harder to recover | Moderate diagnostic window |
| 72+ hours | Healing progressing; semen unlikely unless motility test positive | Limited diagnostic window |
| >7 days | Injuries largely healed; DNA unlikely unless in deep tissues | Minimal acute findings |

3. **Documentation of Injuries (or Lack Thereof)**
   - Were injuries **actually found**?
   - CRITICAL: Normal/no injury findings are common in confirmed abuse cases
   - Research by Adams, Heger, Berenson: 60–90% of children with confirmed abuse show no anogenital injury
   - Were findings **photographed**? Chain of custody of images?
   - Were injuries documented in writing with anatomical descriptors?
   - **Challenge Point:** "No injury" does not equal "no assault."

4. **Colposcope Findings & Interpretation**
   - Colposcope magnification 6–40x used?
   - Specific findings documented (fissures, lacerations, scars, friability)?
   - Who interpreted findings? (Examiner vs. radiologist vs. protocol)
   - Understanding of **normal developmental variation** in anogenital anatomy:
     - Hymenal transections vs. hymenal notches (notches common, not diagnostic)
     - Posterior fourchette variations
     - Anal fissures (may be constipation-related)
   - **Challenge Point:** Misinterpretation of normal anatomy as abuse is documented risk.

5. **Evidence Collection Technique Compliance**
   - Were protocols followed for swabbing (location, pressure, moisture)?
   - Proper specimen labeling and chain of custody?
   - Contamination risk during collection (examiner gloves, equipment)?
   - Samples frozen/stored properly?
   - **Challenge Point:** Improper technique compromises DNA validity.

6. **Toxicology Screen Results**
   - Blood and/or urine drug screen performed?
   - If victim alleges incapacity (date rape): toxicology critical
   - Absence of drug screen when incapacity is core element is weakness
   - **Challenge Point:** Prosecution may not obtain toxicology; absence is defense advantage.

7. **Patient History Documentation vs. Forensic Findings Consistency**
   - Did examiner document victim's account of assault (time, location, acts)?
   - Does SANE documentation match victim's statement to police?
   - Inconsistencies between "what victim said" and "what was found" are red flags
   - **Challenge Point:** Discrepancies undermine cohesive narrative.

8. **Injury Healing Timeline Analysis**
   - If injuries are documented: when would they heal?
   - Genital lacerations: typically 3–10 days
   - Bruising: color progression allows rough dating (not precise)
   - Scarring: develops over weeks
   - **Challenge Point:** Medical expert may opine on healing timeline to challenge delay theories.

9. **Alternative Explanations for Physical Findings**
   - Consensual intercourse causes similar minor injuries
   - Prior sexual activity, gynecological conditions, sexual practices
   - Menstruation, hormonal changes, medical conditions
   - Infection, inflammation from non-sexual causes
   - **Challenge Point:** Physical findings alone insufficient for rape diagnosis.

## MODULE B — SEXUAL ASSAULT KIT / DNA ANALYSIS

### Chain of Custody Verification

DNA evidence is only as reliable as its chain of custody. Critical audit points:

**Questions to Answer:**
1. Was kit collected per LADOJ (Louisiana Department of Justice) protocol?
2. Who collected the kit? (SANE vs. physician vs. law enforcement)
3. How long between alleged assault and kit collection?
4. Proper specimen labels, sealing, storage temperature documented?
5. Any break in chain of custody?
6. How long was kit stored before analysis?
7. DNA extraction and analysis date vs. collection date (degradation risk)?

### DNA Mixture Interpretation Challenges

**What is a DNA mixture?**
- Multiple DNA sources in single evidence item
- Common in sexual assault kits (victim + accused + unknown)
- Interpretation is probabilistic, not absolute

**Major Challenge Framework:**

1. **Probabilistic Genotyping & TrueAllele**
   - Bayesian statistical approach to mixed profile interpretation
   - Software (TrueAllele, STRmix) assigns probability to suspected profile match
   - **Critical Weakness:** Results vary based on assumptions fed into software
   - Prosecution often presents as "probability accused is source" but excludes defense expert scrutiny
   - **Defense Challenge:** Demand defense expert independent analysis; question assumptions

2. **Touch DNA Limitations**
   - DNA transferred from touching without penetration/bodily fluid contact
   - Present on skin, clothing, surfaces touched
   - Does NOT indicate sexual contact or assault
   - Can be transferred indirectly (touching object, then person)
   - **Defense Strategy:** Touch DNA found in area of alleged assault may be innocent contact

3. **Secondary & Tertiary DNA Transfer**
   - Accused's DNA may be on alleged victim's body from innocent prior contact
   - Can be transferred indirectly through mediator (shared clothing, bedding, bathroom)
   - Research shows DNA can transfer without direct contact in some cases
   - **Defense Advantage:** Innocent DNA presence does not prove assault

4. **Y-STR Analysis Scope & Limitations**
   - Male-specific DNA markers
   - Cannot identify individual male; only establishes male genetic contributor
   - Useful for screening presence of male DNA
   - Does NOT identify accused specifically
   - **Defense Point:** Y-STR match is presence, not identity

5. **Degradation & Contamination Risks**
   - Environmental exposure degrades DNA (heat, moisture, bacterial growth)
   - Contamination from handling, equipment, lab
   - Storage time and temperature critical
   - **Challenge Point:** Kit stored for months/years; degradation compromises reliability

6. **Lab Accreditation & Analyst Qualifications**
   - Is lab accredited (AABB, ISO/IEC 17025)?
   - Analyst certified? How many profiles analyzed?
   - Procedures documented and followed?
   - **Demand:** Lab records, proficiency testing, QA/QC documentation

7. **Consensual Intercourse Defense & DNA Considerations**
   - Accused's DNA presence expected if consensual intercourse occurred
   - DNA alone cannot distinguish consensual from non-consensual contact
   - Alleged victim's consent to sexual contact is dispositive if proven
   - **Defense Strategy:** DNA consistency with consensual encounter, not inconsistent with defense

## MODULE C — RAPE SHIELD LAW ANALYSIS (La. C.E. Art. 412)

### General Prohibition

**La. Code of Evidence Article 412** broadly excludes evidence of alleged victim's prior sexual behavior or reputation for sexual conduct. Purpose: prevent jury from judging victim based on sexual history.

**Rule:** Evidence of victim's sexual behavior or reputation is inadmissible, **except** as specified in exceptions.

### Exceptions to Rape Shield — Defense Use

#### Exception 1: Consent Defense (Prior Sexual Relationship)

**Availability:** Where consent is issue and victim and accused had prior sexual relationship.

**Example:**
- Accused and alleged victim previously dated and had consensual sex
- Alleged victim consents to sexual conduct on date of alleged assault
- Accused may testify about prior sexual history to support consent defense

**Requirements:**
- Defense must make good faith offer of proof
- Prior relationship must be with accused
- Must be probative to consent issue
- Court balances probative value vs. prejudice

#### Exception 2: Source of Semen/Injury/Disease

**Availability:** Where issue is whether alleged victim's injury or condition is related to alleged assault.

**Example:**
- Alleged victim has sexually transmitted disease (STD)
- Prosecution claims accused transmitted disease
- Defense may introduce evidence victim had other sexual partners who could have transmitted disease

**Requirements:**
- Evidence must be probative to medical question
- Specific evidence of other source required
- Not general reputation or behavior

#### Exception 3: Pattern of Behavior

**Availability:** In extraordinary circumstances where evidence of prior sexual conduct shows pattern similar to alleged conduct.

**Threshold:** Extremely high; rarely granted

**Example:** Alleged victim had pattern of fabricating rape allegations against multiple partners (rare success)

#### Exception 4: Constitutionally Required Evidence

**Availability:** Where excluding evidence violates accused's Sixth Amendment right to confront witnesses or Due Process.

**Example:**
- Alleged victim's motive to fabricate (revenge from failed relationship)
- Prior inconsistent sexual conduct statements bearing on credibility

**Standards:** *Crawford v. Washington*, *Davis v. Washington* — defendant entitled to confront accusers about prior statements

### La. C.E. Art. 412.1 — Sexually Assaultive Offenses Exception

**Special Procedure** if alleged victim in sexually assaultive offense case has prior sexual conduct evidence to present or defense seeks to introduce such evidence:

1. **Pretrial Motion Required:** Defense must file written motion with offer of proof (not in jury presence)
2. **Specificity Required:** Explain why evidence is admissible under exception
3. **Hearing:** Judge conducts hearing to determine admissibility (victims may testify about circumstances)
4. **Written Ruling:** Judge issues written decision stating exception applies and reasoning
5. **Limiting Instruction:** If admitted, jury receives specific limiting instruction

**Burden:** Defense bears burden of showing exception applies; preponderance of evidence standard

### La. C.E. Art. 412.2 — Prior Acts of Defendant

**Prosecution's Access to Defendant's Prior Sex Offenses:**

**Rule:** In sexually assaultive offense prosecution, evidence of defendant's **other crimes involving sexual conduct** is admissible without complying with rape shield exceptions.

**Application:** Prosecution may introduce accused's prior sex offense convictions or arrests (even if not convicted) under broader admissibility standard.

**Defense Challenge:**
- Demand specifics: what prior acts?
- Require proof of prior conviction (not mere allegation)
- Object to unfair prejudice (352 balancing)
- Request limiting instruction distinguishing propensity inference

**Constitutional Limits:**
- Due Process: prior act must be reliable (not speculative)
- Sixth Amendment: accused entitled to cross-examine witnesses testifying about prior acts
- Probative value analysis required

### Procedural Requirements for Rape Shield Motions

**When Defense Seeks Admission of Victim Sexual Behavior/Reputation:**

1. **Written Motion (Pre-Trial)**
   - Specific description of evidence sought
   - Offer of proof (what will evidence show)
   - Reason evidence falls within exception

2. **Motion Hearing**
   - In camera, outside jury presence
   - Defense presents evidence; prosecution responds
   - Judge determines admissibility in writing

3. **If Admitted:**
   - Jury receives specific limiting instruction
   - Instruction defines scope (e.g., "solely on issue of consent")

**When Prosecution Seeks Admission of Victim Sexual Behavior:**

1. Similar written motion and hearing process
2. Burden on prosecution to show exception applies
3. Victim may request closed-circuit TV or protective measures

## MODULE D — FORENSIC INTERVIEW CHALLENGE FRAMEWORK

### Integration with dw-child-forensic-interview-auditor

For full technical audit of forensic interview methodology, see **dw-child-forensic-interview-auditor** skill. This module provides **sex offense-specific challenge points**.

### Summary Challenge Points for Sex Offense Cases

1. **Interviewer Training & Qualifications**
   - What training did interviewer complete?
   - Certification in specialized child interview protocol?
   - How many interviews has interviewer conducted?
   - **Sex Offense Challenge:** Interviewer bias toward finding abuse is documented risk

2. **Interview Protocol Compliance**
   - Was evidence-based protocol used (NCMEC, ICAN, state protocol)?
   - Were open-ended questions used first?
   - **Sex Offense Challenge:** Leading questions and suggestive techniques common in older interviews

3. **Recording & Completeness**
   - Is full video/audio recording available?
   - No editing or truncation?
   - Multiple interviews? If so, all recorded?
   - **Challenge:** Prosecution may use excerpts only

4. **Alleged Victim Age & Developmental Stage**
   - Age at interview?
   - Language development appropriate?
   - Memory capabilities for age?
   - **Challenge:** Young children prone to suggestion; memory details unreliable

### Delayed Disclosure Research Context

**Critical Defense Concept:** Delayed disclosure of sexual assault is extremely common and does **not** indicate fabrication.

**Research Findings:**
- 70–90% of child victims delay disclosure (weeks, months, years)
- Common reasons: fear, shame, self-blame, dependency on perpetrator, threats
- Some children initially deny abuse when first asked
- Delayed disclosure not evidence of false allegation

**Defense Strategy:**
- Use delayed disclosure research to counter prosecution argument: "Why did victim wait to report?"
- Education of judge/jury: delay is normal trauma response
- Reference: *State v. Broadway* (Louisiana case recognizing delayed disclosure)

### Recantation Research & Significance

**Definition:** Child's retraction of abuse allegation after initial disclosure.

**Documented Frequency:**
- Recantations occur in 4–8% of substantiated abuse cases
- When recantation occurs, it often indicates:
  - Victim pressure from non-offending parent or family
  - Victim fear of consequences (family separation, foster care)
  - Confusion about responsibility (child feels blamed)
  - Ambivalence about disclosure

**Defense Use:**
- If alleged victim recants: this may indicate false allegation OR indicate victim coercion/fear
- Expert testimony on recantation dynamics may be admissible
- Cross-examine about pressures on victim to recant

### Multiple Disclosure Analysis

**Issue:** Alleged victim's story may change between interviews.

**Why Changes Occur:**
- Memory develops over time (normal development)
- Different interviewer style elicits different recall
- Child anxiety increases disclosure consistency
- Leading questions shape later responses

**Defense Audit:**
1. Compare all interviews chronologically
2. Document what details changed
3. Distinguish confabulation (false memory) from normal memory development
4. Identify leading questions that may have shaped later disclosures

## MODULE E — EXPERT WITNESS ISSUES IN SEX CASES

### CSAAS (Child Sexual Abuse Accommodation Syndrome) — Admissibility Challenges

**What is CSAAS?**
- Developed by Dr. Roland Summit (1983)
- Framework describing behavioral responses of abused children
- Five categories: secrecy, helplessness, entrapment/accommodation, delayed disclosure, recantation

**Admissibility Status in Louisiana:**
- Prosecution frequently seeks to introduce CSAAS testimony
- Courts often allow as relevant to counter defense theories about victim behavior
- **Key Case:** *State v. Foret*, 628 So.2d 1116 (La. 1993) — Louisiana recognizes CSAAS

**Defense Challenge Framework:**

1. **Scientific Basis Weakness**
   - CSAAS developed from clinical observation, not empirical research
   - Not universally observed in abused children
   - Can describe both abused and non-abused children's behavior
   - **Challenge:** Does not reliably distinguish abuse from non-abuse

2. **Propensity Inference Problem**
   - Prosecution uses CSAAS to explain why victim's behavior is "consistent with abuse"
   - Creates improper inference: "symptoms = abuse occurred"
   - Violates propensity rule (cannot infer conduct from behavior pattern alone)
   - **Cross-Examination:** "Can CSAAS symptoms occur in non-abused children?" (YES)

3. **Delayed Disclosure Misuse**
   - Prosecution: "Delayed disclosure is consistent with CSAAS, so abuse likely occurred"
   - Defense counter: Delayed disclosure is common in false allegations too
   - CSAAS cannot distinguish true from false abuse allegations
   - **Motion:** Exclude CSAAS as unreliable for guilt/innocence determination

4. **Proper Limited Use**
   - CSAAS may be admissible solely to explain victim's behavior choices (not as evidence of abuse)
   - Expert can describe accommodation syndrome without opining guilt/innocence
   - Limiting instruction: "Expert's testimony about accommodation syndrome is not evidence of guilt"

### Rape Trauma Syndrome — Limits of Expert Testimony

**Similar Issues to CSAAS:**
- Behavioral pattern in trauma survivors
- Common in assault victims BUT also in other trauma (PTSD, depression, anxiety)
- Cannot distinguish true assault from false allegations
- **Defense Strategy:** PTSD symptoms do not prove rape occurred

### "Grooming" Testimony — Scientific Basis Challenges

**What is "Grooming"?**
- Adult's process of building trust with child to facilitate abuse
- Prosecution uses expert testimony to explain:
  - Why accused had access to victim
  - Why relationship appeared normal to others
  - Why victim was vulnerable

**Defense Challenges:**
1. **Definition Vagueness:** "Grooming" lacks precise behavioral definition
2. **Confirmation Bias:** Once abuse alleged, all prior contact reframed as "grooming"
3. **False Positives:** Many adult-child mentoring relationships include trust-building (coaches, teachers)
4. **Scientific Basis:** Limited peer-reviewed research on grooming "predictability"

**Cross-Examination Seeds:**
- "Is earning a child's trust inherently grooming?"
- "Can you distinguish grooming from legitimate mentoring?"
- "Have you researched false grooming allegations?"

### Defense Experts: False Memory, Suggestibility, Alternative Diagnoses

**Expert Types Available to Defense:**

1. **False Memory Specialist**
   - Expert in memory malleability, source confusion, false memory development
   - Can testify about how interview techniques (leading questions, repetition) create false memories
   - Research: Dr. Elizabeth Loftus (leading researcher)
   - **Admissibility:** Generally admissible in sexual abuse cases when interviewing methods are at issue

2. **Child Development Expert**
   - Testifies to age-appropriate memory capabilities, language development, suggestibility by age
   - Explains why young children's detailed recollection may be unreliable
   - **Admissibility:** Generally admissible; helps jury understand child witness limitations

3. **Mental Health Alternative Diagnoses**
   - PTSD, depression, anxiety can present with symptoms prosecution attributes to abuse
   - Prior trauma history may explain current symptoms without new abuse
   - Misdiagnosis of PTSD as abuse indicator
   - **Expert:** Psychiatrist or clinical psychologist qualified in diagnostic assessment

4. **Forensic Interview Methodology Expert**
   - Reviews interview for compliance with best practices
   - Identifies leading questions, repetitive questioning, coercive techniques
   - **Admissibility:** Increasingly accepted; relates to reliability of child's statements

### Medical Expert Challenges: Normal Exam Interpretation

**Issues:**
- Non-medical testifying about normal anatomy as if abnormal
- Misidentification of normal developmental variation as injury
- Absence of injury presented as absence of evidence rather than evidence of absence

**Expert Qualifications:**
- Pediatric gynecologist or pediatrician with abuse medicine training
- Must have reviewed adolescent/child anatomy literature
- Must acknowledge range of normal findings

**Cross-Examination Themes:**
- "What percentage of confirmed abuse cases have normal exams?" (Often 60–90%)
- "Explain normal hymenal variations in children" (narrow list of normal findings often misinterpreted)
- "Is presence of defendant's DNA inconsistent with consensual contact?" (NO)

## MODULE F — REGISTRATION & COLLATERAL CONSEQUENCES ANALYSIS

### La. R.S. 15:540 et seq. — Sex Offender Registration Act (SORA)

**Critical Issue:** Registration often called "hidden penalty" in plea negotiations. Defense must understand full scope.

### Tier Classification System

| Tier | Offenses | Duration | Community Notification |
|------|----------|----------|------------------------|
| **Tier 1** | Simple sexual battery, indecent behavior | 10 years | No public notification |
| **Tier 2** | Sexual battery, aggravated sexual battery, oral sexual battery, crime against nature | 25 years | Limited notification (law enforcement, schools, institutions) |
| **Tier 3** | Rape, aggravated rape, molestation of juvenile, pornography involving juveniles, human trafficking for sexual purposes | Lifetime | Public notification (community, media, website) |

**Strategic Note:** Difference between Tier 1 and Tier 3 is enormous for plea negotiations.

### Registration Requirements & Restrictions

**1. Reporting Requirements:**
- In-person registration within 10 days of release
- Annual renewal (some jurisdictions quarterly)
- Address, employment, school enrollment changes within 10 days
- Internet identifiers (usernames, email accounts) must be provided
- Failure to register: felony charge

**2. Residency Restrictions:**
- Cannot reside within 1,000 feet of school, childcare facility, park
- Restriction applies throughout state
- May be homeless but must register
- **Practical Effect:** Severe housing limitations in urban areas

**3. Internet Restrictions (La. R.S. 14:91.5):**
- Cannot use Internet anonymously
- Cannot knowingly access minor-oriented chat rooms
- Cannot have online profile without disclosure of offender status
- Restrictions vary by tier but all are significant

**4. Employment Consequences:**
- Many employers conduct sex offender registry checks
- Government employment generally barred
- Professional licenses (teaching, nursing, counseling) often revoked or impossible to obtain
- Private employers frequently refuse employment

**5. Other Collateral Consequences:**
- Passport restrictions
- Housing discrimination
- Educational exclusions
- Financial aid ineligibility
- Mandatory treatment costs
- Polygraph testing requirements
- Vehicle registration restrictions

### Tier Assignment & Judicial Discretion

**Process:**
1. Statute specifies tier based on conviction
2. Limited judicial discretion in some circumstances
3. Appeal possible if incorrect tier assigned

**Defense Opportunity in Plea Negotiations:**
- Charging decisions affect tier classification
- Plea to lower-degree charge may reduce tier assignment
- Example: Plead to sexual battery (Tier 2) instead of rape (Tier 3)
- **Value Analysis:** Lifetime registration (Tier 3) vs. 25-year (Tier 2) is enormous bargaining point

### Juvenile Adjudication Registration Issues

**Special Rules:**
- Juveniles can be required to register
- Tier assignment based on offense (same as adults)
- Duration may be shorter (10–25 years depending on tier)
- Removal from registry possible if offense adjudicated before age 12 and met conditions

**Defense Strategy:**
- Negotiate for expungement eligibility
- Challenge registration for juvenile adjudications (due process arguments)
- Explore delinquency vs. criminal prosecution (registration consequences differ)

## MODULE G — DEFENSE STRATEGY INTEGRATION

### Consent Defense Framework (Where Applicable)

**Critical Caveat:** Consent defense only available when:
1. Alleged victim was age of consent or older (La. R.S. 14:43 applies to 13–17 range)
2. Accused's position was not authority figure
3. Statutory rape charges do not apply

**Consent Elements Prosecution Must Disprove:**
- Alleged victim must testify or evidence show:
  - Willing participation
  - Absence of threat or force
  - Mental capacity to consent (not incapacitated)
  - Clear communication of willingness

**Defense Approach:**
1. Develop detailed timeline of relationship
2. Document consensual communication (texts, social media, witnesses)
3. Establish alleged victim's willingness and initiation in sexual contact
4. Cross-examine on specifics of "no" and physical resistance
5. Expert testimony on consent communication in relationships

**DNA Consistency:** Accused's DNA presence entirely consistent with consensual intercourse. Does not prove consent, but does not disprove it.

### Misidentification Defense

**Framework:** State's case depends on proof that accused committed the alleged conduct.

**Misidentification Elements:**
1. **Eyewitness ID Issues:**
   - Cross-racial identification problems
   - Lighting/visibility at time
   - Distance between witness and accused
   - Prior relationship between witness and accused
   - Suggestiveness of identification procedure

2. **DNA Exclusion:**
   - If kit excludes accused entirely, misidentification certain
   - If mixture and accused profile cannot be excluded, less definitive

3. **Alibi:**
   - Accused was elsewhere at time
   - Gather cell phone records, surveillance footage, witness statements
   - Alibi expert testimony if location-specific

**Expert:** Eyewitness identification expert can testify about reliability factors and misidentification research.

### False Allegation Defense

**Caution:** Never blame victim; focus on evidence and motive.

**Investigative Directions:**
1. **Custody/Family Dynamics:**
   - Is alleged victim involved in custody dispute?
   - Does report benefit one party in custody case?
   - Research: false allegations in custody contexts ~4% (but do occur)

2. **Mental Health Context:**
   - Did alleged victim have recent trauma (loss, rejection)?
   - Documented mental health history?
   - Pattern of similar allegations against others?

3. **Revenge/Anger Motive:**
   - Did accused reject alleged victim's advances?
   - Documented conflict preceding allegation?
   - Text/communication showing anger or revenge motive?

4. **Inconsistency Investigation:**
   - Multiple story versions documented?
   - Discrepancies with physical evidence?
   - Timeline inconsistencies?

**Expert Testimony:** Mental health expert can testify about condition-based false allegations (not blaming victim, but explaining possibility of confabulation/false memory).

### Diminished Capacity / Intoxication Issues

**Context:** May apply to either accused or alleged victim.

**If Alleged Victim Intoxicated:**
- Can intoxicated person consent? (Generally no legal capacity to consent if incapacitated)
- What degree of intoxication? (Evidence standard)
- Did accused know or should have known victim incapacitated?
- Toxicology testing and expert interpretation

**If Accused Intoxicated:**
- Does not excuse sexual conduct but may negate specific intent element (some charges require specific intent)
- May be mitigation factor in sentencing
- Never defense to rape (rape-by-force or rape-by-incapacity do not require specific intent)

### Age-of-Consent & Romeo & Juliet Provisions

**Louisiana Age of Consent:**
- General: 17 years of age
- La. R.S. 14:43 (Simple Rape) applies to victim 13–17 with non-authority-figure accused
- La. R.S. 14:42.1 (2nd Degree Rape) applies to victim under 13

**Romeo & Juliet Affirmative Defense (La. R.S. 14:43.1(E)):**
- Applies only to Sexual Battery charges
- Accused 17–22 years, victim 15–17 years
- Relationship consensual and non-violent
- **Requirements:**
  - Affirmative defense (burden on accused to prove)
  - Accused must be no more than 4 years older than victim
  - No authority over victim
  - Not applicable to rape charges (only sexual battery)

**Defense Strategy:**
- Determine if Romeo & Juliet applies
- Age differential analysis critical
- If applicable, shifts burden to prosecution to disprove affirmative defense elements

### Statute of Limitations Analysis (La. C.Cr.P. Art. 571 et seq.)

**Louisiana Statute of Limitations for Sex Offenses:**

| Charge | Statute of Limitations |
|--------|------------------------|
| Rape (1st, 2nd, 3rd Degree) | No limitation (may charge anytime) |
| Sexual Battery / Molestation | 6 years from offense or discovery |
| Indecent Behavior with Juveniles | 6 years from offense or discovery |
| Pornography Involving Juveniles | No limitation |
| Crime Against Nature | 6 years |

**"Discovery Rule":**
- Statute may start running when offense discovered, not when committed
- Applies especially to child abuse (child may not understand abuse until later)
- **Defense Challenge:** If offense outside statute of limitations even under discovery rule, motion to dismiss

**Strategic Point:** Most sex offenses have no statute of limitations. Challenge only available in limited circumstances (sexual battery, crime against nature if not aggravated).

### Severance Motions for Multiple Counts/Victims

**Issue:** Joinder of multiple counts or multiple victims can be highly prejudicial.

**Severance Standard (La. C.Cr.P. Art. 615):**
- Court may grant severance if joinder results in prejudice
- Risk: jury convicts on weak count because strong count inflames passions
- Multiple victim joinder: jury may assume if guilty of one, guilty of all

**Defense Motion:**
- File written motion before trial
- Argue prejudicial joinder
- Request separate trials for each count/victim
- **Success Factors:** Wide variation in evidence quality, time gap, different alleged victims, different methodologies

### Joinder Challenges

**Improper Joinder Defects:**
- Counts charging same act (cannot join)
- Charges from different indictments (joinder abuse)
- Significant prejudice from joinder
- **Federal Rule 8(a):** Charges properly joined if arising from same conduct or common scheme/plan

**Defense Strategy:**
- Challenge joinder as violating rules
- Move for severance if joinder remains
- Object to jury instructions that allow impermissible propensity inference

## SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION — EVIDENTIARY ISSUES

### CRITICAL Issues (Case-Dispositive)

- **Exculpatory Physical Evidence:** Kit excludes accused entirely; contradicts timeline
- **DNA Exoneration:** Defendant profile absent from kit when prosecution claims involvement
- **Recanted Victim Allegation:** Alleged victim recants and investigation shows pressure/coercion
- **Statute of Limitations Expiration:** Charge barred by statute of limitations
- **Chain of Custody Breakdown:** Kit contaminated, stored improperly, evidence lost

### SIGNIFICANT Issues (Trial-Critical)

- **Delayed SANE Exam:** >5 days post-alleged assault; minimal acute findings
- **DNA Mixture Interpretation Dispute:** Probabilistic genotyping assumptions questionable
- **Leading Forensic Interview:** Multiple suggestive questions documented
- **Inconsistent Victim Statements:** Material discrepancies between interviews/testimony
- **Medical Evidence Misinterpretation:** Normal anatomy findings presented as injury

### MINOR Issues (Evidentiary Detail)

- **Documentation Gaps:** Minor missing lab notations (not affecting result)
- **Procedural Deviations:** Non-critical protocol deviations that do not affect analysis
- **Witness Credibility Details:** Minor inconsistencies in witness statements
- **Timeline Ambiguities:** Exact timing uncertain but not dispositive to elements

## KEY LOUISIANA CASE LAW

### Death Penalty Context

**State v. Kennedy, 957 So.2d 757 (La. 2007); Kennedy v. Louisiana, 554 U.S. 407 (2008)**
- Louisiana allowed death penalty for rape of child
- U.S. Supreme Court held death penalty for non-homicide rape violates Eighth Amendment
- Louisiana capital rape statute struck down
- **Current Impact:** No death penalty for sex offenses; maximum LWOP

### CSAAS Admissibility

**State v. Foret, 628 So.2d 1116 (La. 1993)**
- Louisiana recognizes child sexual abuse accommodation syndrome
- Expert testimony on CSAAS is admissible
- Purpose: explain victim's behavioral choices, not establish guilt
- **Defense Use:** Challenge reliability for guilt/innocence inference

**State v. Chauvin, 769 So.2d 1059 (La. App. 4 Cir. 1989)**
- Child competency framework
- Examines reliability of child witness testimony
- **Modern Application:** Recent courts more skeptical of young child testimony reliability

### Confrontation Clause

**Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36 (2004)**
- Testimonial statements by witnesses unavailable for cross-examination violate Sixth Amendment
- **Sex Offense Application:** If alleged victim available, must testify (cannot use recorded interview as substitute)

**Davis v. Washington, 547 U.S. 813 (2006)**
- Distinguishes testimonial from non-testimonial statements
- 911 calls describing ongoing emergency may be non-testimonial
- Statements made to police describing past events generally testimonial
- **Sex Offense Application:** Most forensic interviews are testimonial; prosecution must produce child witness

### Delayed Disclosure

**State v. Broadway**
- Louisiana recognizes delayed disclosure as normal trauma response
- Defense may educate jury that delay does not indicate fabrication
- Expert testimony on delayed disclosure dynamics admissible

### Jury Instructions & Propensity

**La. C.Cr.P. Art. 813**
- Jury instructions must not permit propensity inferences (prior acts to show propensity to commit crime)
- Must limit to specific purposes (modus operandi, identity, plan, knowledge)
- **Sex Offense Context:** CSAAS and prior-acts evidence require limiting instructions

## GUARDRAILS & PROFESSIONAL STANDARDS

### Never:
1. **Blame the alleged victim.** Audit evidence and process; do not attack credibility on basis of victim's status or prior conduct.
2. **Minimize documented sexual violence.** Acknowledge gravity while maintaining vigorous defense.
3. **Assume guilt from charge.** Presume innocence; investigate thoroughly.
4. **Violate victim privacy.** Use discovery appropriately; do not publicly share sensitive details.

### Always:
1. **Audit evidence rigorously.** Question methodology, qualifications, consistency.
2. **Maintain constitutional focus.** Challenge evidence on legal, not emotional, grounds.
3. **Acknowledge complexity.** Sex offense cases are emotionally and factually complex; simple narratives are suspect.
4. **Protect attorney-client privilege.** These analyses are confidential work product.
5. **Recognize both harms.** Wrongful conviction and sexual violence both cause profound damage.

## FINAL ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK

**Save to:** `01 - Trial Notebook/09 - Case Analysis/Cowork Analysis/`
**For Every Sex Offense Case:**

1. **Identify the burden:** Prosecution must prove every element beyond reasonable doubt
2. **Map the evidence:** Which evidence addresses which elements?
3. **Audit the evidence:** Is collection proper? Is interpretation reliable? Are assumptions documented?
4. **Identify weaknesses:** Delayed disclosure? Inconsistent statements? Normal medical findings? Touch DNA? Misidentification risk?
5. **Develop the defense:** Consent? Misidentification? False allegation? Timing issues? DNA consistency with alternative explanation?
6. **Integrate experts:** What expert testimony strengthens the defense?
7. **Negotiate or try:** What is the strength of the case at each stage?
8. **Consider collateral consequences:** Registration tier impacts plea analysis fundamentally

**Remember:** These cases require intellectual honesty, evidentiary rigor, and unwavering commitment to constitutional protections while acknowledging the serious nature of sexual violence.
