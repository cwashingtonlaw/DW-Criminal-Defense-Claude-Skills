# Defense Strategy Reference Guide

## Consent Defense Framework

**Critical Caveat:** Consent defense only available when:
1. Alleged victim was age of consent or older (La. R.S. 14:43 applies to 13–17 range)
2. Accused's position was not authority figure
3. Statutory rape charges do not apply

**Consent Elements Prosecution Must Disprove:**
- Willing participation
- Absence of threat or force
- Mental capacity to consent (not incapacitated)
- Clear communication of willingness

**Defense Approach:**
1. Develop detailed timeline of relationship
2. Document consensual communication (texts, social media, witnesses)
3. Establish alleged victim's willingness and initiation in sexual contact
4. Cross-examine on specifics of "no" and physical resistance
5. Expert testimony on consent communication in relationships

**DNA Consistency:** Accused's DNA presence entirely consistent with consensual intercourse.

## Misidentification Defense Framework

**Framework:** State's case depends on proof that accused committed the alleged conduct.

**Misidentification Elements:**
1. **Eyewitness ID Issues:**
   - Cross-racial identification problems
   - Lighting/visibility at time
   - Distance between witness and accused
   - Prior relationship between witness and accused
   - Suggestiveness of identification procedure

2. **DNA Exclusion:**
   - If kit excludes accused entirely, misidentification certain
   - If mixture and accused profile cannot be excluded, less definitive

3. **Alibi:**
   - Accused was elsewhere at time
   - Gather cell phone records, surveillance footage, witness statements
   - Alibi expert testimony if location-specific

**Expert:** Eyewitness identification expert can testify about reliability factors and misidentification research.

## False Allegation Defense

**Caution:** Never blame victim; focus on evidence and motive.

**Investigative Directions:**

### Custody/Family Dynamics
- Is alleged victim involved in custody dispute?
- Does report benefit one party in custody case?
- Research: false allegations in custody contexts ~4% (but do occur)

### Mental Health Context
- Did alleged victim have recent trauma (loss, rejection)?
- Documented mental health history?
- Pattern of similar allegations against others?

### Revenge/Anger Motive
- Did accused reject alleged victim's advances?
- Documented conflict preceding allegation?
- Text/communication showing anger or revenge motive?

### Inconsistency Investigation
- Multiple story versions documented?
- Discrepancies with physical evidence?
- Timeline inconsistencies?

**Expert Testimony:** Mental health expert can testify about condition-based false allegations (explaining possibility of confabulation/false memory).

## Diminished Capacity / Intoxication Issues

**If Alleged Victim Intoxicated:**
- Can intoxicated person consent? (Generally no legal capacity to consent if incapacitated)
- What degree of intoxication? (Evidence standard)
- Did accused know or should have known victim incapacitated?
- Toxicology testing and expert interpretation

**If Accused Intoxicated:**
- Does not excuse sexual conduct but may negate specific intent element
- May be mitigation factor in sentencing
- Never defense to rape (rape-by-force or rape-by-incapacity do not require specific intent)

## Age-of-Consent & Romeo & Juliet Defense

**Louisiana Age of Consent:**
- General: 17 years of age
- La. R.S. 14:43 (Simple Rape) applies to victim 13–17 with non-authority-figure accused
- La. R.S. 14:42.1 (2nd Degree Rape) applies to victim under 13

**Romeo & Juliet Affirmative Defense (La. R.S. 14:43.1(E)):**
- Applies only to Sexual Battery charges
- Accused 17–22 years, victim 15–17 years
- Relationship consensual and non-violent
- **Requirements:**
  - Affirmative defense (burden on accused to prove)
  - Accused must be no more than 4 years older than victim
  - No authority over victim
  - Not applicable to rape charges (only sexual battery)

**Defense Strategy:**
- Determine if Romeo & Juliet applies
- Age differential analysis critical
- If applicable, shifts burden to prosecution to disprove affirmative defense elements

## Statute of Limitations Analysis

**Louisiana Statute of Limitations for Sex Offenses:**

| Charge | Statute of Limitations |
|--------|------------------------|
| Rape (1st, 2nd, 3rd Degree) | No limitation (may charge anytime) |
| Sexual Battery / Molestation | 6 years from offense or discovery |
| Indecent Behavior with Juveniles | 6 years from offense or discovery |
| Pornography Involving Juveniles | No limitation |
| Crime Against Nature | 6 years |

**"Discovery Rule":**
- Statute may start running when offense discovered, not when committed
- Applies especially to child abuse (child may not understand abuse until later)
- **Defense Challenge:** If offense outside statute of limitations even under discovery rule, motion to dismiss

**Strategic Point:** Most sex offenses have no statute of limitations.

## Severance Motions for Multiple Counts/Victims

**Severance Standard (La. C.Cr.P. Art. 615):**
- Court may grant severance if joinder results in prejudice
- Risk: jury convicts on weak count because strong count inflames passions
- Multiple victim joinder: jury may assume if guilty of one, guilty of all

**Defense Motion:**
- File written motion before trial
- Argue prejudicial joinder
- Request separate trials for each count/victim
- **Success Factors:** Wide variation in evidence quality, time gap, different alleged victims, different methodologies

## Joinder Challenges

**Improper Joinder Defects:**
- Counts charging same act (cannot join)
- Charges from different indictments (joinder abuse)
- Significant prejudice from joinder
- **Federal Rule 8(a):** Charges properly joined if arising from same conduct or common scheme/plan

**Defense Strategy:**
- Challenge joinder as violating rules
- Move for severance if joinder remains
- Object to jury instructions that allow impermissible propensity inference
