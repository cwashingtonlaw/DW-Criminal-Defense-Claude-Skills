# Forensic Interview Protocols — Identification & Compliance Triage

Before detailed module analysis, identify which forensic interview protocol **should have been used** versus what was actually used.

## CornerHouse RATAC Protocol
**Designed for:** Initial forensic interviews with children suspected of abuse (5-17 years)

**Key stages:**
- **Rapport:** Neutral conversation, comfort-building (5-10 min)
- **Anatomy Identification:** Use of anatomical dolls/diagrams for body part vocabulary (age-appropriate)
- **Touch Inquiry:** Non-leading questions about body touches ("Tell me about touches")
- **Abuse Scenario:** Open narrative of what happened, then open questions
- **Closure:** Supportive ending, clear that interview is over

**Compliance markers:**
- Interview begins with rapport-building on neutral topics
- Anatomical tools used only AFTER child demonstrates understanding of their normal language for body parts
- No leading questions during "touch inquiry" phase
- Anatomy identification does NOT introduce allegations
- Closure phase confirms child understands interview is complete

**Red flags (deviations):**
- Skipping rapport phase
- Introducing allegations before child provides narrative
- Anatomical dolls presented with abuse already suggested
- Leading questions during touch inquiry

## NICHD Protocol (National Institute of Child Health & Development)
**Designed for:** All child interviews (3+ years); research-based, most empirically supported

**Key phases:**
- **Preinterview information gathering** (interviewer reviews case info, plans questions)
- **Rapport building** (neutral topics, with script recommendations)
- **Practice narrative** (child tells story about neutral event, e.g., "Tell me about yesterday")
- **Substantive interview** (open-ended invitation to talk about the alleged event)
- **Follow-up questions** (specific questions following open narrative, not leading)
- **Closing** (any final questions, reassurance)

**Compliance markers:**
- Structured, scripted rapport phase
- Explicit practice narrative before substantive interview
- Interviewer explains: "I don't know what happened. You were there, I wasn't"
- Substantive questioning dominates (open-ended)
- Follow-up questions are clarifying, not suggestive
- Recording is standard (audio/video)
- No anatomical dolls unless child initiates discussion of body parts

**Red flags (deviations):**
- No practice narrative
- Lack of open-ended invitation ("Tell me about what happened")
- Interviewer begins with specific allegations or already-suspected details
- Heavy reliance on yes/no or forced-choice questions
- Anatomical dolls introduced as investigative tool before child's narrative

## Cognitive Interview (context reinstatement, report everything)
**Designed for:** Older children (10+) and adults; enhances memory retrieval

**Key principles:**
- **Context reinstatement** ("Close your eyes. Think about that day. Where were you?")
- **Report everything** ("Tell me everything, even small details")
- **Change order** (ask child to recount events backwards or in different sequence)
- **Change perspective** (describe what others would have seen/heard)

**Compliance markers:**
- Interviewer explicitly invites comprehensive narratives
- Questions invite changing sequence ("Start at the end and work backwards")
- Multiple passes at memory with different organizational approaches
- Open-ended language throughout

**Red flags (deviations):**
- Rigid chronological structure imposed
- Limited to one sequential pass through events
- No use of memory reinstatement techniques

## Step-Wise Interview (Yuille)
**Designed for:** General investigative interviews; balances free narrative with structured questions

**Key phases:**
- **Rapport:** Neutral warm-up
- **Free narrative:** "Tell me everything you remember about..."
- **Open questions:** "Who was there?" "What happened next?"
- **Specific questions:** "What color was his shirt?" (only if not yet covered)
- **Clarifying questions:** Only on gaps in narrative

**Compliance markers:**
- Rapport-building phase distinct
- Free narrative invited before any specific questioning
- Questions follow hierarchical order (open → specific → clarifying)
- Child narrative drives the scope of questions

**Red flags (deviations):**
- Jumping to specific questions before free narrative
- Reordering hierarchy (leading with specifics)
- Multiple rounds of the same specific question

## Finding Words / ChildFirst Protocol
**Designed for:** Younger children (3-8 years); adaptation of NICHD for developmental appropriateness

**Key features:**
- Shorter rapport phase with simple language
- Use of drawing/props sparingly and only for neutral vocabulary building
- Highly open-ended language ("What happened?")
- Simple, short questions (no embedded clauses)
- Patience for long silences (memory retrieval takes longer at this age)
- Frequent clarification ("You said your uncle. Tell me more about that")

**Compliance markers:**
- Language complexity matches child's developmental level
- No multi-part questions ("Did he touch you and did it hurt?")
- Props/drawings used for vocabulary building, not as investigative tools
- Questions pause for child response (no rushed pacing)
- Interviewer tolerates and normalizes "I don't know" responses

**Red flags (deviations):**
- Complex sentence structures
- Compound questions
- Props introduced as prompts for allegations
- Rushing child responses

## Ten-Step Investigative Interview (Law Enforcement Variant)
**Designed for:** Police investigations; structured format for evidence collection

**Steps:**
1. Introduction (interviewer identifies self, explains purpose)
2. Rapport
3. Transition to topic
4. Free narrative
5. Questioning phase (open then specific)
6. Scenario testing (alternative explanations)
7. Review (summarize what child said)
8. Closing
9. Documentation
10. Follow-up planning

**Compliance markers:**
- Clear documentation of each step
- Scenario testing (considering alternative explanations) is explicit
- Open narrative precedes questioning
- No leading questions during fact-gathering phases

**Red flags (deviations):**
- Skipping scenario testing (suggesting only one possibility)
- No documented free narrative phase
- Leading questions presented as "questioning phase"

## TRIAGE DECISION TREE

**Q1: Child's age?**
- 3-5 years → Should use **ChildFirst/Finding Words** or **NICHD**
- 6-9 years → Should use **ChildFirst**, **RATAC**, or **NICHD**
- 10+ years → Can use **Step-Wise**, **Cognitive Interview**, or **NICHD**

**Q2: Who conducted interview?**
- CAC-trained interviewer → Likely **RATAC** or **NICHD**
- Police investigator → Likely **Ten-Step** or unstructured
- Therapist → Likely unstructured (problematic)
- School official → Likely completely unstructured

**Q3: Was a protocol explicitly documented?**
- If YES → Audit for compliance with stated protocol
- If NO → Identify which protocol was most closely approximated, flag deviations

**Q4: Was the interview recorded?**
- If NO → **CRITICAL FLAG** — inability to verify exact questions and child responses
- If YES → Obtain recording for transcript/analysis
