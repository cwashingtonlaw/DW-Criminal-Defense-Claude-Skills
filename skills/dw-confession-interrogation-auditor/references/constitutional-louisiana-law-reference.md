# Constitutional and Louisiana Confession Law Reference

## Quick Reference — Constitutional & Louisiana Confession Law

| Situation | Authority | Key Holding / Application |
|-----------|----------|--------------------------|
| Miranda warnings required before custodial interrogation | *Miranda v. Arizona*, 384 U.S. 436 (1966) | Four warnings required: right to silence, anything-you-say warning, right to counsel, right to appointed counsel |
| Invocation of right to counsel — all questioning must cease | *Edwards v. Arizona*, 451 U.S. 477 (1981) | Once counsel is requested, interrogation must stop until counsel is provided or suspect re-initiates |
| Unambiguous invocation requirement | *Berghuis v. Thompkins*, 560 U.S. 370 (2010) | Invocation of right to silence must be unambiguous; implied waiver from course of conduct is permissible |
| Ambiguous invocation of counsel | *Davis v. United States*, 512 U.S. 452 (1994) | Ambiguous or equivocal reference to counsel is not an invocation; officers may (but are not required to) seek clarification |
| Question-first / two-step technique | *Missouri v. Seibert*, 542 U.S. 600 (2004) | Deliberate question-first technique — obtaining unwarned confession then administering Miranda and re-asking — renders post-warning statements inadmissible |
| Fruit of unwarned statement | *Oregon v. Elstad*, 470 U.S. 298 (1985) | An initial unwarned but voluntary statement does not automatically taint a subsequent warned statement — distinguish from *Seibert* (deliberate vs. inadvertent) |
| 14-day break-in-custody rule | *Maryland v. Shatzer*, 559 U.S. 98 (2010) | Edwards protections expire after 14-day break in custody (release to normal life); continuous incarceration does not restart the clock |
| Juvenile custody analysis — age as factor | *J.D.B. v. North Carolina*, 564 U.S. 261 (2011) | Child's age is a relevant factor in custody determination when known or objectively apparent to officers |
| Juvenile Miranda waiver — totality test | *Fare v. Michael C.*, 442 U.S. 707 (1979) | Totality of circumstances including age, experience, education, background, intelligence |
| Scrupulously honored standard for right to silence | *Michigan v. Mosley*, 423 U.S. 96 (1975) | After right to silence invoked: cease questioning, significant time lapse, fresh warnings before resuming |
| Right to counsel — actual consultation required | *Minnick v. Mississippi*, 498 U.S. 146 (1990) | Suspect must actually consult with counsel before questioning resumes; "making counsel available" is insufficient |
| Re-initiation by suspect | *Oregon v. Bradshaw*, 462 U.S. 1039 (1983) | Suspect must initiate further communication; routine questions about logistics may or may not constitute re-initiation |
| Offense-specific Sixth Amendment right | *McNeil v. Wisconsin*, 501 U.S. 171 (1991) | Sixth Amendment right to counsel is offense-specific; Fifth Amendment/Edwards right is not |
| Voluntariness — judicial hearing required | *Jackson v. Denno*, 378 U.S. 368 (1964) | Trial judge must hold a hearing outside jury's presence to determine voluntariness before confession goes to jury |
| Deception during interrogation | *Frazier v. Cupp*, 394 U.S. 731 (1969) | Deception during interrogation is not per se coercive but is a factor in the totality analysis |
| Post-Miranda silence cannot be used for impeachment | *Doyle v. Ohio*, 426 U.S. 610 (1976) | Using defendant's post-Miranda silence to impeach trial testimony violates Due Process |
| Pre-Miranda silence may be used | *Salinas v. Texas*, 570 U.S. 178 (2013) | Pre-custody, pre-Miranda silence may be used as substantive evidence if Fifth Amendment was not formally invoked |
| Waiver must be voluntary, knowing, and intelligent | *Moran v. Burbine*, 475 U.S. 412 (1986) | Three-part waiver standard |
| Subject of interrogation need not be disclosed | *Colorado v. Spring*, 479 U.S. 564 (1987) | Failure to inform suspect of all subjects of interrogation does not invalidate waiver |
| Selective invocation of rights | *Connecticut v. Barrett*, 479 U.S. 523 (1987) | Defendant may invoke rights selectively (e.g., refuse to give written statement but agree to oral questioning) |
| Louisiana — confessions must be free and voluntary | La. R.S. 15:451 | "Before what purports to be a confession can be introduced in evidence, it must be affirmatively shown that it was free and voluntary" |
| Louisiana — State's burden at suppression hearing | La. R.S. 15:452; *State v. Simmons*, 443 So.2d 512 (La. 1983) | State must prove voluntariness beyond a reasonable doubt |
| Louisiana — electronic recording requirement | La. R.S. 15:453 | Custodial interrogations for crimes of violence must be electronically recorded |
| Louisiana — juvenile interrogation protections | La. Ch.C. Art. 808 | Heightened protections for juvenile confessions including interested adult requirement |
| Louisiana — motion to suppress confession | La. C.Cr.P. Art. 703 | Procedural vehicle for challenging admissibility of confessions |
| Louisiana — voluntariness standard | *State v. Blank*, 293 So.3d 1136 (La. 2020) | Totality of circumstances test — court examines length, mental condition, physical condition, rights advisement, conditions |
| Louisiana — totality of circumstances | *State v. Hernandez* | Conduct of law enforcement and characteristics of the accused; was the confession the product of rational intellect and free will? |
