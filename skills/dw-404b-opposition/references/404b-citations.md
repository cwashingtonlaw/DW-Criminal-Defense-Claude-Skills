## 1. Constitutional & Statutory Framework

### Louisiana

- **La. C.E. Art. 404(B)(1)** — General exclusionary rule. Evidence of other crimes, wrongs, or acts is not admissible to prove character and conduct in conformity therewith. May be admissible for limited purposes only (motive, opportunity, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity, absence of mistake, integral act / res gestae).
- **La. C.E. Art. 403** — The Art. 403 balancing test. Even relevant other-crimes evidence must be excluded if its probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice, confusion of the issues, misleading the jury, or considerations of undue delay or waste of time. Louisiana's Art. 403 balancing is more protective than Federal Rule 403 in this context — see *Prieur*, 277 So.2d at 128.
- **La. C.E. Art. 1101** — Article 404(B) applies in all criminal proceedings.

### Federal (for federal-court matters and persuasive authority)

- **Fed. R. Evid. 404(b)** — Federal counterpart, similar two-part inquiry.
- **Fed. R. Evid. 403** — Federal balancing test.
- **Huddleston v. United States, 485 U.S. 681 (1988)** — Preponderance standard for proving the other act occurred (federal). Louisiana applies a stricter clear-and-convincing standard pre-trial — see *Galliano*.

## 2. The Prieur Framework — Cornerstone Authority

### State v. Prieur, 277 So. 2d 126 (La. 1973)

The seminal Louisiana case. Establishes:

1. **Pre-trial notice requirement**: The State must give written notice of its intent to offer other-crimes evidence, identifying with reasonable specificity (a) each act, (b) the date, (c) the legal exception relied upon, and (d) the relevance to a material issue.
2. **Pre-trial hearing**: The defendant is entitled to a contradictory hearing outside the jury's presence before the State may introduce the evidence at trial.
3. **State's burden at the hearing**: Prove by *clear and convincing evidence* that the defendant committed the other acts, that the evidence is relevant to a material issue genuinely contested, that the evidence's probative value substantially outweighs its prejudicial effect, and that the evidence does not constitute prohibited propensity proof.
4. **Limiting instruction**: The court must instruct the jury on the limited purpose for which the evidence is admitted, both at the time of admission and in the final charge.
5. **Heightened scrutiny rationale**: "[E]vidence of other crimes is to be admitted with the greatest reluctance because of the danger that the jury may convict the defendant not of the offense charged but on the propensity established by the other crimes evidence." Prieur, 277 So.2d at 128.

**Key quote often used in opposition memoranda:** *"The probative value of [other crimes] evidence must be weighed against possible prejudice."* Prieur, 277 So.2d at 128.

### State v. Goffner, 449 So. 2d 1054 (La. App. 1st Cir. 1984)

Sharpens the notice requirement: "kitchen sink" notices that lump multiple acts together, fail to identify which exception is claimed for each act, or claim every Art. 404(B) exception in the alternative are *facially inadequate* and the evidence must be excluded. Cite when attacking notice specificity.

### State v. Hatcher, 372 So. 2d 1024 (La. 1979)

Confirms the contradictory-hearing requirement and the State's pre-trial burden. Often cited together with *Prieur*.

## 3. Independent Relevance — The "Material Issue Genuinely Contested" Requirement

### State v. Martin, 377 So. 2d 259 (La. 1979)

Other-crimes evidence is admissible only when relevant to a material issue *genuinely contested at trial*. If the defendant has not raised a defense that puts the issue in play, the State cannot inject the issue and then use other crimes to rebut it. **Use heavily when the State claims "intent" or "knowledge" but the defense is a denial of the act itself.**

### State v. Rose, 2006-0402 (La. 2/22/07), 949 So. 2d 1236

Reaffirms Martin. Material issue must be genuinely disputed, not merely formally in issue. The State cannot manufacture relevance by predicting a defense the defendant has not asserted.

### State v. Kennedy, 2000-1554 (La. 4/3/01), 803 So. 2d 916

Identity exception requires more than mere similarity — must show a "modus operandi" so distinctive that it functions as a "signature." Repeated use of force or general criminal conduct is not a signature. Cite when the State invokes the identity exception loosely.

## 4. State's Burden — Clear and Convincing Evidence

### State v. Galliano, 2002-2849 (La. 1/10/03), 839 So. 2d 932

The State must prove by *clear and convincing evidence* that the defendant committed the other acts before they may be introduced at trial. Mere accusation, arrest, or even charge is not enough. **Use when the State relies on uncharged or dismissed prior acts.**

### State v. Code, 627 So. 2d 1373 (La. 1993)

Even an acquittal does not automatically bar 404(B) admission, but the State still must prove commission by clear and convincing evidence — and the prior acquittal is a fact the trial court must weigh.

## 5. The Art. 403 Balancing Test — Probative Value vs. Unfair Prejudice

### State v. Lisotta, 98-648 (La. App. 5th Cir. 12/16/98), 726 So. 2d 57

Art. 403 balancing is not a tie-breaker — when probative value is even debatable, exclusion is the default for other-crimes evidence. The test is whether probative value *substantially outweighs* prejudice; close calls go to exclusion.

### State v. Williams, 99-0223 (La. 11/17/00), 769 So. 2d 1154

Factors for the balancing test:
- The State's actual need for the evidence (is it cumulative?)
- The strength of the inference linking the other acts to the issue
- The temporal remoteness of the prior acts
- The similarity of the prior acts to the charged conduct
- The risk of jury confusion
- The risk of conviction by propensity

### State v. Crawford, 95-1352 (La. App. 3d Cir. 4/3/96), 672 So. 2d 197

When the only inference available from the prior act is propensity ("he did it before, so he probably did it this time"), the evidence is per se inadmissible — there is no probative value to weigh, only prejudice.

## 6. Integral Act / Res Gestae Doctrine

### La. C.E. Art. 404(B)(1) "integral act" exception

Acts that form an inseparable part of the charged conduct (the "res gestae") are admissible without Prieur notice. **But:** The integral act doctrine has been narrowed. Mere temporal proximity is not enough.

### State v. Brewington, 601 So. 2d 656 (La. 1992)

Integral acts are those "necessary to complete the story of the crime." If the State can prove the charged conduct without the prior act, the prior act is not integral.

### State v. Taylor, 2001-1638 (La. 1/14/03), 838 So. 2d 729

Integral act exception does not swallow the Prieur rule. When the State seeks to introduce conduct as "integral" but the conduct could be excised without losing the narrative, Prieur notice is required.

## 7. Exception-by-Exception Defense Arguments

### Motive

- **State v. Welch, 615 So. 2d 300 (La. 1993)** — Motive must be a genuine, contested issue. Generic "motive to commit any crime" is not enough.
- The defense should argue: the State has charged motive into the case only to import propensity evidence.

### Intent

- **State v. Robinson, 2005-1320 (La. App. 4th Cir. 5/30/07), 958 So. 2d 1267** — Intent exception requires that intent is a contested element. If defense is denial-of-act, intent is not in issue.

### Identity

- **State v. Hills, 99-1750 (La. 5/16/00), 761 So. 2d 516** — Modus operandi must be sufficiently distinctive to amount to a signature. Generic similarities (same type of crime, same neighborhood) are not enough.

### Knowledge / Absence of Mistake

- **State v. Jackson, 625 So. 2d 146 (La. 1993)** — Knowledge exception requires the State to articulate what specific knowledge is at issue and how the prior act establishes it.

### Plan / Scheme

- The "common plan" exception is narrow. It requires a *true* plan in the defendant's mind connecting prior and charged acts — not merely a pattern of similar conduct. The defense should challenge whether the prior act was actually planned with the charged act, or whether the State is simply re-labeling propensity as "plan."

## 8. Limiting Instructions and Jury Confusion

### State v. Sanders, 93-0001 (La. 11/30/94), 648 So. 2d 1272

Even with a limiting instruction, the prejudicial effect of other-crimes evidence may be incurable. The court must consider whether a limiting instruction can realistically contain the prejudicial spillover.

### Limiting instruction language (defense-favorable formulation)

> "You have heard evidence that the defendant may have committed acts other than the act charged in this case. You may not consider this evidence to decide that the defendant has bad character or is the kind of person who commits crimes, and you may not use it as proof that he committed the offense charged. You may consider it only for the limited purpose of [purpose claimed by the State]. If you find the State has not proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant committed the other acts, you must disregard this evidence entirely."

## 9. Federal-Court Authority (for federal cases or persuasive value)

- **United States v. Beechum, 582 F.2d 898 (5th Cir. 1978) (en banc)** — Two-step framework: (1) evidence must be relevant to an issue other than character, (2) probative value must not be substantially outweighed by undue prejudice.
- **Old Chief v. United States, 519 U.S. 172 (1997)** — Evidentiary alternatives matter — when the State can prove its point through less prejudicial means, the more prejudicial evidence must be excluded under Rule 403.
