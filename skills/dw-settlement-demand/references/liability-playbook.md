# Liability Playbook

How to build the FACTS and LAW sections of the demand from the crash report, witness statements, photos, and depositions.

---

## 1. The Crash Sentence

One tight opening sentence that locates the crash in time, place, and mechanism. This is the most-read sentence of the entire demand — get it right.

Pattern:
> On [Day, Date] at approximately [Time], [Defendant Name] [wrongful action verb] [Plaintiff Name]'s vehicle [location detail], causing severe injuries to [Plaintiff].

Examples:
- *"On Wednesday, March 15, 2023 at approximately 4:45 PM, Defendant John Smith rear-ended Plaintiff Mary Jones's stopped vehicle on Interstate 10 westbound at Mile Marker 158 in Jefferson Davis Parish, causing severe injuries to Mrs. Jones's cervical and lumbar spine."*
- *"On July 28, 2024 at approximately 9:15 AM, Defendant Brittany Davis failed to yield the right-of-way at the intersection of Louisiana Highway 14 and Mallard Cove Road and struck the driver-side door of Plaintiff Ernest Vallot's vehicle, causing traumatic brain injury, cervical disc herniation, and lumbar disc protrusion."*

Tone: factual, not editorial. Don't say "carelessly," "recklessly," or "negligently" in the lead sentence — let the officer's finding and citation do that work in the paragraphs that follow.

---

## 2. Plaintiff's Lawful Action

Always establish what the plaintiff was lawfully doing at the moment of impact. This forecloses comparative fault.

Patterns:
- Rear-end: *"[Plaintiff] was lawfully stopped at a red traffic signal."*
- T-bone (favorable signal): *"[Plaintiff] was traveling lawfully southbound on [Road] within the posted 35 mph speed limit, with the right of way and a green traffic signal."*
- Left-turn case: *"[Plaintiff] was traveling lawfully on [Road] with the right of way."*
- Pedestrian: *"[Plaintiff] was lawfully walking in a marked crosswalk with the pedestrian signal activated."*
- Pull-out: *"[Plaintiff] was traveling lawfully on [Road] within the posted speed limit and in [his/her] lane of travel."*

Always cite the source — even if it's the police report.

---

## 3. Defendant's Wrongful Action

Source from:
1. Police report officer's narrative (best)
2. Witness statements
3. Defendant's own deposition or recorded statement
4. Physical evidence (skid marks, vehicle damage location, scene photos)

Patterns:
- Rear-end: *"Defendant [Name], traveling in the same direction in the same lane, failed to maintain a safe stopping distance and struck the rear of [Plaintiff]'s vehicle at approximately [Speed Estimate] mph."*
- T-bone: *"Defendant [Name] failed to yield the right-of-way and proceeded through a steady red traffic signal, striking the [right/left] side of [Plaintiff]'s vehicle."*
- Failure to yield from a side street: *"Defendant [Name] pulled from [Side Street] into the path of [Plaintiff]'s oncoming vehicle without yielding, contrary to the duties imposed by La. R.S. 32:124."*
- Left turn across path: *"Defendant [Name] attempted a left turn across the path of [Plaintiff]'s oncoming vehicle without yielding, contrary to the duties imposed by La. R.S. 32:122."*

---

## 4. Officer's Findings

If the police report has a narrative paragraph that helpfully describes the crash, quote it in full as an indented block quote. If the report identifies a contributing factor, quote that line.

Example:
> The investigating officer, [Officer Last Name] of [Agency], documented the cause of the crash as follows:
>
> > *"V-1 [Defendant's vehicle] was traveling westbound on US-90 when the driver failed to maintain control of the vehicle on the curve and crossed into the eastbound lane, where V-1 struck V-2 [Plaintiff's vehicle] in a near head-on collision. V-1's driver admitted at the scene that she had been looking down at her phone."*
>
> ([Crash Report], LSP Report No. ####, Officer Narrative ¶3.)

If a citation was issued:
> The officer issued [Defendant] a citation for [statute] — [statute name]. On [date], [Defendant] paid the citation, resolving the offense by payment.

For the "ticket-paid-equals-admission" move:
> Under Louisiana law, payment of a traffic citation operates as an admission against interest in subsequent civil proceedings. [Defendant]'s payment of the citation for [violation] is admissible at trial as an admission that [he/she] committed the violation.

---

## 5. Witness Corroboration

For each independent witness:
> [Witness Name] observed the crash and provided a [recorded statement / written statement / sworn deposition] on [Date]. [Witness] confirmed that:
> - [one-sentence observation #1]
> - [one-sentence observation #2]
>
> ([Witness] Statement, [Date]; or [Witness] Dep., [Date], p. ##.)

Order witnesses by independence — neutral third parties first; passengers in plaintiff's vehicle second; passengers in defendant's vehicle (if friendly) third.

---

## 6. Vehicle Damage & Scene Evidence

Reference repair estimates, total-loss valuations, and scene photos to corroborate the mechanism:

> [Plaintiff]'s [Year Make Model] sustained $[Amount] in damage and was [repaired / declared a total loss with an ACV of $X]. The damage pattern — [location, severity, deformation description] — is consistent with the high-energy [mechanism] described in the crash report. (See Exhibit __ — Vehicle Damage Photos; Exhibit __ — Repair Estimate / Total Loss Valuation.)

For workplace and premises cases:
> [Scene description with photo references. Identify the dangerous condition, document how long it existed, and reference any safety standard or industry custom violated.]

---

## 7. Defendant Deposition Admissions

If the defendant has been deposed and made an admission, this is high-impact material — set it apart as an indented block quote:

> In [his/her] deposition, [Defendant] testified as follows:
>
> > Q: Did you see the plaintiff's vehicle before you struck it?
> > A: No, I did not.
> > Q: Why not?
> > A: I was looking at my phone.
>
> ([Defendant] Dep., [Date], p. ##, ll. ##–##.)

---

## 8. Sole-Fault Statement (verbatim)

Close the FACTS section with the firm's sole-fault statement:

> Based on information and belief, the defendant, [DEFENDANT NAME], was solely responsible for causing the wreck/accident giving rise to this [suit/claim]. Based on information and belief, the defendant, [DEFENDANT NAME], has no good faith legal or factual basis for alleging fault or comparative fault on behalf of the plaintiff.

Render this verbatim every time. It signals the defense that comparative-fault arguments are anticipated and unavailing.

---

## 9. LAW Subsection (mediation_paper / hybrid only)

The LAW subsection layers in statutory and case authority. Three-paragraph structure:

### Paragraph 1 — Statutory duty
Quote the applicable Louisiana traffic statute(s) verbatim. See `louisiana-statutes.md` for the menu and full statutory text.

Example for a rear-end case:
> Louisiana Revised Statutes § 32:81 — Following Vehicles, provides:
>
> > A. The driver of a motor vehicle shall not follow another vehicle more closely than is reasonable and prudent, having due regard for the speed of such vehicle and the traffic upon and the condition of the highway.
>
> [Defendant]'s admitted action of following [Plaintiff]'s vehicle at a distance that did not permit [him/her] to stop in time constitutes a direct violation of La. R.S. 32:81.

### Paragraph 2 — Case authority
Cite controlling Louisiana case authority. See the case bank below.

### Paragraph 3 — Per-se negligence closer
> [Defendant]'s violation of [statute] constitutes negligence per se under Louisiana law. There is no factual or legal basis for any allocation of comparative fault to [Plaintiff].

---

## 10. Per-Mechanism Case Authority Bank

### Rear-end collisions
- **Mart v. Hill**, 505 So.2d 1120 (La. 1987) — establishes the rear-end presumption: "a following motorist in a rear-end collision is presumed to have breached the standard of conduct prescribed by R.S. 32:81 and hence is presumed negligent." The presumption is rebuttable but rarely overcome.
- **Eubanks v. Brasseal**, 310 So.2d 550 (La. 1975) — rear-end presumption applied.
- **Anderson v. May**, 195 So.2d 463 (La. App. 1 Cir. 1967) — duty to maintain safe stopping distance.

### Failure to yield (intersection)
- **Toston v. Pardon**, 874 So.2d 791 (La. 2004) — left-turn duty; left-turning driver bears burden of showing turn was safe.
- **Thornhill v. State DOTD**, 95-1083 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/4/96), 672 So.2d 348 — failure-to-yield right-of-way at intersection; defendant's violation of R.S. 32:123 found to be sole legal cause.

### Pulling from private drive / side street
- **Thomas v. Phoenix Insurance Co.**, 247 La. 268, 170 So.2d 651 (1965) — driver entering highway from private drive has duty to yield to highway traffic; collision creates presumption of fault.

### Following too closely
- **Coleman v. Rabon**, 561 So.2d 897 (La. App. 2 Cir. 1990) — applying R.S. 32:81; rear-driver liable for failing to allow adequate stopping distance.

### Careless operation
- **Thornhill v. State DOTD** (above) — quoted R.S. 32:58 in full; violation supports negligence-per-se framing.

### Pedestrian struck in crosswalk
- **Sinitiere v. Lavergne**, 391 So.2d 821 (La. 1980) — duty of motorist toward pedestrians; lookout and avoidance.
- **Baumgartner v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co.**, 356 So.2d 400 (La. 1978) — pedestrian-motorist comparative fault analysis.

### Course-and-scope / respondeat superior
- **LeBrane v. Lewis**, 292 So.2d 216 (La. 1974) — four-factor test for course-and-scope.
- **Orgeron v. McDonald**, 93-1353 (La. 7/5/94), 639 So.2d 224 — vicarious liability for employee's tortious acts.

### Negligent entrustment
- **Hardy v. Anderson**, 06-1726 (La. App. 1 Cir. 6/8/07), 964 So.2d 1090 — entrustment of vehicle to a driver known to be unsafe.

### Premises liability (slip-and-fall, falling object)
- **La. R.S. 9:2800.6** — merchant liability statute.
- **White v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc.**, 97-0393 (La. 9/9/97), 699 So.2d 1081 — merchant constructive notice analysis.

### Workplace injury (LHWCA / Jones Act / general maritime)
- Apply jurisdiction-specific authority; for vessel-based injuries, see the Jones Act + general maritime negligence framework.

## 11. Course-and-Scope / Negligent-Entrustment Combo

When the defendant is an employee:

> At the time of the subject crash, [Defendant Driver] was acting within the course and scope of [his/her] employment with [Employer]. [Employer] is vicariously liable for [Defendant Driver]'s negligence under the doctrine of respondeat superior. LeBrane v. Lewis, 292 So.2d 216 (La. 1974).
>
> In addition, [Employer] is independently liable for negligent entrustment. [Employer] knew or should have known that [Defendant Driver] [specific risk factor — was unlicensed / had no CDL / had prior at-fault crashes / had failed drug screens / was driving a vehicle with known mechanical defects]. (Source: [employer records / Defendant Driver deposition / OSHA filings].) Despite this knowledge, [Employer] entrusted [Defendant Driver] with [the vehicle / the equipment] that caused the subject crash.

---

## 12. Pre-Existing Conditions / Eggshell-Plaintiff Framing

If the plaintiff has a prior injury history at any injured body region, address it head-on:

> [Plaintiff] has a documented history of [prior condition] at [body region]. Prior to the subject crash, [Plaintiff] was [working / functional / asymptomatic] for [N] years, as shown by [employer records / fitness-for-duty records / medical chronology entries pre-DOI].
>
> The subject crash aggravated [Plaintiff]'s prior condition to the point where [specific functional change]. [Treating Physician]'s opinion is that the crash directly caused the aggravation. ([Provider Name], [Date], Bates ####.)
>
> Under Louisiana's eggshell-plaintiff doctrine, the defendant takes the plaintiff as [he/she] is found and is liable for the full extent of the injuries caused — including the aggravation of any pre-existing condition. See American Motorist Ins. Co. v. American Rent-All, Inc., 579 So.2d 429 (La. 1991).

Never hide a prior injury. Defense will find it and use the omission against credibility.

---

## 13. Common Liability Pitfalls

- **Don't speculate about defendant's intent or distraction.** If you can't source it, don't write it.
- **Don't characterize the defendant's character** ("habitually negligent," "reckless and indifferent"). Stick to actions.
- **Don't promise unproduced documents** ("we have witness statements that prove..." — either attach the statements or don't reference them).
- **Don't ignore unfavorable facts.** Address them — under-prepare the defense by demonstrating that the firm already has answers.
- **Don't argue legal doctrine at length.** The LAW subsection is one paragraph per statute and one paragraph per leading case — not a full brief.

---

## 14. Comparative-Fault Defense Anticipation

If the case has any comparative-fault risk, the FACTS section should subtly foreclose the defense's arguments before raising them:

- Plaintiff was wearing a seatbelt (if so) — cite the airbag deployment / EMS scene note.
- Plaintiff was sober — cite the EMS BAC test or hospital tox screen.
- Plaintiff was not on the phone — cite phone records produced in discovery or the deposition statement.
- Plaintiff was traveling within the speed limit — cite the police report or accident-reconstruction opinion.
- Plaintiff did not have time/ability to avoid — cite the engineering/reconstruction analysis if available.

Don't list every defense — that signals concern. Just foreclose the ones the defense has signaled it will raise.
