# Mediation Position Paper Template (`mode = mediation_paper`)

The roman-numeral scaffold. Used when the paper is going to the mediator (with defense counsel cc'd). Narrative, humanizing, persuasive — the goal is to move the mediator emotionally and intellectually before mediation, so the mediator carries the plaintiff's framing into the defense room.

---

## Document skeleton

```
[FIRM LETTERHEAD — centered]

[DATE]

[MEDIATOR ADDRESSEE BLOCK]                          [DEFENSE COUNSEL ADDRESSEE BLOCK]

[Optional confidentiality marking: PERSONAL AND CONFIDENTIAL / MEDIATION CONFIDENTIAL]

RE:   [caption per house-style.md §4, including MEDIATION DATE / TIME / LOCATION]

MEDIATION POSITION PAPER

I. INTRODUCTION

   [Client portrait — 1–3 sentences, deliberately human, not lawyerly.
    Identifies who the plaintiff is in the world (job, family, role), not what
    happened to them. Example:
    "Sharonda Brooks is a 36-year-old mother of 3 boys. She works in
    management at an apartment complex and is the sole provider for her
    children."]

   [Optional 1–2 sentence framing of why we're here, e.g.:
    "On [DATE], that life was upended by [DEFENDANT]'s negligence. This paper
    sets out why a meaningful resolution above the policy limits is necessary."]

II. LIABILITY

   A. FACTS
   [Crash narrative with full detail:
    (1) one-paragraph mechanism summary
    (2) plaintiff's lawful action at moment of impact
    (3) defendant's wrongful action with crash-report cite
    (4) officer's narrative quote and citation issued
    (5) ticket-paid-equals-admission move when applicable
    (6) witness corroboration list
    (7) vehicle damage / scene photo references
    (8) defendant deposition admission block-quote if available
    (9) sole-fault statement (verbatim per house-style §12)
    (10) for course-and-scope cases, the respondeat superior + negligent
         entrustment combo]

   B. LAW
   [Statute + case authority:
    (1) Quote the applicable Louisiana traffic statute verbatim (full text
        indented). See louisiana-statutes.md.
    (2) Cite controlling Louisiana case authority — e.g., Thornhill v. State
        DOTD; Mart v. Hill (rear-end presumption); Toston v. Pardon (left turn).
    (3) Per-se negligence closer (verbatim per house-style §12).]

III. DAMAGES

   [Lead paragraph: humanize the medical journey before turning to specifics.
    Example: "The injuries [Client] sustained in the subject crash have
    fundamentally altered every aspect of [her] life. The person [Client]
    had been up to that point ceased to exist on [DATE]."]

   [Optional 8-domain ADL list — each domain a sub-bullet with specific
    impact statement. Use technique #3 from persuasion-playbook.md.]

   [Optional client block-quote: "In her own words, [Plaintiff] testified:"
    followed by an indented verbatim deposition passage.]

   1. TREATMENT
   [Either lighter (refer to attached chronology with high-points summary)
    or heavier (full per-provider narrative with surgical reports and
    imaging findings quoted verbatim).]

   2. PERMANENCY
   [One-paragraph permanency statement — specific permanent impairments,
    permanent work restrictions, functional losses with provider source.]

   3. PAST AND FUTURE ECONOMIC LOSS
   [Past medicals: per-provider table or grand total.
    Future medicals: lump sum OR Polk-style projection table.
    Past lost wages.
    Future lost earning capacity with W-2 history if claimed.]

   4. LIFE EXPECTANCY
   [SSA-table figure for the plaintiff. Sets the multiplier for any future
    damages projection.]

IV. JURY VERDICT RESEARCH AND/OR SETTLEMENT DEMAND

   [Body-region buckets — one section per region:
    1. SOFT TISSUE / 2. CERVICAL AND LUMBAR / 3. TBI / 4. SHOULDER / 5. KNEE
    
    Per bucket:
    - Lead-in (verbatim per house-style §12)
    - 4–8 quantum cases as bulleted parentheticals, each ending with
      the $ amount in bold
    - Recommendation closing (verbatim per house-style §12)]

   [Optional synthesis paragraph after the last bucket:
    "In total, considering the foregoing cases and the injuries and
    treatment that [Client] has endured and will endure, a general damage
    compromise of $[Total GD] is warranted in this case."]

V. JUDICIAL INTEREST

   [Always fully computed for mediation papers. Year-by-year per
    louisiana-judicial-interest.md, with daily-rate math.]

VI. DEMAND

   [Two-step anchor (verbatim per house-style §12):
    "If [Client] is forced to trial in this matter, we would seek a
    judgment of $[TRIAL ANCHOR]. Nevertheless, in the interest of seeking
    an amicable resolution to this matter we will resolve [Client]'s case
    today for a total of $[TODAY NUMBER] inclusive of all costs and fees."]

   [Optional policy-limits posture statement (verbatim per house-style §12).]

   [RECAP table is OPTIONAL in mediation papers — include only if the
    attorney specifically wants it. Mediation papers tend to be narrative,
    not transactional.]

Sincerely,



[ATTORNEY NAME]

[No response deadline — mediation papers do not bear deadlines.]

[Attachments list]
```

---

## Tonal targets

- **Human first, math second.** The mediator should know the plaintiff as a person before being asked to value the case.
- **Specific over general.** Every claim should land on a concrete particular: a date, a provider, a procedure, a quoted sentence, a photo, a dollar figure.
- **Acknowledge weaknesses honestly.** Pre-existing conditions, prior crashes, gaps in treatment, defense expert opinions — name them, then explain why they don't move the value. Mediators trust counsel who don't hide problems.
- **Defense will see it too.** The mediator will share the substance of the paper (even if not verbatim) with the defense. Don't write anything you wouldn't say with the defense in the room.

---

## Length targets

- Surgical mediation paper: **8–15 pages** + attachments
- Multi-injury / TBI / catastrophic case: **15–25 pages** + attachments

Mediators generally prefer 10–15 pages. Push detail into the medical chronology, expense worksheet, and a separate liability-evidence exhibit binder.

---

## Sections to weight heavily

The sections that move the value most in mediation:
1. **Introduction client portrait** — sets the mediator's frame for the entire paper.
2. **ADL impact list** — specifics on what the plaintiff cannot do anymore. Most persuasive single section.
3. **Client block-quote** — credibility weight beyond what counsel can write directly.
4. **Permanency statement** — locks in the case as a long-tail case rather than a soft-tissue case.
5. **Quantum buckets with body-region splits** — gives the mediator a defensible number to push to the defense.
6. **Policy-limits posture statement** — communicates BATNA. Use only when truthful; if you're bluffing the mediator will sense it.

---

## What NOT to do

- Don't write a brief. The mediation paper is persuasion, not legal argument. Legal authority belongs in the LAW subsection (one paragraph) — not throughout.
- Don't repeat the medical records verbatim. Use the chronology as an exhibit; pull only the verbatim quotes that matter.
- Don't insult defense counsel or the carrier. The mediator has to work with them too.
- Don't promise things you cannot deliver ("we have ten more documents we haven't shown you" — either show them now or don't mention them).
- Don't leave the demand number ambiguous. Mediators hate "we'll talk numbers at mediation" papers. Set both the trial anchor and the today number.
- Don't forget the policy-limits posture if the case is worth more than the policy. If you don't communicate BATNA, the defense will assume policy limits is the ceiling.
