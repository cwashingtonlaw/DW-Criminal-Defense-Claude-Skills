# Settlement Demand Letter Template (`mode = demand`)

The letter-style scaffold. Used when the document is going primarily to defense counsel or the claims adjuster. Transactional, math-heavy, deadline-bearing.

---

## Document skeleton

```
[FIRM LETTERHEAD — centered]

[DATE]

[DEFENSE COUNSEL OR ADJUSTER ADDRESSEE BLOCK]

[Optional cc block — mediator if applicable]

RE:   [caption per house-style.md §4]

[Optional salutation]

SETTLEMENT DEMAND LETTER  (or SETTLEMENT OFFER)

[Optional one-paragraph framing]

A. FACTS

[Crash narrative — one tight paragraph or two:
 (1) one-sentence crash locator (date / time / place / mechanism)
 (2) plaintiff's lawful action at moment of impact
 (3) defendant's wrongful action with crash-report cite
 (4) officer's finding + citation issued + ticket-paid status
 (5) witness corroboration list, if any
 (6) vehicle-damage / mechanism evidence
 (7) sole-fault statement (verbatim per house-style §12)]

B. MEDICAL TREATMENT

   a. Injuries
   [Injury summary: ICD-10 table OR PARAMETER grid OR bulleted list.
    Cite source records.]

   b. Treatment
   [Per-provider headed paragraphs in chronological order of first visit, OR
    "see attached medical chronology" pointer when a polished chronology is attached.
    Each paragraph: dates / visits / chief complaints / findings / treatments /
    surgical interventions / status at discharge or current.]

   c. Impact on ADLs
   [Optional in demand mode but recommended.
    Either an enumerated 8-domain list OR a single dense paragraph
    covering work, sleep, household function, recreation, sexual function,
    parenting, mood, and transportation.]

   d. Medical Records / Bills
   [Pointer to the attached medical-chronology and expense-worksheet
    exhibits with exhibit numbers.]

C. SPECIAL DAMAGES

   [Per-provider table — S.No / Provider / Start Date / End Date / Total Charges
    with Grand Total at bottom.]

   [If lost wages claimed:
    Past lost wages: $______ (with citation to wage doc).
    Future lost wages: $______ (with calculation showing W-2 history × worklife years).]

   [If future medicals: lump sum OR Polk-style projection table.]

   [Out-of-pocket expenses if claimed.]

D. GENERAL DAMAGES

   [If single body region:
    A review of Louisiana law shows that similarly situated persons to [Client]
    have recovered the following amounts:
    • [Case 1 with $ amount in bold]
    • [Case 2 with $ amount in bold]
    • ...
    Considering the foregoing cases... a compromise of $[X] would adequately
    compensate [Client] for [body region] injuries (past and future).]

   [If multiple body regions, repeat the above for each region as 1./2./3.
    sub-buckets. De-dup the cases across buckets.]

E. JUDICIAL INTEREST

   [If suit filed: fully computed year-by-year per louisiana-judicial-interest.md.
    If pre-suit: one-line acknowledgment that interest will accrue from judicial demand.]

F. DEMAND

   In the interest of seeking an amicable resolution to this matter we will resolve
   [Client]'s case today for a total of $[X] inclusive of all costs and fees.

   [RECAP TABLE]

   PAST MEDICAL EXPENSES        $ ______
   FUTURE MEDICAL EXPENSES      $ ______
   PAST LOST WAGES              $ ______
   FUTURE LOST WAGES            $ ______
   GENERAL DAMAGES              $ ______
   JUDICIAL INTEREST            $ ______
   TOTAL                        $ ______

   PLEASE RESPOND WITHIN [15 / 30] DAYS INDICATING YOUR ACCEPTANCE, REJECTION,
   OR COUNTER OF THIS REASONABLE SETTLEMENT COMPROMISE.

Sincerely,



[ATTORNEY NAME]

PS: [optional — agreement to mediate or "let me know if you need records"]

[Attachments list]
```

---

## When the case is going into mediation but the document is still styled as a demand

This is common — the firm cc's the mediator on a settlement demand letter rather than writing a separate mediation paper. Adjust:

- Add the mediator to the addressee block (cc'd at the end) or as a second side-by-side block.
- Add the PDDS / MAPS / mediator-org matter number to the RE block.
- Add mediation date/time/location to the RE block.
- Append PS: "plaintiff is agreeable to trying to mediate this matter in good faith."

Body structure stays the letter-style A./B./C./D./E./F.

---

## When to skip sections

- **B.a. Injuries summary** — skip if there are only 1–2 diagnoses; describe inline in the treatment narrative.
- **B.b. ADL impact** — skip in demand mode if the case is pure economic damages (e.g., commercial property damage standing in for soft-tissue claim).
- **C. Future medical expenses** — skip if no future treatment is documented. Write "Not factored in" rather than guessing a figure.
- **D sub-buckets** — collapse to a single bucket for simple soft-tissue cases.
- **E. Judicial interest** — keep section header even if writing "TBD at trial" so the structural symmetry holds.

---

## Length targets

- Soft-tissue pre-suit demand: **3–5 pages** + attachments
- Surgical case demand: **5–8 pages** + attachments
- Multi-injury / multi-surgery demand: **8–12 pages** + attachments

If the draft exceeds the upper bound, look for opportunities to push detail into the attached medical chronology and expense worksheet rather than re-narrating the records inside the demand.

---

## Voice and tone

- Professional. Clinical. Math-forward.
- Use the passive voice for the defendant's wrongful action only when active voice attributes a fact the file does not support. Otherwise active voice ("Defendant Jones rear-ended the plaintiff's vehicle") beats passive ("Plaintiff's vehicle was rear-ended").
- Avoid intensifiers ("extreme," "horrifically," "egregiously"). The math should do the persuasion.
- Avoid hyperbole on damages. "The pain is unbearable" is weaker than "Mrs. Smith has been unable to sleep on her right side since the crash."
- Avoid characterizing the defense ("the carrier has dragged its feet"). Save aggression for the mediation paper.

---

## What NOT to do

- Don't argue legal doctrine beyond what's needed to lock in liability — demand letters are not summary-judgment briefs.
- Don't anchor at a "wishlist" number 5× plausible jury value — defense will treat the demand as unreasonable and harden their position.
- Don't release records the attorney hasn't approved attaching.
- Don't reveal trial strategy that the defense can use later.
- Don't make speaking concessions ("plaintiff did not see the defendant's vehicle in time" — even if true, it gives away comparative-fault ammunition).
