# Persuasion Playbook

Sixteen persuasion techniques distilled from the firm's most polished prior work (especially the Brooks 2020 and Polk 2015 mediation papers) and from 2025 industry best practices for plaintiff-side demand and mediation writing. Each technique includes when to use it and a short template.

The skill should select techniques based on mode and case profile — not every technique fits every case.

---

## 1. Open with a Human Portrait, Not the Crash

**When:** Any mediation paper. Strongest for sympathetic plaintiffs (single parents, sole providers, first responders, teachers, military, etc.).

**Why:** The mediator's first impression of the plaintiff sets the frame for every dollar figure that follows. Lead with the person; the negligence comes second.

**Template:**
> [Plaintiff Name] is a [age]-year-old [job title] [marital/family status]. [Plaintiff] has worked at [Employer] for [N] years, is the sole provider for [N] children, and [one human detail — coaches little league, cares for elderly parents, attends [Church], volunteers at...]. [Plaintiff]'s life before [Date] was [one-sentence description of pre-crash baseline].

Limit to 2–4 sentences. Don't make the plaintiff sound like a movie character — make them sound real and recognizable.

---

## 2. Life Before / Life After

**When:** Mediation papers for cases with significant disruption to daily life. Especially powerful for permanent-injury cases.

**Why:** Forces the mediator to perceive the plaintiff as a person who lost something, not as a claim file.

**Template:**
> Before the subject crash, [Plaintiff] [3-bullet list of pre-crash baseline — work, recreation, family role, physical capacity].
>
> After the subject crash, [Plaintiff] [3-bullet list of post-crash reality — what changed, what was lost, what is permanent].

Use Brooks 2020's framing: *"The person [Plaintiff] had been up to that point ceased to exist."* Don't reach for melodrama; let the specifics carry the weight.

---

## 3. Enumerated ADL Impact List

**When:** Always for mediation papers. Optional but recommended for surgical demands.

**Why:** Specificity wins. "Loss of enjoyment of life" is filler; "four nights of good sleep in two years, all under post-surgical anesthesia" is unforgettable.

**Template (8-domain Brooks-style):**

1. **Bathing.** [Plaintiff] [specific impact — cannot stand long enough to shower; requires shower chair; needs spouse's help to wash hair].
2. **Self-Grooming.** [Plaintiff] [cannot raise arms to brush hair; cannot bend to put on shoes; mother must dress younger child because of lifting restrictions].
3. **Laundry.** [Plaintiff] [cannot carry the laundry basket; cannot reach into the front-loading washer].
4. **Cooking.** [Plaintiff] [cannot stand at stove for more than 5 minutes; cannot lift cast iron; relies on family for meal prep].
5. **Cleaning.** [Plaintiff] [cannot push vacuum; cannot bend to clean tub; hired help for $X/month].
6. **Driving.** [Plaintiff] [unable to drive for 6 months post-surgery; still cannot drive at night; ongoing dizziness with head turns].
7. **Working.** [Plaintiff] [missed [N] weeks; returned light-duty; reduced earning capacity of $X/year per [Source]].
8. **Sleeping.** [Plaintiff] [specific pattern — wakes [N] times per night; cannot find a position; chronic insomnia].

Each domain sources to a specific record citation, deposition quote, or attorney's interview note.

## 4. Direct Client Block-Quote

**When:** Mediation papers when a strong deposition passage exists. Don't use unless the plaintiff's testimony is genuinely powerful and unsanitized.

**Why:** A quote in the plaintiff's own voice carries credibility that counsel's prose cannot. It makes the plaintiff a person, not a case.

**Template:**
> In her own words, [Plaintiff] testified at her deposition:
>
> > [Indented verbatim block from the deposition. Two-paragraph maximum. Pick a passage that conveys (a) ongoing pain, (b) lost identity or capacity, or (c) hopelessness about recovery. Use ellipses to trim, but never to change meaning.]
>
> ([Plaintiff] Dep., [Date], p. ##, ll. ##–##.)

Avoid passages where the plaintiff was visibly coached or scripted. Pick passages where the plaintiff was raw, specific, and contemporaneous.

## 5. Statute + Case Authority in Liability

**When:** Mediation papers always; demand letters for contested-liability cases.

**Why:** Converts "the defendant ran the stop sign" from a factual claim into a per-se-negligence legal conclusion the mediator can quote to the defense. Quoting the statute verbatim signals to defense that the firm is trial-ready.

**Template:** See `liability-playbook.md` §9 — three-paragraph structure (statute / case / per-se closer).

---

## 6. Future-Medical Projection Table

**When:** Cases with documented recurring procedures (facet injections, epidurals, PT, prescriptions) and a life-expectancy figure.

**Why:** Turns abstract "future damages" into a specific arithmetic anchor. The mediator can defend the number to the defense because it's math.

**Template:** See `damages-playbook.md` §2c.

---

## 7. Judicial Interest as a Free Anchor

**When:** Always, when suit has been filed.

**Why:** Judicial interest is mathematical, not discretionary. It adds 10%+ to the value of the case under a label that feels objective.

**Template:** See `louisiana-judicial-interest.md`. Always compute year-by-year for mediation papers.

---

## 8. Trial Number / Today Number (Two-Step Anchor)

**When:** Every mediation paper. Optional for hybrid mode.

**Why:** The mediator hears the higher number first (anchors high) and the defense hears a "compromise" second (feels like a concession even though both numbers are negotiated). Single-number demands don't have this leverage.

**Template:**
> If [Plaintiff] is forced to trial in this matter, we would seek a judgment of $[TRIAL ANCHOR]. Nevertheless, in the interest of seeking an amicable resolution to this matter we will resolve [Plaintiff]'s case today for a total of $[TODAY NUMBER] inclusive of all costs and fees.

**Math:** trial anchor = sum of all components (past meds + future meds + past LW + future LW + GD + JI). Today number = 60–80% of the trial anchor, depending on case strength.

---

## 9. Policy-Limits Posture Statement

**When:** Cases worth more than the known policy limits AND the defense has been given an opportunity to tender. Use only when the firm will actually walk away from a policy-limits offer.

**Why:** Communicates BATNA. Without it, defense assumes the plaintiff will eventually settle for limits.

**Template:**
> The defendants ignored the opportunity to resolve this claim for the policy limits in [date of prior policy-limits demand]. We will never agree to resolve this claim for the policy limits. If the defendants are not prepared to resolve this claim for significantly more than the policy limits there is no need to attempt mediation.

**Don't bluff** — mediators sense bluffs and discount the demand. Use only when the firm is genuinely willing to try the case for excess verdict.

---

## 10. Body-Region Damages Buckets

**When:** Every case with more than one body region injured.

**Why:** A single $750k GD ask looks inflated. The same $750k split as $50k soft tissue + $200k cervical + $500k surgical knee looks built from parts. Each part is defensible against comp cases for that region.

**Template:** See `damages-playbook.md` §5 and `louisiana-quantum-cases.md`. One bucket per body region; each bucket gets its own comp cases and its own recommendation amount.

---

## 11. Anchor by Repetition (Bold the Dollar Figures)

**When:** Always in the GD section.

**Why:** The reader's eye lands on the dollar amount each time it appears in bold. The firm's GD sections cite 4–8 cases per bucket, each ending in a bolded dollar figure — that's 4–8 anchors per page.

**Template:**
> *Smith v. Jones*, 123 So.2d 456 (La. App. 1 Cir. 2010) — cervical fusion with permanent residuals; awarded **$300,000** for general damages.

Bold the dollar figure. The amount before it is dressing.

---

## 12. "Subject [Crash/Collision/Incident]" Formula

**When:** Every reference to the crash after the first mention.

**Why:** Distinguishes the case-at-bar from any prior accidents (common defense play in soft-tissue cases). Reads professionally and unambiguously.

**Template:**
- First reference: full crash sentence with date, place, mechanism.
- Subsequent references: "the subject crash" / "the subject collision" / "the subject incident."

Avoid "the accident" alone — too generic.

---

## 13. Pre-Existing Condition / Aggravation Framing (Eggshell Plaintiff)

**When:** Plaintiff has a prior injury at any injured body region.

**Why:** Defense will find every prior — never hide them. The eggshell-plaintiff framing turns the prior into an aggravator rather than a comparator.

**Template:**
> [Plaintiff] had pre-existing [condition] at [body region] that was [asymptomatic / managed / non-disabling] for the [N] years preceding the subject crash. The subject crash aggravated [Plaintiff]'s prior condition to the point where [specific functional change — required surgery, became permanently disabling, etc.]. Under Louisiana's eggshell-plaintiff doctrine, [Defendant] takes [Plaintiff] as [he/she] is found and is liable for the full extent of the injuries caused, including the aggravation. *American Motorist Ins. Co. v. American Rent-All, Inc.*, 579 So.2d 429 (La. 1991).

---

## 14. Course-and-Scope + Negligent-Entrustment Combo

**When:** Defendant is an employee acting in the course of employment.

**Why:** Two theories of liability against the employer are stronger than one. Particularly powerful when there's a knowledge component (no CDL, prior bad record, mechanical defect known).

**Template:**
> At the time of the subject crash, [Defendant Driver] was acting within the course and scope of employment with [Employer]. [Employer] is vicariously liable under the doctrine of respondeat superior. *LeBrane v. Lewis*, 292 So.2d 216 (La. 1974).
>
> Additionally, [Employer] is independently liable for negligent entrustment. [Employer] knew or should have known that [Defendant Driver] [specific risk factor]. (Source: [employer records / depo / OSHA filings].) [Employer] is liable for negligently entrusting [Defendant Driver] with the [vehicle / equipment] used in the subject crash.

---

## 15. Ticket-Paid-Equals-Admission

**When:** Defendant was issued a citation and paid it (rather than contested).

**Why:** Payment of a traffic citation operates as an admission against interest in subsequent civil proceedings. Defense often forgets this evidence is on the table.

**Template:**
> The investigating officer issued [Defendant] a citation for [statute] — [statute name]. On [date], [Defendant] paid the citation, resolving the offense by payment. Under Louisiana law, payment of a traffic citation operates as an admission against interest in subsequent civil proceedings. ([Citation source.])

---

## 16. Multiple-Witness Corroboration List

**When:** Two or more independent witnesses observed the crash.

**Why:** Demonstrates trial-readiness and forecloses "swearing contest" defenses. Defense will calibrate their offer against the strength of the plaintiff's case at trial — witness lists matter.

**Template:**
> The following independent witnesses observed the subject crash and have provided statements/depositions:
>
> - **[Witness 1]** — [one-sentence observation summary]. ([Statement/Dep cite.])
> - **[Witness 2]** — [one-sentence observation summary]. ([Statement/Dep cite.])
> - ...

Order witnesses by independence (neutral third parties first).

## Three Additional 2025-Best-Practices Layers

These build on the firm's historic toolkit with techniques drawn from current plaintiff-magazine and mediator literature.

### 17. Acknowledge Weaknesses, Don't Hide Them
Mediators trust counsel who name their problems and explain them. Pre-existing injuries, treatment gaps, defense expert opinions — name them in a "Defense Has Suggested..." paragraph and explain why they don't move the value. Hidden weaknesses are landmines; acknowledged weaknesses are managed.

**Template:**
> We anticipate the defense will raise [the prior injury / the treatment gap / the defense IME / the social media post]. We address that here directly: [acknowledgment + counter-narrative].

### 18. Concrete, Specific Detail Over General Claims
The mediator-magazine literature is unanimous on this: specificity persuades, generality does not. Replace every "loss of enjoyment of life" with a specific lost activity. Replace every "severe pain" with a specific functional limitation. Replace every "ongoing treatment" with provider names, dates, and visit counts.

### 19. Tell the Story Once, Cleanly, in Order
Don't repeat the crash story in every section. Tell it once in FACTS. Tell the medical story once in TREATMENT. Tell the damages story once in DAMAGES. Each section advances; none doubles back. The mediator should not re-read the same facts in a new framing — that's a sign of disorganization.

## Anti-Patterns (Things That Hurt the Demand)

| Anti-pattern | Why it backfires |
|--------------|------------------|
| Unrealistic anchor 5× plausible jury value | Defense treats as unreasonable, hardens position, may walk away |
| Personal attacks on defense counsel | Mediator has to work with them too; signals plaintiff has weak facts |
| Withholding records until mediation | Adjuster needs 2–4 weeks to get authority; surprises hurt your settlement |
| Pages of legal argument like a brief | Mediator skims; the brief sits unread |
| Fabricated or unverified citations | Catastrophic credibility kill once defense checks |
| Unsourced factual claims | Defense cross-checks everything; one bad claim taints all |
| Hidden pre-existing conditions | Defense finds them; omission is worse than disclosure |
| Math discrepancies between prose and RECAP | Defense will note them and discount the demand |
| Threats ("we will pursue the maximum") | Empty when not backed by a credible trial plan |
| Excessive intensifiers ("horrifically," "egregiously," "outrageously") | Reads as theatrical; reduces credibility |
| Generic boilerplate not customized to the case | Mediator notices and discounts |
| Promised exhibits not attached | Looks careless or evasive |
