# Louisiana Statutes — Liability & Damages

Verbatim statutory text the skill can quote in the LAW subsection of a mediation paper, or cite inline in a demand. Each statute is paired with its typical use case.

---

## La. R.S. 32:58 — Careless Operation

Use when defendant's general operation of the vehicle was unsafe, without a more specific violation.

> Any person operating a motor vehicle on the public roads of this state shall drive in a careful and prudent manner, so as not to endanger the life, limb, or property of any person. Failure to drive in such a manner shall constitute careless operation.

**Common pairings:**
- Defendant drifted out of lane
- Defendant lost control of vehicle on a curve
- Defendant was distracted (phone, food, passenger)
- Defendant struck a parked vehicle
- Any "I don't know what happened" defendant

**Key case:** *Thornhill v. State DOTD*, 95-1083 (La. App. 1 Cir. 4/4/96), 672 So.2d 348.

---

## La. R.S. 32:81 — Following Vehicles; Exceptions

The rear-end statute. Use whenever the defendant was the rear driver.

> A. The driver of a motor vehicle shall not follow another vehicle more closely than is reasonable and prudent, having due regard for the speed of such vehicle and the traffic upon and the condition of the highway.

**Key case:** *Mart v. Hill*, 505 So.2d 1120 (La. 1987) — establishes the rear-end presumption: a following motorist in a rear-end collision is presumed to have breached R.S. 32:81 and is presumed negligent.

---

## La. R.S. 32:104 — Turning Movements and Required Signals

For lane-change and turning cases.

> A. No person shall turn a vehicle at an intersection unless the vehicle is in proper position upon the roadway as required in R.S. 32:101, or turn a vehicle to enter a private road or driveway, or otherwise turn a vehicle from a direct course or move right or left upon a roadway unless and until such movement can be made with reasonable safety. No person shall so turn any vehicle without giving an appropriate signal in the manner hereinafter provided.

**Common pairings:**
- Unsafe lane change
- Turning without signaling
- Turning into an occupied lane

---

## La. R.S. 32:121 — Vehicles Approaching or Entering Intersection (Right of Way)

For uncontrolled intersections.

> A. When two vehicles approach or enter an intersection from different highways at approximately the same time, the driver of the vehicle on the left shall yield the right of way to the vehicle on the right.

---

## La. R.S. 32:122 — Vehicle Turning Left

The left-turn statute.

> The driver of a vehicle within an intersection intending to turn to the left shall yield the right of way to any vehicle approaching from the opposite direction which is within the intersection or so close thereto as to constitute an immediate hazard.

**Key case:** *Toston v. Pardon*, 874 So.2d 791 (La. 2004) — left-turning driver bears burden of showing turn was safe; high duty of care.

---

## La. R.S. 32:123 — Stop Signs and Yield Signs

For stop-sign-runner cases.

> A. Preferential right of way may be indicated by stop signs or yield signs as authorized in R.S. 32:42.
>
> B. Except when directed to proceed by a police officer or traffic-control signal, every driver of a vehicle approaching a stop intersection indicated by a stop sign shall stop before entering the crosswalk on the near side of the intersection or, in the event there is no crosswalk, shall stop at a clearly marked stop line, but if none, then at the point nearest the intersecting roadway where the driver has a view of approaching traffic on the intersecting roadway before entering the intersection.

---

## La. R.S. 32:124 — Vehicle Entering Highway from Private Road or Driveway

The pull-out statute. Use when defendant pulled from a private drive or side street onto a roadway.

> The driver of a vehicle about to enter or cross a highway from a private road, driveway, alley or building, shall stop such vehicle immediately prior to driving onto a sidewalk or onto the sidewalk area extending across such private road, driveway, alley or building entrance, and shall yield the right of way to all approaching vehicles so close as to constitute an immediate hazard.

**Key case:** *Thomas v. Phoenix Insurance Co.*, 247 La. 268, 170 So.2d 651 (1965).

---

## La. R.S. 32:295.1 — Safety Belt Use

For seatbelt-defense neutralization. Louisiana prohibits introduction of non-use of seatbelt as a defense to liability or damages in most personal-injury cases.

> A. (1) Each driver of a passenger car, van, or truck having a gross vehicle weight rating of ten thousand pounds or less, in this state shall have a safety belt properly fastened about his or her body at all times when the vehicle is in forward motion.

> E. In any action to recover damages arising out of the ownership, common maintenance, or operation of a motor vehicle, failure to wear a safety belt in violation of this Section shall not be considered evidence of comparative negligence. Failure to wear a safety belt in violation of this Section shall not be admitted to mitigate damages.

This neutralizes the seatbelt defense entirely. Cite if defense has signaled it.

## La. R.S. 9:2800.6 — Merchant Liability Statute (Slip-and-Fall)

For premises liability in retail and similar settings.

> A. A merchant owes a duty to persons who use his premises to exercise reasonable care to keep his aisles, passageways, and floors in a reasonably safe condition. This duty includes a reasonable effort to keep the premises free of any hazardous conditions which reasonably might give rise to damage.
>
> B. In a negligence claim brought against a merchant by a person lawfully on the merchant's premises for damages as a result of an injury, death, or loss sustained because of a fall due to a condition existing in or on a merchant's premises, the claimant shall have the burden of proving, in addition to all other elements of his cause of action, all of the following:
> (1) The condition presented an unreasonable risk of harm to the claimant and that risk of harm was reasonably foreseeable.
> (2) The merchant either created or had actual or constructive notice of the condition which caused the damage, prior to the occurrence.
> (3) The merchant failed to exercise reasonable care. In determining reasonable care, the absence of a written or verbal uniform cleanup or safety procedure is insufficient, alone, to prove failure to exercise reasonable care.

**Key case:** *White v. Wal-Mart Stores, Inc.*, 97-0393 (La. 9/9/97), 699 So.2d 1081.

## La. R.S. 13:4202 — Rates of Judicial Interest

Interest on tort judgments accrues from the date of judicial demand. See `louisiana-judicial-interest.md` for the annual rate table.

---

## La. R.S. 13:4203 — Interest from Judicial Demand

> Legal interest shall attach from date of judicial demand on all judgments, sounding in damages, "ex delicto," which may be rendered by any of the courts.

This is the foundational statute for the JI section of every demand. Always cite this when computing or referencing judicial interest.

## La. Civil Code Provisions (Use Sparingly)

The firm's historic style avoids Civil Code citations and instead anchors on quantum cases and traffic statutes. But for academic completeness or for cases against unrepresented defendants:

### Art. 2315 — Reparation of Damages
> Every act whatever of man that causes damage to another obliges him by whose fault it happened to repair it.

### Art. 2316 — Negligence, Imprudence, Want of Skill
> Every person is responsible for the damage he occasions not merely by his act, but by his negligence, his imprudence, or his want of skill.

### Art. 2317 — Acts of Others / Things in One's Custody
For premises and product liability cases.

### Art. 2320 — Vicarious Liability (Master / Servant)
For respondeat superior cases.

### Art. 2323 — Comparative Fault
The Louisiana comparative-fault statute. Defense will invoke this; the demand should foreclose comparative-fault allocation under the sole-fault statement.

> In any action for damages where a person suffers injury, death, or loss, the degree or percentage of fault of all persons causing or contributing to the injury, death, or loss shall be determined, regardless of whether the person is a party to the action or a nonparty.

For each crash mechanism, the skill should default to the matching statute(s):

| Mechanism | Default statute(s) | Companion authority |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Rear-end | R.S. 32:81 | *Mart v. Hill* (rear-end presumption) |
| Stop-sign violation | R.S. 32:123 | — |
| Left-turn across path | R.S. 32:122 | *Toston v. Pardon* |
| Pull-out from side road | R.S. 32:124 | *Thomas v. Phoenix* |
| Lane-change | R.S. 32:79 / 32:104 | — |
| Failure to yield at uncontrolled intersection | R.S. 32:121 | — |
| Speeding | R.S. 32:64 | — |
| Distracted / inattentive | R.S. 32:58 (careless operation) | *Thornhill v. DOTD* |
| Lost control on curve | R.S. 32:58 (careless operation) | — |
| DUI | R.S. 32:300 / 14:98 | — |
| Pedestrian struck in crosswalk | R.S. 32:212 | *Sinitiere v. Lavergne* |
| Premises slip-and-fall | R.S. 9:2800.6 | *White v. Wal-Mart* |

The skill should always quote the statute verbatim in the LAW subsection of mediation papers and cite (without quoting) in the FACTS section of demands.
