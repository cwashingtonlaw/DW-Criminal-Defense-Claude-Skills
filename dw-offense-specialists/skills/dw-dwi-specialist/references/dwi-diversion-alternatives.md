# DWI Diversionary Programs & Alternative Dispositions (Louisiana)

## Overview

Not every DWI case must result in conviction. Louisiana law and parish policies provide alternative dispositions, diversion programs, and reduced-charge negotiations that can minimize criminal exposure, avoid DWI license suspension, and address substance abuse.

---

## First Offense Diversionary Options

### Article 894 Probation (Adjudication Withheld)

**Statute:** La. C.C.P. Art. 894

**Requirement:**
- First DWI offense only
- State consent (prosecutor discretion; mandatory in some parishes)
- Defendant meets suitability criteria (no serious criminal history, stable employment/housing)
- Trial or guilty plea

**Outcome:**
- No criminal conviction recorded
- Adjudication withheld (deferred judgment)
- Probation period: typically 2-3 years
- Upon successful completion: charge can be dismissed and record expunged

**Conditions Typically Imposed:**
- Probation supervision (monthly or quarterly meetings)
- Substance abuse evaluation and treatment/counseling
- DWI education course (ASAP or equivalent)
- Community service: 40-100 hours
- Fines: typically $300-500 (less than full sentence)
- Ignition interlock: often waived if Art. 894 approved

**Advantage to Defense:**
- No DWI conviction
- Avoids mandatory jail time
- License suspension may be reduced or modified
- Post-sentence, record can be expunged (La. C.C.P. Art. 894.1)
- Employment/professional licensing benefits (no DWI conviction to report)

**Negotiation Strategy:**
- Emphasize first offense status
- Present client stability (employment, housing, family ties)
- Obtain substance abuse evaluation pre-negotiation (shows good faith)
- Propose treatment/counseling commitment upfront
- Request prosecutor consent for Art. 894 in plea negotiation

### Substance Abuse Treatment Court

**Availability:** Some Louisiana parishes offer specialized DWI/substance abuse treatment courts (check local availability).

**Eligibility:**
- Typically available for 1st or 2nd offense
- Drug court model with intensive monitoring and treatment
- Requires significant client motivation and treatment compliance

**Program Model:**
- Frequent court appearances (weekly or bi-weekly)
- Urinalysis testing (random drug screens)
- Substance abuse treatment (outpatient or inpatient)
- Counseling and support groups (AA/NA)
- Case management coordination

**Outcome Upon Successful Completion:**
- Charges dismissed or reduced
- No conviction record
- Treatment completion as mitigation for any imposed sentence

**Advantage:**
- Addresses underlying substance abuse
- Court supervision ensures compliance
- Can result in charge dismissal
- Record may be expungeable

## Charge Reduction Negotiations

### Reduced to Reckless Operation (La. R.S. 14:99)

**Statute:** La. R.S. 14:99 - Operating a vehicle in a negligent/reckless manner (NOT a DWI)

**Advantages:**
- NOT a DWI conviction (critical)
- No mandatory DWI license suspension (90 days to permanent per offense level)
- No ignition interlock requirement
- No substance abuse treatment mandate
- Lower penalties

**Penalties for Reckless Operation:**
- Fine: $25-$100
- Jail: Up to 30 days (rarely imposed)
- License suspension: NOT mandatory DWI suspension; ordinary traffic violation suspension applies

**Prosecution Motivation to Negotiate:**
- Weak testing evidence (breath/blood challenges)
- Protocol deviations in SFST
- Constitutional issues (stop/arrest legality)
- Witness credibility problems
- Case strength concerns

### Reduced to Careless Operation (La. R.S. 32:58)

**Statute:** La. R.S. 32:58 - Careless operation of vehicle (traffic violation, NOT DWI)

**Advantages:**
- NOT a DWI conviction
- No DWI license suspension
- No ignition interlock
- No substance abuse treatment requirement
- Minimal criminal exposure

**Penalties for Careless Operation:**
- Fine: $300-$1,000
- NO jail time
- License suspension: Not applicable (traffic violation only)

**Prosecution Motivation:**
- Same as Reckless Operation (weak evidence, protocol issues, constitutional problems)
- Higher threshold for reduction (used when evidence particularly weak)

### Negotiation Strategy for Reduced Charge

**Approach:**
1. Identify specific weaknesses in prosecution's case (breath/blood audit findings, SFST protocol deviations, constitutional issues)
2. Quantify weaknesses: "Intoxilyzer not calibrated within tolerance," "Officer not NHTSA SFST certified," "20-minute observation period not documented"
3. Propose reduced charge as compromise (avoids trial risk, prosecution avoids evidentiary challenges)
4. Emphasize first offense/client stability to make reduction palatable to DA
5. Offer probation/community service to sweeten offer

**Timing:** Approach after discovery complete and weaknesses documented. Early negotiation with assistant DA may result in dismissal or charge reduction. Late negotiation (near trial) may be too late.

## Alternative Sentencing Options

### Ignition Interlock in Lieu of License Suspension (R.S. 32:378.2)

**Statute:** La. R.S. 32:378.2

**First Offense Option:**
- Instead of 90-day license suspension, defendant may install ignition interlock device
- Device cost: ~$75/month ($900/year), paid by defendant
- Interlock requirement: typically 1 year (can be reduced with good compliance)
- Allows defendant to drive to work/necessary places while monitored

**Advantages:**
- Maintains employment (can drive to work)
- Avoids hardship of license suspension
- Demonstrates commitment to sobriety monitoring
- Service provider reports to court (good compliance shows responsibility)

**Negotiation Point:**
- In plea negotiations, request prosecutor/judge approve ignition interlock alternative upfront
- Can be attractive condition for Art. 894 probation

### Day Reporting (Electronic Monitoring)

**Alternative to jail time:**
- Defendant reports to day reporting center daily or per court order
- Electronic monitoring (ankle monitor) tracks location and curfew compliance
- No jail bed required
- Maintains employment, family ties

**Judicial Discretion:**
- Judge may impose day reporting instead of straight jail time
- Particularly effective for first offenders with stable employment

**Negotiation Point:**
- Propose day reporting as alternative to jail minimum in plea negotiation
- Shows client commitment to supervision without incarceration

## Pre-Trial Diversion Programs

**Availability:** Parish-dependent (check with local DA office)

**Model (Varies by Parish):**
- Agreement between defendant and DA's office (pre-trial)
- Charges filed but held in abeyance pending program completion
- Typically 6-12 months duration
- Conditions: counseling, community service, AA/NA attendance, counseling fees

**Completion Outcome:**
- Charges dismissed upon successful completion
- No conviction record
- Record may be expungeable

**Eligibility Criteria (Parish-Specific):**
- First offense (varies; some parishes accept 2nd offense)
- Stable employment/housing
- No prior felonies
- Clean compliance record during program

**Prosecution Motivation:**
- Addresses substance abuse without trial
- Court docket cleared (case resolved)
- Reduced jail/prison population impact

## DWI Court / Sobriety Court Programs

**Model:** Court-supervised intensive monitoring and treatment (similar to Drug Court)

**Availability:** Check if parish has DWI Docket or Sobriety Court

**Eligibility:**
- Typically 1st or 2nd offense
- Identified substance abuse issues
- Willingness to participate in treatment court (significant commitment required)

**Program Components:**
1. Frequent court appearances (weekly or bi-weekly)
2. Frequent urinalysis testing (random, unannounced)
3. Substance abuse treatment (outpatient, group counseling, individual therapy)
4. Case management and monitoring
5. Judge sanctions/incentives for compliance or non-compliance

**Length:** 12-24 months typical

**Successful Completion Benefits:**
- Charges dismissed or significantly reduced
- Conviction avoided
- Sentence reduction (if plea entered)
- Record potentially expungeable
- Real treatment addressing underlying substance abuse

**Negotiation Point:**
- Request referral to DWI Court as alternative to traditional prosecution
- Frame as addressing public safety and treatment (favorable to DA)

## Substance Abuse Evaluation & Treatment

**Critical Strategic Tool (Even If Not Formally Diversionary)**

### Pre-Sentence Evaluation

**Obtain:**
- Substance abuse evaluation by licensed counselor (LSAC preferred)
- 1-3 hour evaluation assessing drinking history, substance abuse patterns, mental health, treatment needs
- Report with recommendations for counseling/treatment levels

**Timing:**
- Obtain BEFORE plea negotiation or sentencing
- Use to negotiate reduced sentence or Art. 894 eligibility
- Present to DA as evidence of client taking responsibility

### Treatment Enrollment

**Strategy:**
- Enroll client in counseling/AA BEFORE pleading or being sentenced
- Use enrollment as mitigation (shows good faith commitment to recovery)
- Continue treatment throughout case resolution
- Present ongoing compliance to court/prosecutor

**Advantages:**
- Judges favor clients already engaged in treatment
- Demonstrates responsibility and motivation
- May persuade DA to consent to Art. 894 or reduced charge
- If convicted, treatment compliance is strong mitigation for favorable sentencing

## License Suspension Mitigation

### Implied Consent License Suspension

**Separate from Criminal Case:**
- R.S. 32:414 provides separate administrative license suspension
- Occurs automatically upon DWI arrest for refusal or failed test
- Suspension period: 90 days (1st offense) to permanent (4th+ offense)

### Right to Hearing

**La. R.S. 32:414 (OWI Hearing):**
- Defendant has right to administrative hearing challenging suspension
- Hearing before Office of Motor Vehicles (OMV) or hearing officer
- Limited scope: only whether refusal occurred and proper advisement given (NOT whether DWI proved)
- Hearing held within 30 days of request

**Defense Strategy:**
- Challenge implied consent advisement (was R.S. 32:661 properly given?)
- Challenge refusal (was it unequivocal, or ambiguous?)
- If successful: license suspension avoided (even if criminal case lost)

### Ignition Interlock Option (Mentioned Above)

- Available in lieu of suspension for 1st offense
- Can negotiate as part of plea agreement

## Record Expungement Strategy

### First Offense Art. 894 Cases

**Post-Discharge (Upon Completion):**
- File La. C.C.P. Art. 894.1 motion to expunge
- Court grants expungement routinely (successful Art. 894 completion)
- Record sealed; treated as if never arrested/charged
- Can answer "no" to questions about DWI arrest (certain employment applications)

### Charge Reduction / Reckless or Careless Operation

**Limited Expungement Available:**
- La. C.C.P. Art. 215.1 allows some expungement of non-felony charges
- Varies by circumstances and charge disposition
- Consult expungement statute for specific eligibility

### Trial Acquittal

**Post-Acquittal:**
- File La. C.C.P. Art. 215.1 motion for expungement
- Court must grant if acquitted
- Record sealed

## Negotiation Checklist

**Use this when negotiating alternative disposition:**

- [ ] Offense level identified (1st/2nd/3rd/4th+)
- [ ] Testing evidence audit completed (identify weaknesses)
- [ ] SFST audit completed (identify protocol deviations)
- [ ] Constitutional issues identified (stop/arrest legality)
- [ ] Client substance abuse evaluation obtained (treatment recommendation)
- [ ] Client enrolled in counseling/AA (pre-negotiation)
- [ ] Client employment/housing stability documented
- [ ] Prior criminal history reviewed (emphasize clean record if applicable)
- [ ] Eligibility for Art. 894 confirmed (with prosecutor input)
- [ ] Parish diversion programs researched (availability confirmed)
- [ ] Reduced charge (Reckless/Careless Operation) proposed with evidentiary justification
- [ ] Ignition interlock alternative explained to client
- [ ] DWI Court / Sobriety Court availability confirmed (if applicable)
- [ ] Prosecutor receptiveness gauged (timing of negotiation approach)

## Parish-Specific Considerations

**Important:** Diversionary options vary significantly by parish:
- Orleans Parish (4th Circuit): Robust Art. 894 programs
- Jefferson Parish (4th Circuit): DWI Court available
- St. Bernard Parish (4th Circuit): Limited diversion
- Calcasieu Parish (3rd Circuit): Check local DA policies
- Caddo Parish (2nd Circuit): Check local DA policies

**Action:** Contact local DA Victim Assistance or Prosecutor's Office for:
- Art. 894 eligibility and consent requirements
- DWI Court / Sobriety Court availability and criteria
- Pre-trial diversion programs
- Standard sentencing practices for first offense
