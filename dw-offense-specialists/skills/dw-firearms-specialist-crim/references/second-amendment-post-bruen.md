## New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen (2022)

### The Paradigm Shift

**BEFORE BRUEN:** Multi-factor means-end scrutiny
- Government could justify firearm restrictions using public safety interest
- Courts balanced individual right against state interest
- Very deferential to government

**AFTER BRUEN:** Historical tradition test
- Rejected all balancing tests
- New standard: text-history-and-tradition framework
- Government must show historical analogue for modern regulation

### The Bruen Test

**Step 1: Text**
- Does the Second Amendment's text cover the regulated conduct?
- If yes, presumptively protected

**Step 2: History and Tradition**
- Is there a historical tradition of comparable regulation?
- Must find actual historical example from Founding era (1791) or shortly thereafter
- NOT sufficient to have only modern reasons for regulation
- Must show longstanding American tradition of regulating the conduct

**Step 3: If No Historical Tradition Found**
- Modern regulation presumptively violates Second Amendment (unless modest variation on historical theme)
- Government must prove "historical pedigree" to justify

### Application to § 922(g) Felon-in-Possession

**CORE DEBATE (UNRESOLVED):**

Was there a Founding-era tradition of disarming all persons convicted of felonies?

**Evidence:** Very limited evidence of categorical felony disarmament in Founding era
- Some historical instances of "disqualification" from owning certain weapons
- But no blanket "all felons banned" rule in 1791
- Historical evidence supports disarmament of certain criminals (traitors, violent offenders) but not all

**Implication:** § 922(g)(1) (all felons) may be vulnerable to Bruen challenge

BUT SEE: Rahimi development (below) suggesting some categories survive.

## United States v. Rahimi (2024) — SCOTUS

### The Case

Defendant subject to civil domestic violence restraining order sought to challenge § 922(g)(8) (restraining order disqualification) and § 922(g)(9) (DV conviction disqualification) under Bruen.

### SCOTUS Holding (5-4)

**§ 922(g)(8) and (g)(9) survive Bruen challenge**

- Court found historical analogue in Founding-era laws disarming dangerous persons
- Domestic violence was recognized threat in early America; states disarmed DV perpetrators
- Restraining order prohibition = reasonable variation on historical DV regulations
- DV conviction prohibition = straightforward application of historical principle

### Key Ratio

**"Historical tradition" doesn't require historical twin, but "analogue" suffices**

- Restraining orders didn't exist in 1791
- But DV regulations existed, and modern restraining orders serve analogous function
- Court doesn't need exact modern parallel to justify regulation
- Broader principle (disarming dangerous persons, especially in DV context) suffices

### Implication for Broader § 922(g)

- Suggested some § 922(g) prohibitions may survive Bruen (those with historical analogue)
- DV crimes and restraining orders: CLEARLY survive
- All felons: still ambiguous (what about nonviolent felons?)
- Unlawful drug users: arguably not covered (Founding-era drug laws didn't exist)

## Circuit Split Post-Rahimi: Is § 922(g)(1) Fully Disqualifying?

### Range v. Attorney General (3rd Circuit 2023)

**Key Case in Favor of Nonviolent Felons**

**Facts:**
- Defendant convicted of false income tax return (nonviolent felony)
- Convicted 20+ years prior; no violence in record

**Holding:**
- Court suggested nonviolent felons may retain Second Amendment rights
- "Historical tradition" of disarming violent criminals; less clear for nonviolent
- Offered framework for as-applied challenge based on offense type

**Implication:**
- Nonviolent felon category may NOT be fully disqualifying under Bruen
- Predicates matter (violent vs. nonviolent)
- As-applied challenge possible

**POST-RAHIMI STATUS:**
- Unclear whether Range survives Rahimi logic
- Rahimi may absorb Range (narrowing scope of Second Amendment protection)
- OR Rahimi may coexist (violent/DV crimes covered by history; nonviolent crimes not)
- **5th Circuit (Louisiana) has not definitively resolved**

### United States v. Daniels (5th Circuit)

**Marijuana user § 922(g)(3) challenge**

- Defendant with marijuana use history (not convicted felon) possessed gun
- Challenged § 922(g)(3) (unlawful drug user disqualification) under Bruen
- Status: ongoing development; check recent 5th Cir. decisions for resolution
- If Daniels invalidates § 922(g)(3), major change to federal firearms law

### Other Circuit Approaches

- **2nd Cir.:** Upheld felon-in-possession without deep Bruen analysis (following precedent)
- **9th Cir.:** Multiple felon challenges pending
- **11th Cir.:** Generally upheld § 922(g)(1) but acknowledged Bruen tension

## When to Raise Second Amendment Challenge

### GOOD CANDIDATES

**1. Nonviolent Felon Predicate**
- Defendant convicted of white-collar crime (fraud, tax evasion, embezzlement)
- Defendant convicted of regulatory violation
- Defendant convicted of property crime (theft, burglary without violent elements)
- No violence history in record
- Strong Range v. AG argument

**2. Weak Historical Tradition Argument**
- Defendant subject to restraining order but no DV conviction (weaker post-Rahimi)
- Defendant with single felony conviction 20+ years prior; otherwise law-abiding
- State felon category may NOT meet federal predicate definition

**3. As-Applied Challenge**
- Defendant's specific predicate crime unlikely to have Founding-era disarmament tradition
- Defendant poses no danger; no violence/weapons involvement in prior record
- Narrow tailoring argument (why this specific crime requires disarmament?)

### POOR CANDIDATES

**1. Violent Felony Predicate**
- Defendant convicted of robbery, burglary (home invasion), assault with weapon
- Clearly within historical tradition of disarming violent criminals
- Post-Rahimi, very unlikely to succeed

**2. Drug-Related Conviction**
- Defendant convicted of drug trafficking, major drug possession
- Post-Rahimi questions whether apply, but unclear advantage
- Better to challenge ACCA predicates (not 2A protection)

**3. Domestic Violence**
- Conviction for DV crime OR subject to DV restraining order
- Rahimi explicitly upheld both; virtually no chance of success

### TIMING AND PROCEDURAL ISSUES

**Standing:**
- Defendant must be "regulated party" (person possessing/seeking to possess firearm)
- Federal prosecution for § 922(g) violation = has standing
- No purely hypothetical "if I wanted a gun" challenges

**Ripeness:**
- Challenge must arise in actual prosecution (not hypothetical)
- Cannot preemptively challenge before arrested
- Federal prosecution = ripe case or controversy

**Facial vs. As-Applied:**
- FACIAL CHALLENGE: § 922(g)(1) wholly unconstitutional (very hard to win)
- AS-APPLIED CHALLENGE: § 922(g)(1) unconstitutional as applied to defendant's predicate (better odds)

**Burden of Proof:**
- Defendant raises Second Amendment issue
- Government must show historical tradition supporting disqualification
- Clear and convincing standard (some circuits)

## Strategy for Second Amendment Challenges

### Step 1: Predicate Audit
- Identify specific prior conviction(s) being used as basis for disqualification
- Determine if violent felony or nonviolent felon

### Step 2: Research Historical Tradition
- Was there a Founding-era principle disarming persons convicted of THIS TYPE of crime?
- Violent crimes: yes (historical tradition of disarming violent criminals)
- Nonviolent crimes: weaker (less clear historical tradition)
- DV crimes: yes (Rahimi confirmed)

### Step 3: Assess Post-Bruen Circuit Precedent
- Check 5th Circuit (Louisiana) for latest approach
- Range v. AG: promising for nonviolent felons
- Rahimi: narrows scope to DV/violence

### Step 4: Develop Bruen Argument
- Text: "Shall not be infringed" — broad language presumptively covers firearm possession
- History: lack of historical disarmament tradition for this specific crime type
- Tradition: no comparable Founding-era regulation

### Step 5: Prepare Fallback Arguments
- If Bruen challenge fails: ACCA predicate validity (categorical approach)
- If ACCA predicate valid: argue burden at sentencing (mitigation)

## Sample Second Amendment Motion Framework

**IF RAISING BRUEN CHALLENGE:**

**TITLE:** Motion to Dismiss or Sever Count [X] as Violating Second Amendment Rights Under New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen

**SECTIONS:**

1. **Defendant's Second Amendment Right (Text)**
   - "Right of the people to keep and bear Arms shall not be infringed"
   - Presumptively covers firearm possession

2. **Historical Tradition of Disarmament for Defendant's Specific Predicate Crime**
   - Identify predicate: [Crime type and date]
   - Research Founding-era analogue
   - Argue lack of historical tradition

3. **Bruen Test Applied**
   - Text: covered
   - Historical tradition: lacking (especially for nonviolent predicates)
   - Conclusion: modern prohibition lacks sufficient historical pedigree

4. **Range v. Attorney General and Circuit Precedent**
   - Cite 3rd Cir. language on nonviolent felons
   - Argue 5th Circuit should follow analogous reasoning

5. **Rahimi Distinction**
   - Acknowledge DV crimes/restraining orders survive
   - Distinguish defendant's non-DV predicate

6. **As-Applied Nature of Challenge**
   - This is narrow as-applied challenge to defendant's specific predicate
   - Not facial challenge to § 922(g) broadly

## Risk Assessment

### High-Risk (Likely to Fail)
- Defendant with violent felony predicate (robbery, assault, burglary)
- Defendant subject to DV restraining order
- Challenge based solely on "right to bear arms" without historical tradition argument
- Facial challenge (attacking § 922(g) wholesale)

### Medium-Risk (Mixed Results)
- Defendant with nonviolent felony predicate (tax evasion, fraud, regulatory violation)
- Post-Rahimi uncertainty on Range v. AG's continued vitality
- 5th Circuit hasn't clearly resolved nonviolent felon category
- May succeed on as-applied basis; unlikely on facial

### Low-Risk (Potentially Successful)
- Defendant with very old nonviolent conviction (>20 years)
- Defendant with clear demonstration of law-abiding conduct since conviction
- Predicate crime with weak Founding-era disarmament tradition (modern regulatory crime)
- As-applied challenge with strong historical tradition argument

## Key Takeaways

1. **Bruen changed the test:** Historical tradition test is now controlling; means-end scrutiny rejected
2. **Rahimi didn't resolve § 922(g)(1) fully:** DV crimes/restraining orders clearly upheld; nonviolent felons ambiguous
3. **Range v. AG (3rd Cir.) offers hope:** Suggests nonviolent felons may retain Second Amendment rights
4. **5th Circuit hasn't resolved:** Louisiana practitioner must research current 5th Cir. precedent MONTHLY
5. **As-applied challenges have better odds:** Narrower argument; focuses on defendant's specific predicate
6. **Historical tradition is critical:** Must find evidence that Founding-era legislatures disarmed this class of persons
7. **This area evolves constantly:** What's law today may change by next month; ALWAYS update before filing

## Recommended Approach for Defense Counsel

**INITIAL:** Assess whether Bruen challenge is viable (nonviolent predicate? Historical tradition argument?)

**IF VIABLE:**
1. File motion to stay § 922(g) proceedings pending resolution of related circuit precedent
2. Research 5th Circuit thoroughly (casedev:search + recent decisions)
3. Draft as-applied Bruen memorandum (not facial)
4. Cite Range v. AG as persuasive authority
5. Prepare historical tradition evidence (expert historian if needed)
6. Distinguish Rahimi (DV crimes) from defendant's non-DV predicate

**IF NOT VIABLE:**
1. Pivot to ACCA predicate challenges (Johnson/Mathis categorical approach)
2. Argue sentencing mitigation (despite § 922(g) validity)
3. Pursue suppression motion if search/seizure issues exist
