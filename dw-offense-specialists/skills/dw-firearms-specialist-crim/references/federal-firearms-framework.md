# Federal Firearms Framework Reference

## 18 U.S.C. § 922(g) — Prohibited Persons

### Overview
Prohibits nine categories of persons from possessing firearms or ammunition. This is the primary federal firearms charge.

### Penalty
- **Up to 15 years imprisonment** (increased from 10 by Bipartisan Safer Communities Act 2022)
- Can run consecutive to other charges

---

## Nine Categories of Prohibited Persons

### 1. Convicted of Crime Punishable by Imprisonment >1 Year (§ 922(g)(1))

**CRITICAL NOTE: Federal definition differs from Louisiana felony definition**

**Federal Test:**
- Any offense punishable by imprisonment for more than one year
- NOT whether offense is classified as felony or misdemeanor under state law
- NOT whether person actually served time
- Only what the sentence COULD have been

**Examples:**
- Louisiana misdemeanor with 2-year sentence possible = federal prohibitor
- Louisiana felony with <1 year authorized sentence = NOT federal prohibitor
- Wobbler charges (can be charged as felony or misdemeanor) must be analyzed by actual adjudication

**Rehaney/Lara Issues:**
- Does the Louisiana conviction constitute a "crime punishable by >1 year" under federal law?
- Federal courts must look to elements of offense, not how state classified it
- Some Louisiana crimes may not meet federal threshold

**ACCA Interaction:**
- If defendant has 3+ prior violent felonies or serious drug offenses, § 924(e) applies (15-year mandatory minimum)
- Predicates must be categorically "violent" or "serious drug" (Taylor/Mathis test)

### 2. Fugitive from Justice (§ 922(g)(2))

- Any person fleeing to avoid prosecution, conviction, imprisonment, or testifying
- Active warrant = fugitive status
- Even bench warrant from traffic case triggers prohibition

### 3. Unlawful User of Controlled Substance (§ 922(g)(3))

- **Marijuana use counts as disqualifier**, even in states with legal marijuana
- Federal marijuana prohibition remains; state legalization irrelevant for § 922(g)
- Proven by: positive drug test, possession, admission, defendant's statements
- NOT limited to felony drug convictions (simple possession triggers this)
- One-time use may trigger disqualification (no threshold for regular/habitual use)

### 4. Adjudicated Mental Defective (§ 922(g)(4))

- Prior adjudication of mental illness
- Involuntary commitment to mental institution
- Court finding of incompetence to stand trial
- Note: Merely seeing mental health professional does NOT trigger this

### 5. Illegal Alien (§ 922(g)(5))

- Not lawful permanent resident of U.S.
- Includes: temporary visa holders, DACA recipients, overstays, undocumented status
- Must be documented via I-94 records or immigration status

### 6. Dishonorable Discharge (§ 922(g)(6))

- Military dishonorable discharge only (from U.S. Armed Forces)
- Other-than-honorable discharge does NOT trigger this category
- Must be actual dishonorable discharge (most severe military punishment)

### 7. Domestic Violence Conviction (§ 922(g)(9))

- Misdemeanor crime of domestic violence as defined by 18 U.S.C. § 921(a)(33)
- Includes: Louisiana DV battery convictions (R.S. 14:35.3)
- NOTE: Misdemeanor-level conviction suffices (not felony only)
- Includes intimate partner, former spouse, person with whom defendant shares child, cohabitant
- Crime must involve use, attempt, threat of physical force OR endangerment

**Rahimi (2024) Development:**
- See separate section below; domestic violence restraining order ALSO disqualifies (§ 922(g)(8))

### 8. Subject to Protective Order (§ 922(g)(8))

- Court order (civil or criminal) against defendant
- Must find reasonable cause that defendant committed DV act OR order issued to prevent harassment/threatening/stalking
- Order must be enforceable under state law
- Includes temporary protective orders (TPOs)

**United States v. Rahimi (2024):**
- SCOTUS upheld § 922(g)(8) under Bruen historical tradition test
- Court found historical analogue (Founding-era domestic violence regulations)
- Prevents all persons under DV restraining orders from possessing firearms
- Separate from § 922(g)(9) (conviction-based) — restraining order alone suffices

### 9. Renounced U.S. Citizenship (§ 922(g)(7))

- Rare category; person explicitly renounced citizenship
- Must be documented via State Department
- Less commonly charged

## 18 U.S.C. § 924(c) — Using or Carrying Firearm During Crime of Violence or Drug Trafficking Crime

### MANDATORY CONSECUTIVE SENTENCING — HIGHEST-STAKES ISSUE IN FIREARMS CASES

### Overview
Provides mandatory consecutive sentences when firearm is used or carried during underlying crime.

### Penalties (MANDATORY CONSECUTIVE)

- **Base (carried only):** 5 years minimum consecutive
- **Brandished (displayed/pointed):** 7 years minimum consecutive
- **Discharged (fired):** 10 years minimum consecutive
- **Caused death/serious injury:** 25+ years minimum consecutive (life possible)
- **Second or subsequent § 924(c) offense:** 25 years minimum consecutive

### Key Point: Consecutive
- These sentences RUN CONSECUTIVE to underlying crime sentence
- Example: Drug trafficking 10 years + § 924(c) 7 years = 17 years minimum combined
- Cannot be reduced by guideline adjustments or downward departures (post-Booker)

### "In Furtherance" Element

**Not automatic merely because gun and crime occur together**

- Firearm must facilitate, promote, or advance the underlying crime
- Mere presence in vehicle/residence during drug transaction ≠ "in furtherance"
- Gun must have nexus to crime (carried for protection, used in threat, etc.)
- Fact-intensive jury question

**Examples:**
- Drug dealer carries gun to protect stash or during sale = in furtherance
- Gun left in car while defendant inside selling drugs = arguably NOT in furtherance
- Gun visible/shown to buyer as intimidation = in furtherance

### Interaction with § 922(g)

**Two separate charges:**
1. § 922(g) — Felon in possession of firearm (status crime)
2. § 924(c) — Using/carrying firearm during drug/violence crime (conduct crime)

Can be charged together, creating stacked mandatory minimums.

## 18 U.S.C. § 924(e) — Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA)

### MANDATORY 15-YEAR MINIMUM SENTENCE

### Trigger

**Three Prerequisites:**
1. Defendant convicted of violating § 922(g)(1) (felon in possession)
2. Defendant has **three or more** prior convictions
3. Prior convictions qualify as "violent felonies" OR "serious drug offenses"

### Mandatory Minimum
- **15 years imprisonment** (no reduction, no probation)
- Completely mandatory
- Guideline range irrelevant

### "Violent Felony" Definition (Taylor Categorical Approach)

**Elements Test (not conduct test):**
- Offense must have element of force or substantial risk that force will be used against another person
- Look to statutory definition (elements), not how defendant actually committed crime

**Typical Violent Felonies:**
- Homicide crimes (murder, manslaughter)
- Robbery (inherent force)
- Burglary (invasion of dwelling, risk of confrontation)
- Arson (extreme danger)
- Aggravated assault (use of force)

**NOT Violent Felonies:**
- Fraud, embezzlement, tax evasion (no force)
- Simple drug possession (no force)
- DUI (no force element typically)

**Johnson v. United States (2015):**
- "Residual clause" struck down as void for vagueness
- Cannot use unclear language like "serious risk of harm"
- Must rely on explicit elements test only

**Mathis v. United States (2016):**
- Distinguish between elements and means
- Look to required elements, not possible factual scenarios
- Wobbler offenses: look to actual adjudication/charging to determine which version

### "Serious Drug Offense" Definition

- Federal felony drug offense
- Trafficking in: cocaine, methamphetamine, heroin, LSD, fentanyl, etc.
- Minimum quantity varies by drug
- Simple possession does NOT count (unless triggers mandatory minimum separately)

### Predicate Conviction Challenge Strategy

**CRITICAL: Every predicate must categorically qualify**

- Obtain charging documents (indictment, information)
- Obtain guilty plea colloquy (transcript)
- Obtain statutory elements for each predicate
- Apply Taylor/Mathis categorical test
- Challenge predicates that don't meet federal "violent felony" or "serious drug" definition

**Example Challenge:**
- Defendant has prior Louisiana aggravated battery conviction
- Federal aggravated battery statute requires element of "serious bodily injury"
- Louisiana statute requires element of "motor vehicle" (felony vehicle flight)
- Neither matches "violent felony" definition = predicate invalid
- If valid predicates drop below 3, ACCA mandatory minimum disappears

### 5th Circuit Development

- Track current case law for Louisiana crime predicates
- Circle splits on whether certain Louisiana crimes qualify
- Burglary predicate: whether Louisiana burglary (broad) meets federal definition
- Vehicle flight predicate: whether constitutes "violent felony"

## Federal Sentencing Guidelines (USSG § 2K2.1)

### Base Offense Level

- § 922(g) felon in possession base: 12-14 (depends on weapon type)
- Handgun/rifle: 12-13
- Machine gun: 24+
- Sawed-off shotgun: 18+

### Enhancements

- Prior convictions: +2 for each prior federal firearm conviction
- Number of weapons: +2 for 2-4 weapons; +3 for 5+
- Trafficking in firearms: +8 to +16
- Criminal history category: A-F based on prior record

### Criminal History Category

- Calculated from prior sentences (felony convictions, state/federal)
- Category I (lowest) to VI (highest)
- Determines final guideline range

### Final Guideline Range

- Base offense level + enhancements = offense level
- Offense level + criminal history category = guideline range
- Judge must consider guideline range (post-United States v. Booker, advisory only)

### Mandatory Minimums Override Guidelines

**CRITICAL:**
- If § 924(e) ACCA applies: 15-year mandatory minimum overrides guideline range
- If § 924(c) applies: 5-25 year mandatory consecutive overrides guideline range
- Guideline range becomes irrelevant when mandatory minimum exceeds it

## Federal vs. State Sentencing Comparison

| Factor | Louisiana R.S. 14:95.1 | Federal § 922(g) |
|--------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Sentence Range** | 10-20 years hard labor | Up to 15 years (advisory guidelines) |
| **Mandatory Minimum** | Yes: 10-20 hard labor | No (unless ACCA applies = 15 years) |
| **Parole Eligibility** | No parole (mandatory) | Supervised release after 85% served |
| **Cleansing Period** | 10 years from sentence end | No cleansing |
| **Guideline Enhancements** | Habitual offender bill | USSG § 2K2.1 base 12-14 |
| **ACCA Overlay** | Not applicable | 15-year mandatory if 3+ predicates |
| **§ 924(c) Risk** | Not applicable | 5-25 years mandatory consecutive |
| **Consecutive Exposure** | Single charge typically | Can stack § 922(g) + § 924(c) + ACCA |

## Quick Reference: Stacking Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Felon in Possession (State Only)
- Charge: R.S. 14:95.1
- Sentence: 10-20 years hard labor (mandatory)
- Parole: None

### Scenario 2: Felon in Possession (Federal Only)
- Charge: § 922(g)(1)
- Guideline range: 12-18 months (example)
- Sentence: Judge discretion (up to 15 years)
- Parole: Possible (85% rule)

### Scenario 3: Felon + Drug Trafficking + § 924(c)
- § 922(g)(1): 12-18 months guideline
- Drug trafficking: 5-40 years guideline (depending on quantity/type)
- § 924(c): 7 years mandatory consecutive (if brandished)
- **Total minimum: 7+ years (§ 924(c) consecutive alone)**

### Scenario 4: ACCA Trap (3+ Predicates)
- § 922(g)(1): 12-18 months guideline
- ACCA: 15-year mandatory minimum (if 3+ violent/drug predicates)
- **Result: 15 years mandatory (guideline irrelevant)**

---

## Key Takeaways

1. § 922(g) is status crime; § 924(c) is conduct crime (can charge together)
2. Federal adoption risk is HIGH in drug + gun cases
3. Predicate conviction quality is make-or-break (ACCA, cleansing period)
4. § 924(c) mandatory consecutive can double total sentence exposure
5. ACCA 15-year mandatory is catastrophic if triggered; challenge every predicate
6. Federal sentencing can be more lenient than state (parole available; no mandatory minimum unless ACCA/§ 924(c))
7. Forum selection (state vs. federal) should drive plea negotiation strategy

   - Lesser-used category

### 18 U.S.C. § 924(c) — Using or Carrying Firearm During Crime of Violence or Drug Trafficking Crime

**MANDATORY CONSECUTIVE SENTENCING — HIGHEST STAKES ISSUE IN FIREARMS CASES**

**PENALTIES:**
- Base: 5 years consecutive (minimum)
- Brandished: 7 years consecutive
- Discharged: 10 years consecutive
- Discharged and caused death/serious injury: 25+ years consecutive
- **Second or subsequent § 924(c) conviction:** 25 years minimum

**"IN FURTHERANCE" REQUIREMENT:**
- Not just mere presence of gun near crime
- Gun must facilitate, promote, or have reasonable nexus to underlying crime
- Example: gun in car during drug transaction may not be "in furtherance" if never shown
- Much fact-intensive inquiry

**FEDERAL SENTENCING IMPLICATIONS:**
- § 924(c) sentence runs CONSECUTIVE to underlying crime (drug trafficking, robbery, etc.)
- Creates massive total sentence exposure
- Example: Drug trafficking 10 years + § 924(c) 7 years = 17 years minimum
- Cannot be shortened by guideline adjustments

### 18 U.S.C. § 924(e) — Armed Career Criminal Act (ACCA)

**15-YEAR MANDATORY MINIMUM SENTENCE**

**TRIGGER:**
- Defendant convicted of being felon in possession (§ 922(g)(1))
- AND has three or more prior convictions for violent felonies or serious drug offenses

**"VIOLENT FELONY" DEFINITION (Taylor categorical approach):**
- Must have elements of force or substantial risk that force will be used
- Typically: homicide, robbery, burglary, arson, aggravated assault
- NOTE: Johnson v. United States (2015) struck down "residual clause" as void for vagueness
- Must use elements test (Mathis v. United States, 2016)

**"SERIOUS DRUG OFFENSE" DEFINITION:**
- Federal felony drug offense (controlled substance)
- Trafficking in cocaine, methamphetamine, heroin, etc.
- NOT simple possession (unless it triggers mandatory minimum separately)

**PREDICATE CHALLENGE STRATEGY:**
- Every predicate must qualify categorically
- Use Taylor/Mathis categorical approach
- Get actual crime of conviction documents (indictment, plea colloquy, statute)
- Some Louisiana felonies may not meet federal definition of "violent" or "serious drug"
- Circuit split on whether Louisiana crimes qualify

### Federal Sentencing Guidelines (USSG § 2K2.1)

- Base offense level for § 922(g) possession: typically 12-14 (depending on weapon type)
- Enhancements: prior convictions, number of weapons, trafficking in firearms
- Criminal history category: calculated from prior convictions
- Final guideline range (before mandatory minimums override)
- REMEMBER: § 924(c) mandatory consecutive sentences override guideline calculations
