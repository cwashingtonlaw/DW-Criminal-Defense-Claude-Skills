# Louisiana Firearms Statutes Reference

## R.S. 14:95 — Illegal Carrying of Weapons

### Definition
Intentionally carrying any firearm or other weapon in a manner prohibited by law.

### Prohibited Conduct

**1. Concealed Handgun Without Permit**
- Carrying handgun in manner that conceals it without lawful permit
- Exception: within home, workplace, or vehicle lawfully

**2. Carrying While Intoxicated**
- Carrying firearm while under influence of alcohol or drugs
- Intoxication standard: impairs judgment/coordination

**3. Carrying on School Property**
- Any firearm or weapon on school grounds, buildings, or buses
- Exception: authorized peace officers, security personnel
- R.S. 14:95.2 (1000-foot school zone) carries enhancement

**4. Carrying at Parade/Demonstration**
- Carrying firearm at parade, procession, demonstration, or public event

**5. Carrying in Courthouse**
- Firearm in courthouse or judicial building (federally also prohibited)

### Penalties

**First Offense:**
- Up to 6 months imprisonment and/or $500 fine
- Misdemeanor level

**Subsequent Offense:**
- Up to 3 years imprisonment
- Can be felony if carries multiple priors

### Defenses

- Valid concealed carry permit (must check permit against location restrictions)
- Permit-exempt locations (lawful occupancy of home, vehicle, workplace)
- No knowledge of firearm (reasonable mistake)
- Officer unaware of valid permit (good faith reliance on permit)

### Exemptions (R.S. 40:1379.3)

- Licensed peace officers (sworn law enforcement)
- Military personnel in military capacity
- Persons with valid concealed carry permit
- Lawful occupants of homes, vehicles
- Persons at authorized shooting ranges

## R.S. 14:95.1 — Felon in Possession of Firearm

### CRITICAL STATUTE — MANDATORY SENTENCING

### Elements

1. Defendant has been convicted of a felony (in Louisiana or elsewhere)
2. Defendant intentionally possesses a firearm
3. Firearm is accessible to defendant
4. Defendant knows of the possession
5. Firearm is not a destructive device under NFA (not registered)

### Penalties

**MANDATORY:**
- 10 to 20 years hard labor at Angola
- **NO probation, parole, or suspension of sentence** — completely mandatory
- Good-time credit may be withheld or limited (interpretations vary)
- Second offense: enhanced penalties possible under habitual offender bill

### Predicate Conviction Audit Checklist

**CRITICAL: Many "felonies" don't actually disqualify**

- [ ] Was it a CONVICTION? (not deferral under La. C.C.P. Art. 893)
  - Deferral = conviction doesn't enter judgment; defendant still not "convicted felon"
  - Check judgment for deferral language
- [ ] Is it actually a FELONY?
  - Some charges are "felony charges" but adjudicated as misdemeanor
  - Check sentencing range imposed vs. authorized range
  - Wobbler offense? (Can be charged/tried as either felony or misdemeanor)
- [ ] Has it been EXPUNGED?
  - LSHB 1018 (2017): some felonies expungeable after 5-10 years
  - Expungement = conviction is dismissed/set aside
  - Must check with LSDC or state police records
- [ ] Was BOYKIN colloquy proper?
  - Failure to administer Boykin inquiry on guilty plea = void conviction
  - Check plea transcript for Boykin advisement
  - Defective Boykin = predicate doesn't count
- [ ] EXACT date of conviction vs. date of sentence completion?
  - Cleansing period (see below) runs from END of sentence, not conviction date
  - Critical distinction

### 10-Year Cleansing Period (R.S. 14:95.1(C))

**OFTEN OVERLOOKED DEFENSE**

- Cleansing period clock: starts from **completion of sentence**
- Completion includes parole/probation termination date
- NOT from date of conviction
- Example: Convicted 2018 of felony, released from parole 2020, present day 2026
  - Cleansing period: 2020 + 10 years = 2030 (not yet expired)
  - BUT if released in 2015, then 2015 + 10 = 2025 (expired)
- If within cleansing period, § 95.1 charge should be dismissed
- Must obtain certified records of parole release date

### Possession Element Analysis

**ACTUAL POSSESSION:**
- Firearm on person's body (waistband, hand, jacket pocket)
- In direct physical control
- Clear and unambiguous

**CONSTRUCTIVE POSSESSION:**
- Vehicle (glove box, under seat, trunk, center console)
- Residence (bedroom, common area, closet)
- Shared spaces (apartment with roommates, workplace)
- Must apply Harris factors: awareness, dominion/control, guilty knowledge
- Proximity alone is insufficient

## R.S. 14:95.2 — Firearm-Free Zone (School Property)

### Definition
Possession of firearm within 1,000 feet of school property.

### Elements
1. Defendant intentionally possesses firearm
2. Possession occurs within 1,000 feet of school building/grounds/vehicle
3. Defendant knows of proximity to school

### Penalties
- Enhanced penalty on top of underlying R.S. 14:95 charge
- Can be separate charge or enhancement to R.S. 14:95.1

### School Property Definition
- School buildings
- School grounds (campus)
- School buses (during operation)
- Playgrounds and athletic facilities

### Exceptions
- Authorized law enforcement
- School security personnel (authorized to carry)
- Licensed security at school event
- Lawful occupant (teacher's residence on school property — rare)

## R.S. 14:95.5 — Possession by Domestic Abuse Conviction Offender

### Trigger
- Prior conviction for domestic abuse battery (R.S. 14:35.3)
- Defendant currently possesses firearm

### Penalty
- Up to 5 years imprisonment
- Can be charged separately or as enhancement

### Overlap with Federal Law
- 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9) mirrors this (federal DV prohibitor)
- Defendant may face both state and federal charges
- Also overlaps with R.S. 14:95.10 (protective order)

## R.S. 14:95.10 — Prohibited Possession (Protective Order/DV Conviction)

### Two-Part Prohibition

**Part 1: Domestic Abuse Conviction**
- Defendant previously convicted of domestic abuse battery
- Currently possesses firearm
- Overlaps with R.S. 14:95.5

**Part 2: Protective Order**
- Court issued domestic violence protective order against defendant
- Defendant is subject to order
- Order prohibits firearm possession
- Often in civil context (separate from criminal case)

### Overlap with Federal Law
- 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(8) — federal restraining order prohibitor
- Rahimi (2024): restrained persons' § 922(g)(8) disqualification upheld under Bruen
- Defendant may face both state charge and federal § 922(g) exposure

## Concealed Carry Permitting (R.S. 40:1379.3)

### Permit Requirements
- Louisiana resident (18+ years old)
- Not prohibited person (felon, mental patient, etc.)
- Background check (state police)
- Permit issued by sheriff
- Valid for 4 years

### Permit Exemptions (No Permit Required)
- Lawful occupant of home or workplace
- Lawful occupant of vehicle
- Persons on licensed shooting ranges
- Licensed peace officers
- Military personnel (military capacity)

### Restrictions Even with Permit
- Schools (1000-foot zone still applies)
- Courthouses
- Federal buildings
- Private property where owner prohibits
- Parades/demonstrations (may be prohibited)

### Defense Strategy
- Verify permit validity at time of alleged offense
- Check permit limitations (certain locations excepted?)
- Officer error in reading permit (good faith reliance)
- Permit was valid but officer unaware

## Castle Doctrine / Stand Your Ground (R.S. 14:20)

### Justifiable Homicide (Self-Defense)

### Elements
1. Defendant used force against another person
2. Defendant was in place where defendant had right to be (castle doctrine)
3. Defendant reasonably believed force was necessary to prevent death/great bodily harm
4. Defendant did not provoke incident
5. No duty to retreat (stand your ground)

### Application to Firearms Cases
- If defendant had firearm for lawful self-defense purpose, may affect sentencing mitigation
- NOT a defense to illegal carrying charge itself
- May explain why defendant possessed firearm (context for mitigation)
- Separate from guilt/innocence on carrying charge

### Castle Doctrine Locations
- Own home (dwelling)
- Vehicle
- Workplace (with permission)
- Any place where person has right to be

### Duty to Retreat
- Louisiana: no duty to retreat (stand your ground)
- Can remain in place and use defensive force (including firearm)
- Unlike some states with "duty to retreat" before using force

### R.S. 14:95 — Illegal Carrying of Weapons

**ELEMENTS:**
- Intentionally carrying any firearm or other weapon in manner prohibited by statute
- Manner includes: concealed handgun without proper permit, carrying while intoxicated, carrying on school property, carrying at parade/demonstration, carrying in courthouse

**PENALTIES:**
- First offense: up to 6 months imprisonment and/or $500 fine
- Subsequent offense: up to 3 years imprisonment

**DEFENSES:**
- Valid concealed carry permit (R.S. 40:1379.3)
- Permit-exempt locations (residence, vehicle, workplace)
- No intent/knowledge of carrying
- Permit obtained but officer unaware (reasonable mistake of law)

### R.S. 14:95.1 — Felon in Possession of Firearm

**CRITICAL STATUTE — MANDATORY SENTENCING**

**ELEMENTS:**
1. Defendant is a convicted felon (within Louisiana or elsewhere)
2. Defendant intentionally possesses a firearm
3. Firearm is accessible to defendant
4. Defendant knows of possession
5. Firearm is not a destructive device and not subject to NFA registration

**PENALTIES:**
- 10 to 20 years hard labor at Angola
- **NO probation, parole, or suspension of sentence** — fully mandatory
- No good-time credit applicable in some interpretations

**PREDICATE CONVICTION AUDIT (MUST DO):**

Is the prior conviction actually a disqualifying felony?

1. **Was it a conviction?** (not a deferral under La. C.C.P. Art. 893, not a deferred adjudication)
2. **Was it actually a felony?** (Some charges can be adjudicated as misdemeanors or felonies depending on sentencing)
3. **Has it been expunged?** (Check LSHB 1018 — some felonies can be expunged after 5-10 years)
4. **Was Boykin colloquy proper?** (Failure to comply with Boykin in guilty plea = void conviction)
5. **What is the specific statute of conviction?** (Some crimes are wobbler offenses)

**10-YEAR CLEANSING PERIOD — R.S. 14:95.1(C):**
- Cleansing period runs from completion of sentence, NOT from date of conviction
- If prior felony sentence ended 9 years ago, defendant is still within window
- Completion includes parole/probation termination
- Document exact dates of sentence and parole release

### R.S. 14:95.2 — Firearm-Free Zone

- Possession within 1000 feet of school property: enhanced penalty
- Carries additional crime (separate charge possibility)
- School property defined broadly (building, grounds, buses)

### R.S. 14:95.5 — Prohibited Possession (Domestic Abuse)

- Defendant convicted of domestic abuse battery (R.S. 14:35.3)
- Possession of firearm while under such conviction: prohibited
- Overlaps with federal § 922(g)(9) domestic violence prohibitor

### R.S. 14:95.10 — Protective Order/Domestic Abuse

- Possession prohibited while under protective order
- Or while convicted of domestic abuse crime
- Overlaps with federal law (18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(8))

### Concealed Carry Permit Exemptions

- R.S. 40:1379.3 outlines permit requirements
- Certain locations exempt (vehicle, residence, workplace with employer consent)
- Permit required for public carry in populated areas

### Castle Doctrine / Stand Your Ground

- R.S. 14:20 (Justifiable homicide) — self-defense principles
- May apply as mitigation if firearm used in self-defense scenario
- Separate from illegal carrying charge itself
