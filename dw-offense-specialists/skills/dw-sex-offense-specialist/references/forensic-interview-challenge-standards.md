# Forensic Interview Challenge Standards

## Interviewer Qualification Audit

- What training did interviewer complete?
- Certification in specialized child interview protocol?
- How many interviews has interviewer conducted?
- **Sex Offense Challenge:** Interviewer bias toward finding abuse is documented risk

## Interview Protocol Compliance Checklist

- Was evidence-based protocol used (NCMEC, ICAN, state protocol)?
- Were open-ended questions used first?
- **Sex Offense Challenge:** Leading questions and suggestive techniques common in older interviews

## Recording Standards

- Is full video/audio recording available?
- No editing or truncation?
- Multiple interviews? If so, all recorded?
- **Challenge:** Prosecution may use excerpts only

## Developmental Stage Expectations

- Age at interview?
- Language development appropriate?
- Memory capabilities for age?
- **Challenge:** Young children prone to suggestion; memory details unreliable

## Delayed Disclosure Research

**Critical Defense Concept:** Delayed disclosure of sexual assault is extremely common and does **not** indicate fabrication.

**Research Findings:**
- 70–90% of child victims delay disclosure (weeks, months, years)
- Common reasons: fear, shame, self-blame, dependency on perpetrator, threats
- Some children initially deny abuse when first asked
- Delayed disclosure not evidence of false allegation

**Defense Strategy:**
- Use delayed disclosure research to counter prosecution argument: "Why did victim wait to report?"
- Education of judge/jury: delay is normal trauma response
- Reference: *State v. Broadway* (Louisiana case recognizing delayed disclosure)

## Recantation Research & Significance

**Definition:** Child's retraction of abuse allegation after initial disclosure.

**Documented Frequency:**
- Recantations occur in 4–8% of substantiated abuse cases
- When recantation occurs, it often indicates:
  - Victim pressure from non-offending parent or family
  - Victim fear of consequences (family separation, foster care)
  - Confusion about responsibility (child feels blamed)
  - Ambivalence about disclosure

**Defense Use:**
- If alleged victim recants: this may indicate false allegation OR indicate victim coercion/fear
- Expert testimony on recantation dynamics may be admissible
- Cross-examine about pressures on victim to recant

## Multiple Disclosure Methodology

**Issue:** Alleged victim's story may change between interviews.

**Why Changes Occur:**
- Memory develops over time (normal development)
- Different interviewer style elicits different recall
- Child anxiety increases disclosure consistency
- Leading questions shape later responses

**Defense Audit:**
1. Compare all interviews chronologically
2. Document what details changed
3. Distinguish confabulation (false memory) from normal memory development
4. Identify leading questions that may have shaped later disclosures
