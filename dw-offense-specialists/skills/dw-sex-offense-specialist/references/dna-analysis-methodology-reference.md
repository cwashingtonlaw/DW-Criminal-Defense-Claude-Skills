# DNA Analysis Methodology Reference

## Chain of Custody Checklist

DNA evidence is only as reliable as its chain of custody. Critical audit points:

**Questions to Answer:**
1. Was kit collected per LADOJ (Louisiana Department of Justice) protocol?
2. Who collected the kit? (SANE vs. physician vs. law enforcement)
3. How long between alleged assault and kit collection?
4. Proper specimen labels, sealing, storage temperature documented?
5. Any break in chain of custody?
6. How long was kit stored before analysis?
7. DNA extraction and analysis date vs. collection date (degradation risk)?

## DNA Mixture Interpretation Challenges

**What is a DNA mixture?**
- Multiple DNA sources in single evidence item
- Common in sexual assault kits (victim + accused + unknown)
- Interpretation is probabilistic, not absolute

## Probabilistic Genotyping (TrueAllele/STRmix)

- Bayesian statistical approach to mixed profile interpretation
- Software (TrueAllele, STRmix) assigns probability to suspected profile match
- **Critical Weakness:** Results vary based on assumptions fed into software
- Prosecution often presents as "probability accused is source" but excludes defense expert scrutiny
- **Defense Challenge:** Demand defense expert independent analysis; question assumptions

## Touch DNA Limitations

- DNA transferred from touching without penetration/bodily fluid contact
- Present on skin, clothing, surfaces touched
- **Does NOT indicate sexual contact or assault**
- Can be transferred indirectly (touching object, then person)
- **Defense Strategy:** Touch DNA found in area of alleged assault may be innocent contact

## Secondary & Tertiary DNA Transfer

- Accused's DNA may be on alleged victim's body from innocent prior contact
- Can be transferred indirectly through mediator (shared clothing, bedding, bathroom)
- Research shows DNA can transfer without direct contact in some cases
- **Defense Advantage:** Innocent DNA presence does not prove assault

## Y-STR Analysis

- Male-specific DNA markers
- Cannot identify individual male; only establishes male genetic contributor
- Useful for screening presence of male DNA
- **Does NOT identify accused specifically**
- **Defense Point:** Y-STR match is presence, not identity

## Degradation & Contamination Risks

- Environmental exposure degrades DNA (heat, moisture, bacterial growth)
- Contamination from handling, equipment, lab
- Storage time and temperature critical
- **Challenge Point:** Kit stored for months/years; degradation compromises reliability

## Lab Accreditation Standards

**Questions to Demand Answers For:**
- Is lab accredited (AABB, ISO/IEC 17025)?
- Analyst certified? How many profiles analyzed?
- Procedures documented and followed?
- **Demand:** Lab records, proficiency testing, QA/QC documentation

## Consensual Intercourse Defense & DNA Considerations

- Accused's DNA presence expected if consensual intercourse occurred
- **DNA alone cannot distinguish consensual from non-consensual contact**
- Alleged victim's consent to sexual contact is dispositive if proven
- **Defense Strategy:** DNA consistency with consensual encounter, not inconsistent with defense
