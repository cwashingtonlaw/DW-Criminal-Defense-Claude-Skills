# Breath & Blood Testing Standards for DWI Defense

## Overview

DWI convictions frequently rest on breath or blood alcohol test results. Every test has technical vulnerabilities. This reference guides systematic audit of test methodology to identify admissibility challenges under La. C.C.P. Art. 215.

---

## BREATH TESTING: Intoxilyzer 9000

### Basic Methodology
- **Principle:** Infrared spectroscopy measures ethanol absorption in breath sample
- **Detection Range:** 0.0 to ~0.40 BAC
- **Two-Sample Protocol:** Two independent samples required; both must be within 0.020 BAC of each other

### Critical Audit Checklist

#### Operator Certification
- [ ] Was operator DWI Detection & SFST certified (24-hour NHTSA course)?
- [ ] Was certification current at time of testing?
- [ ] If refresher training required, was it current (8-hour refresher)?
- [ ] Obtain copy of certification from arresting agency

#### Calibration Records
- [ ] When was Intoxilyzer last calibrated? (Must be within 1 year of test date)
- [ ] Calibration solutions used: 0.08% BAC reference solution (primary), 0.20% (secondary)?
- [ ] Were calibration results within acceptable tolerance (±0.005 BAC)?
- [ ] Is calibration documentation signed by certified technician?
- [ ] Any calibration failures or out-of-tolerance results since last successful calibration?

#### 20-Minute Observation Period
- **Critical Issue:** Suspect must be under continuous observation for 20 minutes immediately before breath sample
- [ ] Was suspect continuously observed for full 20 minutes before test?
- [ ] Any opportunity for suspect to consume food, drink, or tobacco?
- [ ] Any belching, regurgitation, or vomiting during observation period?
- [ ] Any mouth alcohol contamination risk (mouthwash, gum, candy)?
- [ ] Officer's BWC or written notes documenting observation?

#### Duplicate Sample Agreement
- [ ] Were two independent breath samples collected?
- [ ] Did both samples agree within 0.020 BAC? (e.g., 0.120 and 0.125 acceptable; 0.120 and 0.145 NOT acceptable)
- [ ] If samples did NOT agree, was third sample required? (Some jurisdictions)
- [ ] Intoxilyzer printout showing both sample results?

#### Maintenance & Service Records
- [ ] Any documented malfunctions, repairs, or service calls since last calibration?
- [ ] Any recalls or technical bulletins issued for this specific unit?
- [ ] Maintenance logs showing routine cleaning and service?
- [ ] Any known defects or issues with that Intoxilyzer model?

#### Operating Conditions
- [ ] Radio frequency interference (RFI): Was testing location free from cell phones, two-way radios, strong RF sources?
- [ ] Temperature: Was Intoxilyzer operating within acceptable temperature range (typically 15-30°C)?
- [ ] Humidity: Excessive moisture in testing location?
- [ ] Alcohol fumes in booking area (could contaminate ambient air and affect readings)?

### Common Intoxilyzer Vulnerabilities

**Mouth Alcohol Contamination:** Residual alcohol in mouth/throat can be detected as exhaled breath alcohol, artificially inflating BAC. Defenses:
- Recent drinking (within 30 min) raises BAC
- Dentures, caps, bridges with alcohol residue
- Mouthwash or oral hygiene products
- Belching/regurgitation

**RFI (Radio Frequency Interference):** Strong RF signals can cause erroneous readings. Defenses:
- Booking area near dispatch radio
- Cell phone use by officer or suspect
- High-power transmitters nearby
- Documented RFI susceptibility

**Calibration Drift:** Instrument may drift out of calibration between calibration dates. Defenses:
- Time elapsed since last calibration
- Frequency of use (more use = higher drift risk)
- Environmental conditions affecting calibration

**Assumption of Steady State:** Intoxilyzer assumes BAC is stable at test time. Defenses:
- Rising BAC: BAC still rising at test time (see rising-bac-defense.md)
- Absorption phase: Test conducted while suspect still absorbing alcohol

## BLOOD TESTING

### Basic Methodology
- **Principle:** Gas chromatography (GC) analysis measures ethanol concentration in blood serum or plasma
- **Sample:** Typically whole blood or serum
- **Anticoagulant:** Sodium fluoride/potassium oxalate (prevents clotting, inhibits bacterial fermentation)
- **Detection Range:** Highly sensitive, measures down to 0.01% BAC

### Critical Audit Checklist

#### Phlebotomist Qualifications
- [ ] Who drew the blood? (Name, credentials)
- [ ] Is phlebotomist certified? (RN, LPN, certified phlebotomist)
- [ ] Was phlebotomist trained in DWI blood draw procedures?
- [ ] Any prior complaints or disciplinary action against phlebotomist?

#### Blood Draw Protocol
- [ ] Vial type correct: sodium fluoride/potassium oxalate (gray-top tube)?
- [ ] Was vial sealed and unopened before use?
- [ ] Proper volume collected? (typically 5-7 mL in gray-top tube)
- [ ] Blood properly mixed with anticoagulant (inversion 8-10 times)?
- [ ] Skin preparation correct (rubbing alcohol or iodine, allowed to dry before puncture)?
- [ ] Was client's arm cleaned with something other than alcohol? (Isopropyl alcohol residue can contaminate sample)

#### Chain of Custody
- [ ] Unbroken documentation from draw to lab?
- [ ] Vial labeled at time of draw with client's name, date, time?
- [ ] Who handled vial between draw and lab receipt?
- [ ] Sealed vial or tamper-evident packaging?
- [ ] Lab receipt documented with signature, date, time?
- [ ] Any gaps or unexplained time periods?
- [ ] Any signatures missing or illegible?

#### Storage Conditions
- [ ] Temperature maintained: refrigerated (4°C) or frozen?
- [ ] Time from draw to testing (should be prompt; delays increase fermentation risk)?
- [ ] Condition of vial when received by lab (sealed? damaged? leaked?)?
- [ ] Storage location (secure, temperature-controlled)?

#### Fermentation Risk
- **Risk:** Bacterial fermentation of glucose can produce ethanol post-draw, artificially inflating BAC
- [ ] Time between draw and testing: How many days? (Risk increases >5 days without refrigeration)
- [ ] Was sample refrigerated continuously?
- [ ] Was anticoagulant sodium fluoride present? (Prevents/inhibits fermentation)
- [ ] Blood type and glucose level of suspect? (High glucose = higher fermentation risk)

#### Laboratory Analysis
- [ ] Lab accreditation: ASCLD/LAB or equivalent?
- [ ] Analyst qualifications: Training, certifications, prior DWI analyses?
- [ ] Gas chromatography methodology: Headspace GC? Standard protocol?
- [ ] Reference standards used: What BAC control samples tested?
- [ ] Quality assurance: Duplicate analysis of same sample?
- [ ] Calibration and maintenance of GC instrument?

#### Lab Report Review
- [ ] Written lab report provided to defense?
- [ ] Specific GC parameters documented (retention time, peak area)?
- [ ] Reference standards and controls documented?
- [ ] Any notation of unusual results, retests, or problems?
- [ ] Analyst signature and date?
- [ ] Basis for final BAC conclusion?

### Common Blood Testing Vulnerabilities

**Chain of Custody Breaks:** Missing documentation or unexplained storage creates reasonable doubt about sample integrity.

**Fermentation:** Most common defense. Anticoagulant failure, improper storage, or time delay allows bacteria to produce ethanol.

**Contamination:** Alcohol-based skin prep, improper vial, or handling can affect BAC result.

**Lab Methodology Issues:** Non-standard GC protocols, inadequate quality assurance, or analyst training deficiencies can render result inadmissible.

**Retrograde Extrapolation:** Blood BAC at test time ≠ blood BAC at time of driving. Challenge prosecution's calculations (see rising-bac-defense.md).

## URINE TESTING (Rare)

**Not recommended forensically; rarely used in Louisiana DWI cases.**

- Urine alcohol level does NOT reliably correlate to BAC
- Metabolism varies widely; urine lags blood BAC by hours
- Not admissible for DWI BAC calculation in many states
- If encountered, challenge reliability and admissibility

## REFUSAL CASES: Special Considerations

### Implied Consent Advisement (R.S. 32:661)

**Suspect has right to:**
1. Refuse breath test
2. Request independent chemical test at own expense
3. Be informed of license suspension consequences of refusal

**Audit checklist:**
- [ ] Was R.S. 32:661 advisement given verbatim (Louisiana has specific statutory language)?
- [ ] Was advisement given in reasonable time after arrest?
- [ ] Did officer explain consequences of refusal?
- [ ] Was refusal clearly documented in incident report?
- [ ] Was refusal unequivocal (clear "no") or ambiguous?

### Unequivocal Refusal Requirement
- Louisiana requires clear, unambiguous refusal
- Statements like "I'm not sure," "I want to talk to a lawyer," or hesitation may NOT constitute refusal
- Document officer's exact wording of request and suspect's exact response

### Administrative License Suspension (R.S. 32:414)
- Officer must issue notice of intent to suspend within 30 days
- Suspect has right to hearing at OWI office (no longer DPS)
- Scope of hearing is whether refusal occurred, NOT whether DWI was committed

## Expert Witness Retention

**When to retain forensics/toxicology expert:**

1. **Breath test challenged:**
   - Calibration issues
   - Observation period defect
   - RFI concerns
   - Rising BAC defense
   - Expert should have Intoxilyzer 9000 specific knowledge

2. **Blood test challenged:**
   - Chain of custody problems
   - Fermentation risk
   - Lab methodology defects
   - Analyst qualifications
   - Retrograde extrapolation issues
   - Expert should have forensic blood alcohol analysis experience

3. **Retrograde extrapolation:**
   - Challenge prosecution's BAC-at-driving calculation
   - Expert must understand Widmark formula, individual variation, absorption kinetics

**Expert qualifications to verify:**
- Relevant certifications (NAAFS, ASFS, forensic chemistry degrees)
- Publications in peer-reviewed journals
- Prior trial experience in DWI cases
- Specific knowledge of testing methodology being challenged
- No prior convictions or disciplinary action
