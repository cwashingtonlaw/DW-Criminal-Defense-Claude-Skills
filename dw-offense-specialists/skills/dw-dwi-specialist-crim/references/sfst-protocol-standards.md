# Standardized Field Sobriety Test (SFST) Protocol Standards

## Overview

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) developed Standardized Field Sobriety Tests with specific protocols, clues, and validation rates. Deviations from protocol undermine reliability. This reference enables systematic SFST audit.

---

## SFST Validation & Error Rates (Per NHTSA Research)

| Test | Sensitivity | Specificity | Validation Notes |
|------|-------------|-------------|------------------|
| **HGN** | 77% | 91% | Most reliable; detects impairment at ~0.08 BAC |
| **WAT** | 68% | 77% | Affected by environmental factors, instructions |
| **OLS** | 65% | 81% | Most affected by age, weight, medical conditions |

**Key Point:** These validation rates assume PERFECT protocol adherence. Any deviation reduces reliability. Deviations can justify expert testimony challenges and jury instructions on test unreliability.

## TEST 1: Horizontal Gaze Nystagmus (HGN)

### Definition
Nystagmus = involuntary jerking eye movement. HGN occurs at extreme lateral gaze; alcohol impairment causes earlier onset and more pronounced jerking.

### NHTSA Protocol

**Instruction to Suspect:**
"Keep your head still, eyes on [stimulus]. Follow with your eyes only."

**Stimulus Movement:**
- Smooth, slow movement (takes 3-4 seconds per pass)
- Stimulus size approximately 4 inches
- Held at eye level
- Moved from center toward extreme lateral gaze

**Required Passes:** Minimum 3 complete passes per eye (6 passes total)

### Clues (Per NHTSA)
**4 clues per eye assessed; maximum 6 clues total (2 eyes × 3 clues each)**

*Note: Updated NHTSA guidance recognizes 7 clues total (per eye: lack of smooth pursuit, distinct nystagmus at maximum deviation, onset before 45 degrees); this reference uses traditional 6-clue scoring.*

**Clue 1: Lack of Smooth Pursuit**
- Eye movement is not smooth when following stimulus
- Movement is jerky or catches rather than gliding
- Assess each eye separately

**Clue 2: Distinct and Sustained Nystagmus at Maximum Deviation**
- Sustained jerking when eye held at extreme lateral position
- Must be obvious jerking (not borderline)
- Assess each eye separately

**Clue 3: Onset of Nystagmus Before 45 Degrees**
- Nystagmus begins BEFORE eye reaches 45-degree angle from nose
- Normal (non-impaired) eyes don't nystagmus until extreme lateral gaze (45+ degrees)
- Early onset = strong impairment indicator
- Assess each eye separately

### HGN Scoring
- 6 clues (maximum) = strong indicator of impairment (≥0.08 BAC)
- 4 clues = moderate indicator
- 2 clues = weak indicator
- 0 clues = no indication of impairment

### Medical Conditions Causing Nystagmus (Defense Challenges)

- **Neurological:** Cerebellar disorders, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, traumatic brain injury
- **Inner Ear:** Vertigo, labyrinthitis, Meniere's disease, benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)
- **Medications:** Anticonvulsants (phenytoin), sedatives, antidepressants, antihistamines
- **Medical:** Hypoglycemia, stroke, TIA, thyroid disorder
- **Other:** Caffeine jitteriness, prolonged fatigue, contact lens irritation, eye surgery recovery
- **Age:** Natural age-related nystagmus in elderly

### Protocol Deviations That Undermine Reliability

- [ ] Stimulus moved too quickly (jerky instead of smooth)
- [ ] Stimulus too large or held wrong distance from eyes
- [ ] Fewer than 3 complete passes per eye
- [ ] Officer not assessing each eye separately
- [ ] Officer not starting from center (should always return to center between passes)
- [ ] Inadequate lighting (especially night-time stops with flashlight only)
- [ ] Wind, rain, or environmental factors affecting suspect's vision
- [ ] Suspect wearing corrective lenses or contact lenses (not removed as appropriate)
- [ ] Suspect with nystagmus from medical condition not identified

## TEST 2: Walk-and-Turn (WAT)

### Definition
Suspect walks a straight line (heel-to-toe), turns, and returns. Impaired coordination/balance manifests as specific clues.

### NHTSA Protocol

**Instruction Phase:**
- Demonstrate walk (officer walks 9 steps heel-to-toe, turns, returns)
- Instruct suspect: "Walk heel-to-toe along this line (point to line). When you reach the end, turn and return."
- Observe if suspect follows instructions correctly DURING demonstration

**Performance Phase:**
- Suspect walks 9 steps forward
- Turns (specified foot sequence varies; NHTSA recommends turning on one foot)
- Walks 9 steps back to starting point
- Arms at sides (not used for balance)

### Clues (Maximum 8 Clues)

**Clue 1: Can't Balance During Instructions**
- Starts walking before officer finishes instructions
- Doesn't maintain balance while listening/watching demonstration
- Holds arms out excessively
- Shows obvious inability to maintain stance

**Clue 2: Starts Too Soon**
- Begins walking before officer says "Walk"
- Begins before completion of instructions
- Shows impulsivity/inability to wait

**Clue 3: Stops Walking**
- Pauses or hesitates during walk (breaks up walk into segments)
- Must be distinct pause (not just normal walking rhythm)

**Clue 4: Doesn't Touch Heel-to-Toe**
- Gap between heel of one foot and toe of other foot
- Gap >1 inch = clue
- Only counts if gap is on majority of steps

**Clue 5: Steps Off Line**
- Foot placement is off the line (left or right)
- Both feet must track line reasonably well
- Occasional minor deviation acceptable
- Persistent or obvious deviation = clue

**Clue 6: Uses Arms for Balance**
- Raises arms significantly (>6 inches from sides) to balance
- Windmill arm movements
- Obviously using arms to stabilize body

**Clue 7: Wrong Number of Steps**
- Takes fewer than 9 steps or more than 9 steps in either direction
- Count must be 9 steps forward, 9 steps back

**Clue 8: Improper Turn**
- Doesn't follow specified turn instruction
- Loses balance during turn
- Doesn't remain on line during turn
- Spins or staggers during turn

### Environmental Factors Affecting Reliability (Defense Challenges)

- [ ] Road surface: wet, uneven, sloped, gravel, potholed
- [ ] Lighting: inadequate (night with flashlight only), glare
- [ ] Weather: wind, rain, snow affecting balance
- [ ] Footwear: high heels, sandals, heavy boots, new shoes
- [ ] Physical limitations: age (65+), leg injuries, arthritic conditions, back problems, neurological conditions
- [ ] Weight: obese suspects have altered balance
- [ ] Inner ear disorders or vertigo
- [ ] Recent alcohol consumption (absorption phase; BAC still rising)
- [ ] Anxiety or panic disorder (affects coordination)
- [ ] Officer not demonstrating walk clearly or ambiguous instructions

### Protocol Deviations

- [ ] Officer did not demonstrate walk
- [ ] Instructions not clearly given or not understood by suspect
- [ ] Line not clearly visible or marked
- [ ] Suspect wearing inappropriate footwear but officer didn't note (should assess reasonability)
- [ ] Environmental hazards present (wet pavement, gravel, uneven surface)
- [ ] Officer didn't allow suspect to remove shoes if requested
- [ ] Officer failed to assess each specific clue systematically

## TEST 3: One Leg Stand (OLS)

### Definition
Suspect balances on one leg (off ground ~6 inches) for 30 seconds. Impaired balance manifests as specific clues.

### NHTSA Protocol

**Instruction Phase:**
- Demonstrate: "Stand on your left leg and raise your right leg about 6 inches off the ground. Keep both arms at your sides. Count aloud: 1-1001, 1-1002, etc. (or count: one-thousand-one, one-thousand-two). Do not look down. Do you understand?"

**Performance Phase:**
- Suspect performs test for 30 seconds
- Officer observes for clues

### Clues (Maximum 4 Clues)

**Clue 1: Sways While Balancing**
- Body sways side-to-side or forward-backward while standing on one leg
- Swaying must be noticeable and not just minor micro-movements
- Obvious loss of balance axis

**Clue 2: Uses Arms for Balance**
- Raises arms significantly (>6 inches from sides) to maintain balance
- Windmill arm movements
- Obviously using arms to stabilize body

**Clue 3: Hopping**
- Bounces up and down on standing foot
- Cannot maintain stationary position on one leg

**Clue 4: Puts Foot Down**
- Puts raised foot back on ground before 30 seconds elapsed
- Indicates loss of balance/inability to maintain position

### Medical Conditions & Factors Affecting Reliability (Defense Challenges)

- [ ] Age: 65+ years (natural balance degradation)
- [ ] Weight: overweight/obese suspects struggle with single-leg balance
- [ ] Physical limitations: leg injury, arthritis, back pain, knee problems, foot injury
- [ ] Inner ear disorder: vertigo, BPPV, labyrinthitis
- [ ] Neurological conditions: neuropathy, cerebellar disorder, Parkinson's disease
- [ ] Medical conditions: diabetes, low blood sugar, seizure disorder, cardiovascular disease
- [ ] Medications: anticonvulsants, sedatives, antidepressants (balance side effects)
- [ ] Recent alcohol consumption: BAC still rising (absorption phase)
- [ ] Anxiety/panic disorder
- [ ] Poor footwear (high heels, slippery soles, new shoes)
- [ ] Uneven or sloped surface
- [ ] Wind or environmental conditions

### Protocol Deviations

- [ ] Officer did not demonstrate OLS
- [ ] Instructions unclear or suspect didn't understand
- [ ] Surface uneven or sloped (should be relatively level)
- [ ] Suspect wearing inappropriate footwear without notation
- [ ] Officer did not observe full 30-second period
- [ ] Officer standing too close or too far (could affect suspect's balance)
- [ ] Environmental distractions (traffic, noise, weather)
- [ ] Test performed on incline or uneven pavement

## Non-Standardized Tests: UNRELIABLE (Challenge Aggressively)

**These tests have NO NHTSA validation and NO established error rates:**
- Finger-to-nose test
- Alphabet recitation (forward or backward)
- Counting backward (like 100-99-98)
- Romberg balance test
- Modified position of attention test

**Defense argument:** These tests lack scientific validation. NHTSA does not endorse them. Officer may have misapplied SFST clues to these non-standardized tests. Expert testimony available to testify these tests are unreliable.

## SFST Trainer Certification Requirements

### Initial NHTSA DWI Detection & SFST Course
- **Duration:** 24 hours (3-4 day course)
- **Content:** DWI detection techniques, physiology of impairment, SFST administration protocols, clue recognition, scoring, documentation
- **Certification:** Awarded upon completion and passing written/practical exam
- **Valid for:** Typically 2-3 years (varies by jurisdiction)

### Refresher Training
- **Requirement:** Periodic refresher courses (8 hours typically)
- **Frequency:** Required every 2-3 years to maintain certification
- **Check:** Verify officer's training documentation is current

### Audit Checklist

- [ ] Officer attended 24-hour NHTSA DWI Detection & SFST initial course
- [ ] Course completion certificate dated and signed
- [ ] Refresher training current (if older than 2-3 years, should have refresher)
- [ ] Officer name and certification number on certificate
- [ ] Instructor name and credentials on certificate
- [ ] Request officer's training file from agency

## Body Cam / Dash Cam Video Comparison

**Critical defense tool:** Compare BWC/dash cam footage of SFST to officer's written account.

### Common Discrepancies to Identify

- [ ] Officer claims 6 HGN clues; video shows fewer or ambiguous nystagmus
- [ ] Officer reports 4+ WAT clues; video shows acceptable heel-to-toe contact and line tracking
- [ ] Officer claims improper turn; video shows reasonable turn executed
- [ ] Officer reports "sways"; video shows minor weight shift, not impaired sway
- [ ] Environmental factors visible in video that officer didn't note (rain, gravel, uneven surface, inadequate lighting)
- [ ] Officer's instructions inaudible or unclear on video
- [ ] Suspect's balance appears reasonable on video; officer's written account exaggerates impairment
- [ ] Lighting insufficient for accurate SFST assessment (officer used flashlight only at night)

### Video Absence or Quality Issues

- [ ] No BWC footage recorded (equipment malfunction, not activated, deleted)
- [ ] No clear view of suspect's performance (officer's body obscures view, camera angle poor)
- [ ] Video timestamp differs from incident report time
- [ ] Gaps in video (equipment cycling off, battery failure)

**Argument:** Absence of video or poor quality combined with exaggerated clues on police report raises credibility concerns. Jury instruction may be warranted on weight of officer testimony without corroborating video.

## Expert Witness Testimony on SFST

**When to retain SFST expert:**
1. Multiple protocol deviations documented
2. Medical condition explaining clues (physician expert on inner ear disorder, neurological condition)
3. Environmental factors significantly affecting test validity
4. Officer not NHTSA trained or training expired
5. Excessive number of clues inconsistent with BAC result (rising BAC defense)
6. Video contradicts officer's written account

**Expert qualifications:**
- DWI Detection & SFST certification (NHTSA course)
- Law enforcement or research background with SFST
- Published research on SFST reliability/limitations
- Prior expert testimony in DWI cases
- No criminal convictions
