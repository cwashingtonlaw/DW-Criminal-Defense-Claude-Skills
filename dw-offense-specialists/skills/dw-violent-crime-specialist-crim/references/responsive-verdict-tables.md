# Responsive Verdict Tables — Louisiana Violent Offenses

**ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT — PRIVILEGED & CONFIDENTIAL — PREPARED IN ANTICIPATION OF LITIGATION**

This reference compiles the responsive verdicts available under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 814 for the homicide, battery, kidnapping, robbery, and burglary offenses covered by `dw-violent-crime-specialist-crim`. Responsive verdicts are the lesser-included offenses the trial judge MUST list on the verdict form when a covered charge goes to the jury, unless the State and defense affirmatively agree otherwise on the record.

Use this table:
- During Module G (heat-of-passion conversion) to map every available off-ramp from a murder count.
- During pretrial motion drafting to plan jury-charge conferences and confirm the verdict form will reflect the strongest non-conviction outcome short of acquittal.
- During trial-day strategy to confirm the responsive verdict the defense will argue in closing.

The Article 814 list is mandatory; the trial court may not subtract responsive verdicts unilaterally. Per La. C.Cr.P. Art. 814(C), the State or the defense may move to exclude a responsive verdict that is not supported by the evidence, but the moving party carries the burden. Defense counsel should resist any State motion to strip manslaughter, negligent homicide, or aggravated battery off the form whenever the evidence even arguably supports the lesser verdict.

---

## 1. First Degree Murder — La. R.S. 14:30 (Art. 814(A)(1))

Responsive verdicts in descending order of severity:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of second degree murder (La. R.S. 14:30.1).
3. Guilty of manslaughter (La. R.S. 14:31).
4. Guilty of negligent homicide (La. R.S. 14:32).
5. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- Manslaughter is on the form by operation of statute. The State cannot block it absent a complete absence of "sudden passion or heat of blood" evidence — and that evidence may come from the State's own witnesses (911 call, prior altercation, scene statements). Defense should argue manslaughter survives even on a "thin" record.
- Negligent homicide is the floor on accidental-discharge and unintended-result theories. Pair with self-defense (Module D) when the defendant claims justified force that exceeded what was necessary.
- Death-eligibility (Module H) makes the responsive verdict map even more important: every step down the ladder removes the capital exposure.

---

## 2. Second Degree Murder — La. R.S. 14:30.1 (Art. 814(A)(2))

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of manslaughter (La. R.S. 14:31).
3. Guilty of negligent homicide (La. R.S. 14:32).
4. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- The mandatory life-without-parole sentence on La. R.S. 14:30.1 means manslaughter is the single most consequential responsive verdict in Louisiana criminal practice — a guilty-of-manslaughter return cuts maximum exposure from LWOP to 40 years (post-2017 amendment).
- For juvenile defendants, *Miller v. Alabama*, 567 U.S. 460 (2012) and *Montgomery v. Louisiana*, 577 U.S. 190 (2016) sentencing issues attach at the second degree murder verdict; the manslaughter responsive verdict avoids the *Miller* hearing entirely.
- Felony-murder cases (La. R.S. 14:30.1(A)(2)) generally have weaker sudden-passion evidence; defense may need to lean on negligent homicide or full acquittal instead.

---

## 3. Manslaughter — La. R.S. 14:31 (Art. 814(A)(3))

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of negligent homicide (La. R.S. 14:32).
3. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- When manslaughter is the top count (rare; usually it is a responsive verdict from murder), negligent homicide remains available as a step-down.
- Aggravated battery is NOT a responsive verdict to manslaughter under Art. 814 — even though both involve violence, the legislature kept the homicide ladder distinct.

---

## 4. Negligent Homicide — La. R.S. 14:32 (Art. 814(A)(4))

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- No further step-down. This is the homicide floor. If the State will not concede insufficient evidence on negligent homicide, the binary verdict makes acquittal-or-conviction the live question for the jury.

---

## 5. Aggravated Battery — La. R.S. 14:34 (Art. 814(A)(13))

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of second degree battery (La. R.S. 14:34.1).
3. Guilty of simple battery (La. R.S. 14:35).
4. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- Second degree battery is on the form by statute. It carries a lower exposure (up to 8 years vs. 10) and removes the "dangerous weapon" element — useful when the weapon characterization is contested (object vs. fist, foot vs. shod foot, etc.).
- Simple battery as a responsive verdict opens a misdemeanor exit from a felony charge. Insist on it whenever the injury evidence is ambiguous.

## 7. Aggravated Assault — La. R.S. 14:37 (Art. 814 — assault series)

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of simple assault (La. R.S. 14:38).
3. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- Aggravated assault frequently stacks alongside aggravated battery and aggravated kidnapping counts arising from the same incident; defense should track each verdict form separately to avoid an inadvertent compromise verdict that combines the worst elements of multiple counts.

---

## 8. Aggravated Kidnapping — La. R.S. 14:44 (Art. 814(A)(19))

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of second degree kidnapping (La. R.S. 14:44.1).
3. Guilty of simple kidnapping (La. R.S. 14:45).
4. Guilty of false imprisonment (La. R.S. 14:46).
5. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- Aggravated kidnapping carries mandatory life without parole. Every step down the ladder eliminates the LWOP exposure — and second degree kidnapping (40 years max, with at least 2 years without benefit) is a substantial reduction.
- False imprisonment is a misdemeanor — a major exit ramp when the "ransom or thing of value" element of aggravated kidnapping is weak.

1. Guilty.

3. Guilty of false imprisonment (La. R.S. 14:46).
4. Not guilty.

## 10. Aggravated Burglary — La. R.S. 14:60 (Art. 814(A)(42))

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of simple burglary (La. R.S. 14:62).
3. Guilty of unauthorized entry of an inhabited dwelling (La. R.S. 14:62.3).
4. Guilty of criminal trespass (La. R.S. 14:63).
5. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- The "armed with a dangerous weapon," "after entering arms himself with a dangerous weapon," and "commits a battery" prongs of La. R.S. 14:60 each independently aggravate the offense. If the State proves entry but cannot prove any of the three prongs, simple burglary becomes the realistic verdict.
- Unauthorized entry of an inhabited dwelling is a powerful step-down when intent-to-commit-a-felony is the contested element.

---

## 11. Home Invasion — La. R.S. 14:62.8

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of unauthorized entry of an inhabited dwelling (La. R.S. 14:62.3).
3. Guilty of criminal trespass (La. R.S. 14:63).
4. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- Home invasion is a relative latecomer to Art. 814; if the Article 814 list does not yet expressly enumerate it, defense should brief responsive verdicts under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 815 (general lesser-included rule) and confirm with the trial court at the charge conference. Mark `[VERIFY CITATION]` when relying on the exact Article 814 line item.

## 12. Armed Robbery — La. R.S. 14:64 (Art. 814(A)(22))

Responsive verdicts:

1. Guilty.
2. Guilty of first degree robbery (La. R.S. 14:64.1).
3. Guilty of simple robbery (La. R.S. 14:65).
4. Guilty of attempted armed robbery.
5. Guilty of attempted first degree robbery.
6. Guilty of attempted simple robbery.
7. Not guilty.

Strategic notes:
- Armed robbery carries 10–99 years without benefit. First degree robbery (3–40 years without benefit) and simple robbery (up to 7 years) are the principal off-ramps. The "armed with a dangerous weapon" element is always the hinge — if the weapon was not displayed or was simulated, first degree robbery (which only requires the victim's "reasonable belief" the offender was armed) is the correct landing point.
- La. R.S. 14:64.3 firearm enhancement adds a consecutive 5 years if the dangerous weapon was a firearm — but only if the jury convicts on the underlying armed robbery. A first-degree-robbery verdict eliminates the 14:64.3 add-on entirely.
