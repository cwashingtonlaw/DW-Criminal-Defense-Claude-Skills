# Sentencing Exposure Matrix — Louisiana Violent Offenses

**ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT — PRIVILEGED & CONFIDENTIAL — PREPARED IN ANTICIPATION OF LITIGATION**

This matrix lays out the statutory exposure for every offense covered by `dw-violent-crime-specialist-crim`, including the mandatory minimum, the statutory maximum, parole/probation eligibility, the firearm enhancement under La. R.S. 14:64.3 where applicable, and the habitual-offender multiplier under La. R.S. 15:529.1. Use this table for plea-versus-trial modeling, pretrial sentencing memoranda, and mitigation strategy.

The numbers below reflect the statutes as written. Always verify the current text against Westlaw or the Louisiana Legislature's site before filing — the legislature has revised the homicide and habitual-offender statutes repeatedly in recent sessions. Mark `[VERIFY CITATION]` where any number drifts from current text and refresh before sentencing.

## 1. Homicide Tier

### La. R.S. 14:30 — First Degree Murder

- **Mandatory minimum:** life imprisonment at hard labor without benefit of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence (LWOP).
- **Statutory maximum:** death (with capital-sentencing procedures under La. C.Cr.P. Arts. 905 et seq.).
- **Death-eligibility triggers:** the enumerated aggravating circumstances under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 905.4 (e.g., killing during enumerated felony, killing of a peace officer, killing of more than one person, killing of a victim under 12).
- **Parole/probation:** none on LWOP; not applicable on death.
- **Habitual offender:** not applicable in any practical sense — the underlying sentence is already maximum.
- **Juvenile-defendant note:** *Miller v. Alabama*, 567 U.S. 460 (2012), and *Montgomery v. Louisiana*, 577 U.S. 190 (2016), require an individualized hearing before mandatory LWOP can attach to a juvenile offender. La. C.Cr.P. Art. 878.1 implements that procedure for Louisiana. Capital sentencing of juveniles is barred by *Roper v. Simmons*, 543 U.S. 551 (2005).

### La. R.S. 14:30.1 — Second Degree Murder

- **Mandatory minimum:** life imprisonment at hard labor without benefit of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence (LWOP).
- **Statutory maximum:** same — LWOP is the only sentence.
- **Parole/probation:** none.
- **Habitual offender:** the LWOP sentence is unaffected by 15:529.1 enhancement in operation.
- **Juvenile-defendant note:** *Miller / Montgomery* hearings under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 878.1 are mandatory; absent a 878.1 finding of permanent incorrigibility, the sentence is parole-eligible after 25 years (La. R.S. 15:574.4(E) — verify current text). This is the single most important issue to preserve in any juvenile second-degree-murder case.

### La. R.S. 14:31 — Manslaughter

- **Mandatory minimum:** none (zero floor unless habitual or specific-victim enhancement attaches).
- **Statutory maximum:** 40 years at hard labor.
- **Parole:** eligible (subject to general La. R.S. 15:574.4 rules and any specific-victim enhancements).
- **Probation/suspension:** generally available under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 893, subject to court discretion.
- **Specific-victim enhancement:** under La. R.S. 14:31(B), if the victim was killed as a result of receiving a battery and was under the age of 10, the minimum becomes 10 years without benefit. Verify current statutory text.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers (see § 5 below). A second-felony habitual on a 40-year max yields a 13.33–80 year range; a third-felony (with one violent predicate) yields life or up to twice the longest possible sentence — verify current statutory text.

### La. R.S. 14:32 — Negligent Homicide

- **Mandatory minimum:** none.
- **Statutory maximum:** 5 years at hard labor (basic), with enhancements where the victim was under 10 years old or where the killing was caused by a dog under the offender's control.
- **Parole/probation:** eligible.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers; the floor is generally not the binding constraint.

## 2. Battery / Assault Tier

### La. R.S. 14:34 — Aggravated Battery

- **Mandatory minimum:** none (zero floor; fine alone is permissible at the low end).
- **Statutory maximum:** 10 years at hard labor and/or fine up to $5,000.
- **Parole/probation:** eligible.
- **Firearm enhancement:** La. R.S. 14:34 has its own firearm provisions in subsections (B)/(C); confirm current text. The general La. R.S. 14:64.3 firearm enhancement does NOT apply (14:64.3 is robbery-specific).
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers.

### La. R.S. 14:34.1 — Second Degree Battery

- **Mandatory minimum:** none.
- **Statutory maximum:** 8 years at hard labor and/or fine up to $2,000.
- **Parole/probation:** eligible.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers.

### La. R.S. 14:34.5 — Battery on a Police Officer

- **Mandatory minimum:** the statute carries enhanced minimums when the battery produces injury, when the offender uses a firearm or dangerous weapon, or where the victim was a peace officer engaged in official duties. Verify current statutory text — the legislature has revised this section multiple times.
- **Statutory maximum:** varies by subsection (typically up to 5 years for the basic offense; higher with enhancements).
- **Parole/probation:** restricted under several subsections (verify whether specific subsection bars parole/probation).
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers.

### La. R.S. 14:37 — Aggravated Assault

- **Mandatory minimum:** none.
- **Statutory maximum:** 6 months in parish jail and/or $1,000 fine (basic offense — misdemeanor); enhanced versions involving dangerous weapons or firearms can be felonies. Verify current statutory text — the firearm-aggravated assault subsection (14:37.4) is its own felony with up to 10 years.
- **Parole/probation:** eligible.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers on felony grade only.

## 3. Kidnapping Tier

### La. R.S. 14:44 — Aggravated Kidnapping

- **Mandatory minimum:** life imprisonment at hard labor without benefit of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence (LWOP).
- **Statutory maximum:** LWOP (capital eligibility eliminated for non-homicide offenses by *Kennedy v. Louisiana*, 554 U.S. 407 (2008) — confirm current statutory text post-*Kennedy* amendments).
- **Parole/probation:** none.
- **Habitual offender:** not in practical operation.

### La. R.S. 14:44.1 — Second Degree Kidnapping

- **Mandatory minimum:** 5 years at hard labor (with at least 2 years to be served without benefit of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence). Verify current statutory text.
- **Statutory maximum:** 40 years at hard labor.
- **Parole:** eligible after the without-benefit period expires.
- **Probation/suspension:** unavailable on the without-benefit portion.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers.

## 4. Burglary / Home Invasion / Robbery Tier

### La. R.S. 14:60 — Aggravated Burglary

- **Mandatory minimum:** 1 year at hard labor.
- **Statutory maximum:** 30 years at hard labor.
- **Parole/probation:** eligible.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers; 14:60 is on the violent-felony list (La. R.S. 14:2(B)) and triggers the violent-predicate cascade in 15:529.1.

### La. R.S. 14:62.8 — Home Invasion

- **Mandatory minimum:** 5 years at hard labor (verify current text — basic).
- **Statutory maximum:** 25 years at hard labor (basic); higher with enumerated aggravators (presence of children under 12, presence of disabled person, etc.).
- **Parole:** restricted on the without-benefit portion under enumerated aggravators.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers.

### La. R.S. 14:64 — Armed Robbery

- **Mandatory minimum:** 10 years at hard labor without benefit of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence.
- **Statutory maximum:** 99 years at hard labor without benefit.
- **Parole/probation:** none on the entire sentence — the entire 10-to-99-year range is without-benefit.
- **Firearm enhancement:** La. R.S. 14:64.3 — if the dangerous weapon was a FIREARM, the court SHALL impose an additional 5 years at hard labor without benefit of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence, to be served CONSECUTIVELY to the underlying armed-robbery sentence.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers; armed robbery is on the violent-felony list.

### La. R.S. 14:64.1 — First Degree Robbery

- **Mandatory minimum:** 3 years at hard labor without benefit of parole, probation, or suspension of sentence.
- **Statutory maximum:** 40 years at hard labor without benefit.
- **Parole/probation:** none on the entire sentence.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers; on the violent-felony list.
- **Firearm enhancement:** La. R.S. 14:64.3 by its terms attaches to "armed robbery" (14:64), not first degree robbery — confirm whether the State has tried to extend it. Defense should object to any 14:64.3 enhancement on a 14:64.1 conviction.

### La. R.S. 14:64.3 — Armed Robbery; Firearm Enhancement

- **Operation:** consecutive 5 years at hard labor without benefit, ON TOP OF the underlying La. R.S. 14:64 armed-robbery sentence.
- **Trigger:** the dangerous weapon used in the armed robbery was a firearm.
- **Mandatory minimum effect:** raises the practical floor from 10 years (armed robbery) to 15 years.
- **Defense angles:** challenge whether the weapon was a firearm at all (toy gun, simulated weapon → drops the case to first degree robbery instead of armed robbery, eliminating 14:64.3 entirely); challenge whether the weapon was operable; argue conversion to first degree robbery as a responsive verdict.

### La. R.S. 14:65 — Simple Robbery

- **Mandatory minimum:** none.
- **Statutory maximum:** 7 years at hard labor and/or fine up to $3,000.
- **Parole/probation:** eligible.
- **Habitual offender:** subject to multipliers; on the violent-felony list.

## 5. Habitual Offender Multipliers (La. R.S. 15:529.1)

The habitual-offender statute is a separate sentencing enhancement that the State must charge by bill of information, prove at a hearing, and elect into the sentence. It does NOT change the underlying conviction; it changes the sentence imposed. Always route to `dw-habitual-offender-auditor-crim` for predicate-conviction validity (Boykin colloquy, 10-year cleansing, prior-conviction proof).

Operative multipliers (verify current statutory text — the legislature has narrowed and re-expanded these provisions multiple times in the last decade):

- **Second-felony offender:** sentence is not less than one-third of the longest possible sentence and not more than twice the longest possible sentence prescribed for the new offense.
- **Third-felony offender:** sentence is not less than two-thirds of the longest possible sentence and not more than twice the longest possible sentence; if the new offense and any of the prior felonies are on the violent-felony list (La. R.S. 14:2(B)) or are sex offenses, the minimum becomes life without benefit.
- **Fourth-felony-or-more offender:** life imprisonment without benefit if any prior or the present offense is a violent felony, sex offense, or drug offense punishable by 10+ years; otherwise the standard multiplier applies.

Cleansing period: La. R.S. 15:529.1(C) provides a window (verify current length — has changed between 5, 7, and 10 years across recent sessions) within which a prior conviction must fall to count as a predicate. The window runs from the completion of the prior sentence to the commission of the new offense.

For every violent-crime case, build the habitual-exposure spreadsheet:
| Predicate | Statute | Date of conviction | Date sentence completed | On 14:2(B) list? | Within cleansing? | Predicate quality (Boykin, etc.) |

Route to `dw-habitual-offender-auditor-crim` to perform the audit and to `dw-sentencing-mitigation-specialist-crim` to model the post-enhancement exposure for plea negotiation.

## 6. Juvenile-Specific Sentencing (Miller / Montgomery)

For any defendant who was under 18 at the time of the offense and is charged with first or second degree murder:

- *Miller v. Alabama*, 567 U.S. 460 (2012): mandatory LWOP for juvenile homicide offenders violates the Eighth Amendment. An individualized sentencing hearing is required.
- *Montgomery v. Louisiana*, 577 U.S. 190 (2016): *Miller* applies retroactively on collateral review.
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 878.1 implements the *Miller* hearing in Louisiana. Without a Art. 878.1 finding of permanent incorrigibility, the juvenile must be parole-eligible.
- La. R.S. 15:574.4 (verify current subsection) provides the parole eligibility framework — generally 25 years served before parole consideration for juvenile second-degree-murder convictions.

For non-homicide juvenile cases, *Graham v. Florida*, 560 U.S. 48 (2010), bars LWOP — relevant to aggravated kidnapping and (by extension) any other offense currently carrying LWOP for a juvenile.

Route to `dw-sentencing-mitigation-specialist-crim` for mitigation packet, neuropsych evaluation referrals, and *Miller* hearing preparation.

## 7. Strickland-Adjacent Plea Counseling Risk

When a defendant is choosing between a manslaughter plea (40-year max, parole eligible) and going to trial on a second-degree-murder charge (mandatory LWOP), the disparity is so large that ineffective-assistance-of-counsel risk is real. *Strickland v. Washington*, 466 U.S. 668 (1984), and its plea-stage progeny require counsel to:

- Accurately advise the client of the maximum and likely sentence on each option.
- Explain parole eligibility in concrete terms (what date, what likelihood).
- Document the advice and the client's decision in writing.
- Run habitual-offender and firearm-enhancement scenarios before the plea.

Maintain a written plea-advice memo in the file, dated and signed by counsel, recording the sentencing exposure analysis you delivered to the client.

## Module H Operational Guidance — Death-Eligibility, Mandatory LWOP, Juvenile LWOP, Firearm Enhancement (moved verbatim from SKILL.md)

Read by SKILL.md Module H. This block was relocated from the skill body without modification.

### H.1 — Death-eligibility (La. R.S. 14:30)

If the State has filed (or signaled) a notice of intent to seek the death penalty under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 905.2.1, the case enters capital posture:
- Voir dire is *Witherspoon / Witt*-qualified.
- Mitigation investigation begins immediately — route to `dw-sentencing-mitigation-specialist-crim`.
- Independent capital-qualified counsel rules apply (Louisiana Indigent Defender Board capital standards).
- Aggravating-circumstance theory must be matched element-by-element against La. C.Cr.P. Art. 905.4.
- Plea negotiations must include LWOP-vs.-death modeling.

### H.2 — Mandatory LWOP (La. R.S. 14:30.1; 14:44; non-capital 14:30)

LWOP cases compress all of the trial-defense effort into the verdict-form architecture. There is no sentencing mitigation that softens the LWOP outcome — the only way out is acquittal, manslaughter conversion, or charge reduction by plea. Route to `dw-plea-negotiation-analyzer-crim` for plea modeling.

### H.3 — Juvenile LWOP (*Miller v. Alabama* / *Montgomery v. Louisiana*)

For any defendant under 18 at the time of the offense:
- *Miller v. Alabama*, 567 U.S. 460 (2012) — mandatory LWOP for juvenile homicide offenders is unconstitutional. Individualized hearing required.
- *Montgomery v. Louisiana*, 577 U.S. 190 (2016) — *Miller* is retroactive on collateral review. Confirms a substantive Eighth Amendment rule.
- La. C.Cr.P. Art. 878.1 — Louisiana's *Miller* hearing procedure. Without an Art. 878.1 finding of permanent incorrigibility, the juvenile must be parole-eligible (typically after 25 years of service per La. R.S. 15:574.4).
- *Roper v. Simmons*, 543 U.S. 551 (2005) — bars death penalty for juvenile offenders.
- *Graham v. Florida*, 560 U.S. 48 (2010) — bars LWOP for non-homicide juvenile offenders (relevant to 14:44 aggravated kidnapping if applied to a juvenile).

Mitigation investigation for the Art. 878.1 hearing requires neuropsychological evaluation, school records, family-history mitigation, brain-development science. Route to `dw-sentencing-mitigation-specialist-crim`.

### H.4 — Firearm enhancement on armed robbery (La. R.S. 14:64.3)

5 years consecutive, without benefit, on top of the 10-99 year armed-robbery sentence. Triggers when the dangerous weapon was a firearm. Defense angles:
- Push for 14:64.1 first-degree-robbery responsive verdict (eliminates 14:64.3 entirely).
- Challenge the "firearm" characterization (replica? inoperable? simulated?).
- Verify the State actually charged 14:64.3 — the enhancement must be charged and proved.

### H.5 — Habitual offender (La. R.S. 15:529.1)

See Module I and `references/sentencing-exposure-matrix.md` § 5. Build the predicate audit early — habitual exposure can transform a 7-year simple-robbery floor into a life-without-benefit ceiling.

## Module I — Habitual Offender Exposure Analysis (moved verbatim from SKILL.md)

Read by SKILL.md Module I. This block was relocated from the skill body without modification.

For every defendant with a prior felony record, build the habitual-exposure spreadsheet:

| # | Predicate offense | Statute | Date of conviction | Date sentence completed | On 14:2(B) violent list? | Within cleansing period? | Boykin colloquy verified? | Predicate quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Workflow:
1. Pull all certified conviction documents (route to `dw-habitual-offender-auditor-crim` for the audit).
2. Verify each predicate is actually a felony of conviction (not deferred adjudication; not expunged; not vacated).
3. Verify each predicate's Boykin colloquy was constitutional (right to counsel, right to jury trial, right against self-incrimination — defendant must have been advised and waived knowingly and voluntarily).
4. Verify cleansing period (La. R.S. 15:529.1(C)) — confirm current length, which has changed across recent sessions.
5. Determine which (if any) predicates are on the violent-felony list (La. R.S. 14:2(B)) — drives the cascade to mandatory life under third- or fourth-felony enhancement.
6. Model the post-enhancement exposure for each scenario (no bill, second-felony bill, third-felony bill with violent predicate, etc.).
7. Feed the model into `dw-plea-negotiation-analyzer-crim` for plea-versus-trial decision.

Defense priorities:
- **Attack each predicate's validity** (Boykin, expungement, identity).
- **Negotiate a no-bill** as part of plea structure where possible.
- **Object to the enhancement charge** if the prosecution missed a procedural step (timely filing of the bill, proper service, hearing requirements under La. R.S. 15:529.1(D)).
- **Document the constitutional minimum** — under *State v. Dorthey*, an enhancement that is constitutionally excessive (grossly disproportionate) can be reduced. Build the *Dorthey* record at sentencing.
