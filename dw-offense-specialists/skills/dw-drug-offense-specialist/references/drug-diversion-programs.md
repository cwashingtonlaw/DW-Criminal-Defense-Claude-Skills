# Drug Diversion Programs Reference
## Louisiana Art. 893, Art. 890, Drug Court, and Pre-Trial Diversion

---

## CRITICAL: ASSESS DIVERSION ELIGIBILITY IMMEDIATELY

Before committing to trial strategy, ALWAYS evaluate whether defendant qualifies for diversionary relief. These programs can completely change the case trajectory and provide exit ramps from prosecution.

---

## ARTICLE 893 FIRST OFFENDER DIVERSION

### Statutory Basis

**La. C.Cr.P. Article 893** — "Deferral of sentence; first offenders"

"If the defendant pleads guilty or nolo contendere to a felony, other than a crime of violence, and the court finds that this is the first felony conviction of the defendant, the court may, with the consent of the district attorney, defer imposition of the sentence."

### Eligibility Criteria (STRICT, NON-WAIVABLE)

Defendant qualifies for Art. 893 diversion ONLY if ALL criteria are met:

1. **First FELONY drug offense** (prior misdemeanor drug convictions OK; prior felony ANY KIND = disqualifying)
2. **No prior felony convictions** of ANY type (violent crime, property crime, drug, etc.)
3. **Charged offense is a felony** (not applicable to misdemeanor drug charges)
4. **Charged offense is NOT a crime of violence** (most drug offenses qualify; possession/PWID OK; manufacturing might be questioned)
5. **Prosecutor consent** (DA must agree, but cannot arbitrarily refuse; judge has discretion once criteria met)
6. **Judge consent** (Judge must agree; once defendant meets criteria and DA consents, judge retains limited discretion)

### Red Flags That Disqualify

- [ ] Defendant has ANY prior felony conviction (even non-drug)
- [ ] Defendant charged with violent crime (trafficking charge w/ gun enhancement, distribution + aggravated assault, etc.)
- [ ] Defendant has prior felony drug conviction (this is SECOND offender, not first)

### Procedure

**Step 1: Assess eligibility** (your job)

**Step 2: Propose to DA** — Early in case, propose Art. 893 to district attorney
- "Our client qualifies for Article 893 diversion. First felony drug offense, no prior felonies. We request you consent to deferred adjudication."
- Some DAs are favorable (especially for substance abuse cases); others are restrictive

**Step 3: DA response**
- If DA consents: Move to plea agreement incorporating Art. 893
- If DA refuses: Difficult to force, but defendant retains right to request at sentencing phase

**Step 4: Plea entry**
- Defendant pleads guilty or nolo contendere to felony drug charge
- Court delays adjudication (finding of guilt is deferred)
- Defendant sentenced to probation instead of prison

### Probation Terms

**Length:** 2-5 years (typically 3 years)

**Conditions (typical):**
- Attend substance abuse treatment (outpatient or inpatient, depending on severity)
- Mandatory drug testing (frequency: monthly, weekly, or random)
- Supervised probation (report to probation officer: monthly or more frequent)
- No arrests or new criminal charges
- Maintain employment or education
- No association with known drug users/dealers (common condition)
- Community service hours (often 50-200 hours)
- Probation fees (monthly fee to probation department)
- Restitution (if victim, though rare in drug possession cases)

### Successful Completion: THE GOLDEN OUTCOME

**If defendant successfully completes probation:**
- Conviction is SET ASIDE (dismissed, annulled, or expunged per jurisdiction)
- Defendant may legally answer "NO" to "Have you been convicted of a felony?" on job applications
- Record is sealed or expunged (depending on jurisdiction and offense)
- No civil disabilities from conviction (restored voting rights, occupational licenses, etc.)

**This is DRAMATICALLY better than conviction + incarceration.** Ex-offenders face lifetime employment, housing, education barriers. Art. 893 wipes the slate clean.

### Non-Completion: Risk

If defendant violates probation (new arrest, failed drug test, missed treatment):
- Court can revoke probation
- Sentence is imposed (defendant goes to prison for original felony term)
- Loss of Art. 893 benefit (no more diversion available)

**Defendant must understand:** Probation is probationary period to demonstrate rehabilitation. One serious violation, and diversion is lost.

### CRITICAL STRATEGIC POINT

**Art. 893 is a game-changer for first-time drug offenders.** If defendant qualifies:
- Strongly negotiate with DA for consent (offer guilty plea in exchange)
- Emphasize defendant's rehabilitation potential, lack of criminal history, substance abuse treatment readiness
- If DA refuses, preserve issue for sentencing (judge may still order Art. 893 at sentencing)
- Cost-benefit analysis: Accepting guilty plea with Art. 893 probation >> trial risk of conviction + prison term

## ARTICLE 890 SUSPENSION/DEFERRAL OF SENTENCE

### Statutory Basis

**La. C.Cr.P. Article 890** — "Suspension of sentence"

Similar to Art. 893, but available to defendants with prior convictions.

### Key Differences from Art. 893

| Feature | Art. 893 | Art. 890 |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Prior felony convictions? | NO (disqualifying) | YES (allowed) |
| Conviction set aside? | YES (if completed) | NO (conviction remains) |
| Probation supervision? | YES | YES |
| Employment/licensing impact? | Set aside = clean slate | Conviction remains (harder for employment) |
| When available? | First felony drug only | Any time (judge discretion) |

### When to Use Art. 890

If defendant has prior felony conviction (disqualifying for Art. 893), Art. 890 may still be available.

**Example:** Second drug felony. Defendant ineligible for Art. 893. But judge may still suspend sentence and place on probation under Art. 890.

**Advantage:** Avoids incarceration in favor of probation/treatment.

**Disadvantage:** Conviction remains on record (not set aside).

### Eligibility and Procedure

Less strict than Art. 893. Judge has discretion.

- Plea to felony charge
- Mitigation evidence (substance abuse, no prior violence, support system)
- Judge decides whether to suspend sentence and impose probation

## DRUG COURT (SPECIALIZED DOCKET)

### Overview

Drug court is a specialized court docket designed for non-violent offenders with substance abuse disorders. Rather than traditional prosecution-conviction-incarceration, drug court focuses on treatment, rehabilitation, and reintegration.

### Jurisdictional Availability

**Critical:** Drug courts exist in only some parishes. Check locally:
- Orleans Parish: Yes (well-established)
- Calcasieu Parish: Yes
- East Baton Rouge: Yes
- Jefferson Parish: Yes
- Most major parishes: Yes
- Rural parishes: May not have drug court

**Action:** Immediately determine if defendant's parish has drug court.

### Eligibility Criteria

**General requirements:**
1. Non-violent felony drug offense (simple possession, PWID typically OK; distribution OK; manufacturing OK; trafficking OK if no violence)
2. Substance abuse disorder diagnosis (clinical assessment required)
3. Voluntary participation (defendant must consent; not mandatory)
4. No violent felony prior (violent crime in history = disqualifying)
5. No disqualifying charge (some jurisdictions exclude trafficking, manufacturing, firearm-related offenses)

**Parish-specific variations:** Each parish has different eligibility criteria. Some are more inclusive (accept more offenses), some are restrictive.

### Program Structure (Typical 4-Phase Model)

**Phase 1: Intensive Treatment (0-3 months)**
- Mandatory inpatient or intensive outpatient treatment
- Weekly court appearances
- Frequent drug testing (2-3x/week or random)
- Group therapy, individual counseling
- Stabilization on medications (if needed)

**Phase 2: Stabilization (3-6 months)**
- Transitioning to outpatient treatment
- Biweekly or monthly court appearances
- Less frequent drug testing
- Beginning employment/education goals
- Continued counseling

**Phase 3: Reintegration (6-12 months)**
- Employment/education focus
- Reduced court appearances
- Drug testing continues but less frequent
- Community service
- Rebuilding social support networks

**Phase 4: Transition/Graduation (12+ months)**
- Minimal court involvement
- Occasional check-ins
- Final drug testing
- Graduation ceremony
- Case closure or conviction dismissal

**Total duration:** 12-24 months typical (can be shorter or longer depending on progress)

### Completion Outcome

**Successful completion (typical):**
- Case dismissed, or
- Conviction set aside/expunged, or
- Sentence probated (jurisdiction-dependent)

**Failure to complete:**
- Removed from drug court docket
- Returns to traditional prosecution
- Judge may impose sentence on pending conviction

### Drug Court Advantages

- Focuses on treatment, not punishment
- Avoids incarceration (defendant remains in community)
- Mandatory treatment addresses root cause (substance abuse)
- Regular court monitoring provides accountability
- Peer support (other drug court participants)
- Employment/education support
- Successful completion = case dismissed or conviction set aside

### Drug Court Disadvantages

- Intensive time commitment (frequent court appearances, treatment sessions)
- Ongoing drug testing (cost to defendant: typical $10-50/test, 2-3x/week initially)
- May require time away from work/school (initial phases)
- Strict probation conditions (association restrictions, curfew, etc.)
- Failure = criminal conviction and sentence imposition

### Strategic Evaluation: When to Use Drug Court

**Pursue drug court if:**
- Defendant has substance abuse disorder (clinical diagnosis)
- Defendant is motivated for treatment
- Defendant's parish has established drug court
- Defendant is willing to commit 12-24 months to program
- Risk of conviction/incarceration is high in traditional prosecution

**Avoid drug court if:**
- Defendant is not amenable to treatment
- Defendant lacks support system for compliance
- Employment/caregiving obligations make intensive court/treatment schedule impossible
- Art. 893 first offender diversion is available (simpler, faster)

## PRE-TRIAL DIVERSION (DA DISCRETION)

### Overview

District attorney may offer pre-trial diversion — defendant avoids prosecution entirely if conditions are met.

### Process

1. **Before prosecution:** Before charges are formally filed or early in prosecution
2. **DA offers terms:** "Charges will be dismissed if you complete X requirements"
3. **Typical terms:** (Vary by DA and parish)
   - No arrest during diversion period (6-12 months)
   - Drug testing (monthly or random)
   - Substance abuse treatment (outpatient)
   - Community service hours (50-200)
   - Diversion program fees ($200-$1,000)
   - No contact with known drug users/dealers

4. **Completion:** Charges dismissed; no conviction
5. **Non-completion:** Criminal prosecution proceeds

### Advantages

- No conviction (case dismissed if completed)
- Faster than drug court (6-12 months vs. 12-24 months)
- Less intensive than drug court (fewer court appearances)
- Case never prosecuted officially

### Disadvantages

- DA has sole discretion (cannot force DA to offer)
- Diversion fees out of pocket (no public funding)
- Conditions still strict (treatment, testing, community service)
- One violation = prosecution proceeds

### Negotiation Strategy

Early in case:
- Propose pre-trial diversion to DA
- Emphasize defendant's cooperation, lack of criminal history, treatment readiness
- Offer enhanced probation terms to sweeten offer (increased treatment, community service)

## MARIJUANA-SPECIFIC DIVERSION (POST-ACT 274, 2024)

### Louisiana Act 274 (2024) Changes

**First offense, small amount (<14g):**
- Misdemeanor (not felony)
- Fine up to $300
- No jail time
- Increased likelihood of diversion (misdemeanor treatment, not felony prosecution)

**Second offense:**
- Misdemeanor
- Up to 30 days jail
- Fine

**Third+ offense:**
- Felony charges apply

### Strategic Benefit

If client charged with small amount marijuana:
- Reduction to misdemeanor possible (Route to dw-plea-negotiation-analyzer)
- Misdemeanor = easier diversion negotiations
- Expungement eligibility after completion (misdemeanor expungeable)

## FEDERAL DRUG COURT AND FEDERAL DIVERSION

### Federal Cases

If defendant is charged federally (DEA, FBI, etc.):

- **No Art. 893 equivalent** — Federal sentencing guidelines mandatory minimums apply
- **Federal Drug Court** — Some federal districts have drug court programs (limited, more restrictive than state drug courts)
- **Compassionate Release (18 U.S.C. § 3582(c)(1)(A))** — Sentence reduction after substantial compliance with treatment (rare)

**Strategy:** If federal charges, coordinate with federal public defender or federal defense counsel. Federal drug diversion options are severely limited compared to state options.

## ELIGIBILITY ASSESSMENT CHECKLIST

For every drug case, immediately assess:

- [ ] **Art. 893 eligible?** (First felony drug offense? No prior felonies?)
  - If YES: Prioritize negotiating with DA for consent
  - If NO: Is defendant disqualified by prior felony, or crime-of-violence charge?

- [ ] **Art. 890 available?** (Has prior felony, but still eligible for sentence suspension?)
  - Consult with judge/DA on likelihood

- [ ] **Drug Court available in this parish?**
  - If YES: Is defendant eligible (non-violent, substance abuse diagnosis)?
  - If NO: Pursue other options

- [ ] **DA offers pre-trial diversion?**
  - Early negotiation with DA

- [ ] **Marijuana offense?**
  - Act 274 reductions possible?
  - Misdemeanor diversion more feasible than felony?

**Output:** Diversion Eligibility Assessment memo to case file, highlighting best path forward.

## NEGOTIATION LEVERAGE

### Use Diversion Availability to Negotiate

If defendant qualifies for Art. 893 or drug court:

**To DA:** "Our client qualifies for Art. 893 diversion. Accepting guilty plea saves prosecution time/cost. Defendant commits to treatment and probation. This is a win for the system."

**To Judge (if DA refuses):** "At sentencing, we request Art. 893 application. Defendant meets all criteria. First felony, no prior convictions, substance abuse treatment readiness."

**To Defendant:** "Art. 893 offer gives you a clean slate if you complete probation. Trial risk = conviction + prison time. Diversion risk = probation failure only if you violate. What's your motivation for treatment?"

## DOCUMENTATION AND FOLLOW-UP

For diversion cases:

**Track:**
- Treatment compliance (attendance, progress)
- Drug testing results (all negative? Any failures?)
- Court appearances (no missed dates)
- Community service hours (completed)
- Probation officer reports (any violations reported?)

**Before completion deadline:** File motion for early completion or certificate of completion. Present evidence of success to judge. Request conviction set-aside at completion.
