# State vs. Federal Firearms Prosecution Matrix

## Overview
Firearms charges uniquely expose defendants to dual jurisdiction prosecution. This matrix compares Louisiana state law with federal law to inform forum selection strategy.

---

## Side-by-Side Statute Comparison

### Felon-in-Possession Comparison

| Issue | Louisiana R.S. 14:95.1 | Federal 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1) |
|-------|------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Predicate Definition** | Louisiana felony conviction | Any crime punishable by >1 year imprisonment |
| **Prior Conviction Source** | Louisiana only (or out-of-state if convicted) | Any jurisdiction (state, federal, foreign) |
| **Custody Requirement** | No time served required | No time served required |
| **Actual Sentence vs. Authorized Sentence** | N/A | Authorized sentence controls (not actual) |
| **Wobbler Crimes** | N/A | Judge/prosecutor discretion at charging/adjudication |

### Sentencing Comparison

| Issue | Louisiana R.S. 14:95.1 | Federal § 922(g) |
|-------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Sentence Range** | 10-20 years hard labor (MANDATORY) | Up to 15 years (ADVISORY GUIDELINE) |
| **Judge Discretion** | None (mandatory minimum 10 years) | Full discretion to sentence 0-15 years |
| **Parole Eligibility** | Not eligible; mandatory hard labor | 85% rule applies; supervised release possible |
| **Good-Time Credit** | Varies (interpretations differ) | Good-time credits apply (federal system) |
| **Restitution/Fines** | Possible but secondary | Standard part of sentence |
| **Probation** | Expressly prohibited | Not applicable; supervised release post-release |

### Cleansing / Collateral Consequences

| Issue | Louisiana R.S. 14:95.1 | Federal § 922(g) |
|-------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Cleansing Period** | 10 years from completion of sentence (R.S. 14:95.1(C)) | NO cleansing period; permanent |
| **Expungement Impact** | Expunged conviction may eliminate predicate (depends on type) | Expunged conviction may still count (depends on federal interpretation) |
| **Wobbler/Reclassification** | If reclassified to misdemeanor, may escape § 95.1 | Federal courts still look to original offense elements |

### Enhancement Overlay

| Issue | Louisiana R.S. 14:95.1 | Federal § 922(g) |
|-------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Habitual Offender Bill** | Possible; 4 or more prior convictions = habitual (R.S. 15:529.1) | N/A |
| **ACCA (15-year mandatory)** | N/A | Yes; if 3+ violent felonies or serious drug offenses |
| **§ 924(c) Stacking** | N/A | Yes; 5-25 years mandatory consecutive during drug/violence crime |
| **Gun-Specific Guidelines** | R.S. 14:95 enhanced if firearm involved | USSG § 2K2.1 base offense level 12-14 |

## Federal Adoption Risk Assessment

### Triggers for Federal Prosecution

**HIGH RISK:**
- ATF/FBI involvement in investigation
- Multi-defendant conspiracy/organized crime
- Drug trafficking nexus (gun + drugs)
- Prior federal convictions
- Federal grand jury already convened
- Firearms trafficking (bulk purchases, altered serials)
- Interstate movement of weapon

**MEDIUM RISK:**
- Single firearm seizure during drug stop
- Gun found in vehicle with drugs nearby
- Felon in possession charge in federal-friendly district
- Defendant has prior federal conviction

**LOW RISK:**
- Simple R.S. 14:95 carrying (concealed without permit)
- School zone charge (R.S. 14:95.2)
- State-based domestic violence firearm prohibition
- Isolated firearm without drug/crime nexus

### Federal Adoption Timeline

- Federal prosecutors can "adopt" state cases at any point
- Often happens after state charging/grand jury indictment
- ATF/FBI may join investigation mid-case
- Defense should anticipate federal indictment even if currently state-only

## Forum Analysis: Which Is More Favorable?

### STATE COURT ADVANTAGES (Louisiana)

**Jury Pool:**
- Parish-specific jury (may have pro-gun Second Amendment leanings in rural areas)
- Local defense bar more familiar with local judges
- Specific judge assignment known early (may be favorable/unfavorable)

**Sentencing:**
- No mandatory minimum if defendant avoids § 95.1 predicates (push for misdemeanor carrying)
- R.S. 14:95 first offense: 6 months / $500 (far less than federal)
- Parole possible on sentences >2 years (in some cases)
- Some judges more lenient than others (research specific judge)

**Procedural:**
- State discovery rules may be more favorable (La. C.C.P.)
- State voir dire rules (different from federal)
- State expert witness standards (may differ)

**Collateral Consequences:**
- State conviction = felon status under state law only
- Does not trigger federal § 922(g)(1) unless future federal prosecution
- Expungement possible (under LSHB 1018, some felonies expungeable)

### STATE COURT DISADVANTAGES

**Sentencing:**
- R.S. 14:95.1: mandatory 10-20 years hard labor (no parole, no suspension)
- Habitual offender bill possible (if 4+ priors = mandatory 25/40/50/life)
- No parole on hard labor sentence

**Predicate Audit Difficulty:**
- Louisiana predicate definition broad (many "felonies" qualify)
- Cleansing period (10 years) often overlooked by defense but real issue
- Judge may not be receptive to technical predicate challenges

**Federal Adoption Risk:**
- State conviction may become federal predicate for future § 922(g) charge
- State DV conviction = federal § 922(g)(9) disqualifier
- If convicted in state, federal prosecution likely follows

### FEDERAL COURT ADVANTAGES

**Sentencing:**
- Up to 15 years (not mandatory minimum unless ACCA/§ 924(c))
- Judge has guideline discretion (post-Booker)
- Possible probation/supervised release (instead of hard labor)
- Good-time credits available
- Parole-like supervised release (after 85% served)

**Jury Pool:**
- Federal jury (multi-district) may be less pro-gun
- Diverse urban jury pool (if multi-county district)
- Federal courts routinely handle firearm cases

**Predicates:**
- Federal definition of predicate more rigid (>1 year authorized sentence)
- Some Louisiana crimes may NOT meet federal threshold
- Range v. Attorney General (3rd Cir.): nonviolent felons may retain Second Amendment rights

### FEDERAL COURT DISADVANTAGES

**Mandatory Minimums:**
- ACCA: 15-year mandatory if 3+ violent/drug predicates
- § 924(c): 5-25 years mandatory consecutive (if gun used during drug/violence crime)
- No escape from ACCA/§ 924(c) mandatory if triggered

**Sentencing Enhancement:**
- Guideline base level 12-14 for § 922(g)
- Enhancements for multiple weapons, trafficking
- Even with lenient judge, guidelines inform sentence

**Technicality Risk:**
- Federal courts more formalistic (ACCA predicate analysis very strict)
- Johnson/Mathis categorical approach defeats many predicates but requires expertise
- Wrong legal theory can blow entire ACCA challenge

## Plea Strategy: State vs. Federal Forum Selection

### SCENARIO A: Felon in Possession Only (No Drug/Violence Nexus)

**STATE ADVANTAGE:**
- Plead to R.S. 14:95 (simple carrying) instead of R.S. 14:95.1 (felon)
- R.S. 14:95 first offense: 6 months / $500 (vs. 10-20 years)
- Avoid predicate issues entirely
- Avoid federal § 922(g) predicates

**FEDERAL ADVANTAGE:**
- Federal judge has discretion; no mandatory minimum
- Could sentence below guideline (downward departure)
- But risk of ACCA 15-year mandatory if 3+ predicates

**RECOMMENDATION:** Push state prosecutor to offer R.S. 14:95 simple carrying to avoid federal adoption risk. If that fails, assess ACCA predicate strength before deciding state vs. federal negotiation.

### SCENARIO B: Felon in Possession + Drug Trafficking

**STATE:** R.S. 14:95.1 + drug charge = 10-20 years hard labor (mandatory) on gun, separate drug sentence
**FEDERAL:** § 922(g) + drug trafficking + § 924(c) = 5-25 years mandatory consecutive PLUS drug sentence = potentially higher total

**RECOMMENDATION:** Generally push for state-only if federal adoption risk is high. But if drug quantity is large, federal might offer better plea deal to both charges. Run numbers on both forums.

### SCENARIO C: Defendant Has 3+ Prior Violence/Drug Convictions (ACCA Risk)

**STATE:** R.S. 14:95.1 = 10-20 years hard labor (no escape, predicate doesn't matter)
**FEDERAL:** § 922(g) with ACCA = 15-year mandatory (if predicates valid), but could escape if predicates fail categorical test

**RECOMMENDATION:** Aggressive predicate challenge in federal court (Johnson/Mathis). If can knock out one predicate, ACCA disappears (only 2 left). In state court, must accept 10-20 years (no predicate issues). If predicates are weak, federal is better. If predicates are strong, state may be worse (mandatory 10-20 years vs. federal 15 mandatory ACCA).

## Practical Strategic Considerations

### When State Court Is Better
1. Defendant has no federal prior convictions
2. Predicates are questionable (fewer than 3 for ACCA)
3. Can avoid R.S. 14:95.1 charge entirely (simple carrying)
4. Judge known to be lenient on firearms cases
5. Federal adoption unlikely (no drug nexus, local police only)
6. § 924(c) exposure does NOT exist (gun not used during drug/violence crime)

### When Federal Court Is Better
1. ACCA predicates are weak (can be challenged categorically)
2. Drug quantity is large (federal may offer package deal)
3. State judge known to be harsh on firearms
4. Federal judge known to be favorable (check PACER records)
5. Can negotiate below guideline minimum
6. Defendant can fight Second Amendment challenge in federal court (Range v. AG / nonviolent felon angle)

### Dual Prosecution Risk
- If charged in state, federal prosecution likely follows
- Plea in state may become basis for federal prosecution
- BUT: State conviction can be used as predicate for federal § 922(g) later
- Strategy: Maximize mitigation in state court to minimize federal sentence if adoption occurs

### Recommended Approach
1. Immediately assess federal adoption risk (ATF/FBI involvement? Drug nexus? Multi-defendant?)
2. If state-only: push for R.S. 14:95 simple carrying plea (6 months / $500)
3. If federal adoption likely: analyze ACCA predicates (Johnson/Mathis) and § 924(c) "in furtherance" strength
4. Compare state 10-20 mandatory hard labor vs. federal guidelines + ACCA/§ 924(c) mandatory
5. Negotiate forum-specific plea (state lesser charge vs. federal guideline plea with ACCA predicate challenge)
6. If both forums active: consider sequential strategy (state first, then federal with credit for time served)
