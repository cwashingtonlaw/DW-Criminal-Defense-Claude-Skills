# Annotation Catalog

The twelve recurring annotation types, with worked language. Adapt; don't invent a new taxonomy per
transcript.

Every annotation: **states the finding, names the action, and gives the counter-argument.** An
annotation that only argues one way gets quoted in a motion and embarrassed at the hearing.

Rank by consequence, not chronology.

## 6. Absence of expected content

> **00:40:35.** Mr. Brown identifies only a 2019 white Cadillac Escalade and a 2014 white Nissan
> Maxima. **NO Toyota Camry is mentioned anywhere in this interview, and the detective did not ask
> about one.** Compare the State's theory that Brown's Camry was the getaway vehicle and that Brown

theory, which is why it cannot be automated.

## Writing rules

**Name the action.** "LISTEN TO THIS PASSAGE." "Demand the native files." Never "this is notable."

**Quantify.** "27 minutes 54 seconds," not "a significant portion."

**Give the counter-argument in the same annotation**, not a footnote.

**Cite the source of every comparison** — Bates, Case Brain section, or the runtime it came from.

**Say when you can't tell.** "That inference is arithmetic from file size at a constant bitrate, not
measurement, and should be treated as a lead rather than a fact" is more useful than false confidence,
and it is what keeps the annotations trustworthy when one of them is load-bearing.
