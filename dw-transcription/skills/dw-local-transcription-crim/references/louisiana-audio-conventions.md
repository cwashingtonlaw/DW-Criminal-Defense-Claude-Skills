# Louisiana Audio Conventions

Whisper is trained on general English. South Louisiana place names, French-derived surnames, and local
pronunciations defeat it in predictable ways. These corrections are safe because the intended word is
forced by geography.

---

## The one that catches everyone

**Iowa, Louisiana is pronounced "EYE-oh-way."** Whisper renders it `Iowa way`, `I-O-way`, `Ioway`.

Real exchange, corrected:

> `And this is a Lac-Cassine or Iowa way?` → **"And this is Lacassine or Iowa?"**

The speaker is naming two neighboring Calcasieu-area communities. Nothing to do with the state.

## Surnames

French, Creole and Cajun surnames are frequently mangled. **Do not correct a person's name from
geography alone** — mark `[PHONETIC]` and resolve from another source.

Observed corrections, each resolved by a *different recording* where the person spelled their own name
on the record:

| First rendering | Correct | How resolved |
|---|---|---|
| Iselyn Malva | **Iselyn Malvo** | She spells it: "M-A-L-V-O" |
| Taronda Malboro | **Tarhonda Malbrough** | Folder name in another item |
| Desomnd Brown | **Desmond Brown** | He spells it: "D-E-S-M-O-N-D" |

Others to expect: Boudreaux, Thibodeaux, Hebert (AY-bear), Guidry, Landry, Broussard, Fontenot,
Arceneaux, Comeaux, Trahan, LeBlanc, Prejean, Doucet, Melancon, Chiasson, Babineaux.

**Hebert** is the classic trap — pronounced "AY-bear," transcribed as *A-Bear*, *Hebert*, *Aybear*.

## Speech patterns

Not errors. Transcribe as spoken.

- **"y'all"** — never expand to "you all."
- **"making groceries"** — shopping.
- **"pass a good time," "where y'at," "making do"** — verbatim.
- **"my people"** — extended family or close friends.
- **"stay"** for "live" — *"she stayed back there too."* Do not correct to "lived."
- **"baby mama" / "baby daddy"** — verbatim; these are relationship terms in the record.
- **"catch a charge," "on paper"** (on probation/parole), **"tricked off the street."**
- **"every blue moon"** — rarely.
- **"neutral ground"** — median (New Orleans area).
- **"lagniappe"** — a little extra.

Dropped copulas and habitual "be" (*"he been feeling like that," "she in a good mood"*) are AAVE
grammar, not transcription errors. **Do not standardize them.** A transcript that launders a witness's
voice into standard English is less accurate and will be impeached on cross.

---

## Numbers spoken aloud

Whisper is unreliable on strings of digits — phone numbers, dates of birth, Social Security numbers,
case numbers, addresses. **Flag every one for verification.** These matter (a DOB transposition
already invalidated an NCIC query in one file) and they are exactly what ASR gets wrong.

Convention: transcribe as heard, and annotate the runtime so the attorney can check the digits against
the audio in one pass.
