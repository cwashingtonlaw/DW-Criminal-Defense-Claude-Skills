# Tiering and Scope

Agree the tier before processing anything. These are different jobs with different costs, and
conflating them produces a document that looks finished and is not.

| | Working transcript | Court-reporter transcript |
|---|---|---|
| Line numbers, page:line citation | no | yes |
| Speaker labels | machine, marked as machine | named examiners, block level |
| Proper nouns | as recognized | hand-verified or `[PHONETIC]` |
| Front matter | header + coverage warning | full spec |
| Annotations | none | full catalog |
| Cost | automated | **~1 working session per hour of audio** |
| Safe to quote in a filing | no | after the attorney checks the cited passage |

## Sizing the job honestly

The hand pass dominates. For 63 minutes of audio (~11,200 spoken words) it is the bulk of the work:
merging ASR fragments into Q./A. turns, correcting what is certain, marking what isn't, catching that
"Iowa way" is Iowa and that a Miranda negation survived two decodes.

Six hours of audio is **six sessions, not one long one.**

## What to say when asked for more than can be done well

Say the number, propose an order, and name the failure mode:

> Your six items are 6 hours 26 minutes, roughly six times the #39 job. Doing them at that quality
> means one per working session; doing all six at once means the later ones quietly become working
> transcripts with numbered lines, which is worse than useless because it looks finished. I'll take
> them in this order — [ranked by evidentiary weight] — starting now.

## Ranking which items get the full treatment

1. **The client's own statement.** Always first.
2. **Co-defendant statements** — Bruton, Massiah, and the State's theory usually rests here.
3. **Informant interviews** — Giglio.
4. **Recordings tied to physical evidence** (the person whose DNA is on the casing).
5. **Child forensic interviews** — high weight, and handled under protective order with initials only.
6. **Everything else** — working transcript unless something in it earns promotion.

Fully-retrieved items outrank partially-retrieved ones: a court-reporter transcript of an item missing
28 minutes is a court-reporter transcript of a fragment, and should say so on page 1.
