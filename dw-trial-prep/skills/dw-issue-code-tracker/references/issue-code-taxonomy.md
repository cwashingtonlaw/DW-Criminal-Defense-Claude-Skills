## Category 1: UNIVERSAL (14 codes)

Apply to ALL criminal cases. Default status on initialization: `Open`.

| Code | Name | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| U-01 | Charges | *Charging document accuracy, lesser-included offenses, enhancements, statute validity, election of charges.* |
| U-02 | Criminal History (Defendant) | *Defendant's prior convictions, predicate exposure for habitual billing, 404(b)/Prieur risk for trial, Boykin validity audits, sentencing enhancement priors. Witness criminal history tracked under U-05 Exculpatory (Giglio) and U-06 Witnesses/Credibility.* |
| U-03 | Crime Scene | *Scene-level processing — photography (gaps, angles, lighting), scene control and security, walkthrough protocols, missed or overlooked evidence at the scene, scene reconstruction issues, secondary scene identification. Item-level chain of custody tracked under U-04 Evidence-Physical. Mark N/A for cases without a physical scene (e.g., white-collar fraud, possession-only with Terry stop).* |
| U-04 | Evidence - Physical (Chain & Handling) | *Item-level chain of custody from collection forward, evidence room handling, transfer logs, lab submission protocols, preservation conditions, spoliation, weight discrepancies (drug cases), generic contamination during handling. Forensic-specific chain issues tracked under H-01/H-02/H-03 (ballistics/DNA/prints) and R-01/R-02 (SANE/SAK). Scene-level collection tracked under U-03.* |
| U-05 | Evidence - Exculpatory (Brady/Giglio) | *Direct Brady material (favorable facts, exculpatory evidence), alternative suspect evidence, Giglio impeachment of State witnesses (deals, benefits, prior false statements, witness criminal history). "Addressed" status means: Brady demand filed, received discovery audited, identified gaps demanded, monitoring posture established for late disclosures. Brady is a continuing obligation — re-open if new disclosures arrive. Witness credibility issues independent of Brady tracked under U-06. Investigation gaps that suggest undiscovered Brady tracked under U-12.* |
| U-06 | Witnesses/Credibility (State Witnesses) | *Credibility analysis of each State witness — internal inconsistencies (within their own statements over time), external inconsistencies (against other witnesses or physical evidence), bias, motive to lie, prior inconsistent statements, witness criminal history. "Addressed" means: every State witness has a Witness Analysis Card (`dw-witness-statement-analyzer`) and is on the Witness Threat Matrix (`dw-witness-threat-matrix`). Brady/Giglio aspects also tracked under U-05. Eyewitness identification procedure issues tracked under U-07. Defense witnesses tracked separately as needed.* |
| U-07 | Eyewitness Identification | *Identification procedure analysis under Manson factors — photo array protocols, show-up procedures, in-court ID taint, cross-racial ID reliability, weapon focus, stress, lighting, duration of view, confidence inflation. "Addressed" means: every ID procedure audited (`dw-eyewitness-identification-auditor`), suppression motion filed if grounds exist, expert testimony on ID reliability secured if needed, jury instruction drafted (`dw-jury-instructions-builder`). Mark N/A for cases without an identification issue (parties knew each other, defendant confessed and ID is uncontested, no eyewitness component).* |
| U-08 | Interrogation/Statements (Defendant) | *Analysis of all defendant statements to law enforcement or State agents — custody determination, Miranda adequacy, knowing/intelligent waiver, invocation analysis, voluntariness under totality of circumstances, coercive interrogation techniques (Reid, etc.), false confession risk factors (age, capacity, mental health, sleep deprivation, length of questioning), pre-Miranda statements, post-charge Sixth Amendment (Massiah) issues, jail calls, statements to informants. "Addressed" means: every defendant statement catalogued, custody/Miranda/voluntariness analyzed (`dw-confession-interrogation-auditor`), suppression motion filed if grounds exist, false confession expert retained if applicable. Witness/complainant interview protocol issues tracked under U-06 (general witness credibility) or R-09 (child forensic interviewing). Mark N/A only if defendant made zero statements to anyone law enforcement might call.* |
| U-09 | Expert Reliability (Daubert/Foret) | *Foundational expert qualifications, methodology reliability under Foret/Daubert (La. C.E. Art. 702), junk science exclusion challenges, scope creep (testimony beyond qualifications), basis-for-opinion adequacy (Art. 703), and disclosure compliance (Art. 705). "Addressed" means: every State expert vetted (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), Daubert/Foret motion filed where grounds exist, defense experts retained where needed, cross-examination outlines drafted. Specialty-specific technical challenges tracked under H-01/H-02/H-03/H-04 (homicide forensics) or R-01/R-02 (sex assault forensics).* |
| U-10 | Timeline/Alibi | *Master chronology of the offense and surrounding events, including: State's reconstruction of events, defense alibi analysis (whereabouts, corroborating evidence, witnesses), forensic and digital timestamps (BWC, CSLI, text/call logs, surveillance), inconsistency flagging across witnesses and physical evidence, unaccounted-for time gaps. "Addressed" means: master timeline built (`dw-timeline-builder`), conflicts catalogued, alibi witnesses identified and statements taken, La. C.Cr.P. Art. 727 alibi notice filed if asserting alibi defense, BWC/CSLI/digital timestamps integrated. CSLI methodology challenges tracked under U-14. Electronic communication content tracked under U-11.* |
| U-11 | Electronic Communication & Digital Evidence | *Phone and digital evidence content — texts/SMS, call logs, emails, social media (Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, WhatsApp), dating app conversations, app-specific data (Cash App, Venmo, fitness/health apps, smart-device data), photos, videos, browser history, deleted artifacts (SQLite/WAL recovery). Authentication under La. C.E. Art. 901, foundation requirements, and Crawford/Confrontation Clause issues for absent declarants. "Addressed" means: extraction reviewed (`dw-forensic-dump-analyzer`), extraction methodology audited (`dw-mobile-forensic-auditor`), platform records audited (`dw-social-media-auditor`), authentication objections preserved, Confrontation Clause issues flagged. Constitutional acquisition (search warrant scope) tracked under U-13. CSLI/cell tower location data tracked under U-14. Timeline integration tracked under U-10.* |
| U-12 | Deficient Investigation | *Investigative gaps and tunnel vision — leads not pursued, alternative suspects not investigated, witnesses not interviewed, forensic evidence not collected or tested, surveillance/BWC not preserved, records not subpoenaed, follow-up not conducted, crime scene not re-processed. Cross-examination ammunition for the lead detective and roadmap for defense investigation. "Addressed" means: investigation gap analysis completed, defense investigator tasked (`dw-defense-investigator-tasking`), alternative suspect leads pursued, missing-evidence subpoenas issued, cross-exam outline drafted for lead detective. Brady/Giglio disclosure failures (where the State has the evidence and isn't producing it) tracked under U-05. Scene-level collection failures tracked under U-03 (without precluding logging here as well — investigation deficiency analysis can overlap with scene-processing analysis).* |
| U-13 | Search & Seizure / 4th Amendment | *Constitutional analysis of every State intrusion — warrants (probable cause, particularity, staleness, Franks challenges), warrantless searches (and applicable exceptions), consent searches (voluntariness, scope, authority), Terry stops (reasonable suspicion, frisk scope), vehicle searches (automobile exception, inventory, plain view), residential entries (warrant, consent, exigency, hot pursuit, knock-and-announce), phone search scope (Riley v. California, particularity for digital devices), standing analysis, inevitable discovery/independent source. Includes Article I § 5 (Louisiana Constitution) where more protective than federal law. "Addressed" means: every search/seizure event catalogued, suppression motions filed where grounds exist (`dw-suppression-motion`), Franks hearings requested where applicable, fruit-of-poisonous-tree analysis completed for derivative evidence. Phone content authentication tracked under U-11. CSLI/cell-tower-specific Carpenter issues tracked under U-14. 5th Amendment custodial statement issues tracked under U-08.* |
| U-14 | CSLI/Geolocation Evidence | *Cell site location information, GPS tracking, tower dumps, Stingray/IMSI catchers, geofence warrants, Google location history. Constitutional framework: Carpenter v. United States (warrant required for historical CSLI), general warrant doctrine for geofence, Jones/Karo GPS framework, evolving Stingray disclosure obligations. Technical framework: cell sectorization and coverage area inflation (rural towers especially), tower-pinging frequency, network load affecting which tower handles a call, indoor/outdoor signal differences, State expert overstatement of precision. "Addressed" means: every geolocation event catalogued and source documented, constitutional acquisition audited (`dw-cell-site-geolocation-auditor`), suppression motions filed where Carpenter or general-warrant grounds exist, defense network expert retained if precision claims are case-dispositive, cross-examination outline drafted on coverage area limitations. Constitutional acquisition more broadly tracked under U-13. Phone content and authentication tracked under U-11.* |

## Category 2: HOMICIDE (8 codes)

Apply if case type includes homicide (any degree). Default status on initialization: `Open`.

| Code | Name | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| H-01 | Forensic - Ballistics | *Firearms and tool-mark forensic analysis — bullet/cartridge case examination, tool-mark comparison methodology (NAS 2009 Report and PCAST 2016 Report criticisms of feature-comparison methods), NIBIN/IBIS database hits (preliminary match vs. confirmed identification — distinction frequently blurred at trial), gunshot residue (GSR) analysis and false-positive issues, serial number restoration, distance-of-fire estimates, weapon-specific vs. caliber/manufacturer-only linkage. "Addressed" means: ballistics expert vetted (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), tool-mark methodology challenged where appropriate, NIBIN match scope clarified, GSR contamination defenses developed (`dw-firearms-specialist`), defense ballistics expert retained if warranted. Bullet/casing chain of custody tracked under U-04. General expert framework challenges (qualifications, scope creep) tracked under U-09. Mark N/A for non-firearm homicides (strangulation, blunt force, stabbing, poisoning, asphyxiation).* |
| H-02 | Forensic - DNA | *DNA analysis in homicide cases — STR profiling, mixture interpretation (PCAST 2016 criticism of complex 3+ contributor mixtures), probabilistic genotyping software black-box issues (TrueAllele, STRmix), statistical reporting (likelihood ratios, random match probability inflation), low-copy-number stochastic effects, allele drop-in/drop-out, touch/transfer DNA and secondary transfer defenses, degradation effects, contamination during collection or processing. "Addressed" means: DNA expert vetted (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), methodology-specific challenges identified (mixture interpretation, LCN, secondary transfer), defense expert retained if warranted, secondary-transfer or innocent-presence defenses developed where applicable, statistical reporting scrutinized for inflation. Sample chain of custody tracked under U-04. Scene-level collection tracked under U-03. General expert framework challenges tracked under U-09. ME's reliance on physical evidence for COD tracked under H-04. Mark N/A if no DNA evidence is in the case.* |
| H-03 | Forensic - Fingerprints | *Latent print analysis — ACE-V methodology (Analysis, Comparison, Evaluation, Verification) with documented reliability concerns (NAS 2009 Report's criticism of no validated error rate, PCAST 2016 Report's criticism of individualization claims, *Brandon Mayfield* case demonstrating false-positive misidentification). Subjective examiner judgment (no standardized minimum point requirement), partial print interpretation, aged/degraded print issues, surface limitations, AFIS database hits (preliminary candidate vs. confirmed identification — distinction frequently blurred at trial), exclusion grounds when prints don't match defendant. "Addressed" means: latent print examiner vetted (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), ACE-V methodology challenged where appropriate, individualization language objected to, AFIS match scope clarified, defense fingerprint expert retained if warranted, exclusionary print evidence (defendant's prints absent from key surfaces) preserved for trial. Lift card/digital image chain of custody tracked under U-04. Scene-level collection tracked under U-03. General expert framework challenges tracked under U-09. Mark N/A if no fingerprint evidence is in the case.* |
| H-04 | Medical Examiner / Cause of Death | *Forensic pathology and medico-legal death investigation — Cause of Death (COD: physical cause), Manner of Death (homicide / suicide / accident / natural / undetermined), Time of Death (TOD methodology and error margins from rigor mortis, livor mortis, body temperature, vitreous potassium, entomological evidence), Mechanism of Death (physiological pathway), and injury interpretation (defensive wounds, hesitation marks, antemortem vs. postmortem, sequencing). Defense attack surfaces include: multifactorial causation disputes (intoxication + trauma + pre-existing conditions), equivocal manner-of-death cases (homicide vs. suicide vs. accident), TOD imprecision affecting alibi defenses, self-inflicted vs. inflicted distinctions, skull fracture and blunt-force interpretation, asphyxiation marker ambiguity. "Addressed" means: autopsy report and toxicology obtained and audited, ME deposed if available, defense forensic pathologist retained for independent review, contested findings identified for cross-examination (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), photographs and slides reviewed by defense expert. Ballistics-informed wound interpretation tracked under H-01. DNA/tissue findings tracked under H-02. General expert framework challenges tracked under U-09.* |
| H-05 | Specific Intent & Premeditation Indicators | *Mens rea analysis under Louisiana specific-intent framework (R.S. 14:10(1)) — evidence of advance planning, timing, cooling-off period, prior threats/statements, weapon procurement, scouting, intent formation in the moment vs. over time. Defense theories that negate or mitigate specific intent: voluntary intoxication (R.S. 14:15(2)), mental capacity/diminished responsibility, heat of passion / sudden quarrel (mitigation to manslaughter under R.S. 14:31), provocation. Distinguishes first-degree (specific intent + aggravators) from second-degree (specific intent without aggravators) from manslaughter (specific intent with sudden passion). "Addressed" means: specific intent evidence catalogued, mitigation theories identified, intoxication or capacity defenses developed if applicable, jury instructions on lesser-included offenses drafted in coordination with H-06 (`dw-jury-instructions-builder`), defense expert (psychiatrist, toxicologist) retained if needed. Felony murder analysis tracked under H-07. Self-defense as complete justification (rather than mitigation) tracked under H-08.* |
| H-06 | Verdict Ladder & Lesser-Included Strategy | *Strategic analysis of the responsive verdict menu under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 814 — what charges and lesser-included offenses the jury will see, and how to position the case for a lower rung. For homicide cases, this includes the full Louisiana ladder: 1st-degree murder (R.S. 14:30) → 2nd-degree murder (R.S. 14:30.1) → manslaughter (R.S. 14:31, including heat-of-passion and the felony-manslaughter alternative) → negligent homicide (R.S. 14:32). Mental state distinctions under Louisiana law: specific intent (R.S. 14:10(1)), general intent (R.S. 14:10(2)), criminal negligence (R.S. 14:12), sudden passion / heat of blood. "Addressed" means: applicable responsive verdicts identified, lesser-included jury instructions drafted (`dw-jury-instructions-builder`), strategic decision documented on whether to seek or oppose specific lesser-included instructions (sometimes defense prefers all-or-nothing first-degree-or-acquit; sometimes defense wants the manslaughter floor), trial themes aligned with verdict-ladder strategy. Specific-intent negation and mitigation analysis tracked under H-05. Felony murder verdict-ladder issues tracked under H-07. Self-defense (complete justification) tracked under H-08.* |
| H-07 | Felony Murder | *Felony murder analysis under Louisiana law — first-degree felony murder (R.S. 14:30(A)(1), requiring an enumerated qualifying felony: aggravated/forcible rape, aggravated/second-degree kidnapping, aggravated escape, aggravated arson, aggravated/simple/second-degree robbery, aggravated/simple burglary, drive-by shooting, terrorism, ritualistic acts) and second-degree felony murder (R.S. 14:30.1(A)(2), broader enumerated list). Attack surfaces: (1) underlying felony viability (if the State can't prove the underlying felony, felony murder collapses); (2) enumerated-felony list compliance (verify the State's underlying felony is statutorily listed for the degree charged); (3) co-felon liability scope (`State v. Pierre` framework for non-killer co-felons); (4) proximate cause and temporal scope ("during the perpetration or attempted perpetration"); (5) withdrawal defense for co-felons who exited before the killing; (6) merger doctrine where the underlying felony is too closely fused with the homicide. "Addressed" means: underlying felony charge audited for elemental viability, enumerated-list compliance verified, motions to quash filed where felony-murder theory fails on its face (`dw-pretrial-motion-library`), bill of particulars filed if the State hasn't specified the underlying felony, co-felon liability and withdrawal defenses developed if applicable, verdict-ladder strategy coordinated with H-06. Underlying felony charging defects also tracked under U-01. Specific-intent (non-felony-murder) homicide theories tracked under H-05. Verdict-ladder strategy generally tracked under H-06. Mark N/A if no felony-murder count is charged.* |
| H-08 | Self-Defense / Justification | *Affirmative defense framework under Louisiana law — R.S. 14:18 (justification generally), R.S. 14:19 (use of force in defense), R.S. 14:20 (justifiable homicide, including 14:20(A)(4) "stand your ground" and 14:20(B) Castle Doctrine presumption), R.S. 14:21 (aggressor doctrine and withdrawal exception), R.S. 14:22 (defense of others). Burden-shifting framework: once defendant produces evidence of self-defense, State must disprove self-defense beyond a reasonable doubt. Attack surfaces and analytical components: (1) aggressor determination and withdrawal; (2) imminence of unlawful force; (3) reasonableness (subjective and objective); (4) proportionality of response (necessity of lethal force); (5) Castle Doctrine presumption applicability if in home/business/vehicle; (6) no-duty-to-retreat where lawfully present; (7) verbal-provocation-only limitation (mere words insufficient); (8) defense of others; (9) imperfect self-defense (honest but unreasonable belief — mitigation to manslaughter, coordinated with H-05); (10) victim's character for violence (Art. 404(A)(2) admissibility once self-defense raised). "Addressed" means: factual basis for self-defense investigated and developed, R.S. 14:20/14:21/14:22 elements analyzed against case facts, defense theory locked in (perfect vs. imperfect self-defense vs. defense of others), self-defense and burden-shifting jury instructions drafted (`dw-jury-instructions-builder`), victim's prior bad acts and character-for-violence evidence developed if Art. 404(A)(2) is triggered, cross-examination outlines drafted to support the self-defense narrative. Imperfect self-defense as mitigation also tracked under H-05 (specific-intent mitigation). Verdict-ladder positioning tracked under H-06. Victim's character/prior bad acts also tracked under U-06 (witness/victim credibility) and U-12 (deficient investigation if police failed to investigate victim's history). Mark N/A if self-defense is not asserted or not factually available.* |

## Category 3: RAPE / SEXUAL ASSAULT (11 codes)

Apply if case type includes any sexual offense. Default status on initialization: `Open`.

| Code | Name | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| R-01 | SANE Examination | *Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner exam audit — protocol compliance against the DOJ Office on Violence Against Women's National Protocol for Sexual Assault Medical Forensic Examinations (2nd Edition, 2013) and IAFN (International Association of Forensic Nurses) certification standards (SANE-A adult/adolescent, SANE-P pediatric). Attack surfaces: (1) examiner qualifications — distinguish certified SANE-A/P from non-certified ER nurse; (2) protocol deviations in history-taking, evidence collection sequence, photographic documentation, kit packaging; (3) time-since-assault and DNA collection-window sensitivity (probative-value erosion at 72-120+ hours); (4) contamination factors documented or undocumented (patient bathing, urinating, eating, clothing changes between assault and exam); (5) documentation gaps (incomplete body diagrams, missing photographs, inconsistent injury measurements); (6) examiner training currency and supervisory oversight (was this the examiner's first SAK? when was last training?); (7) cross-contamination between samples during collection. "Addressed" means: SANE chart and kit documentation obtained and audited (`dw-sex-offense-specialist`), examiner credentials verified, protocol deviations identified and preserved for cross, defense SANE expert retained if methodology is contested (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), Daubert/Foret motion filed if examiner's qualifications fall short of testimony scope. Kit contents and post-collection chain tracked under R-02. Broader medical evidence (hospital records, follow-up care, pre-existing conditions) tracked under R-06. General expert framework challenges tracked under U-09. SAK chain of custody after collection tracked under U-04 and R-02. Mark N/A if no SANE exam was conducted (historical/cold case, delayed disclosure, child forensic interview-only cases — see R-09).* |
| R-02 | Sexual Assault Kit / DNA | *SAK and DNA analysis specific to sexual assault prosecutions — covers the kit's life from post-collection through trial presentation. Attack surfaces: (1) chain of custody from hospital → law enforcement → crime lab → court (multi-agency handoffs, gap risk); (2) backlog and delayed testing (degradation, contamination, storage condition concerns); (3) storage conditions (temperature, humidity, light exposure); (4) DNA mixture interpretation in sexual assault context (PCAST 2016 criticisms — defendant DNA + complainant DNA + possible consensual-partner DNA + contamination); (5) touch DNA vs. seminal fluid DNA distinctions (touch DNA supports innocent-presence and transfer defenses); (6) statistical inflation (random match probabilities and likelihood ratios in mixtures); (7) Y-STR profile limitations (matches all paternally-related males — much weaker than autosomal STR match); (8) probabilistic genotyping software black-box issues (TrueAllele, STRmix — source code access disputes, due process and Confrontation challenges); (9) Confrontation Clause / analyst-availability — was the testifying witness the actual analyst, or a lab supervisor? (`Bullcoming v. New Mexico`, `Melendez-Diaz v. Massachusetts`, `Williams v. Illinois`); (10) consensual-partner sample collection adequacy (was the complainant's consensual partner DNA collected for elimination purposes — if not, mixture interpretation is compromised). "Addressed" means: SAK chain of custody fully documented and audited (`dw-chain-of-custody-auditor`), lab reports obtained and analyzed by defense, defense DNA expert retained if mixture or LCN issues exist (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), Confrontation Clause objections preserved with subpoenas to actual analysts, probabilistic genotyping source-code discovery demanded if applicable, statistical reporting scrutinized for inflation (`dw-sex-offense-specialist`). SANE exam-conduct issues tracked under R-01. General chain of custody framework tracked under U-04. General expert framework challenges tracked under U-09. DNA analysis methodology technical issues also informed by H-02 work in homicide cases. Mark N/A if no SAK was collected or no DNA evidence is in the case.* |
| R-03 | Consent Defense | *Affirmative defense framework for adult sexual assault cases under Louisiana law — R.S. 14:41 (rape, requiring lack of "lawful consent"), R.S. 14:42 (first-degree/aggravated rape circumstances), R.S. 14:42.1 (second-degree/forcible rape — prevented from resisting), R.S. 14:43 (third-degree/simple rape — incapacity to consent), R.S. 14:43.1 (sexual battery), R.S. 14:43.3 (oral sexual battery). Note: Louisiana is a "lack of consent" jurisdiction — the State must prove absence of consent rather than the defense having to prove affirmative consent. Attack surfaces and evidence categories: (1) prior relationship and history of consensual encounters (rape shield analysis required — see R-04); (2) electronic communications before/during/after encounter (texts, dating app messages, social media — see U-11); (3) witness observations of pre-encounter interaction; (4) behavior consistent with consent (voluntary travel to defendant's location, voluntary removal of clothing, voluntary participation indicators); (5) absence of physical resistance or injury (carefully framed — non-resistance is not legal consent, but is evidentially relevant to State's force/threat element); (6) post-encounter behavior (delayed disclosure timing, continued voluntary contact — coordinates with R-05); (7) withdrawal-of-consent scenarios (consent given but allegedly withdrawn mid-act). Strategic decision point: consent defense is typically a binary strategic choice — full consent theory vs. complete denial. Mixing consent with identity defense ("I didn't do it, but if I did, she wanted it") is generally untenable before juries. "Addressed" means: strategic choice on defense theory (consent vs. denial vs. mistaken belief) made and locked in, all consent-supporting evidence catalogued and authenticated (`dw-sex-offense-specialist`), rape shield analysis completed and Art. 412 motion filed where applicable (R-04), electronic communications authenticated and admissibility preserved (U-11), trial themes aligned with consent theory, victim credibility cross-examination drafted in coordination with R-08. Rape shield admissibility tracked under R-04. Delayed disclosure analysis tracked under R-05. Incapacity-to-consent (involuntary intoxication, unconsciousness) tracked under R-07. Victim credibility generally tracked under R-08. Communications-as-evidence authentication tracked under U-11. Mark N/A if consent is not the defense theory (identity defense, total denial, child victim under 13, defendant's exclusive theory is incapacity-only).* |
| R-04 | Rape Shield (Art. 412) | *Evidentiary framework governing admissibility of complainant's prior sexual conduct under La. C.E. Art. 412. Procedural requirements: (1) written motion with offer of proof filed at least 15 days before trial (Art. 412(C)(1)) — calendar-critical deadline; (2) in camera hearing to determine admissibility (Art. 412(C)(2)); (3) specific instances only — reputation and opinion evidence about complainant's sexual character is generally inadmissible. Statutory exceptions for admissibility (Art. 412(B)): (1) 412(B)(1) — past sexual behavior with persons other than the accused, offered to show another source for semen, pregnancy, disease, or injury; (2) 412(B)(2) — past sexual behavior with the accused, offered on the issue of consent; (3) 412(B)(3) — constitutional exception when exclusion would violate defendant's confrontation or due process rights (`Davis v. Alaska` framework). Attack surfaces: (1) identifying which 412(B) exception applies and articulating it in the motion; (2) drafting a sufficiently specific offer of proof (vague allegations will be excluded); (3) preserving constitutional fallback arguments under 412(B)(3) where statutory exceptions don't fit; (4) preparing for in camera hearing with admissible specific instances and corroborating evidence; (5) coordinating with R-03 consent defense theory so the Art. 412 evidence supports the substantive defense narrative. "Addressed" means: Art. 412 analysis completed against the defense theory, written motion drafted with specific offer of proof and filed at least 15 days before trial (`dw-sex-offense-specialist`, `dw-pretrial-motion-library`), in camera hearing requested and prepared, constitutional 412(B)(3) fallback briefed if statutory exceptions don't fit, ruling preserved for appeal regardless of outcome. Substantive consent defense theory tracked under R-03. Prior false accusations (analyzed under Art. 608 credibility framework, not Art. 412) tracked under R-11. General victim credibility tracked under R-08. Mark N/A if no rape shield issue is present (consent not at issue, no prior sexual conduct evidence sought).* |
| R-05 | Delayed Disclosure | *Analysis of timing between alleged assault and complainant's report to police, and the State's expert testimony explaining delayed disclosure as "consistent with trauma response." Defense attack surfaces: (1) State expert reliability under Daubert/Foret — including CSAAS (Child Sexual Abuse Accommodation Syndrome, originally proposed by Roland Summit in 1983 as a CLINICAL concept, NOT a diagnostic tool — courts increasingly limit its admissibility when offered to prove abuse); (2) trauma-informed interviewing expert testimony that blends valid science with overreach claims that delayed disclosure proves abuse; (3) cherry-picking of disclosure-timing research literature by State experts; (4) independent indicators of motive to fabricate during the disclosure window (custody dispute, financial dispute, jealousy, retaliation, attention-seeking); (5) memory reliability issues in long-delayed cases (repressed/recovered memory criticism post-1990s memory wars, memory contamination from repeated retelling, suggestive therapy questioning, family discussions, media exposure, source monitoring errors); (6) inconsistencies between initial disclosure account and subsequent statements (which disclosure version is the "true" one). Strategic considerations: adult cases vs. child cases require different defense framing — adult delayed-disclosure can be aggressively contested with motive-to-fabricate evidence; child delayed-disclosure framing must be more nuanced because some delay is developmentally expected. "Addressed" means: disclosure timeline reconstructed, all disclosure versions catalogued and compared (`dw-witness-statement-analyzer`), State expert qualifications and methodology audited under Daubert/Foret if delayed-disclosure or CSAAS testimony is anticipated (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`, U-09), defense expert retained if memory contamination or long-delay issues are case-dispositive, motive-to-fabricate evidence developed during the disclosure window, cross-examination outline drafted for State's trauma expert (`dw-sex-offense-specialist`). General victim credibility tracked under R-08. Child forensic interview protocol issues tracked under R-09. General expert framework challenges tracked under U-09. Mark N/A if disclosure was prompt (within hours of the alleged assault) and no delayed-disclosure expert testimony is anticipated.* |
| R-06 | Medical Evidence | *Medical evidence outside the SANE exam and SAK — covers the complainant's broader medical record context. Scope includes: (1) hospital records before, during, or after the alleged assault (ER visits, follow-up GYN appointments, urgent care visits); (2) pre-existing medical conditions affecting interpretation of physical findings (prior surgeries, prior trauma, anatomical variations, prior childbirth); (3) medical records contradicting or corroborating the complainant's account of the assault; (4) inconsistent histories given to different healthcare providers (impeachment material); (5) subsequent medical visits where the complainant's account may have evolved; (6) pregnancy / STI test results; (7) mental health and counseling records (privilege analysis required — see below). Defense attack surfaces: (a) lack of genital trauma significance — State will argue absence of injury is "consistent with assault" because consensual sex rarely causes injury; defense must contextualize without reinforcing State's framing; (b) healing-timeline analysis when injuries are documented — does the healing stage at exam time corroborate or contradict the alleged time of assault?; (c) pre-existing condition explanations for findings the State attributes to assault; (d) inconsistent injury patterns vs. complainant's account; (e) cross-provider history inconsistencies. Privileged records framework: HIPAA federal, La. C.E. Art. 510 (healthcare provider privilege), La. R.S. 13:3734.1 (mental health privilege) — accessing mental health and counseling records requires in camera review procedures and specific subpoena practice. "Addressed" means: full medical record subpoenas issued to all relevant providers, subsequent medical visits identified and reviewed, pre-existing conditions catalogued, defense medical expert retained if injury interpretation is contested (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), inconsistent provider histories preserved as impeachment material, privileged record access pursued through proper procedures where applicable, Brady demand made for all medical records the State has obtained or has access to (U-05). SANE exam-conduct issues tracked under R-01. SAK and DNA results tracked under R-02. Brady aspects of withheld medical records tracked under U-05. Failure to obtain relevant medical records tracked under U-12. Victim credibility cross-examination drafted in coordination with R-08. Mark N/A if no medical records exist beyond the SANE exam itself.* |
| R-07 | Incapacity / Inability to Consent | *Statutory framework under R.S. 14:43 (third-degree rape / simple rape) — sexual intercourse where the complainant is "incapable of resisting or of understanding the nature of the act by reason of a stupor or abnormal condition of the mind produced by an intoxicating agent or by any cause." Distinct prosecutorial theory from force-based rape (R.S. 14:42, 14:42.1). Defense attack surfaces: (1) anterograde amnesia (blackout) vs. legal incapacity — alcoholic blackouts cause memory loss WITHOUT necessarily causing incapacity; complainants frequently assume "I must have been incapable because I don't remember," but the science distinguishes amnesia from incapacity; this is often the central defense argument; (2) State's reliance on complainant's after-the-fact self-assessment of intoxication level; (3) toxicology timing failures — date-rape drugs (Rohypnol, GHB, ketamine, scopolamine) have very narrow detection windows (typically hours, not days), and most SAKs are collected too late to detect; hair testing captures longer windows but is rarely done; (4) State expert overreach in pharmacology/toxicology testimony — claims of certainty beyond what BAC and metabolite data support; (5) missing baseline tolerance data — BAC alone doesn't establish incapacity without individual tolerance baseline; (6) polypharmacy and alternative explanations — prescription medications, illness, fatigue, prior trauma, sleep deprivation may explain symptoms attributed to incapacity; (7) impairment vs. incapacity distinction — Louisiana law requires more than mere impairment; (8) drug-facilitated sexual assault (DFSA) — covertly vs. self-administered distinction matters legally and forensically; absence of toxicology evidence is itself a defense argument when the State's theory requires drug facilitation. "Addressed" means: medical and toxicology records obtained and analyzed, complainant's intoxication account compared to objective indicators (BAC if measured, witness observations, video), defense pharmacology/toxicology expert retained if State plans expert testimony (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), anterograde amnesia / blackout literature briefed for cross-examination of State's expert (`dw-sex-offense-specialist`), DFSA hair-testing demanded if applicable and timing supports it, jury instructions on incapacity element drafted (`dw-jury-instructions-builder`). Substantive consent defense theory tracked under R-03. General victim credibility tracked under R-08. Child incapacity (age-based, not intoxication-based) issues are statutory under R.S. 14:42 and tracked under R-09 forensic interviewing protocols. General expert framework challenges tracked under U-09. Mark N/A if incapacity is not the State's theory of non-consent.* |
| R-08 | Victim Credibility | *Sex-offense-specific credibility analysis of the complainant, who plays a dual role as victim and primary State witness. Distinct from general witness credibility (U-06) because sexual assault credibility implicates rape shield (R-04), prior allegations (R-11), delayed disclosure (R-05), and trauma-narrative framing in ways general witness analysis does not. Cross-examination dynamics differ — aggressive credibility attacks on alleged victims must be calibrated for jury reception, with trial-theme alignment essential. Analytical dimensions: (1) internal inconsistencies — within the complainant's own statements over time (initial outcry, 911 call, SANE history, police statements, grand jury, prior testimony, deposition, trial testimony); (2) external inconsistencies — against witnesses, physical evidence, electronic communications, video, medical records; (3) motive to fabricate — custody/divorce dispute, financial dispute, jealousy, revenge, attention-seeking, immigration status concerns, criminal exposure (witnesses sometimes accuse to deflect from their own legal issues), retaliation; (4) bias — relationship dynamics with defendant, family pressure, social/religious community pressure, peer dynamics; (5) substance use affecting the account — variations based on intoxication state at different recountings; (6) mental health factors where admissible after privilege analysis (R-06); (7) trauma-narrative framing — State's expected effort to recast inconsistencies as "consistent with trauma response" (coordinates with R-05). Strategic considerations: aggressive complainant cross requires calibration for jury reception — a hostile cross can backfire even when factually justified. Effective credibility work usually combines a respectful demeanor with surgical inconsistency-by-inconsistency challenges and powerful documentary impeachment. "Addressed" means: complainant Witness Analysis Card built (`dw-witness-statement-analyzer`), every recounting catalogued and compared, all inconsistencies mapped (internal and external), motive-to-fabricate evidence developed and corroborated, complainant placed on Witness Threat Matrix with damage and vulnerability scores (`dw-witness-threat-matrix`), cross-examination outline drafted with calibrated theme and impeachment sequencing (`dw-cross-exam-architect`), trial themes aligned with credibility theory. General witness/credibility framework tracked under U-06. Rape shield admissibility tracked under R-04. Delayed disclosure framework tracked under R-05. Prior false allegations tracked under R-11. Mental health record privilege analysis tracked under R-06. Mark N/A only if there is no complainant in the case (extremely rare — typically only in cases involving incapacitated victims who cannot testify, where credibility is not the primary issue).* |
| R-09 | Child Forensic Interviewing | *Forensic interview audit for child sexual assault cases — focused on the child complainant's structured interview, typically conducted at a Children's Advocacy Center (CAC). Protocol authorities: NICHD Protocol (National Institute of Child Health and Human Development structured interview, research-validated), RATAC and successor CornerHouse Forensic Interview Protocol, ChildFirst®, APSAC Practice Guidelines, and Louisiana CAC institutional protocols (e.g., Children's Advocacy Center of Southwest Louisiana for Calcasieu cases). Louisiana statutory framework: R.S. 15:440.1 et seq. (tender years exception — videotaped statements of children under 14 in sex offense cases admissible with specific procedural requirements). Defense attack surfaces: (1) interviewer training and certification — many "forensic interviewers" have minimal formal training or are former social workers without specialized credentialing; (2) suggestive questioning — leading questions, multiple-choice questions, "tag" questions, presupposition embedded in questions; (3) prior questioning before the formal interview — caregivers, teachers, therapists, prior law enforcement contact may have contaminated the account; (4) multiple-interview problems — number and sequencing of interviews increases suggestion and contamination risk; (5) recording adequacy — full audio/video vs. partial recording; chain of custody on the recording; (6) developmental appropriateness — questioning calibrated to child's age and developmental level; (7) suggestibility research application — Ceci & Bruck and other empirical literature on child suggestibility, source monitoring errors, false memories from suggestive interviewing; (8) Confrontation Clause issues — child witness availability for trial under `Crawford v. Washington` and `Maryland v. Craig` (closed-circuit testimony framework); (9) hearsay exception cumulative-evidence concerns — La. C.E. Art. 803(2) excited utterance, Art. 803(4) medical diagnosis, Art. 804 unavailability, plus tender years exception. "Addressed" means: forensic interview video and transcripts obtained and audited (`dw-child-forensic-interview-auditor`), interviewer credentials verified, protocol deviations identified and preserved for cross, prior questioning chain reconstructed (caregivers, therapists, prior interviews), suggestibility expert retained if interview reliability is case-dispositive (`dw-expert-witness-evaluator`), tender years exception challenges drafted under La. R.S. 15:440.1 procedural requirements, Confrontation Clause objections preserved, Daubert/Foret motion filed against State's forensic interviewer expert testimony if applicable. Adult complainant credibility tracked under R-08. Delayed disclosure framework (including child-specific framing) tracked under R-05. Defendant interrogation issues tracked under U-08. General expert framework challenges tracked under U-09. Mark N/A in adult-complainant cases (no child forensic interview at issue).* |
| R-10 | Physical Evidence / Scene (Sex Offense) | *Sex-offense-specific scene and physical evidence analysis — integrates scene processing and physical evidence work that's distinct from the SANE exam (R-01) and the SAK (R-02). Scope: (1) scene of alleged assault — bedroom, hotel room, vehicle, residence, outdoor location — including processing adequacy, photographic documentation, scene security and preservation; (2) clothing recovery — clothing worn during the alleged assault, tested separately from SAK clothing; absence-of-damage analysis (intact zippers, buttons, fabric) inconsistent with forcible removal; (3) bedding — sheets, blankets, pillowcases as DNA/fluid recovery surfaces; absence of expected DNA where State's theory predicts presence; (4) condoms and condom packaging — recovery (or non-recovery) significance; (5) vehicle scenes — full vehicle processing often deficient when assault alleged in vehicle; (6) outdoor scenes — preservation challenges from weather, time degradation, contamination. Defense attack surfaces: (a) scene processing failures (photos not taken, scene not secured, surface swabs not collected); (b) selective collection (police collecting only State-favorable evidence and missing exculpatory items); (c) delayed scene processing when complainants disclose hours or days later; (d) defense-needed scene evidence the State didn't collect (clothing absence-of-damage, undisturbed bed, condom in trash demonstrating consensual encounter); (e) documentation inconsistencies between scene photos and complainant's account. Strategic role: R-10 work often drives defense scene re-visits, independent photography, witness canvassing of the location, and subpoenas for surveillance video from nearby properties. "Addressed" means: scene photographs and processing reports obtained and audited, scene re-visited by defense investigator if access available (`dw-defense-investigator-tasking`), selective-collection issues documented, defense-narrative scene evidence preserved (absence-of-damage clothing, undisturbed scene indicators, surveillance from nearby properties), chain of custody verified on all scene-recovered items (`dw-chain-of-custody-auditor`), DNA results from scene-recovered items audited under R-02 framework (`dw-sex-offense-specialist`). General scene-level processing tracked under U-03. SANE exam-conduct issues tracked under R-01. SAK and DNA results tracked under R-02. Item-level chain of custody tracked under U-04. Investigation gap analysis tracked under U-12. Mark N/A if no scene was processed (e.g., delayed-disclosure cases where the original location is no longer accessible or available for analysis).* |
| R-11 | Prior False Allegations (Art. 608) | *Evidence of complainant's prior false sexual accusations or pattern of similar fabricated claims — operates under La. C.E. Art. 608 (credibility impeachment by specific instances of conduct), NOT under Art. 412 rape shield. CRITICAL DISTINCTION: prior false accusations are credibility evidence governed by Art. 608, not sexual-conduct evidence governed by Art. 412 — frequently misapplied by counsel filing Art. 412 motions and losing. Where applicable, may also implicate La. C.E. Art. 404(B)(2) character evidence rules and constitutional confrontation principles (`Davis v. Alaska`). Categories of admissible prior allegation evidence: (1) demonstrably false prior accusations (recanted by complainant, judicially determined false, independently contradicted by physical evidence); (2) pattern of similar false claims showing scheme or plan; (3) prior accusations followed by recantation. Categories generally NOT admissible without higher showing: (a) merely unfounded prior complaints (unfounded ≠ proven false); (b) prior allegations that were investigated and dropped without recantation; (c) prior allegations later substantiated by acquittal alone. Procedural framework: Art. 608(B) generally prohibits extrinsic evidence of specific instances but allows cross-examination on specific instances; in limine motion typically required for admissibility ruling; in camera hearing may be requested. Constitutional fallback: confrontation-rights exception under `Davis v. Alaska` framework (similar to Art. 412(B)(3) constitutional carve-out). Defense attack surfaces and investigation: (i) identifying prior accusations through social media, court records, prior law enforcement contact, school records, family/community knowledge; (ii) building "demonstrably false" record (recantations on the record, judicial findings, physical-evidence contradictions, witnesses to recantation); (iii) drafting in limine motion under Art. 608 framework; (iv) preparing for in camera hearing; (v) preserving constitutional confrontation argument as fallback. "Addressed" means: prior allegation investigation completed (`dw-defense-investigator-tasking`), demonstrably-false threshold analysis completed for each identified prior accusation, in limine motion drafted under Art. 608 framework (NOT Art. 412), supporting documentation assembled (recantations, judicial findings, contradicting evidence), constitutional confrontation fallback briefed if Art. 608 framework is too restrictive, ruling preserved for appeal regardless of outcome (`dw-sex-offense-specialist`). Substantive victim credibility analysis tracked under R-08. Rape shield (prior sexual conduct, NOT prior false accusations) tracked under R-04. General witness credibility framework tracked under U-06. Mark N/A if no prior allegations (true, false, or unfounded) by the complainant exist.* |
