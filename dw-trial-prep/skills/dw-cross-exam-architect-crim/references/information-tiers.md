# Information Tiers (Step 1)

Step 0.58 has already pulled the analyzed upstream material — DMAR, auditor reports, Brady/Giglio, witness card, prior sworn testimony, witness history. These three tiers cover the remainder.

## Essential — do not draft without these

1. **Witness type** — law enforcement, expert, civilian, cooperator, custodian. Determines the Step 3 module.
2. **Charges** — all counts with statutory citations. Cross is bounded by what the State must prove.
3. **Case theme, one sentence** — e.g. *"This case is about shortcuts and sloppy police work."* This becomes the spine of every chapter header and must appear in at least one chapter title.
4. **Defense theory** — what happened, from the defense's perspective.
5. **Key concessions needed** — what the attorney needs this witness to give. A cross without a target is a fishing trip.

If any of these is missing, present the ranked gap checklist and ask. Do not draft.

## Strategic — request if not provided

- Prior rulings on scope, motions in limine, or suppression orders affecting this witness — these convert a `[SCOPE FLAG]` from speculative to concrete
- Trial strategy goal: reasonable doubt vs. full exculpation. Changes whether chapters aim at concession or destruction
- The attorney's preferred cross style: destructive vs. incremental concession
- Whether sequestration was requested and granted (La. C.E. art. 615) — an unrequested order is a waived advantage, and a granted one opens the case-agent angle
- Jury composition or venue factors bearing on how a given attack lands
- Expected order of witnesses, and whether this witness follows one whose testimony they may have heard

## Contextual — gather from the file

- Prior inconsistent statements across all sources — auto-scanned at Step 5, but note any the attorney already knows about
- Discovery gaps expected for this witness type but absent — drives the Step 6 report and the omission chapters
- Impeachment material already identified upstream at Step 0.58 that has not yet been converted into a question sequence
- Whether any exhibit this cross depends on has been ruled inadmissible or is subject to a pending motion

## Presentation rule

Present everything missing as a **single ranked checklist**, most blocking first, rather than asking one question at a time. The attorney can answer six items in one pass; six separate questions across six turns is how prep stalls.
