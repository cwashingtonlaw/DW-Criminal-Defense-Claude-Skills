### The 16th JDC trap — read this every time

The 16th Judicial District comprises **Iberia, St. Martin, and St. Mary** parishes, but it **straddles two appellate circuits**. Iberia and St. Martin appeal to the **Third Circuit**; St. Mary appeals to the **First Circuit**.

Practical consequence for cross-examination: an evidentiary ruling that is settled Third Circuit law is only persuasive authority in a St. Mary Parish trial, and vice versa. When building a chapter that depends on a contested evidentiary point, confirm which circuit's authority controls before relying on it to justify a line of questioning to the court.

### The two Fifth Circuits — write which one you mean

**"5th Circuit" is ambiguous in a Louisiana criminal document, because there are two.**

- **U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit** — governs federal matters in all three Louisiana districts. This is the firm's federal default and it is correct.
- **Louisiana Fifth Circuit Court of Appeal** — Jefferson, St. Charles, St. James, and St. John the Baptist parishes. **No parish in the firm's footprint is in it**, so a state-court citation to the Louisiana Fifth Circuit is almost certainly a mistake in a D&W matter.

**Convention:** write `5th Cir.` for the federal court and `La. 5th Cir.` for the state court. Never a bare "5th Circuit" in a deliverable — the reader cannot tell which you mean, and the two carry different authority.

For **state** appellate authority, use the parish's own circuit from §1: First, Third, or Fourth.

## 2. Scope of Cross-Examination — Louisiana is WIDE OPEN

**La. C.E. art. 611(B):** *"A witness may be cross-examined on any matter relevant to any issue in the case, including credibility."*

In a **Louisiana criminal trial**, cross-examination is **not limited to the scope of direct**. This is the single most important tactical difference between state and federal practice in this skill, and it governs how the `[SCOPE FLAG]` guardrail operates.

### When to apply `[SCOPE FLAG]`

Apply the flag **only** when one of these is true:

1. **The case is in federal court.** Fed. R. Evid. 611(b) limits cross to the subject matter of direct plus credibility, with the court's discretion to allow more. In W.D. La., E.D. La., or M.D. La., the scope objection is live.
2. **A specific in limine ruling or protective order limits this witness.** Cite the ruling in the NOTES column.
3. **The question strays into an area independently barred** — privileged material, La. C.E. art. 412 rape shield, sealed juvenile records, a suppressed statement being used substantively.

### When NOT to apply `[SCOPE FLAG]`

Do **not** flag a question in a Louisiana state criminal trial merely because the subject was not raised on direct. That is proper cross under art. 611(B). Flagging it trains the attorney to abandon good questions and concedes ground the State is not entitled to.

If the State objects "beyond the scope" in state court, the answer is art. 611(B) — put that citation in the NOTES column of any chapter likely to draw the objection.

**Civil caveat (not applicable to this criminal skill):** art. 611(B) contains a narrower rule for civil cases where an adverse party calls a witness for specific matters. This skill is criminal-only; ignore the civil proviso.

## 3. Impeachment by Prior Conviction — use art. 609.1, NOT art. 609

**La. C.E. art. 609.1 is the criminal rule. La. C.E. art. 609 is the civil rule.** Citing art. 609 in a criminal cross-examination outline is an error. Always cite **art. 609.1**.

| Subsection | Rule | Cross-exam use |
|---|---|---|
| **609.1(A)** | "In a criminal case, every witness by testifying subjects himself to examination relative to his criminal convictions, subject to limitations set forth below" | Every witness who takes the stand is open to conviction impeachment — no balancing prerequisite as in FRE 609 |
| **609.1(B)** | Only **convictions** are admissible. No inquiry into arrest, arrest warrant, indictment, prosecution, or acquittal | Hard stop — do not draft a question about a State witness's arrest history absent another theory (e.g., bias/deal under *Giglio*) |
| **609.1(C)** | Normally only the **fact** of conviction, **name** of offense, **date**, and **sentence**. Details become admissible when: (1) witness denies the conviction or denies recollection; (2) witness testifies to exculpatory facts or circumstances surrounding it; (3) probative value outweighs unfair prejudice/confusion/misleading | **This is a door-opening sequence.** Draft the chapter so the witness's expected denial or self-serving explanation opens the door to the underlying facts |
| **609.1(D)** | Pending appeal does not exclude the conviction; goes to weight | Do not abandon impeachment because the conviction is on appeal |
| **609.1(E)** | Pardon or annulment **based on a finding of innocence** bars the impeachment | Check the basis of any pardon before relying on the conviction |
| **609.1(F)** | Juvenile adjudications generally inadmissible (exception: habitual offender proceedings) | Do not draft juvenile-record questions for a trial cross |

**Drafting rule:** whenever a chapter uses a prior conviction, structure it as a 609.1(C) sequence — establish the fact/name/date/sentence first, then hold the details in reserve in the NOTES column, marked: `DETAILS HELD IN RESERVE — admissible only if witness denies or explains (La. C.E. art. 609.1(C)(1)-(2))`.

**Flagged:** La. C.E. arts. 609.1 and 611 are cited from current published sources as of this skill revision. Louisiana amends its evidence code regularly — verify the current text before relying on either in a filed pleading or a contested evidentiary argument.

## 3.5 Character for Truthfulness — Louisiana BARS What Federal Practice Allows

**Flagged:** La. C.E. art. 608 is quoted from a current published source as of this skill revision. Louisiana amends its evidence code frequently — `[VERIFY current text]` before relying on this in a contested evidentiary argument or a filed pleading.

**This is the most dangerous state/federal trap in cross-examination, and it runs the opposite direction from what most attorneys expect.**

**La. C.E. art. 608(B):** *"Particular acts, vices, or courses of conduct of a witness may not be inquired into or proved by extrinsic evidence for the purpose of attacking his character for truthfulness, other than conviction of crime as provided in Articles 609 and 609.1 or as constitutionally required."*

| | Louisiana state court | Federal court |
|---|---|---|
| Specific instances of conduct probative of truthfulness | **BARRED** — art. 608(B) | **PERMITTED on cross** — FRE 608(b) (extrinsic proof still barred) |
| Reputation / opinion as to truthfulness | Permitted within art. 608(A) limits | Permitted, FRE 608(a) |
| Prior convictions | Permitted — **art. 609.1** | Permitted — FRE 609 |

### Drafting rule

In a **Louisiana state criminal trial**, do NOT draft questions like:
- "You lied to your employer about your timesheet, didn't you?"
- "You've been dishonest on a job application before, haven't you?"
- "You falsified a report in an unrelated matter, correct?"

Each is proper federal cross under FRE 608(b) and **improper** in Louisiana state court under art. 608(B) unless it (a) is a conviction under art. 609.1, or (b) is constitutionally required — the *Davis v. Alaska* / confrontation-clause route.

### What art. 608(B) does NOT bar — art. 607(C)

Art. 608(B) governs attacks on **general character for truthfulness**. It is not a general ban on credibility questioning, and reading it that way gives away too much.

**La. C.E. art. 607(C):** *"Except as otherwise provided by legislation, a party, to attack the credibility of a witness, may examine him concerning any matter having a reasonable tendency to disprove the truthfulness **or accuracy** of his testimony."*

That is broad, and it is the route most often overlooked. Attacking the **accuracy of the account this witness just gave** is not the same as attacking his character for truthfulness. Perception conditions, memory decay, intoxication, distraction, assumptions, and internal implausibility are all art. 607(C) territory and art. 608(B) does not reach them.

`[UNVERIFIED]` — the boundary between art. 607(C) intrinsic attack and the art. 608(B) bar is contested territory and the circuits draw it differently. Where a chapter sits near that line, research the controlling circuit for the parish before trial. Route through `dw-case-law-researcher-crim`.

### The four lawful routes to the same ground in Louisiana

When the impeachment material is conduct rather than a conviction, do not abandon it — re-route it:

1. **Bias, interest, corruption, or defect of capacity.** **Art. 607(D)(1)**: *"Extrinsic evidence to show a witness' bias, interest, corruption, or defect of capacity is admissible."* Conduct showing the witness has a deal, a grudge, or exposure of his own is admissible as **bias**, not as character — and may be proved extrinsically. This is the strongest route; take it whenever it is available.
2. **Accuracy of this testimony.** **Art. 607(C)** — any matter with a reasonable tendency to disprove the truthfulness or accuracy of the testimony given. Aim at the account, not the man.
3. **Prior inconsistent statement.** Art. 613 — an inconsistency is a statement, not a character trait. Foundation is required only before **extrinsic** proof, not before asking.
4. **Constitutionally required.** Where exclusion would violate confrontation, preserve the issue and argue it. Flag: `[ART. 608(B) — arguing constitutional exception; preserve record]` and apply `error-preservation-protocol.md`.

**Auto-check.** Before finalizing any chapter attacking credibility, ask: is this a conviction (609.1), a bias fact (607(D)(1)), an attack on the accuracy of this testimony (607(C)), or a prior inconsistent statement (613)? If it is none of the four and the forum is Louisiana state court, art. 608(B) probably bars it. Mark the chapter `[608(B) REVIEW REQUIRED]` rather than deleting it — and attach the preservation bullet so the ground is on the record if the court agrees with the State.

## 4. State vs. Federal Cross-Examination — Quick Toggle

| Issue | Louisiana state court | Federal court (W.D./E.D./M.D. La.) |
|---|---|---|
| Scope of cross | **La. C.E. art. 611(B)** — any matter relevant to any issue, including credibility | **FRE 611(b)** — limited to subject of direct + credibility, court's discretion to expand |
| Prior convictions | **La. C.E. art. 609.1** | **FRE 609** — different balancing, 10-year limit, different treatment of dishonesty crimes |
| Character for truthfulness — **major divergence, see §3.5** | **La. C.E. arts. 607, 608** — art. 608(B) **BARS** inquiry into particular acts, vices, or courses of conduct | **FRE 608(b)** — **PERMITS** cross-examination on specific instances of conduct probative of truthfulness |
| Prior inconsistent statement foundation | **La. C.E. art. 613** | **FRE 613** — historically more permissive, but FRE 613(b) was amended effective Dec. 1, 2024 to require that the witness be given the opportunity to explain or deny **while testifying**, subject to the court's discretion. `[VERIFY current text before relying on the contrast]` |
| Expert scope / reliability | **La. C.E. arts. 702–705** | **FRE 702** — *Daubert* applies directly |
| Reports of examinations and tests (lab/forensic reports) | **La. ## 5. Jurisdiction Resolution Protocol

Before drafting any chapter:

1. Pull `{{PARISH}}` and `{{COURT}}` from the active Case Brain.
2. Look up the row in §1 above. Record the trial court, the **La. appellate circuit**, and the federal district.
3. State all three in the Step 2 Pre-Draft Confirmation block. For state matters name the parish's Louisiana circuit specifically rather than leaving it implicit.
4. If the parish is **not** in the §1 table, do not guess the circuit. Prompt the attorney: *"This matter is in [Parish] Parish, which is outside the firm's usual footprint. Confirm the judicial district and Court of Appeal before I cite controlling authority."*
5. If the matter is federal, switch the entire evidentiary toggle in §4 to the federal column and note it in every chapter's NOTES.
