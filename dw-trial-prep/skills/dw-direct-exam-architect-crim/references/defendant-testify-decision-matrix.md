# Defendant Testify-or-Not Decision Matrix

Consumed by `dw-direct-exam-architect-crim/SKILL.md` STEP 0.6 (Defense Witness Lineup Audit) and STEP 3 (Defendant module). Used pretrial to make the testify-or-not decision and again on the morning of trial to reaffirm.

The decision is the **defendant's alone** (Rock v. Arkansas, 483 U.S. 44 (1987)). Counsel advises. This matrix is counsel's analytical framework — it does not replace the defendant's choice but informs it.

---

## How to Use This Matrix

1. **Rate each factor 1–5** based on the strength of that factor for this case (1 = weak / does not apply, 5 = strong / fully applies).
2. **Sum the FOR factors.**
3. **Sum the AGAINST factors.**
4. **Compute FOR minus AGAINST.**
   - **Score > +10:** Strong indication FOR testifying.
   - **Score +1 to +10:** Marginal FOR — consider testifying with heavy rehearsal.
   - **Score 0:** Coin flip — testify only if the defendant strongly wants to and the temperament factor is solid.
   - **Score -1 to -10:** Marginal AGAINST — consider not testifying.
   - **Score < -10:** Strong indication AGAINST testifying.
5. **Apply the override rules** (below).
6. **Document the decision** in writing, signed by attorney and defendant.
7. **Reaffirm on morning of trial** after the State rests — facts may have shifted.

The score is advisory. Override is allowed for strong attorney intuition or strong client preference. If overriding, document the override basis.

---

## Factors Weighing FOR Testimony

### F1 — Affirmative defense requiring defendant explanation (rate 1–5)

Some affirmative defenses are practically un-presentable without the defendant testifying:
- **Self-defense:** State of mind, perception of threat, immediacy of belief — usually requires defendant
- **Consent (sex offense):** Defendant's understanding of consent at the time
- **Duress / coercion:** Defendant's belief in threat
- **Necessity:** Defendant's perception of imminent harm and available alternatives
- **Entrapment:** Defendant's pre-disposition / lack thereof
- **Insanity / diminished capacity:** Defendant's mental state (often paired with expert testimony)
- **Lack of intent (specific intent crimes):** Defendant's actual mental state
- **Mistake of fact:** Defendant's actual belief

Rate 5 if the defense is one of the above and the affirmative defense is central. Rate 1 if no affirmative defense or the defense is reasonable-doubt-only.

### F2 — No alternative way to present defense narrative (rate 1–5)

Even without a formal affirmative defense, the defense narrative may require the defendant's voice. Rate 5 if no other witness, no documentary evidence, no expert can convey the defense story without the defendant. Rate 1 if other witnesses and documents fully establish the defense narrative.

### F3 — Client articulate, credible, controllable on cross (rate 1–5)

Temperament factor. Rate based on:
- Verbal clarity and articulation
- Ability to listen to questions and answer them
- Emotional regulation under pressure
- Ability to acknowledge mistakes without minimizing
- Body language credibility (eye contact, posture, demeanor)
- Performance in prior depositions, statements, hearings
- Mock cross performance

Rate 5 if the client is a strong communicator who can stay disciplined on cross. Rate 1 if the client is evasive, talkative, argumentative, or emotionally volatile under pressure.

> **Critical:** A high score on this factor cannot rescue a case where the substance hurts (high AGAINST score). A great communicator who has nothing helpful to say should not testify. But a low score here is a near-veto regardless of substance.

### F4 — No prior convictions admissible under La. C.E. art. 609.1 (rate 1–5)

Or convictions successfully sanitized via motion in limine. Rate based on:
- Number and recency of priors
- Crimen falsi status (theft, fraud, perjury — most damaging)
- Sanitization outcome
- "Felony conviction" generic vs. specific offense identification

Rate 5 if no admissible priors. Rate 4 if priors are old/sanitized to "felony conviction." Rate 3 if one or two priors admissible with offense named. Rate 1 if multiple recent felony convictions admissible un-sanitized, especially crimen falsi.

### F5 — State's case weak — defendant explanation closes the gap (rate 1–5)

If the State's case is already weak on a key element, defendant's testimony can be the final piece that closes the gap to acquittal. Rate 5 if reasonable doubt is already present and defendant testimony seals it. Rate 1 if State's case is strong and defendant testimony is unlikely to change the verdict.

> **Caution:** If reasonable doubt is already present from the State's own weak case, sometimes the better play is to rest without testifying and argue the doubt in closing. Putting the defendant up risks letting the State recover. Weigh F5 against the AGAINST score.

### F6 — Jury expects to hear from defendant (rate 1–5)

In some cases — especially serious felonies, cases with strong emotional narrative, cases where the defendant is the only person who can explain something — juries hold silence against the defendant despite the Griffin instruction. This effect is most pronounced in:
- Homicide cases
- Sex offense cases
- Cases involving children
- Cases with sympathetic complainants
- Cases where defense theme requires defendant agency (e.g., "he did it because he was scared")

Rate 5 if jury expectation is strong (verified by jury research, focus group, or attorney experience in the venue). Rate 1 if the case turns on procedural failures or expert evidence and jury expectation of defendant testimony is low.

### F7 — Defendant strongly wishes to testify (rate 1–5)

The defendant's preference matters. Rate 5 if defendant has consistently and clearly stated they want to testify and understands the risks. Rate 1 if defendant is reluctant or only willing if counsel insists.

> **Counsel cannot force the defendant either way.** The matrix is to inform — not override — the defendant's choice. If the defendant strongly wishes to testify against counsel advice, document the advice and the defendant's decision. Provide a written summary of the testify-or-not analysis to the defendant before final decision.

## Factors Weighing AGAINST Testimony

### A1 — Prior convictions admissible under La. C.E. art. 609.1 that will harm credibility (rate 1–5)

Mirror of F4 inverted. Rate based on the same factors but scored for damage potential. Rate 5 if multiple recent felony convictions are admissible un-sanitized, especially crimen falsi. Rate 1 if no priors or fully sanitized.

> **Worst case:** Defendant has multiple recent crimen falsi convictions (theft, fraud, identity theft, perjury, false personation). In federal court, FRE 609(a)(2) makes these automatically admissible and FRE 609(b) imposes a 10-year limit with reverse-balancing beyond it.
>
> **⚠ Louisiana is harsher, and a prior version of this matrix stated the opposite.** **La. C.E. art. 609.1 contains NO 10-year limit and no balancing prerequisite for the fact of conviction.** Art. 609.1(A): "In a criminal case, every witness by testifying subjects himself to examination relative to his criminal convictions." An old conviction does not age out in Louisiana state court the way it does federally — do not advise the defendant that a conviction is too old to come in. The only balancing in art. 609.1 is at (C)(3), and it governs whether the **details** of a conviction come in, not the fact of it. `[VERIFY current text before relying on this in a testify decision.]`

### A2 — Client temperament — angry, evasive, talks too much, will spar with prosecutor (rate 1–5)

Mirror of F3 inverted. Failure modes:
- Argues with the prosecutor instead of answering
- Volunteers information beyond the question
- Becomes visibly angry or frustrated
- Lectures the prosecutor, judge, or jury
- Makes sarcastic remarks
- Shows contempt for procedure or the criminal justice system
- Talks over the prosecutor
- Falls silent or becomes evasive when challenged
- Cries or breaks down in a way that reads as guilt-driven rather than emotional honesty

Rate 5 if client exhibits multiple failure modes in mock cross. Rate 1 if client is fully controllable.

### A3 — Strong state case where any defendant testimony helps the prosecution (rate 1–5)

When the State's case is overwhelming, every defendant statement on cross becomes a chance for the State to score additional damaging points. Rate 5 if State's case is strong AND defendant testimony has limited upside. Rate 1 if State's case is weak.

> **Strategic principle:** The stronger the State's case, the higher the bar to put the defendant up. The defendant must contribute SOMETHING the State doesn't already have. If the defendant's testimony just rehashes the disputed event, the State will dismantle it on cross.

### A4 — Defense theory is built on reasonable doubt and procedural failures (rate 1–5)

If the defense theory is "the State failed to prove it" — broken chain of custody, suggestive identification, suppressed evidence, biased investigation — the defendant is not the storyteller. The defense lawyer is. Putting the defendant on the stand transforms the trial from "did the State prove it?" to "did the defendant do it?" — the wrong question for this theory.

Rate 5 if the defense theory is purely reasonable doubt / procedural. Rate 1 if the defense theory requires defendant explanation (overlaps with F1, F2).

### A5 — Prior inconsistent statements (custodial statements, jail calls, prior testimony) create impeachment risk (rate 1–5)

Defendant statements made before trial — to police, to detectives, to jail informants, on recorded jail calls, in social media posts, in prior testimony at preliminary hearings or grand jury — are all impeachment material if the trial testimony differs.

Rate 5 if defendant has multiple prior statements with inconsistencies. Rate 4 if defendant gave a custodial statement that differs in any material way from the planned trial account. Rate 3 if jail calls contain incriminating statements. Rate 1 if defendant has no prior statements (consistently invoked the 5th).

> **Worst case:** Defendant gave a Mirandized statement to police that differs from the planned trial account. The State will play the recorded statement on cross. Even with rehearsal, this is near-fatal.

### A6 — Defendant cannot stay disciplined within the agreed scope of testimony (rate 1–5)

Connected to A2 but distinct. Some defendants are articulate but undisciplined — they will testify well on direct but blow scope on cross by volunteering damaging information or addressing topics the defense did NOT raise.

Rate 5 if defendant has demonstrated inability to stay in scope during rehearsal. Rate 1 if defendant has shown they can stick to the script.

> **Scope discipline drill:** During mock cross, have the prosecutor (mock) ask out-of-scope questions. Watch what the defendant does. Disciplined witnesses defer to counsel ("I don't think I should answer that without my lawyer") or stay narrow ("That's not what I was asked about on direct"). Undisciplined witnesses launch into the topic.

## Override Rules

The matrix score is advisory. Certain factors operate as quasi-vetoes regardless of the math.

### Veto rule 1 — Client doesn't want to testify

If the defendant, after full advisement, does not wish to testify, the defendant does not testify. Counsel cannot override.

### Veto rule 2 — Temperament catastrophic

If A2 (temperament) scores 5 — defendant repeatedly demonstrated in mock cross that they cannot control themselves on cross — recommend AGAINST testifying regardless of other factors. The temperament risk overwhelms even strong substantive reasons to testify.

### Veto rule 3 — Multiple impeachable prior statements

If A5 scores 5 — defendant gave a Mirandized statement materially inconsistent with the trial account — recommend AGAINST testifying. Impeachment on the prior statement will eclipse the trial testimony.

### Override rule 1 — Affirmative defense required

If F1 scores 5 — the affirmative defense cannot be presented without the defendant — and counsel cannot reasonably win without that defense, defendant may need to testify even with a negative matrix score. In this case, the prep effort goes into rehearsal, temperament drilling, and motion practice to suppress prior statements before trial.

### Override rule 2 — Client articulate, credible, and chooses to testify

If F3 scores 5 AND F7 scores 5 — defendant is a strong communicator who wants to testify — and there are no veto-level AGAINST factors, defendant testifies even if the matrix is marginal. A strong defendant witness can move the needle.

## Worked Example

Hypothetical case: First-degree battery, complainant is ex-girlfriend, defendant claims self-defense.

| Factor | Score | Note |
|--------|-------|------|
| F1 — Affirmative defense requiring defendant | 5 | Self-defense — state of mind essential |
| F2 — No alternative narrative source | 4 | Defendant only one who saw the moment |
| F3 — Client articulate, controllable | 3 | Articulate but sometimes argues; needs rehearsal |
| F4 — No 609.1 priors | 3 | One battery conviction (8 years). **Note: age does not help in Louisiana state court — no 10-year cutoff under art. 609.1.** Sanitization is a matter of argument, not entitlement |
| F5 — Weak state case | 3 | Complainant credible but inconsistent in statements |
| F6 — Jury expects defendant | 4 | Domestic case, jury expectation high |
| F7 — Defendant wants to testify | 5 | Defendant strongly insists |
| **FOR subtotal** | **28** | |

| Factor | Score | Note |
|--------|-------|------|
| A1 — Damaging priors | 2 | Sanitized to "felony" |
| A2 — Bad temperament | 3 | Tends to argue when challenged |
| A3 — Strong state case | 2 | State case has weaknesses |
| A4 — Reasonable doubt theory | 1 | Affirmative defense, not pure reasonable doubt |
| A5 — Prior inconsistent statements | 3 | Defendant gave a brief recorded statement that omits the self-defense claim |
| A6 — Scope discipline | 3 | Can stay disciplined with rehearsal |
| **AGAINST subtotal** | **14** | |

**FOR minus AGAINST = +14.** Strong indication FOR testifying.

**No veto triggered.** F1 supports affirmative defense need. F7 supports defendant preference.

**Recommendation:** Testify. Heavy rehearsal focused on (a) explaining the omission in the prior recorded statement, (b) scope discipline drilling, (c) temperament under aggressive cross.

## Documentation Requirement

The completed matrix is maintained in the case file as work product. The decision and reaffirmation are signed by attorney and defendant. The signed document is the record evidence that the defendant's testify-or-not decision was made knowingly, voluntarily, and after full advisement — protective against later 6th Amendment ineffective-assistance-of-counsel claims.

Reference: Brookhart v. Janis, 384 U.S. 1 (1966); Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984).
