# Witness-Type Modules — Direct Examination

This reference is consumed by `dw-direct-exam-architect-crim/SKILL.md` STEP 3. Five modules — one per witness type. Each module describes (a) the special prep protocol, (b) the chapter sequencing default, (c) the cross-attack vectors to plan for, (d) the rehearsal/discipline plan, and (e) the foundation/evidentiary checklist.

---

## 1. Defendant

The defendant is the highest-stakes direct witness in any criminal trial. Calling the defendant transforms the trial — jurors who say they will not hold silence against the accused frequently expect to hear from the accused when the defense rests without their testimony. Conversely, putting a poor witness on the stand can lose a case the defense was otherwise winning. The decision is the defendant's alone (Rock v. Arkansas, 483 U.S. 44 (1987)). Counsel advises.

### 1.1 — Pre-trial testify-or-not decision

Route to `defendant-testify-decision-matrix.md` and complete the weighted matrix BEFORE drafting any defendant direct. Do not draft a defendant's outline assuming testimony unless the matrix is complete and signed.

The decision is reaffirmed on the morning of trial after the State rests — facts on the ground may have shifted.

### 1.2 — 5th Amendment waiver protocol (Estelle v. Williams; Brooks v. Tennessee)

When the defendant takes the stand, the 5th Amendment is waived for the subject matter of direct testimony. The waiver protocol:

**On-the-record advisement (outside jury):**
1. *"Mr./Ms. [Defendant], you understand you have the right to remain silent under the 5th Amendment and the State cannot use that silence against you (Griffin v. California, 380 U.S. 609 (1965))?"*
2. *"You understand you have the right to testify in your own defense (Rock v. Arkansas, 483 U.S. 44 (1987))?"*
3. *"You understand that by taking the stand you waive the 5th as to all matters within the scope of your direct testimony — the State can cross-examine you on every topic we cover (La. C.E. Art. 611(B); subject-matter waiver)?"*
4. *"You understand that prior convictions that are admissible under La. C.E. Art. 609 (or federal Rule 609 if WDLA) will be brought out on cross to challenge your credibility?"*
5. *"You understand the decision whether to testify is yours alone, after consultation with me (Brooks v. Tennessee, 406 U.S. 605 (1972))?"*
6. *"Knowing all of this, what is your decision?"*

This colloquy is on the record. The defendant signs an acknowledgment form maintained in the case file.

### 1.3 — Scope-of-cross anticipation (La. C.E. Art. 611)

La. C.E. Art. 611(B) limits cross to subjects raised on direct plus credibility. The defense controls scope through direct. Two strategic postures:

**Narrow scope:** Defendant testifies only to a discrete topic (e.g., alibi at the bar from 8–10 pm). The defense avoids putting the entire case in play. Risk: State and jury may sense the defendant is hiding something. Reward: minimizes cross attack surface.

**Broad scope:** Defendant testifies to the entire State narrative. Risk: cross is unconstrained, every weakness exposed. Reward: jury hears the defendant's full account; some juries punish narrow testimony as evasive.

The Anticipated Cross-Attack Vectors column of every chapter must reflect the chosen scope. Document the scope choice in the outline's STEP 2 confirmation block.

### 1.4 — Rehearsal schedule

| Session | Focus | Duration |
|---------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Read entire outline with client. Walk through chapter by chapter. | 2-3 hours |
| 2 | Practice direct full-through. Note bumps. | 90 min |
| 3 | Mock cross by second-chair attorney or investigator playing prosecutor. Video-record. | 2 hours |
| 4 | Video review with client. Identify tells, evasions, over-explanations, sparring tendencies. | 90 min |
| 5 | Second mock cross with adjustments. | 90 min |
| 6 (eve of trial) | Light review — calm tone, key facts, scope discipline. | 30 min |

For defendants with high temperament risk (per STEP 0.6 risk score), add additional mock cross sessions and consider retaining a witness-prep consultant.

### 1.5 — Demeanor coaching (Estelle v. Williams)

**Attire:** Civilian clothes. Suit if available; dress shirt and slacks minimum. No jail-issued clothing visible to the jury.
**Restraints:** No visible handcuffs, leg shackles, or belly chains. If sheriff insists on stealth restraint, document on the record and challenge under Deck v. Missouri, 544 U.S. 622 (2005) for jury presence.
**Court identification:** Court personnel must not refer to defendant as "inmate," "the defendant in custody," or by jail ID number in jury presence.
**Posture:** Sit upright, face the questioner, turn to face the jury when answering substantive questions. Avoid slouching, fidgeting, eye-rolling.
**Pace:** Pause before answering. Let counsel finish questions. Speak loudly enough for the back row of the jury box.
**Tone:** Respectful to State on cross — "sir/ma'am, yes/no, I don't recall." Do not argue. Do not interrupt. Do not lecture.
**Failure modes to drill out:** sparring with the prosecutor, over-explaining, volunteering information, sarcasm, contempt for procedure, "yes-but" answers.

### 1.6 — Prior conviction sanitization (La. C.E. Art. 609 / Federal Rule 609)

If defendant has prior convictions, the State will seek to introduce them under La. C.E. Art. 609 to impeach credibility once defendant testifies. Sanitization protocol:

1. **Motion in limine** filed pretrial. Argue:
   - Conviction more than 10 years old (per Art. 609(B)) — presumptively excluded
   - Conviction not crimen falsi — limited probative value
   - Conviction nature unduly prejudicial — sanitize to "felony conviction" without naming the offense
   - 403 balancing — probative value substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice
2. **If motion granted in part** — outline reflects the sanitized scope. Direct exam may "get out in front" by acknowledging the conviction in defendant's own words, framed as accepted accountability.
3. **If motion denied** — direct exam MUST address conviction first (rule: get out in front of any 609 conviction the State can use). Chapter sequencing: place the conviction acknowledgment early, frame as the client's lowest point, then move forward.

For federal WDLA, Federal Rule 609 governs — different threshold for crimen falsi (automatic admission), different balancing for non-crimen-falsi convictions.

### 1.7 — Pre-testimony advisement script with the client

The morning of trial, before the defendant takes the stand, the attorney recites:

> *"We have decided you will testify today. Remember: (1) listen to the question, then answer that question and only that question. (2) Short answers. Yes / no / I don't recall when those are true. (3) If you don't understand a question, say so. (4) If you don't remember, say 'I don't remember' — do not guess. (5) Do not argue with the prosecutor. Do not lecture. Do not interrupt. (6) When I ask a question on direct, look at the jury when you answer. On cross, look at the prosecutor — they earned the answer. (7) If the prosecutor cuts you off, do not fight back. I can clean it up on redirect. (8) We rehearsed this. You know the story. Tell the truth in your own words."*

Document this advisement in the case file.

### 1.8 — Foundation/evidentiary checklist (Defendant)

- [ ] Testify-or-not decision matrix complete and signed
- [ ] Decision reaffirmed morning of trial after State rests
- [ ] 5th Amendment waiver colloquy on record
- [ ] Motion in limine on Art. 609 priors filed and ruled
- [ ] Attire confirmed, no restraints visible
- [ ] Rehearsal sessions complete (minimum 4; recommended 6)
- [ ] Mock cross video reviewed
- [ ] Scope decision documented in outline
- [ ] Get-out-in-front strategy planned for every adverse fact
- [ ] Redirect outline drafted in parallel

## 2. Alibi Witness

An alibi witness places the defendant somewhere other than the crime scene at the time of the offense. The alibi witness is the most procedurally regulated defense witness in Louisiana practice — and the most cross-attacked. Every alibi witness has a relationship to the defendant; the State will exploit that relationship.

### 2.1 — La. C.Cr.P. Art. 727 notice prerequisites

Louisiana Code of Criminal Procedure Article 727 requires the defense to provide notice of alibi defense upon written demand from the State.

**Procedural sequence:**
1. State serves written demand for notice of alibi
2. Defense, within the time set by the court, serves notice containing:
   - Specific place(s) the defendant claims to have been at the time of the offense
   - Names and addresses of alibi witnesses
3. State responds with names and addresses of rebuttal witnesses (Art. 727(B))
4. Both sides have continuing duty to disclose newly identified witnesses (Art. 727(C))

**Consequence of non-compliance (Art. 727(D)):** The court may exclude the testimony of any undisclosed alibi witness (with limited good-cause exception). Late disclosure is the most common procedural failure that kills an alibi defense.

**Pre-direct check:** Before drafting any alibi witness direct, verify:
- [ ] Notice of alibi served on State
- [ ] State's rebuttal disclosure received
- [ ] Continuing-duty supplement filed for any newly identified witness
- [ ] Witness's name and address in the notice match the witness being prepped

### 2.2 — Corroboration anchors

An alibi witness's testimony alone is weak — the State will say the witness is biased and lying for the defendant. Corroboration is what makes the alibi credible. For every alibi witness, identify and exhaust:

| Anchor type | Examples | Source register entry |
|-------------|----------|----------------------|
| Timestamped receipts | Sonic drive-in receipt, gas pump receipt, ATM withdrawal | Original paper or POS export |
| Surveillance video | Bar/restaurant CCTV, parking lot cam, doorbell cam, traffic cam | Custodian-authenticated PDF or media file |
| GPS / location data | Defendant's vehicle GPS, phone GPS, ride-share log | Cell extraction or vehicle log |
| Third-party witness | Bartender, server, neighbor — independent of relationship | Affidavit or recorded statement |
| Cell records | CDRs, tower hits placing phone away from crime scene | Subpoenaed carrier records |
| Social media | Geotagged posts during the alibi window | Authenticated screenshot bundle |
| Financial transactions | Credit card swipe, Cash App transaction, ATM use | Subpoenaed bank records |

**Rule:** Every alibi witness direct must reference at least one independent corroboration anchor in the Source column. An alibi resting solely on the witness's word will fail at trial.

### 2.3 — Timeline lock-down

Before trial, the witness and defendant's accounts must lock to the SAME timeline. Coordinate with `dw-timeline-builder-crim`:

1. Build a master alibi timeline showing every witness's position, every document anchor, every State narrative time
2. Identify ANY discrepancy between witness accounts — these become cross attack vectors
3. Resolve discrepancies through interview before trial. Either:
   - The discrepancy is innocent and explained → build the explanation into the direct
   - The discrepancy cannot be explained → consider not calling that witness
4. Lock the timeline. Do not revise mid-trial without notifying every defense witness and counsel.

### 2.4 — Vulnerability to cross

For every alibi witness, the State will explore:

| Cross-attack | Source | Direct-exam preempt |
|-------------|--------|-------------------|
| Relationship bias | "You're his girlfriend, right?" | Acknowledge relationship in opening chapter — own it before they exploit it |
| Opportunity to fabricate | Recent communications with defendant | Address jail calls / texts on direct; show consistency over time |
| Late disclosure | When did the witness first tell the story? | Pinpoint the date and reason — earlier the better |
| Inconsistent statements | Prior accounts that drift | Sanitize on direct OR move in limine |
| Memory selectivity | "You remember 9:42 pm exactly?" | Tie memory to a corroborating anchor — receipt, photo, geotagged post |

The Anticipated Cross-Attack Vectors column for every alibi chapter must reflect these.

### 2.5 — Pre-testimony interview discipline

Alibi witnesses are interviewed multiple times before trial — by defense, by investigator, sometimes by State and police. Every interview is potential impeachment material. Discipline:

- Conduct ALL defense interviews in attorney presence (privilege protection)
- Memorialize each interview in a memo to file (work product, not produced)
- Never script witness verbatim — witness must testify in own words
- Confront the witness with adverse facts (relationship, late disclosure) in advance — do not let the State be the first to raise them
- Identify the witness's "boundary statements" — specific claims they will not retreat from under cross — and verify those are anchored to corroboration

### 2.6 — Foundation/evidentiary checklist (Alibi witness)

- [ ] Art. 727 notice served and current
- [ ] State's rebuttal disclosure received
- [ ] Independent corroboration anchor identified and authenticated
- [ ] Master alibi timeline locked
- [ ] Witness-defendant timeline reconciled
- [ ] Relationship bias acknowledged on direct
- [ ] Disclosure-timing chapter prepared
- [ ] Mock cross conducted
- [ ] Subpoena issued and served
- [ ] Travel/transportation arranged for trial date

## 3. Defense Expert

A defense expert offers opinion testimony grounded in specialized knowledge (La. C.E. Art. 702). Defense experts are deployed to challenge State forensic evidence (DNA, ballistics, accident reconstruction, eyewitness identification, mental state, digital forensics) or to support affirmative defenses (self-defense, mental health). Daubert/Foret is the gatekeeping threshold (State v. Foret, 628 So. 2d 1116 (La. 1993)).

### 3.1 — La. C.Cr.P. Art. 705 disclosure compliance

Louisiana Code of Criminal Procedure Article 705 requires the defense to disclose, upon State request, the substance of any defense expert testimony.

**Required disclosure:**
- Identity (name, address, qualifications)
- CV including education, training, certifications, publications, prior testimony
- Subject matter of expected testimony
- Substance of the opinion to be offered
- Basis of the opinion (data, methodology, materials reviewed)

**Pre-direct check:**
- [ ] Art. 705 disclosure served on State
- [ ] CV produced
- [ ] Written report produced (if expert prepared one — defense not always required to commission a written report, but if one exists it must be produced)
- [ ] Continuing-duty supplement for any new basis material
- [ ] Daubert/Foret challenge anticipated and prep underway (see `dw-expert-witness-evaluator-crim`)

### 3.2 — Qualifications colloquy structure

The qualifications colloquy is the opening chapter of every defense-expert direct. Goal: tender the witness as an expert in the specific subject area BEFORE substantive opinion testimony.

**Chapter structure:**
1. **Education** — degree(s), institution(s), specialty training
2. **Training** — formal coursework, certifications, residencies, professional development
3. **Experience** — years in field, number of cases handled, types of cases
4. **Prior testimony** — number of times testified, jurisdictions, both prosecution and defense (balance is important)
5. **Publications** — peer-reviewed articles, book chapters, treatises
6. **Peer recognition** — board certification, professional society membership, faculty appointments, awards
7. **Tender:** *"Your Honor, I tender Dr. Smith as an expert in the field of [specific area]."*

**Sanitize bias:** If the expert has testified only for the defense, prepare for the State's "hired gun" cross. Address on direct: *"How many times have you testified? For the prosecution? For the defense? Do you turn cases down? Why?"*

### 3.3 — Basis-of-opinion foundation (La. C.E. Art. 702/703)

La. C.E. Art. 703 — An expert may base an opinion on facts or data: (a) perceived by the expert at or before the hearing, (b) made known to the expert at or before the hearing, (c) of a type reasonably relied upon by experts in the particular field.

**Direct-exam sequencing:**
1. Establish the materials reviewed (Source column lists each: police reports, lab reports, photos, videos, raw data)
2. Establish independence — did the expert reach the opinion before or after talking to defense counsel?
3. Establish the methodology applied
4. Tender the opinion

### 3.4 — Hypothetical question structure (when permissible)

Hypothetical questions are permitted but risky. La. C.E. Art. 705 allows expert opinions on direct without prior disclosure of underlying data — but the State may demand underlying data on cross.

**Permissible hypothetical form:** *"Doctor, assume the following facts: [fact 1], [fact 2], [fact 3], all of which are supported by the evidence in this case. Based on those facts and your training and experience, do you have an opinion as to [issue]?"*

**Risk:** If the hypothetical does not match the evidence, the opinion is excludable. Anchor every hypothetical fact to a source register citation.

### 3.5 — Daubert-survival framing

Even if the expert survived pretrial Daubert/Foret challenge, the State may re-litigate methodology on cross. Direct exam preempts by establishing:

1. **Methodology** — specific protocol applied, peer-recognized as valid
2. **Error rates** — known error rates of the methodology, expert's own error rate if measured
3. **Peer review** — methodology published in peer-reviewed journals
4. **General acceptance** — Frye-style general acceptance in the relevant scientific community
5. **Application** — methodology applied correctly to the facts of this case
6. **Replicability** — another competent expert applying the same methodology would reach the same opinion

### 3.6 — Anticipated state cross

Standard State cross-attack vectors for defense experts:

| Vector | Direct-exam preempt |
|--------|--------------------|
| Paid by defense ("hired gun") | Address fee on direct; show prior balanced testimony |
| Prior testimony record (only defense) | Show defense/prosecution split; show case-turn-down rate |
| Excluded from professional standards | Verify current credentials; produce certifications |
| Methodology fringe | Anchor methodology to peer review and general acceptance |
| Application errors | Walk through application step-by-step; document QA review |
| Bias from prior involvement | Disclose any prior contact with the case or parties |

### 3.7 — Foundation/evidentiary checklist (Defense expert)

- [ ] Art. 705 disclosure served
- [ ] CV produced
- [ ] Written report produced (if any)
- [ ] Materials list complete (every item reviewed by expert)
- [ ] Daubert/Foret challenge survived or addressed
- [ ] Qualifications colloquy drafted
- [ ] Hypothetical (if any) anchored to evidence
- [ ] Fee disclosure planned for direct
- [ ] Prior-testimony balance documented
- [ ] Pre-direct meeting between expert and counsel scheduled
- [ ] Coordination with `dw-expert-witness-evaluator-crim` complete

## 4. Character Witness

A character witness offers reputation or opinion testimony about a pertinent character trait of the defendant. La. C.E. Art. 404(A)(1) permits the accused to introduce evidence of a pertinent trait — but doing so opens the door to State rebuttal (Art. 404(A)(1) proviso). Character evidence is high-risk: the door, once opened, lets in a flood.

### 4.1 — La. C.E. Art. 404(A)(1) trait-in-issue limitation

Character evidence is admissible ONLY as to a "pertinent trait." Pertinence is tied to the charge:

| Charge type | Pertinent trait(s) |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Homicide / violence | Peacefulness, non-violence |
| Theft / fraud | Honesty, truthfulness |
| Drug offense | Law-abidingness (narrow; some courts reject as too general) |
| Sex offense | Sexual decency (limited; consider La. C.E. Art. 412 implications) |
| Conspiracy | Law-abidingness, honesty (depends on theory) |

Generalized "good person" testimony is NOT admissible. The trait must match the charge.

### 4.2 — Reputation vs. opinion form (La. C.E. Art. 405)

La. C.E. Art. 405(A) — Methods of proving character: reputation testimony OR opinion testimony. Specific instances of conduct are NOT permitted on direct (Art. 405(A) proviso, with limited exceptions).

**Reputation form:** *"Are you familiar with [Defendant]'s reputation in the community for [trait]?"* / *"What is that reputation?"*

**Opinion form:** *"Based on your personal knowledge of [Defendant], have you formed an opinion as to his character for [trait]?"* / *"What is that opinion?"*

**Personal-knowledge predicate (opinion form):** Establish how long the witness has known the defendant, in what capacity, frequency of contact.

**Reputation predicate (reputation form):** Establish witness's membership in the relevant community, length of association, basis for knowing reputation (conversations heard, general knowledge).

### 4.3 — Sanitization of basis (NO specific bad acts on direct)

Direct exam of character witnesses MUST NOT inquire into specific instances of the defendant's good conduct. La. C.E. Art. 405(A) limits direct to reputation/opinion form. Specific instances are reserved for cross-examination of the witness (and even then under tight limits).

**WRONG (do not draft):**
- *"Tell us about the time the defendant helped his neighbor."*
- *"Has the defendant ever been in a fight to your knowledge?"*

**RIGHT:**
- *"What is the defendant's reputation for peacefulness in your community?"*
- *"Based on your knowledge of him, what is your opinion of his character for peacefulness?"*

### 4.4 — Opening-the-door risk on cross — "have you heard" questions

Once a character witness testifies, the State may cross-examine with "have you heard" or "do you know" questions about specific bad acts of the defendant — provided the State has a good-faith basis for the question (Michelson v. United States, 335 U.S. 469 (1948); La. C.E. Art. 405(A) authorizes).

**Example State cross:** *"Mr. Jones, you testified that the defendant has a reputation for peacefulness. Have you heard that the defendant was arrested for battery in 2019?"*

**The damage:** Even if the witness answers "no," the question alone seeds doubt in the juror's mind. Even if the witness answers "yes, but..." the jury hears the bad act.

**Pre-direct risk assessment:**
1. List every specific bad act the State may be aware of (arrests, convictions, complaints, professional discipline)
2. Identify which acts have a good-faith basis the State can prove
3. Decide whether the character chapter is worth the risk
4. If yes, brief the witness in advance: *"On cross, the prosecutor may ask if you've heard about [specific incidents]. The truthful answer is [yes / no]. Do not argue. Do not explain. Short answers."*

**Decision rule:** If the bad-act inventory contains any item the witness cannot truthfully answer "no" to, reconsider calling the witness.

### 4.5 — Personal-knowledge predicate

Opinion form requires personal knowledge. Reputation form requires community knowledge. Both require foundation:

**Opinion-form foundation chapter:**
1. How do you know the defendant?
2. How long have you known him?
3. How often do you interact?
4. In what settings have you observed him?
5. [Tender:] Have you formed an opinion as to his character for [trait]?
6. What is that opinion?

**Reputation-form foundation chapter:**
1. What community do you and the defendant share? (neighborhood, workplace, church, family, military unit)
2. How long have you been part of that community?
3. How often have you heard the defendant discussed?
4. Based on that, do you know his reputation for [trait]?
5. What is that reputation?

### 4.6 — Foundation/evidentiary checklist (Character witness)

- [ ] Trait pertinence confirmed (matches charge type)
- [ ] Direct in reputation or opinion form (NO specific instances)
- [ ] Personal-knowledge or community-knowledge predicate planned
- [ ] Bad-act inventory complete
- [ ] Open-door risk assessed
- [ ] Witness briefed on cross-examination "have you heard" questions
- [ ] State pre-notified if scheduling order requires
- [ ] Subpoena issued

## 5. Custodian / Foundation Witness

A custodial or foundation witness provides the predicate for admitting a defense exhibit. These witnesses are short, in-and-out, and surgical. The goal is to authenticate the exhibit and get the witness off the stand — every additional minute is cross-attack surface.

### 5.1 — Minimal predicate questions for self-authentication (La. C.E. Art. 902)

La. C.E. Art. 902 lists self-authenticating documents (domestic public documents under seal, certified copies of public records, certified business records, etc.). Where Art. 902 applies, a foundation witness may not be needed — the document authenticates itself.

**Pre-direct check:** Is the exhibit self-authenticating under Art. 902? If yes:
1. Offer the exhibit with the appropriate certification attached
2. Do not call a foundation witness unless the State challenges
3. Hold the witness in reserve

**If the exhibit is NOT self-authenticating**, the foundation witness is needed.

### 5.2 — Business records foundation (La. C.E. Art. 803(6) — four-prong showing)

La. C.E. Art. 803(6) — the business records hearsay exception. Foundation requires:

1. **Regular practice:** Record was made in the regular course of business
2. **Contemporaneous:** Record was made at or near the time of the event by a person with knowledge
3. **Trustworthiness:** Sources of information, method, and circumstances of preparation indicate trustworthiness
4. **Custodian or other qualified witness:** Testimony by custodian or other qualified witness, OR self-authenticating certification under Art. 902(11)

**Direct-exam template (live custodian):**

> Q: Where are you employed? A: [Business name and role]
> Q: How long have you worked there? A: [Years]
> Q: In your role, are you familiar with the records of [type]? A: Yes
> Q: Are those records kept in the regular course of business? A: Yes
> Q: Are they made at or near the time of the events recorded? A: Yes
> Q: Are they made by a person with knowledge of the events? A: Yes
> Q: I am showing you what has been marked as Defense Exhibit [#]. Do you recognize this? A: Yes
> Q: What is it? A: [Description]
> Q: Is it a true and accurate copy of the record as kept by [Business]? A: Yes
> [Offer Exhibit #]

### 5.3 — Chain-of-custody markers

For physical evidence or digital exhibits, foundation includes chain of custody. Establish:

1. **Collection** — when, where, who, condition
2. **Transfer** — every hand the item passed through
3. **Storage** — secure location, locked, controlled access
4. **Integrity** — any seal broken? Any tampering? Hash verified (for digital)?
5. **Production** — when item produced for trial, by whom

Defense exhibits with chain-of-custody questions invite suppression motions from the State. Address chain on direct before the State raises it on cross.

### 5.4 — Stipulation-first strategy

Foundation testimony is mechanical and unremarkable. Most prosecutors will stipulate to authentication of routine documents (medical records, employment records, school records, public records) to save trial time.

**Pre-trial protocol:**
1. Identify every defense exhibit needing foundation
2. Draft a stipulation offer to the State for each
3. If State stipulates → no witness needed; mark exhibit "authentication stipulated" in the catalog
4. If State refuses → call the witness, but only for the refused exhibits

**Why stipulate?** Calling a foundation witness opens that witness to any cross within the scope of direct. A bank records custodian called to authenticate a deposit slip may be cross-examined on every transaction in the defendant's account. Stipulation closes the door entirely.

### 5.5 — Short-cross discipline (in/out — establish foundation, sit down)

Foundation chapters are SHORT. Target structure:

| Chapter | Length |
|---------|--------|
| Witness identification | 30 seconds |
| Business / role | 30 seconds |
| Familiarity with records | 30 seconds |
| Business-records predicate (4 prongs) | 60 seconds |
| Identify exhibit | 30 seconds |
| Authenticate (true and accurate copy) | 15 seconds |
| Offer | 15 seconds |
| **Total** | ~4 minutes |

If the foundation chapter runs longer than 5–7 minutes, something is wrong — the attorney is over-developing the witness and creating cross attack surface. Tighten.

### 5.6 — Foundation/evidentiary checklist (Custodian / foundation witness)

- [ ] Exhibit identified and marked
- [ ] Self-authentication assessed (Art. 902)
- [ ] Stipulation offered to State; result documented
- [ ] If witness needed, business-records four-prong scripted
- [ ] Chain of custody addressed on direct
- [ ] Cross-attack surface assessed — witness limited to foundation scope
- [ ] Subpoena issued
- [ ] Witness arrival coordinated for narrow time window (do not park a custodian witness all day)
