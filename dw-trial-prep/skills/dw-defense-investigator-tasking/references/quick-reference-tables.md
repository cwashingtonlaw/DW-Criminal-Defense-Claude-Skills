# Quick Reference Tables

## Louisiana Investigator Requirements (La. R.S. 15:145 et seq.)

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Licensing Body | Louisiana State Board of Private Investigator Examiners |
| License Required | Yes — for any person engaged in private investigation for compensation |
| Attorney Exemption | Attorneys conducting investigation in their own cases are exempt from PI licensing |
| Employee Status | Investigators must be licensed individually even if employed by a licensed agency |
| Prohibited Acts | Impersonating law enforcement, trespassing, wiretapping, harassment |
| Reporting | Licensed investigators must report any arrest or conviction within 30 days |

## Key Louisiana Criminal Procedure — Investigation-Related

| Article / Statute | Subject | Relevance to Investigation |
|---|---|---|
| La. C.Cr.P. Art. 718 | Discovery — State obligation | Defines what the state must disclose; identifies gaps for independent investigation |
| La. C.Cr.P. Art. 719 | Discovery — Defendant's obligation | Defines what the defense must disclose; informs what investigation products may become discoverable |
| La. C.Cr.P. Art. 721 | Discovery — Regulation | Court authority to regulate discovery; protective orders |
| La. C.Cr.P. Art. 722 | Work product protection | Attorney work product privilege protects investigation materials prepared in anticipation of litigation |
| La. C.Cr.P. Art. 723 | Continuing duty to disclose | Obligation to supplement discovery; must monitor new investigation results for disclosure obligations |
| La. C.Cr.P. Art. 729 | Sanctions for discovery violations | Consequences of failing to disclose; informs documentation requirements |
| La. C.E. Art. 609 | Impeachment by prior conviction | Guides background investigation scope for witness impeachment |
| La. C.E. Art. 607 | Impeachment — General | Authorizes attack on witness credibility by showing bias, interest, corruption, or prior inconsistent statements |
| La. C.E. Art. 608 | Impeachment — Character for truthfulness | Allows opinion/reputation evidence regarding witness truthfulness |
| La. R.S. 15:1303 | Interception of communications | Louisiana is a one-party consent state; investigator may record conversations they participate in |
| La. R.S. 44:1-41 | Public Records Law | Governs access to government records; tool for investigation |

## Common Investigation Deadlines and Retention Periods

| Record Type | Typical Retention / Deadline | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| Business surveillance footage | 7-30 days (varies by system) | Request preservation IMMEDIATELY upon case intake |
| Doorbell camera footage (Ring, Nest) | 30-60 days (cloud storage dependent) | Request preservation within 48 hours |
| Cell tower records (CSLI) | 1-2 years (carrier dependent) | Request or subpoena within 30 days |
| CAD/dispatch records | Varies by agency — often 1-5 years | Request early in case |
| 911 recordings | Varies by agency — often 90 days to 2 years | Request within first week |
| Body camera footage | Varies by agency policy — 90 days to 3 years | Request in initial discovery |
| Traffic camera footage | Often 30-90 days | Request preservation immediately |
| ATM/bank surveillance | Typically 30-90 days | Request preservation immediately |
| Social media content | Can be deleted at any time | Preserve screenshots immediately |
| Medical records | Varies — generally 10 years for adults in Louisiana | Request when needed |
| Phone records (call detail) | Generally 1-2 years | Subpoena within 60 days |
