# Mistrial Trigger Checklist — La. C.Cr.P. Art. 770 vs. Art. 771

**Read this before moving for a mistrial. The path matters.**

Louisiana mistrial law is bifurcated. Art. 770 is mandatory: if a covered comment is made by the judge, district attorney, or court official, the court SHALL grant a mistrial on the defendant's motion. Art. 771 is discretionary: the court MAY grant a mistrial when an admonition is insufficient to assure the defendant a fair trial.

The two articles require different objection language and different preservation steps. Getting it wrong waives the issue.

---

## La. C.Cr.P. Art. 770 — MANDATORY MISTRIAL

**Statutory text (paraphrased):** Upon motion of a defendant, a mistrial shall be ordered when a remark or comment made within the hearing of the jury by the judge, district attorney, or a court official, during the trial or in argument, refers directly or indirectly to:

1. **Race, religion, color, or national origin** — if the remark is not material and relevant and might create prejudice against the defendant;
2. **Another crime committed or alleged to have been committed by the defendant** as to which evidence is not admissible;
3. **The failure of the defendant to testify in his own defense**;
4. **The refusal of the judge to direct a verdict.**

The mistrial is at the defendant's option (defendant's motion) and the court SHALL grant it. An admonition is permitted only if the defendant requests it INSTEAD of mistrial.

### Art. 770 trigger #1 — race / religion / color / national origin

**Common scenarios:**
- Prosecutor's closing argument referring to defendant's ethnicity or country of origin
- Witness testifying about defendant's "type" in a way coded for race
- Officer testifying that the neighborhood is "predominantly [race] and high-crime"

**Must be:** (a) within the hearing of the jury, (b) by judge / DA / court official (NOT a defense witness or civilian witness — see Art. 771 for those), (c) not material and relevant, (d) creates prejudice against defendant.

### Art. 770 trigger #2 — another crime

**Common scenarios:**
- Officer testifies "I knew defendant from prior arrests" (no Prieur notice, no admissibility under 404(B))
- Prosecutor's closing references inadmissible prior conviction
- Witness blurts out "the defendant was on parole at the time"

**Critical distinction:** If the prior crime is admissible under 404(B) with proper Prieur notice, this is NOT a 770 trigger. Limit your motion to crimes inadmissible at trial.

### Art. 770 trigger #3 — comment on failure to testify

**Authority:** *Griffin v. California*, 380 U.S. 609 (1965) — direct or indirect comment on defendant's silence violates the Fifth Amendment.

**Common scenarios:**
- Prosecutor's closing: "Did you hear the defendant explain where he was that night?"
- Prosecutor's closing: "The State's evidence stands uncontradicted" (when only defendant could contradict)
- Direct examination of officer: "Did the defendant ever give you his side of the story?"

**Must be:** indirect references count if the natural and necessary effect is to call attention to defendant's silence.

### Art. 770 trigger #4 — refusal of judge to direct verdict

**Note:** Louisiana criminal procedure doesn't have "directed verdict" by that name (it's an "acquittal" — Art. 778, bench trials only). This trigger is rarely invoked in jury trials.

### Procedure under Art. 770

```
1. OBJECT IMMEDIATELY ("Objection.")
2. REQUEST A SIDEBAR or move the jury out
3. Once outside the jury's presence:
   "Your Honor, we move for a mistrial under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 770(2)
    based on the prosecutor's reference to [other crime / race / silence].
    The remark falls squarely within Art. 770. The mistrial is mandatory."
4. State the EXACT WORDS used by the offending speaker for the record
5. If the court denies the mistrial:
   "Your Honor, we note the denial of our Art. 770 motion. We do NOT
    waive our objection. In the alternative, we request that the court
    admonish the jury under Art. 771."
6. PRESERVE: log the objection, the motion, the ruling, the alternative
   admonition request, and whether admonition was given.
```

### Preservation pitfalls under Art. 770

- **Failure to move for mistrial:** Mere objection does NOT preserve Art. 770. The defendant must MOVE FOR MISTRIAL.
- **Acceptance of admonition without express reservation:** If you ask for admonition WITHOUT first moving for mistrial, you may have waived the mandatory mistrial. Move for mistrial FIRST; request admonition only as an alternative if denied.
- **Failure to renew at end of trial:** For pattern violations, renew at end of State's case and end of defense case.
- **Motion for new trial:** Renew every Art. 770 issue in the post-trial motion for new trial under Art. 851 to preserve weight-of-evidence and pattern-misconduct grounds.

## La. C.Cr.P. Art. 771 — DISCRETIONARY MISTRIAL OR ADMONITION

**Statutory text (paraphrased):** In the following cases, upon the request of the defendant or the state, the court shall promptly admonish the jury to disregard a remark or comment made during the trial, or in argument, when the remark is irrelevant or immaterial and of such a nature that it might create prejudice against the defendant or the state. **In such cases, on motion of the defendant, the court may grant a mistrial if it is satisfied that an admonition is not sufficient to assure the defendant a fair trial.**

**Art. 771 covers:**

1. When the remark or comment is made by the judge, district attorney, or a court official, **and the remark is not within the scope of Art. 770**;
2. When the remark or comment is made by **a witness or person other than the judge, district attorney, or a court official**, regardless of whether the remark or comment is within the scope of Art. 770.

### Art. 771 trigger categories

**A. Argumentative misconduct by State (not within Art. 770):**
- Vouching for State witnesses ("Officer Smith would never lie")
- Appealing to passion or sympathy ("Imagine if it were your daughter")
- Golden-rule arguments
- Misstating evidence in closing
- Commenting on facts not in evidence

**B. Late disclosure of evidence:**
- Brady material disclosed after defense rested
- Discovery violations under Art. 716, 719, 723 surfacing mid-trial
- Surprise testimony beyond what was disclosed

**C. Witness blurts:**
- Lay witness makes inflammatory comment ("I always knew he'd kill someone")
- State witness references inadmissible matter (prior conviction, gang membership) — non-DA / non-court-official, so Art. 771 not 770

**D. Irrelevant prejudicial evidence:**
- Inadvertently admitted evidence later struck
- Photographs, exhibits seen by jury but not admitted

### Procedure under Art. 771

```
1. OBJECT IMMEDIATELY ("Objection.")
2. REQUEST ADMONITION:
   "Your Honor, we request that the court admonish the jury to disregard
    [the witness's last statement / the prosecutor's last comment]."
3. IF ADMONITION GIVEN: log the language; consider whether sufficient
4. IF DEFENSE BELIEVES ADMONITION IS NOT SUFFICIENT:
   "Your Honor, we further move for a mistrial under Art. 771. An
    admonition is not sufficient to assure the defendant a fair trial
    because [specific prejudice — pattern, severity, timing, jury reaction]."
5. IF MISTRIAL DENIED:
   "We note the denial. We do not waive our objection. We renew at the
    close of the State's case."
6. PRESERVE: log objection + admonition request + mistrial motion + ruling.
```

### Preservation pitfalls under Art. 771

- **Failure to request admonition:** Art. 771 contemplates admonition as the first remedy. Failing to request it can be cited against you on appeal as evidence the prejudice was minimal.
- **Failure to move for mistrial:** Even after admonition, if prejudice persists, move for mistrial. Without the motion, the issue is waived.
- **Failing to articulate why admonition is insufficient:** Conclusory motions ("an admonition is not enough") are weak. Specify: pattern (this is the third time), severity (race-coded language), timing (closing argument leaves no chance to cure), jury reaction (visible response from jurors).
- **Failure to renew:** For repeated violations, renew motion at each occurrence and at end of State's case.

## Decision Flow — "Is this a 770 or a 771?"

```
Step 1 — Who said it?
   ├── Judge / DA / court official
   │      └── Step 2A
   └── Witness / other person
          └── Art. 771 (mandatory admonition; discretionary mistrial)

Step 2A — Is the content within Art. 770(1)-(4)?
   ├── Race / religion / color / national origin
   │      → ART. 770 MANDATORY MISTRIAL
   ├── Other crime not admissible
   │      → ART. 770 MANDATORY MISTRIAL
   ├── Comment on defendant's silence
   │      → ART. 770 MANDATORY MISTRIAL
   ├── Refusal to direct verdict
   │      → ART. 770 MANDATORY MISTRIAL
   └── None of above
          → Art. 771 (admonition or discretionary mistrial)

Step 3 — Make the right motion in the right order:
   ├── 770 case: MOVE FOR MISTRIAL FIRST. Admonition only as alternative.
   └── 771 case: REQUEST ADMONITION FIRST. Mistrial as escalation if
                 admonition is insufficient.
```

## The Three-Step Preservation Sequence (memorize)

For ANY potential mistrial issue, run this sequence on the record:

```
STEP 1 — OBJECT
   "Objection — [specific ground]."

STEP 2 — REQUEST RELIEF
   770 case: "We move for a mistrial under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 770(__)."
   771 case: "We request the jury be admonished to disregard."

STEP 3 — ESCALATE IF DENIED
   770 case denied: "We renew. In the alternative, admonition."
   771 case after admonition: "Admonition is insufficient. We move for
                                mistrial under Art. 771."
   Either case: "We do not waive."
```

If the court rules quickly, restate the motion for the record before the next question is asked. The record is the ONLY thing the appellate court will see.

## Common Real-World Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Officer says "I knew defendant from prior arrests"

- **Speaker:** witness (police officer) → Art. 771 (not 770).
- **Action:** Object → motion to strike → request admonition → if pattern, motion for mistrial under Art. 771.
- **Why not 770?** Because it was a witness, not the DA or judge. Art. 770(2) covers DAs, judges, court officials.
- **Caveat:** If the DA ELICITED the response with a leading question knowing the answer, some courts treat as 770 — argue both.

### Scenario 2 — Prosecutor's closing: "The defendant has been silent throughout this trial"

- **Speaker:** DA → Art. 770(3) MANDATORY mistrial.
- **Action:** Object → motion for mistrial under Art. 770(3) → if denied, alternative admonition request, log preservation.
- **Authority:** *Griffin v. California*, 380 U.S. 609 (1965).

### Scenario 3 — State's late Brady disclosure mid-trial

- **Speaker:** N/A (procedural issue).
- **Article:** Not strictly 770/771 — handled as Brady violation. *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83 (1963); *Giglio v. United States*, 405 U.S. 150 (1972).
- **Action:** Move to continue; move to strike testimony; move for mistrial under Art. 775(3) (legal defect making fair trial impossible) or under court's inherent authority. Preserve as Brady error for appellate review.
- **Cross-reference:** `dw-brady-giglio-auditor-crim` skill for full Brady framework.

### Scenario 4 — Juror caught reading newspaper coverage of trial

- **Speaker:** N/A (juror misconduct).
- **Action:** Move to question juror in camera → if exposure to prejudicial coverage, move to strike juror or move for mistrial under Art. 775.
- **Authority:** *Remmer v. United States*, 347 U.S. 227 (1954) — outside influence on juror.

### Scenario 5 — Witness violates sequestration order

- **Action:** Move to strike witness's testimony or limit to matters outside the sequestration violation; admonition; in extreme case, mistrial under Art. 771.
- **Note:** Sequestration violations are usually remedied by impeachment, not mistrial.

### Scenario 6 — Prosecutor uses race-coded language in closing

- **Speaker:** DA → Art. 770(1) MANDATORY mistrial.
- **Action:** Object IMMEDIATELY (do not wait for sentence to end), move for mistrial. Quote exact words for the record.

## Art. 775 — Other Mistrial Grounds (catch-all)

When a situation does not fit 770 or 771, La. C.Cr.P. Art. 775 lists other grounds for mistrial:

1. The defendant consents.
2. The jury is unable to agree on a verdict.
3. There is a legal defect in the proceedings making a fair trial impossible.
4. False statements of a juror prevented a fair selection.
5. The court finds the defendant lacks capacity to proceed.
6. It is physically impossible to proceed.
7. Prejudicial conduct in or outside the courtroom makes a fair trial impossible.

Art. 775(3) and (7) are the catch-alls for issues like late Brady disclosures, juror misconduct, and pervasive prosecutorial misconduct that escapes 770 and 771.

## Post-Trial — Carrying Mistrial Issues Forward

Even if the mistrial motion is denied, preserve the issue in the post-trial motion for new trial under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 851:

- Art. 851(2) — court's ruling on objection during trial shows prejudicial error → cite the denied mistrial motion.
- Art. 851(4) — defendant suffered constitutional deprivation → cite Griffin / Crawford / Brady.
- Art. 851(5) — prejudice of the jury → cite for jury-misconduct mistrials.

Failure to renew in the motion for new trial does NOT waive a properly preserved Art. 770/771 issue, but renewal strengthens the appellate record. Cross-reference `dw-appellate-error-monitor-crim` MODULE D (post-trial motion practice).
