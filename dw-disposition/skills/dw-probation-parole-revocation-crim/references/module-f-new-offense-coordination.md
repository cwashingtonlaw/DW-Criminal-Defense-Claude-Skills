# Module F — New-Offense-Conduct Violations: Coordinating With the New Charge's Defense

When the alleged violation is new criminal conduct, the revocation and the new prosecution are two fronts of one war. Mishandling the revocation hearing can lose the new trial. Coordinate with new-charge counsel **before** any revocation setting; if D&W holds both files, run this module against both.

## F-1. The sequencing problem

The revocation hearing usually arrives **first** (30-day in-custody clock, Art. 900; parole holds push fast). That gives the State a free preview: relaxed evidence rules, no jury, lower proof standard, and — most dangerously — a chance to lock in testimony.

**Default strategy: continue the revocation until the new charge resolves.**
- Grounds: judicial economy; the new case's outcome may resolve the violation; fairness/Fifth Amendment tension in forcing the client to choose between silence at the revocation and defense of the new charge.
- The court is not obliged to continue [VERIFY — confirm Louisiana authority on whether due process requires deferral]; if denied, object on the record and shift to F-2 protections.
- Custody cost check: a continuance with the client in jail on a no-bail parole hold may be worse than a fast hearing — run the Module D math and the client's release posture before asking.

## F-2. Fifth Amendment management when the revocation precedes the new trial

- **Client testimony:** testimony at the revocation hearing is on the record, under oath, and usable in the new prosecution (impeachment at minimum). Default: the client does **not** testify about new-offense conduct at the revocation. If mitigation testimony is essential, confine it strictly to compliance/mitigation topics and object to cross into the new-offense facts.
- **No automatic use immunity** attaches to revocation testimony in Louisiana [VERIFY — research any Louisiana authority granting use immunity for revocation testimony before advising]; consider requesting a protective ruling on the record and preserving the issue if refused.
- **Invocation costs:** at a revocation hearing, silence can practically be held against the client (the proceeding is not a criminal trial). Document the dilemma on the record — it supports both the continuance request and later review.
- **Statements to the PO:** debrief the client immediately — admissions to the supervising officer about the new conduct are the State's cheapest evidence on both fronts. No client interviews with P&P about the new charge without counsel.
- **Mitigation-package screening:** scrub letters, treatment intake notes, and any client statement in the Module C package for factual admissions about the new conduct.

## F-3. Using the revocation hearing defensively (when it proceeds)

- **Free discovery:** cross-examine the State's witnesses (arresting officer, complainant) thoroughly — transcript-locked testimony before the new trial. Order the transcript immediately; feed it to `dw-witness-statement-analyzer-crim` and `dw-cross-exam-architect-crim` on the new case.
- **Exclusionary-rule reality:** suppression does not bar the evidence at the revocation itself (*Pennsylvania Bd. of Probation & Parole v. Scott*, 524 U.S. 357 (1998) (parole); Louisiana treatment of probation hearings [VERIFY — confirm Louisiana rule before conceding]). The suppression fight lives in the new case — route to `dw-suppression-motion-crim` — but the revocation cross can build its factual record.
- **Outcome interplay:** an acquittal on the new charge does not automatically undo a completed revocation (different standards of proof) [VERIFY — confirm Louisiana authority]; conversely a **felony conviction while on parole makes revocation automatic** (La. R.S. 15:574.10). Every plea negotiation on the new charge must therefore price in the revocation consequence — coordinate with `dw-plea-negotiation-analyzer-crim`, and aim pleas at misdemeanors or dispositions that keep the violation technical (Module B).
- **Global resolutions:** the cleanest endgame is often a package: plea on the new charge + agreed revocation disposition (capped sanction, concurrency designation under Art. 901, treatment condition). Draft the proposed global terms into the alternative-sanctions plan.
