# Probation Revocation — Louisiana Statutory Framework (La. C.Cr.P. arts. 899–901)

All article text below was checked against the Louisiana Legislature's published text in August 2026. **The technical-violation sanction scheme has been amended repeatedly (2017 Justice Reinvestment; 2024; and as recently as Acts 2026, No. 622 [VERIFY CITATION — confirm the 2026 act number and its effective text])** — always confirm the governing version per the versioning protocol before quoting a cap to the attorney or the court.

## Art. 899 — Arrest or summons for violation of probation

- The court may issue a **warrant** for arrest (defendant returned to the custody of the court or a designated detention facility) or a **summons** to appear and answer a charge of violation or *threatened* violation.
- A **probation officer may arrest without a warrant** (or authorize a peace officer to do so) on reasonable cause to believe the defendant has violated or is about to violate a condition, or when an emergency makes waiting for a court order an undue risk. The officer's written statement of the arrest basis is a key Module A audit document — demand it.
- **Detainer practice:** when a probationer is booked on a new charge, the probation hold typically arrives as an Art. 899 warrant/detainer that defeats release on the new charge's bond. Attack points: whether the warrant actually issued, on what showing, and how stale the underlying conduct is.
- **Bail:** the court **may grant bail** to a defendant arrested under Art. 899. File for it — a client at liberty builds a compliance record before the hearing. (Bail on the violation arrest is this skill's territory; revocation of a *bail undertaking* is `dw-bond-and-release-motion-crim` Module H.)
- Detention time triggers credit under Art. 880 / Art. 901 — log every custody day (Module D).

## Art. 899.1 — Administrative sanctions for technical violations

The 2017 Justice Reinvestment "swift and certain" alternative to court revocation:

- Available only if the sentencing court **authorized administrative sanctions at sentencing** (check the sentencing minutes).
- Requires, after written notification of the right to a hearing and to counsel: the defendant's **written waiver** of the violation hearing, an **admission** to the violation, and **consent** to the sanction.
- **Jail confinement as an administrative sanction may not exceed 10 days per violation nor 60 days per calendar year.**
- **"Technical violation" (899.1 definition):** any violation of a condition of probation **except a subsequent criminal act** — but a subsequent alleged **misdemeanor possession of marijuana / THC** is still treated as technical.
- Defense uses: (a) argue the matter belongs on the administrative track rather than in a revocation posture; (b) audit any past administrative sanction for a defective waiver (no counsel notice, no written waiver) before it is counted against the client's tier position; (c) confirm yearly jail-day totals were not exceeded.

## Art. 900 — Violation hearing; sanctions

**Hearing clock:** a defendant held in custody after an Art. 899 arrest must be brought before the court **within thirty days** for the hearing; on summons or bail, the hearing is set within a reasonable time. Feed this clock to (or reconcile it with) `dw-deadline-engine-crim`.

**Sanction menu on a violation finding** (the court is never required to revoke):

1. Reprimand and warning;
2. Intensified supervision;
3. Additional or modified conditions;
4. Commitment to a community rehabilitation center (up to six months, no good time/parole credit) [VERIFY — confirm current subsection details];
5. **Revocation** — order the suspended sentence executed;
6. Technical-violation custodial sanctions (below) and, for drug-division participants, an intensive incarceration option [VERIFY — confirm current drug-division sanction terms];
7. Extension of the probation period within statutory maximums [VERIFY — confirm the extension option's current placement in the article].

## Art. 901 — Revocation for commission of another offense; credit

- On revocation, **no credit** is allowed for time spent on probation ("street time") or for time elapsed during suspension.
- The defendant **may receive credit for time served in actual custody while held on the probation violation** (local, state, or out-of-state facility) pursuant to Art. 880.
- When revocation follows a new Louisiana conviction, the court **shall specify in the minutes whether the revoked sentence runs concurrently or consecutively** with the new sentence — silence is a correctable minute-entry issue and a negotiation point. Concurrency argument belongs in every alternative-sanctions plan.
- Revocation for a new offense does not require a conviction on the new charge — an unconvicted new-offense allegation is litigated on the Morrissey/Gagnon record (Module F).
