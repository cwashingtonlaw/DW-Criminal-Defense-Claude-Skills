## The Dorthey Framework

*State v. Dorthey*, 623 So.2d 1276 (La. 1993), established that the habitual offender sentencing provisions, though mandatory, are subject to constitutional review under La. Const. Art. I, Sec. 20 (prohibition against excessive punishment):

> "If the trial judge were to find that the punishment mandated by R.S. 15:529.1 makes no 'measurable contribution to acceptable goals of punishment' or that the sentence amounted to nothing more than 'the purposeless and needless imposition of pain and suffering' and is 'grossly out of proportion to the severity of the crime,' he has the option, indeed the duty, to reduce such sentence to one that would not be constitutionally excessive." *Dorthey*, 623 So.2d at 1280-81.

## Dorthey Analysis Factors

When assessing whether a Dorthey challenge is viable, evaluate:

| Factor | Analysis Questions |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Nature of the Current Offense** | How serious is the current offense? Is it a crime against a person or a property/drug offense? Was anyone harmed? What are the specific facts — was this a minor instance of the offense or an aggravated one? |
| **Nature of the Predicates** | How serious are the predicate offenses? Are they violent or non-violent? How old are they? Is there a pattern of escalation or a pattern of minor, non-violent offenses? |
| **Defendant's Criminal History in Context** | Beyond the predicates, what does the full criminal history show? Is the defendant a career violent offender or someone with a history of non-violent offenses driven by addiction, poverty, or mental health issues? |
| **Proportionality** | Is the enhanced sentence grossly disproportionate to the current offense standing alone? Compare the enhanced sentence to the base sentencing range. Compare to sentences for objectively more serious offenses (e.g., does a fourth-offender enhancement for drug possession produce a sentence exceeding that for manslaughter?) |
| **Goals of Punishment** | Does the enhanced sentence serve legitimate penological goals — deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation, retribution? Or does it amount to the purposeless and needless imposition of pain and suffering? |
| **Mitigating Circumstances** | Age, health, family circumstances, employment history, substance abuse treatment history, mental health diagnoses, military service, cooperation with law enforcement, remorse |
| **Excessive Sentence Jurisprudence** | Has this circuit or the Louisiana Supreme Court found similar enhanced sentences excessive? Has the court upheld similar sentences? |

## Key Dorthey/Excessive Sentence Cases

- *State v. Dorthey*, 623 So.2d 1276 (La. 1993) — foundational case; constitutional review of habitual offender sentences
- *State v. Johnson*, 97-1906 (La. 3/4/98), 709 So.2d 672 — the defendant must show by clear and convincing evidence that the mandatory minimum is constitutionally excessive as applied to him; must rebut the presumption that the Legislature acted reasonably
- *State v. Lindsey*, 99-3256 (La. 10/17/00), 770 So.2d 339 — defendant's burden is heavy; courts should not second-guess legislative judgment lightly
- *State v. Mosby*, 2014-2704 (La. 11/20/15), 180 So.3d 1274 — reaffirmed Dorthey but emphasized the "heavy burden" on the defendant
- *Solem v. Helm*, 463 U.S. 277 (1983) — Eighth Amendment proportionality review for recidivist sentences (federal constitutional floor)
- *Ewing v. California*, 538 U.S. 11 (2003) — narrowed Solem; "three strikes" laws survive Eighth Amendment challenge unless grossly disproportionate
- *Graham v. Florida*, 560 U.S. 48 (2010) — categorical ban on LWOP for juvenile non-homicide offenders (relevant if predicate or current offense involved a juvenile defendant)
