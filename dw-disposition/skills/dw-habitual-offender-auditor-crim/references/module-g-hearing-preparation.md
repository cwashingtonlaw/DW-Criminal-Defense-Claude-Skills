# MODULE G — Habitual Offender Hearing Preparation

## Overview (moved from SKILL.md)

The hearing is the proceeding at which the State must prove the bill. Post-2017, the determination is made by a **jury** (formerly a three-judge panel under repealed La. R.S. 15:529.1(G)). Standard of proof: beyond a reasonable doubt. The State must prove (1) identity, (2) prior convictions, (3) Boykinization (if challenged), (4) felony status, (5) sequence, and (6) cleansing-period compliance. *State v. Payton*, 810 So.2d 1127 (La. 2002).

Defense preparation: build a per-predicate challenge matrix (deficiency, legal argument, exhibit, cross-examination target); prepare cross-examination of the State's fingerprint expert and records custodian; assemble defense exhibits (annotated transcripts, timelines, certified records); preserve all objections; plan post-hearing motions (new trial, La. C.Cr.P. Art. 881.1 reconsideration, Dorthey, La. C.Cr.P. Art. 914 appeal within 30 days).


The habitual offender hearing is the proceeding at which the State must prove the habitual offender bill. Under the post-2017 amendments, the determination is made by a **jury** (previously a three-judge panel under the now-repealed La. R.S. 15:529.1(G)). The standard of proof is **beyond a reasonable doubt**.

## Procedural Requirements

**Filing Requirements — La. R.S. 15:529.1(D):**

The State must file a habitual offender bill of information in open court or in the presence of the defendant. The bill must:
- Allege the current conviction
- List each predicate conviction with sufficient specificity (case number, court, charge, date of conviction)
- Be filed within the time limitations specified in the statute

**Time Limitations for Filing:**

| Situation | Filing Deadline |
|-----------|----------------|
| Current conviction by guilty plea | The habitual offender bill must be filed at the time of or prior to sentencing |
| Current conviction by trial (jury or bench) | The habitual offender bill must be filed at the time of or prior to sentencing |
| Current conviction on appeal — affirmed | The State may file after affirmance if it reserved its right to do so |

**Note:** The exact time limitations have been subject to judicial interpretation. Verify current jurisprudence on the timeliness of the habitual offender bill filing.

## State's Burden of Proof at Hearing

At the habitual offender hearing, the State must prove each of the following beyond a reasonable doubt:

1. **Identity** — The defendant is the same person convicted in each predicate case
2. **Prior convictions** — Each predicate conviction occurred and is valid
3. **Boykinization** — Each predicate obtained by guilty plea was constitutionally valid (if challenged)
4. **Felony status** — Each predicate was a felony conviction
5. **Sequence** — The required sequence of conviction-then-commission was followed
6. **Cleansing period** — No predicate has been cleansed by the passage of time (or that the cleansing period exception applies)

*State v. Payton*, 810 So.2d 1127 (La. 2002) — reaffirmed the State's burden at habitual offender hearings.

## Defense Hearing Strategy

**Challenge to Each Predicate:**

For each predicate identified as deficient in Modules A through D, prepare:

|-----------|-----------|----------------|------------------|-------------------------|
| Predicate 1 | [from audit] | [legal basis for challenge] | [defense exhibit — e.g., plea transcript showing Boykin deficiency] | [State's witness to cross-examine on this point] |
| Predicate 2 | [from audit] | [legal basis for challenge] | [defense exhibit] | [cross-examination target] |
| [etc.] | | | | |

**Cross-Examination of State's Witnesses:**

The State typically presents:
- A fingerprint expert (to prove identity)
- A records custodian (to authenticate predicate conviction records)
- Sometimes the prosecutor or officer from the predicate case

Prepare cross-examination targeting:

**Fingerprint Expert:**
- When were the known and questioned prints collected?
- How many points of comparison were identified?
- Were the prints of sufficient quality for reliable comparison?
- Was the comparison verified by a second examiner?

**Records Custodian:**
- Are the records certified copies?
- Do the records reflect all proceedings in the predicate case, or only selected entries?
- Is the plea transcript included? If not, does one exist?
- Do the records show whether the defendant was represented by counsel?
- Do the records reflect the Boykin colloquy?

**Defense Exhibits:**

Prepare the following defense exhibits for each challenged predicate:

| Exhibit | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Plea transcript (annotated) | Highlight Boykin deficiencies — mark each place where a right should have been addressed but was not |
| Minute entry (annotated) | Show what the minute entry says vs. what it should say for a constitutionally valid plea |
| Timeline chart | Visual showing the sequence of convictions and offenses — highlight any sequence breaks |
| Cleansing period calculation | Visual timeline showing the elapsed time between sentence completion and the subsequent offense |
| Certified records from predicate case | Any records that support the defense challenge (e.g., proof of appeal, vacated conviction, reclassified offense) |

## Post-Hearing Motions

If the court adjudicates the defendant a habitual offender over defense objections:

1. **Motion for New Trial** on the habitual offender adjudication (if applicable)
2. **Motion to Reconsider Sentence** under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 881.1
3. **Dorthey Motion** — constitutional excessive sentence challenge (Module F)
4. **Preserve appellate issues** — ensure all objections are on the record; file written assignments of error
