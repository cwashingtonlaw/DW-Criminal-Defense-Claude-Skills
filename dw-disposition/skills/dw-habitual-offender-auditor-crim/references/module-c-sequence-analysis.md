# MODULE C — Sequence Analysis

## Overview (moved from SKILL.md)

Louisiana's habitual offender law requires a specific sequence: each subsequent offense must have been committed **after** the prior conviction became final. *State v. Johnson*, 432 So.2d 815 (La. 1983) — "The sequence is conviction, then commission of a new felony, then conviction for the new felony."

For each predicate pair, verify (1) the date the prior conviction became final, (2) the date the next offense was committed, and (3) that the latter postdates the former. A conviction becomes final when the appeal time expires (30 days under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 914), or when the appellate court affirms and further review is exhausted. *State v. Baker*, 230 So.3d 207 (La. App. 4th Cir. 2017). Multiple offenses arising from the same criminal episode may constitute a single conviction for habitual purposes. *State v. Parker*, 2003-0924 (La. 4/14/04), 871 So.2d 317.


Louisiana's habitual offender law requires that convictions occur in a specific sequence. The requirement is not merely that the defendant has multiple prior convictions — each subsequent offense must have been committed **after** the conviction for the prior offense became final.

## The Sequence Requirement

Under La. R.S. 15:529.1, the State must prove:

- For a **second offender** enhancement: the current offense was committed after the first predicate conviction became final
- For a **third offender** enhancement: the second predicate offense was committed after the first predicate conviction became final, AND the current offense was committed after the second predicate conviction became final
- For a **fourth offender** enhancement: the same sequential pattern — each offense committed after the prior conviction's finality

*State v. Johnson*, 432 So.2d 815 (La. 1983) — "The statute requires that the offender shall have been convicted of a prior felony and, after such conviction, shall have committed a subsequent felony. The sequence is conviction, then commission of a new felony, then conviction for the new felony."

## Sequence Analysis Worksheet

For each predicate pair (Predicate N and Predicate N+1), verify:

| Sequence Check | Required Proof | What to Audit |
|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Predicate 1 conviction date** | Date the first predicate conviction became final | Was the conviction final (no pending appeal)? If appealed, was it affirmed? What date did finality attach? |
| **Predicate 2 offense date** | Date the second predicate offense was committed | Was the second offense committed AFTER the first conviction became final? If the second offense was committed before the first conviction, the sequence fails |
| **Predicate 2 conviction date** | Date the second predicate conviction became final | Was it final before the current offense? |
| **Current offense date** | Date the current offense was committed | Was the current offense committed AFTER the last predicate conviction became final? |

## Finality: When Does a Conviction Become "Final"?

A conviction becomes final for habitual offender purposes when:

1. The time for filing an appeal has expired and no appeal was taken (30 days from sentencing under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 914), OR
2. If an appeal was taken, when the appellate court affirms the conviction and the time for seeking further review expires, OR
3. If the Louisiana Supreme Court grants writs, when the Supreme Court affirms (or denies writs after an appellate affirmance)

*State v. Baker*, 230 So.3d 207 (La. App. 4th Cir. 2017) — analyzed finality requirements for predicate convictions.

**Audit checkpoint:** For each predicate, determine the exact date of finality. If the defendant appealed, trace the appellate history to determine when the conviction became final. If the appeal was pending when the subsequent offense was committed, the sequence may be broken.

## Common Sequence Deficiencies

| Deficiency | Severity | Explanation |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| Subsequent offense committed before prior conviction's finality | FATAL | Breaks the required sequence; the prior conviction cannot serve as a predicate for purposes of enhancing the subsequent offense |
| Multiple offenses committed on the same date or during the same criminal episode | FATAL (potentially) | If multiple predicates arose from the same criminal episode, they may constitute a single conviction for habitual offender purposes — *State v. Parker*, 2003-0924 (La. 4/14/04), 871 So.2d 317 |
| Conviction date unclear — minute entry is ambiguous | SIGNIFICANT | If the State cannot establish the exact date of conviction, the sequence analysis cannot be completed; burden is on the State to prove the date |
| Appeal history undocumented | MODERATE | Without appellate records, finality cannot be confirmed; the defendant may have an appeal pending that defeats finality |
