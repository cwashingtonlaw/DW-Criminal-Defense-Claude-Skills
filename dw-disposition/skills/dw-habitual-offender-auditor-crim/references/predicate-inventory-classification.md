## Predicate Inventory Table

For each predicate conviction listed in the State's habitual offender bill, extract and record:

| Field | What to Record |
|-------|---------------|
| **Predicate #** | Sequential number as listed in the habitual bill (Predicate 1, Predicate 2, etc.) |
| **Case Number** | Docket/case number of the prior conviction |
| **Court/Parish** | Court and parish where the prior conviction occurred |
| **Charge** | The offense of conviction with statutory citation |
| **Crime of Violence?** | Whether the offense qualifies as a "crime of violence" under La. R.S. 14:2(B) — this determines which enhancement tier applies |
| **Date of Offense** | When the predicate offense was committed |
| **Date of Conviction** | When the guilty plea was entered or the verdict was returned |
| **Manner of Conviction** | Guilty plea, jury verdict, or bench trial |
| **Sentence Imposed** | The sentence imposed for the predicate conviction |
| **Date Sentence Completed** | When the defendant completed the sentence (discharge, end of probation/parole) — critical for cleansing period |
| **Appeal Status** | Whether the conviction was appealed, and the outcome |
| **Documentation Available** | What records exist for this predicate (certified bill, minutes, plea transcript, commitment order) |
| **Deficiency Flags** | Preliminary flags based on initial review — detailed analysis follows in modules |

## Conviction Classification: Crime of Violence Determination

The distinction between a "crime of violence" predicate and a non-violent predicate is outcome-determinative for the enhancement tier. Under La. R.S. 14:2(B), a "crime of violence" includes:

> Enumerated offenses: first and second degree murder, manslaughter, negligent homicide, aggravated and second degree battery, aggravated assault, all degrees of rape, all degrees of sexual battery, all degrees of kidnapping, all degrees of robbery, all degrees of burglary (except unauthorized entry of a place of business), arson, terrorism offenses, assault by drive-by shooting, carjacking, home invasion, vehicular homicide (certain circumstances), stalking (felony), domestic abuse battery (certain circumstances), battery of a dating partner (certain circumstances), human trafficking, and additional offenses enumerated in the statute.

**Note:** La. R.S. 14:2(B) is amended frequently. Verify the version of the statute in effect at the time of the predicate offense AND at the time of the habitual offender adjudication. If the offense was not enumerated as a crime of violence at the time it was committed, it may not qualify regardless of subsequent amendments. *See* State v. Loggins, 2017-0778 (La. 6/27/18), 252 So.3d 877 (analyzing temporal application of crime of violence definitions).
