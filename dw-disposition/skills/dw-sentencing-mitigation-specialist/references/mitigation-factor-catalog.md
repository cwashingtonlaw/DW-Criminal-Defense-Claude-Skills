# Mitigation Factor Catalog & ACE Assessment

## Category 1: Childhood and Family History

**Information to Gather:**
- Birth circumstances (premature birth, prenatal substance exposure, complications)
- Family structure (two-parent, single-parent, grandparent-raised, foster care)
- Parental substance abuse, mental illness, or incarceration
- Exposure to domestic violence
- Physical, sexual, or emotional abuse or neglect
- Number of household moves, school changes
- Stability of housing and basic needs
- Sibling relationships and responsibilities (parentification)
- Extended family support or absence
- Cultural and community influences

### Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) Score Assessment

| ACE Category | Question | Score |
|-------------|----------|-------|
| Physical abuse | Did a parent or adult in the household often push, grab, slap, or throw something at you? | 0 / 1 |
| Emotional abuse | Did a parent or adult in the household often swear at you, insult you, or humiliate you? | 0 / 1 |
| Sexual abuse | Did an adult or person at least 5 years older ever touch you sexually or attempt intercourse? | 0 / 1 |
| Physical neglect | Did you often feel that you didn't have enough to eat, had to wear dirty clothes, or had no one to protect you? | 0 / 1 |
| Emotional neglect | Did you often feel that no one in your family loved you, thought you were important, or looked out for you? | 0 / 1 |
| Domestic violence | Was your mother or stepmother often pushed, grabbed, slapped, or had something thrown at her? | 0 / 1 |
| Household substance abuse | Did you live with anyone who was a problem drinker, alcoholic, or who used street drugs? | 0 / 1 |
| Household mental illness | Was a household member depressed, mentally ill, or did a household member attempt suicide? | 0 / 1 |
| Parental separation/divorce | Were your parents ever separated or divorced? | 0 / 1 |
| Incarcerated household member | Did a household member go to prison? | 0 / 1 |
| **TOTAL ACE SCORE** | | **/10** |

**ACE Score Significance for Sentencing:**
- ACE score of 4+ is associated with significantly increased risk of substance abuse, mental health disorders, criminal behavior, and chronic health conditions
- Research (Felitti et al., 1998) demonstrates dose-response relationship between ACEs and negative outcomes
- ACE data contextualizes -- but does not excuse -- criminal behavior
- Effective mitigation presents ACEs as explanatory factors that make rehabilitation possible with appropriate intervention

## Category 2: Mental Health

**Information to Gather:**
- Diagnosed conditions (depression, anxiety, PTSD, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, personality disorders)
- Age of onset and progression
- Treatment history (inpatient, outpatient, medication, therapy)
- Treatment compliance and barriers to treatment
- Relationship between mental health condition and offense conduct
- Current treatment status and prognosis
- Expert evaluations (psychological, psychiatric, neuropsychological)
- Traumatic brain injury (TBI) history -- mechanism, severity, cognitive effects
- Intellectual disability or developmental delay
- Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD)

**Mental Health Mitigation Framework:**
1. **Diagnose:** What condition exists? (Expert documentation required)
2. **Connect:** How does the condition relate to the offense conduct? (Not causation, but context)
3. **Treat:** What treatment is available and recommended? (Treatment plan)
4. **Demonstrate:** What steps has the client already taken toward treatment? (Compliance evidence)
5. **Project:** What is the prognosis with appropriate treatment? (Expert opinion)

## Category 3: Substance Abuse

**Information to Gather:**
- Substances used (type, duration, frequency, quantity)
- Age of first use
- Progression of use (recreational to dependent)
- Prior treatment attempts (number, type, duration, outcome)
- Current sobriety status and duration
- Relationship between substance use and offense conduct
- Family history of substance abuse
- Co-occurring mental health conditions
- Treatment program availability and client's willingness to participate

**Substance Abuse Mitigation Framework:**
1. Document the addiction as a medical condition (DSM-5 Substance Use Disorder criteria)
2. Show the trajectory: when use began, what escalated it, what treatment was attempted
3. Present treatment failures as part of the disease, not character deficiency
4. Demonstrate current motivation for recovery (if applicable)
5. Propose a treatment-based sentencing alternative with specific program and monitoring

## Category 4: Employment and Education

**Information to Gather:**
- Employment history (chronological, with gaps explained)
- Longest period of stable employment
- Skills, trades, certifications, professional licenses
- Employer references and character letters
- Educational history (highest grade completed, GED, college, vocational)
- Educational achievements despite adversity
- Current employment or employability prospects
- Financial obligations (child support, restitution capacity)

## Category 5: Military Service

**Information to Gather:**
- Branch, dates of service, rank at discharge
- Discharge status (Honorable, General, Other Than Honorable, Bad Conduct, Dishonorable)
- Military Occupational Specialty (MOS)
- Deployment history (location, duration, combat exposure)
- Awards, decorations, commendations
- Service-connected injuries (physical and psychological)
- VA disability rating and benefits
- Combat-related PTSD, TBI, or other conditions
- Transition difficulties upon return to civilian life
- VA treatment history

**Military Mitigation Note:** Service to country is among the most powerful mitigating factors. Even imperfect service records can be presented effectively. Combat exposure and its psychological consequences provide compelling context for post-service criminal behavior. Courts routinely give significant weight to military service. See 18 U.S.C. SS 3553(a) (federal); La. C.Cr.P. Art. 894.1(B) (state).

## Category 6: Community Ties and Rehabilitation

**Information to Gather:**
- Family support system (who will support client upon release)
- Dependent care responsibilities (children, elderly parents)
- Community involvement (church, civic organizations, volunteer work)
- Mentoring relationships (both as mentor and mentee)
- Letters of support (quantity, diversity, specificity)
- Post-arrest rehabilitation efforts (treatment, education, employment)
- Jail conduct record (if incarcerated pretrial)
- Program participation while incarcerated
- Restitution payments or efforts (if applicable)
- Concrete reentry plan
