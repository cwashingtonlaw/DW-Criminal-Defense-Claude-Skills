**Title:** Immigration-Consequences Advisement (Padilla v. Kentucky)

**Purpose:** Because you are not a U.S. citizen, any decision in this criminal case — especially a
guilty or no-contest plea — may carry serious, permanent immigration consequences separate from any
criminal sentence. Under *Padilla v. Kentucky*, 559 U.S. 356 (2010), your attorney must advise you.
Read this with your interpreter and attorney before any plea decision.

**Immigration Consequences You Must Understand (numbered points):**
1. You are not a U.S. citizen; a conviction can affect your right to remain in / return to the U.S.
2. *(Case-specific)* The charged offenses are classified under federal immigration law as … *(compose
   from `immigration-consequences-classification.md`; preserve statutory cites)*.
3. A conviction (plea, no-contest, or trial) for a removable offense makes you deportable
   (8 U.S.C. § 1227(a)(2)) and may result in removal.
4. It may make you inadmissible (permanently, for an aggravated felony) — barred from lawful return,
   a visa, a green card, or citizenship.
5. It may make you ineligible for relief from removal and bar asylum/withholding (particularly
   serious crime) and naturalization.
6. You may face mandatory ICE detention (8 U.S.C. § 1226(c)) with no immigration bond.
7. *(Conditional)* Sex-offense / controlled-substance / firearm / domestic-violence points as
   applicable (see JSON `conditional_points`).
8. A conviction may destroy any status/benefit you hold or seek (LPR, visa, TPS, DACA, asylum).

**About Your Wish to Be Deported (conditional section):** a conviction is not a reliable/controlled
route to deportation (ICE and the immigration court are a separate system); pleading guilty could
make things permanently worse (serve the criminal sentence first, then mandatory detention, a
permanent bar on legal return, and a federal felony under 8 U.S.C. § 1326 on reentry); immigration
goals must be evaluated by immigration counsel before any plea.

**Your Rights and Our Recommendation:** do not accept/reject any plea offer until immigration
counsel reviews it; you have the right to consult immigration counsel; criminal defense counsel is
not immigration counsel and this is general information, not eligibility advice.

**Certifications (signature blocks):** Client acknowledgment; Attorney certification (advised via
qualified interpreter); Interpreter certification (accurate two-way interpretation).
