# Collateral-Consequences Modules (Framework)

Loaded at Step 2A. Use these four modules to advise ANY client — citizen or not — of the
non-immigration collateral consequences of a plea or conviction, then complete the one-page
checklist in `references/plea-counseling-checklist.md`. This is a FRAMEWORK, not a determination:
several consequences turn on the exact statute of conviction, the sentence imposed, and agency
discretion. Apply the same clarity rule as the immigration module — **when a consequence is
clearly triggered, say it plainly ("will"); when it is unclear, say "may — requires
verification" and route to the specialist skill or verifying authority rather than guessing.**
Keep every statutory citation exact; preserve `[VERIFY CITATION]` flags in the output until the
attorney confirms them.

Deeper analysis routes: sex-offense registration → `dw-sex-offense-specialist-crim`; firearms →
`dw-firearms-specialist-crim`; drug-specific consequences → `dw-drug-offense-specialist-crim`;
plea-vs-trial math → `dw-plea-negotiation-analyzer-crim`.

## MODULE CC-A — Sex-Offender Registration & Notification (La. R.S. 15:540 et seq.)

- **Registrable offenses** — defined at La. R.S. 15:541 ("sex offense" and "criminal offense
  against a victim who is a minor"). Check EVERY charge in the plea — including attempts — against
  the definitions; do not assume only Title 14 "sex" articles register.
- **Duration — La. R.S. 15:544**: baseline **15 years** from initial Louisiana registration;
  **25 years** for a sexual offense against a victim who is a minor; **lifetime** for the
  aggravated offenses listed in Subsection E (e.g., Tier III / "aggravated offense" convictions).
  The 15-year period may be reduced to 10 years on petition after a clean record for the entire
  10-year period; lifetime registration may be reduced only in the narrow circumstances of
  Subsection E [VERIFY CITATION — confirm current 15:544 reduction provisions before advising].
- **Tier renewal frequency**: Tier 1 annual, Tier 2 every six months, Tier 3 quarterly, in person
  [VERIFY CITATION — La. R.S. 15:542.1.1 periodic-renewal schedule].
- **Notification duties** — community notification, mailouts, internet identifiers, ID-card and
  driver's-license branding [VERIFY CITATION — La. R.S. 15:542, 15:542.1, and 32:412 branding
  provision], and registration fees.
- **Residency / presence restrictions** — La. R.S. 14:91.1 (unlawful residence or presence of a
  sex offender; 1,000-foot school-related zones) and 14:91.2 (unlawful presence near schools /
  child-related facilities for offenders whose victim was a minor) [VERIFY CITATION — confirm
  current distances, covered locations, and which convictions trigger each].
- **Federal overlay** — SORNA, 34 U.S.C. § 20901 et seq., imposes independent federal duties that
  follow the client across state lines.

**Advise**: registration is not negotiable after conviction of a registrable offense — the tier
and duration flow from the statute of conviction, which makes charge selection in the plea the
main lever.

## MODULE CC-B — Firearms (State + Federal)

- **La. R.S. 14:95.1 (state felon-in-possession)** — applies to convictions for the enumerated
  felonies: crimes of violence (La. R.S. 14:2(B)), sex offenses, drug offenses, burglary,
  unauthorized entry of an inhabited dwelling, felony illegal use of weapons, manufacture/possession
  of a delayed-action incendiary device or bomb, possession of a firearm with CDS, and attempts to
  commit any of these [VERIFY CITATION — confirm the current enumerated list before advising a
  specific charge is outside it]. Violation: 5–20 years at hard labor without benefits plus a
  mandatory fine.
- **10-year cleansing rule** — 14:95.1 does not apply to a person who has no felony conviction
  for **ten years from completion of sentence, probation, parole, or suspension of sentence**.
  State rights effectively revive after that clean decade.
- **18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1) (federal bar)** — lifetime prohibition for any conviction punishable by
  more than one year; **no sunset and no ten-year cleansing**. § 922(g)(9) imposes the same
  lifetime bar for a misdemeanor crime of domestic violence.
- **Interaction — expungement does NOT restore** — Louisiana's 10-year cleansing and an
  expungement under La. C.Cr.P. art. 971 et seq. do not, by themselves, lift the federal § 922(g)
  bar. Under 18 U.S.C. § 921(a)(20), only relief that fully restores civil rights or an
  expungement/pardon without a firearms reservation takes the conviction outside § 922(g)
  [VERIFY CITATION — confirm the current § 921(a)(20) analysis for Louisiana convictions;
  the conservative advice is that a governor's pardon restoring firearm rights is the reliable
  route]. Never advise that state-law restoration alone makes possession lawful.

**Advise**: for an enumerated felony, the client **will** lose firearm rights — state for at
least 10 clean years, federal for life absent extraordinary relief.

## MODULE CC-C — Occupational Licensing & Employment

- **La. R.S. 37:2950 (licensing-restriction reform)** — a person may not be disqualified from a
  licensed trade, occupation, or profession *solely* because of a prior criminal record **unless
  the conviction directly relates** to the occupation. The licensing entity must give written
  reasons, weigh the statutory factors (nature/seriousness of the offense, duties of the
  occupation, time elapsed, aggravating/mitigating circumstances), and answer a pre-application
  eligibility request within 45 days.
- **Carve-outs** — certain boards and professions are excepted from 37:2950's protection (e.g.,
  medical/health-care boards, law enforcement, and others enumerated in the statute)
  [VERIFY CITATION — confirm the current exception list in 37:2950 for the client's specific
  license].
- **Board-specific triggers** — check the client's actual license (nursing, teaching, contractor,
  insurance, securities, real estate, cosmetology, etc.) for mandatory-revocation or
  good-moral-character provisions in its own enabling statute [board-specific — verify].
- **CDL** — commercial driver's license disqualifications under 49 U.S.C. § 31310 and
  49 C.F.R. Part 383 (including lifetime disqualification for using a CMV in felony drug
  distribution) [VERIFY CITATION].
- **Employment** — private-employer background screening; felony vs. misdemeanor labeling; bonding
  and security-clearance effects [general framework — no single controlling statute].

**Advise**: ask what the client does for a living and what licenses/certifications the client
holds or plans to seek BEFORE the plea; this is the most commonly missed consequence.

## MODULE CC-D — Housing, Benefits, Student Aid, Voting

- **Public housing / Section 8** — federally assisted housing screening under 42 U.S.C.
  §§ 13661–13663: PHAs and owners have broad *discretion* to deny for drug-related or violent
  criminal activity, but only two categorical **lifetime** bars — persons subject to lifetime
  sex-offender registration (§ 13663) and persons convicted of methamphetamine production on the
  premises of federally assisted housing [VERIFY CITATION — 42 U.S.C. § 1437n(f) and 24 C.F.R.
  §§ 960.204 / 982.553]. A household member's conviction can jeopardize the whole household's
  assistance. Advise "may" — the decision is PHA-specific.
- **SNAP / TANF** — the federal drug-felony ban, 21 U.S.C. § 862a, does NOT apply in Louisiana:
  the state opted out effective October 1, 2017 [VERIFY CITATION — La. R.S. 46:233.2/46:233.3
  opt-out provision]. A drug felony alone does not bar SNAP/TANF here, though benefits are
  suspended during incarceration and fleeing-felon / probation-violation rules still apply
  [VERIFY].
- **Federal student aid** — the drug-conviction suspension of Title IV eligibility (former
  20 U.S.C. § 1091(r)) was eliminated by the FAFSA Simplification Act (Consolidated
  Appropriations Act, 2021); as of the 2023–24 award year a drug conviction no longer bars
  federal student aid, and incarcerated students may receive Pell Grants through approved
  prison-education programs [VERIFY CITATION — confirm current-year FAFSA treatment].
  Discretionary federal-benefit denial for drug *trafficking* convictions remains available under
  21 U.S.C. § 862 [VERIFY CITATION].
- **Voting** — La. Const. art. I, § 10 and **La. R.S. 18:102(A)(1)**: registration and voting are
  suspended while the client is "**under an order of imprisonment**" for a felony — which
  includes probation and parole, not just custody. Exception (Act 636 of 2018, eff. March 1,
  2019): a person under an order of imprisonment who has **not been incarcerated under that order
  within the last five years** may register and vote after submitting corrections documentation
  to the registrar (election-offense convictions excepted) [VERIFY CITATION — confirm current
  18:102/18:177 mechanics]. Rights fully restore on completion of sentence.
- **Jury service** — felony conviction disqualifies unless rights are restored [VERIFY CITATION —
  La. C.Cr.P. art. 401 juror qualifications].
- **Other flags to screen** — parental rights/custody and adoption proceedings, military
  enlistment or discharge, public office and notary eligibility [general framework — verify per
  client].

## Scope note

State and federal collateral-consequence law changes frequently (licensing reform, benefits
opt-outs, and firearm-rights litigation are all active areas). This reference gives the standard
framework only; the attorney verifies currency before the advisement is signed, and the checklist
in `references/plea-counseling-checklist.md` documents what was advised and when.
