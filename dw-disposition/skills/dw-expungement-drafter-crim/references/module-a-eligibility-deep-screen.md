# Module A — Eligibility Deep Screen (La. C.Cr.P. arts. 976–978, 985.1)

Louisiana expungement law was consolidated into Title XXXIV of the Code of Criminal Procedure (arts. 971–995) by Acts 2014, No. 145. Analyze **each arrest incident separately** — eligibility, venue, and fees all run per arrest, and one motion covers one arrest only.

## A-2. Art. 976 — arrest that did NOT result in a conviction

Grounds (any one suffices):

1. **Not prosecuted and time-barred** — the person was not prosecuted for the offense of arrest and the time limitations of La. C.Cr.P. arts. 571–572 have run. Standard prescriptive periods (art. 572): 6 years (felony necessarily punishable at hard labor), 4 years (felony not necessarily at hard labor), 2 years (misdemeanor punishable by fine or imprisonment), 6 months (fine-only misdemeanor). No time limitation for crimes punishable by death or life imprisonment (art. 571); extended periods apply to certain sex offenses (art. 571.1) — check before declaring an old arrest time-barred.
2. **DA declined to prosecute** for any reason arising out of the arrest — expressly including successful completion of a pretrial diversion program.
3. **Prosecution instituted and finally disposed of** by dismissal, sustaining of a motion to quash, or acquittal.

There is **no waiting period** for an art. 976 motion once a ground exists, and later unrelated convictions do not defeat it. Watch two traps: (a) if prosecution is still possible (not time-barred, no dismissal), the arrest is not yet expungeable; (b) DWI arrests resolved by diversion carry special timing limits — verify the current DWI-diversion restriction in art. 976 before advising `[VERIFY CITATION]`.

## A-3. Art. 977 — misdemeanor conviction

Two routes:

1. **Art. 894(B) set-aside route** — conviction set aside and prosecution dismissed under art. 894(B) (deferred sentence successfully completed). No cleansing period. If the deferral was completed but the set-aside was never obtained, draft the Motion to Set Aside first (Module C).
2. **Five-year route** — more than 5 years have elapsed since completion of sentence, deferred adjudication, or probation/parole, AND no felony conviction during that period AND no pending felony charge, supported by DA certification.

**Exclusions (art. 977(C)):** conviction for (a) a sex offense as defined in La. R.S. 15:541 — except that interim expungement of the underlying felony arrest remains available under art. 985.1; (b) domestic abuse battery; (c) stalking. These misdemeanor convictions are not expungeable.

**First-offense marijuana:** a first-offense possession-of-marijuana misdemeanor conviction may be expunged **90 days** from the date of conviction (special provision in art. 977) — dramatically shorter than the 5-year route, with a reduced fee schedule (Module B).

**DWI note:** misdemeanor DWI is expungeable via the routes above, but art. 894(B) deferral for DWI is itself limited (historically available only once in a 10-year period `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm current art. 894(B) DWI limitation]`), and the expungement filing carries an OMV proof requirement and additional fee (Module B).

## A-4. Art. 978 — felony conviction

Three routes (art. 978(A)):

1. **Art. 893(E) set-aside route** — conviction set aside and prosecution dismissed under art. 893(E) (deferred felony sentence, including specialty-court completion). Practice guides state set-asides are limited per lifetime (commonly stated as two) `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm current art. 893 limit]`.
2. **Ten-year route** — more than 10 years since completion of any sentence/probation/parole, AND no conviction of **any** crime (felony or misdemeanor) during that period, AND no pending charges, supported by DA certification.
3. **First-offender pardon route** — person eligible for or received the automatic first-offender pardon (La. Const. art. IV, § 5(E)(1)), excluding crimes of violence and sex offenses.

**Exclusions (art. 978(B)) — no expungement where the conviction was for:**
- A **crime of violence** as defined by La. R.S. 14:2(B) (subject to the art. 978(E) exception below)
- A **sex offense** or criminal offense against a victim who is a minor as defined in La. R.S. 15:541
- **Drug violations** — most distribution/production offenses under La. R.S. 40:966–970; exceptions exist for possession-level convictions and where a first-offender pardon was obtained — verify the current drug-offense carve-outs against the statute text before advising `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm current art. 978(B) drug exceptions]`
- **Domestic abuse battery** (felony)

**Art. 978(E) exception — expungeable crimes of violence.** After contradictory hearing, the court MAY order expungement of a conviction for: aggravated battery, second degree battery, aggravated criminal damage to property, simple robbery, purse snatching, or illegal use of weapons or dangerous instrumentalities — if more than 10 years have elapsed since completion of sentence, no other conviction during that period, and no pending charges. Additional conditions (e.g., that the offense not involve a family/household member) may apply — verify the current 978(E) condition list before drafting `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm full art. 978(E) conditions]`. This route always requires the contradictory hearing; budget for it.

## A-5. Art. 985.1 — interim expungement

Where a **felony arrest resulted only in a misdemeanor conviction**, the person may at any time obtain an *interim* expungement of the felony arrest entry from the criminal history. The misdemeanor conviction itself remains of record (expungeable later only if independently eligible under art. 977). No cleansing period; practice guides state no limit on the number of interim expungements `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm no-frequency-limit reading]`. Uniform forms for the interim motion and order are codified at arts. 994–995. This is the only relief available for a misdemeanor conviction that arose from a felony sex-offense arrest (art. 977's sex-offense exclusion expressly preserves the art. 985.1 route).

## A-6. Frequency caps — REPEALED (common trap)

Former art. 978(D) (one felony expungement per 15 years) was **repealed by Acts 2020, No. 78**, and the parallel misdemeanor frequency restriction was removed in the same reform — current arts. 977 and 978 contain no once-per-X-years expungement cap `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm the misdemeanor-cap repeal specifics]`. Many older guides, clerk instruction sheets, and DA websites still recite the 5-year/15-year caps. Do not deny eligibility on a frequency ground without checking the current statute text. The practical limits that DO survive: the art. 894(B)/893(E) deferral mechanisms have their own availability limits (A-3, A-4 above).

## A-7. Automated "Clean Slate" expungement (art. 985.2)

Art. 985.2 (Acts 2021/2023 amendments) directs the LSP Bureau of Criminal Identification and Information to identify records eligible under arts. 976–978 and provides an automated, no-cost expungement request process (statutorily contemplated to begin January 1, 2025, effective upon legislative appropriation). Implementation status and covered record types must be verified as of the analysis date `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm art. 985.2 implementation status and scope]`. Where the automated route is live and covers the record, it may spare the client the art. 983 fees — note it in the cost map as an alternative, but do not rely on it for time-sensitive needs.
