# Module D — Effects Counseling + Client Letter

Louisiana expungement is powerful but narrower than clients assume. The counseling section of the deep-screen memo and the client letter must both carry the DOES / DOES NOT framework below. Never let a deliverable imply destruction, invisibility, or restoration of rights that art. 973 does not provide.

## D-1. What expungement DOES (La. C.Cr.P. art. 973)

- Removes the record of arrest/conviction from **public view** — it is no longer a public record; the clerk, arresting agency, and LSP Bureau seal it from ordinary disclosure.
- The person **is not required to disclose** the expunged arrest or conviction, or the fact of expungement, to private employers and others outside the statutory carve-outs.
- Feeds the state background-check channels most private Louisiana employers use, so routine checks should come back clean **once processing completes** (allow ± 2 months post-order for the LSP certificate of compliance; private data brokers may lag longer and may require direct dispute letters).

## D-2. What expungement DOES NOT do

1. **Records are confidential, NOT destroyed.** Law enforcement, criminal justice agencies, prosecutors, and courts retain full access — an expunged conviction still exists for enhancement/predicate purposes (e.g., habitual-offender billing, DWI second-offense enhancement) `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm enhancement-use rule against current art. 973 and jurisprudence]`.
2. **Licensing-board carve-outs.** On written, confidential request, expunged records remain available to the entities enumerated in art. 973, including: Office of Financial Institutions; the state boards of Medical Examiners, Nursing, Dentistry, Psychologist Examiners, Pharmacy, Social Work Examiners, Chiropractic Examiners; the Emergency Medical Services Certification Commission; the Louisiana Attorney Disciplinary Board / Office of Disciplinary Counsel; the Louisiana Supreme Court Committee on Bar Admissions; the Department of Insurance; and the Licensed Professional Counselors Board — plus law-enforcement hiring (POST) channels `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm the current art. 973 entity list before quoting it to a client in a licensing posture]`. A client applying to one of these must answer truthfully to that entity.
3. **Firearms rights are NOT restored by expungement alone.** La. R.S. 14:95.1 prohibits firearm possession by persons convicted of enumerated felonies until its own 10-year cleansing period (from completion of sentence) runs; an expungement does not shorten it. Federally, 18 U.S.C. § 922(g) disabilities (felony; misdemeanor crime of domestic violence under § 922(g)(9)) may persist notwithstanding state expungement — whether a Louisiana expungement lifts the federal disability under 18 U.S.C. § 921(a)(20)/(a)(33) is a fact-specific federal question. **Written rule for all deliverables: "An expungement does not, by itself, restore firearm rights. Do not possess a firearm without a specific attorney opinion on both state and federal law."**
4. **No effect on:** federal records and databases (FBI/NCIC retention rules differ), immigration consequences (expunged convictions generally still count as convictions under federal immigration law — route to **dw-padilla-advisement-crim**), out-of-state records, sex-offender registration obligations, DPS/OMV driving records, private media/news coverage, or civil judgments/restitution.
5. **Not a pardon and not innocence.** A conviction remains a conviction unless separately set aside; only a factual-innocence determination or pardon changes its legal character.

## D-3. Counseling checklist (memo section)

- [ ] Client's stated purpose (job, license, housing, firearms, peace of mind) matched against what expungement actually achieves for that purpose
- [ ] If licensing: is the target board on the art. 973 carve-out list? Counsel disclosure strategy
- [ ] If firearms: 14:95.1 cleansing-period math + federal § 922(g) screen; written no-possession warning until cleared
- [ ] If non-citizen: Padilla routing — expungement is not immigration relief
- [ ] Timing expectations (Module B-7) including private-database lag
- [ ] Duty of candor in future proceedings where the carve-outs apply
