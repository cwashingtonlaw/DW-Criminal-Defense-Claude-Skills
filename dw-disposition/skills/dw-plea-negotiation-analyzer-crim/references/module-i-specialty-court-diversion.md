# Module I — Specialty Court & Diversion Analysis

## Purpose

Whenever a specialty-court or diversion track is offered — or is plausibly available and has not
been explored — evaluate it as a full alternative to the straight-plea structures in Module E,
using the track-comparison matrix below. A diversion or deferral track can change not only the
sentence but the client's permanent record and collateral-consequence profile, so this module
always feeds the Step 11 collateral-consequences gate before any recommendation is finalized.

- **La. R.S. 13:5401** — Reentry courts (workforce-development sentencing option; defendant is
  sentenced to DOC custody with intensive reentry programming) `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm
  participating districts and eligibility]`.
- **DWI / sobriety court** — treatment-track alternative for DWI offenses; interacts with the
  DWI sentencing articles `[VERIFY CITATION]` — coordinate with `dw-dwi-specialist-crim`.
- **La. C.Cr.P. Art. 893(B)** — when the court orders completion of a drug-division, sobriety,
  mental-health, veterans, or reentry program, felony probation may extend to eight years.
- **La. C.Cr.P. Art. 893(E)** — deferred imposition of sentence for an eligible first felony
  offender: guilty plea entered, sentence deferred, probation imposed; on successful completion
  the conviction is **set aside and the prosecution dismissed**. Excluded offenses (crimes of
  violence, sex offenses, others) and the once-only limitation must be checked against the
  current article `[VERIFY CITATION — Art. 893(E) exclusions and limits]`.
- **La. C.Cr.P. Art. 894(B)** — the misdemeanor counterpart: deferral, probation, set-aside, and
  dismissal on completion.
- **La. R.S. 40:983** — conditional discharge for a first controlled-dangerous-substance
  possession offense: probation without entry of judgment of guilt; dismissal on completion is
  **without court adjudication of guilt** `[VERIFY CITATION — confirm current scope]`.
- **DA pretrial diversion** — a creature of prosecutorial discretion, not statute: program terms,
  fees, and eligibility are parish-specific (verify the current Calcasieu Parish DA program
  terms with the DA's office). Charges are held in abeyance or refused; completion produces a
  dismissal or refusal **without any plea being entered**.

## Expungement interaction

Route the actual eligibility analysis and drafting to **dw-expungement-drafter-crim** (sibling
skill; pass the disposition mechanism from the matrix row). The negotiation-stage points:

1. **Track choice controls later record relief.** A no-plea diversion dismissal is expunged as an
   arrest without conviction; an Art. 893(E)/894(B) set-aside unlocks conviction expungement
   without the usual waiting period `[VERIFY CITATION — art. 977/978 mechanics]`; a straight
   conviction waits out the statutory cleansing periods. Bargaining for the track IS bargaining
   for the expungement.
2. **Set-aside + expungement is not erasure.** The conviction can remain visible to law
   enforcement and licensing boards, can serve as a predicate `[VERIFY CITATION — habitual and
   enhancement treatment of set-aside pleas]`, and does not lift the federal firearm bar
   (18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1)).
3. **Immigration.** Under 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(48)(A), a guilty plea plus any restraint counts as a
   "conviction" for immigration purposes **even if later set aside** under Art. 893/894 — only a
   true no-plea diversion avoids this. For any non-citizen, this difference alone can decide the
   track; run the full analysis through `dw-padilla-advisement-crim`.

## Strategic Considerations

1. **Compare against Module A exposure, not against zero.** Diversion fees and 12-24 months of
   intensive supervision are still usually a deep discount against felony exposure — but quantify
   both.
2. **Price the failure mode.** In plea-first tracks the client who fails is sentenced on the
   original charge with the plea already entered and trial rights gone. Assess realistically
   (substance-abuse severity, transportation, ability to pay fees) whether THIS client can
   complete THIS program.
3. **Cost is a real barrier.** Program and supervision fees can be prohibitive for indigent
   clients; ask about fee waivers/sliding scales before recommending `[program-specific — verify]`.
4. **Ask even when no offer is on the table.** Specialty-court availability is unevenly raised by
   the State; an eligible client's counsel should raise it as a counter-offer (Module E
   recommendation framework).
