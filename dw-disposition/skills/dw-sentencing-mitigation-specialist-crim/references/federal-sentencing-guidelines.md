# Federal Sentencing Guidelines (USSG) & 5th Circuit Framework

## Federal Sentencing Overview

**Post-Booker Framework:**
- **United States v. Booker, 543 U.S. 220 (2005):** Guidelines are advisory, not mandatory
- **Gall v. United States, 552 U.S. 38 (2007):** District courts may vary from Guidelines range based on SS 3553(a) factors; appellate courts review for reasonableness
- **Kimbrough v. United States, 552 U.S. 85 (2007):** District courts may disagree with Guidelines policy and impose non-Guidelines sentence

## Step 1: Guidelines Calculation

### A. Determine the Base Offense Level
- Identify the Guideline section applicable to the offense of conviction (USSG Chapter Two)
- Determine the base offense level from the applicable guideline
- Apply Specific Offense Characteristics (SOCs) that increase or decrease the level

### B. Apply Adjustments (USSG Chapter Three)
- **SS 3A1.1:** Vulnerable victim (+2)
- **SS 3A1.2:** Official victim (+3 or +6)
- **SS 3A1.3:** Restraint of victim (+2)
- **SS 3B1.1:** Aggravating role (leader/organizer: +2 to +4)
- **SS 3B1.2:** Mitigating role (minor participant: -2; minimal participant: -4)
- **SS 3C1.1:** Obstruction of justice (+2)
- **SS 3E1.1:** Acceptance of responsibility (-2 or -3)

### C. Calculate Criminal History Category
- Assign criminal history points per USSG SS 4A1.1
- Determine Criminal History Category (I through VI)

**Criminal History Points Table:**

| Prior Sentence | Points |
|---------------|--------|
| Sentence of imprisonment exceeding 1 year and 1 month | 3 points per sentence |
| Sentence of imprisonment of at least 60 days | 2 points per sentence |
| Any other prior sentence | 1 point per sentence |
| Crime committed while under sentence | +2 points |
| Crime committed within 2 years of release | +1 point |

### D. Determine Advisory Guidelines Range
- Cross-reference Total Offense Level with Criminal History Category on the Sentencing Table (USSG Chapter Five, Part A)
- Identify the resulting range (in months)
- Note any mandatory minimums that may override the Guidelines range
- Note any statutory maximums that cap the Guidelines range

## Step 2: SS 3553(a) Factor Analysis

**18 U.S.C. SS 3553(a)** requires the court to consider the following factors:

| Factor | Statutory Language | Defense Application |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **SS 3553(a)(1)** | Nature and circumstances of the offense and history and characteristics of the defendant | Present full mitigation narrative; individualize the defendant |
| **SS 3553(a)(2)(A)** | The need for the sentence to reflect the seriousness of the offense, promote respect for the law, and provide just punishment | Argue that a below-Guidelines sentence still reflects seriousness |
| **SS 3553(a)(2)(B)** | The need to afford adequate deterrence | Argue that a lower sentence achieves deterrence; general deterrence research |
| **SS 3553(a)(2)(C)** | The need to protect the public from further crimes of the defendant | Present low recidivism risk; rehabilitation evidence; community supervision plan |
| **SS 3553(a)(2)(D)** | The need to provide the defendant with needed educational or vocational training, medical care, or other correctional treatment | Argue that treatment is more effective than incarceration; propose BOP programming |
| **SS 3553(a)(3)** | The kinds of sentences available | Present alternatives: probation, home confinement, split sentence, community service |
| **SS 3553(a)(4)** | The applicable Guidelines range | Present Guidelines calculation; note advisory nature post-Booker |
| **SS 3553(a)(5)** | Pertinent policy statements of the Sentencing Commission | Cite Commission studies, amendments, and reports supporting lower sentences |
| **SS 3553(a)(6)** | The need to avoid unwarranted sentence disparities | Present comparable cases; co-defendant sentences; national statistics |
| **SS 3553(a)(7)** | The need to provide restitution to victims | Address restitution capacity and willingness |

## Step 3: Departure Motions

### Downward Departures (USSG SS 5K)

| Departure | Guideline | Basis | Requirements |
|-----------|-----------|-------|-------------|
| **Substantial Assistance** | SS 5K1.1 | Cooperation with government | Government motion required (but see SS 3553(e) safety valve) |
| **Criminal History Overrepresentation** | SS 4A1.3(b)(1) | Criminal history category overstates seriousness | Show that prior convictions are old, minor, or unrelated |
| **Diminished Capacity** | SS 5K2.13 | Significantly reduced mental capacity | Expert evidence; not applicable if offense involved violence or threats |
| **Coercion and Duress** | SS 5K2.12 | Committed offense under unusual duress | Show external pressure beyond normal criminal motivation |
| **Military Service** | SS 5H1.11 | Extraordinary military service | Distinguished service record; combat service; service-connected conditions |
| **Post-Offense Rehabilitation** | SS 5K2.19 | Extraordinary post-offense rehabilitation | Sustained, verified rehabilitation efforts |
| **Aberrant Behavior** | SS 5K2.20 | Single act of aberrant behavior | First offense; spontaneous conduct; inconsistent with character |
| **Safety Valve** | SS 5C1.2 | Drug offenses meeting safety valve criteria | 18 U.S.C. SS 3553(f); no prior violent convictions; no firearm; no serious injury; not leader; full debriefing |

### Variance Arguments (Post-Booker)

Unlike departures, variances are based on SS 3553(a) factors and do not require a specific Guideline provision. The defense may argue for a non-Guidelines sentence based on:

1. **Policy disagreements with Guidelines:** where the Guidelines produce unjust results (Kimbrough)
2. **Individual circumstances:** where the defendant's personal history warrants a lower sentence
3. **Empirical challenges:** where recidivism data shows the Guidelines overestimate risk
4. **Sentencing Commission data:** where the Commission itself has recognized problems with the Guidelines

## Step 4: Fifth Circuit Specific Considerations

**Fifth Circuit Sentencing Standards:**

| Issue | Standard | Key Case |
|-------|----------|----------|
| **Procedural reasonableness** | Court must calculate Guidelines correctly, treat as advisory, consider SS 3553(a) factors, explain basis for sentence | Gall v. United States, 552 U.S. 38 (2007) |
| **Substantive reasonableness** | Appellate court presumes within-Guidelines sentence is reasonable; reviews non-Guidelines sentence for abuse of discretion | United States v. Cooks, 589 F.3d 173 (5th Cir. 2009) |
| **Variance explanation** | Greater variance from Guidelines requires more robust explanation | United States v. Smith, 440 F.3d 704 (5th Cir. 2006) |
| **Criminal history departure** | May depart if category significantly overrepresents criminal history | United States v. Zuniga-Peralta, 442 F.3d 345 (5th Cir. 2006) |
| **Acceptance of responsibility** | Defendant must clearly demonstrate acceptance; not inconsistent with maintaining factual objections to PSR | United States v. Juarez-Duarte, 513 F.3d 204 (5th Cir. 2008) |

## Federal Sentencing Memorandum Structure

```
I.    Introduction and Sentencing Request
II.   Objections to the Pre-Sentence Report (if any)
III.  Guidelines Calculation (defense position)
IV.   Departure Arguments (SS 5K motions)
V.    Variance Arguments (SS 3553(a) analysis)
      A. Nature and circumstances of the offense
      B. History and characteristics of the defendant
      C. Seriousness, deterrence, and public protection
      D. Available sentences and alternatives to incarceration
      E. Avoiding unwarranted sentencing disparities
VI.   Mitigation Presentation
VII.  Comparable Cases / Sentencing Data
VIII. Proposed Sentence and Conditions
IX.   Source Document Appendix (per Source Citation Protocol)
X.    Conclusion
```
