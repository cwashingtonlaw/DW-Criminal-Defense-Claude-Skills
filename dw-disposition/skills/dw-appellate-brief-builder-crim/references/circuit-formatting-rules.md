# Louisiana Appellate Circuit Formatting Rules

This reference catalogs the formatting rules that govern criminal appellate briefs filed in the Louisiana courts of appeal (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th Circuits) and the Louisiana Supreme Court. The Uniform Rules — Courts of Appeal apply across all five circuits; per-circuit local rules supply nuance. The Louisiana Supreme Court has its own rule (Rule X) governing writ applications.

> **Citation status:** All Uniform Rules — Courts of Appeal citations and La. Sup. Ct. Rule X citations in this file should be verified against the current rule text before filing. Page-limit and word-count numbers below are stated as the standard rule but are flagged `[CIRCUIT VERIFY]` because the Louisiana Supreme Court and individual circuits have amended these limits in recent years.

## 1. Uniform Rules — Courts of Appeal (All Five Circuits)

The Uniform Rules — Courts of Appeal apply across all five Louisiana appellate circuits. They are the baseline. Per-circuit local rules add detail.

### 1.1 Rule 2-12.2 — Brief Formatting

**Font:**
- 14-point font for body text
- 12-point font permitted for footnotes
- Common font choices: Times New Roman, Century Schoolbook, Courier (rule allows any standard, readable typeface)

**Line spacing:**
- Body text: double-spaced
- Block quotations of 50 words or more: single-spaced and indented
- Footnotes: single-spaced

**Margins:**
- 1 inch top
- 1 inch bottom
- 1 inch left
- 1 inch right

**Paper size:**
- 8.5" × 11" letter-size paper (US standard)

**Page numbering:**
- Pages numbered consecutively at the bottom (or per the cover-page rule for cover-page numbering)

**Justification:**
- Left-aligned (full justification permitted but not required)

### 1.2 Rule 2-12.2 — Length Limits [CIRCUIT VERIFY current limits]

**Page limit alternative:**
- Original brief (appellant or appellee): 50 pages, NOT counting cover, table of contents, table of authorities, certificate of service, certificate of compliance, or appendix
- Reply brief: 30 pages [CIRCUIT VERIFY]
- Amicus brief: half the length of the brief it supports [CIRCUIT VERIFY]

**Word-count alternative (Rule 2-12.2 allows counsel to use word count instead of page count):**
- Original brief: typically 14,000 words [CIRCUIT VERIFY current limit]
- Reply brief: typically 7,000 words [CIRCUIT VERIFY]
- The word count counts ALL text in the brief except the cover, TOC, TOA, certificates, signature block, and appendix.

**Certificate of compliance** required when using word-count alternative — counsel certifies under penalty of perjury that the brief contains no more than the rule's word limit.

> **Important:** Rule 2-12.2 has been amended in recent years to reflect the move toward word-count standards consistent with federal and other state appellate practice. Always consult the current rule text and any per-circuit administrative orders before relying on a specific limit.

### 1.3 Rule 2-12.3 — Cover Page

The cover page must contain:

1. The name of the court (e.g., "COURT OF APPEAL, FIRST CIRCUIT, STATE OF LOUISIANA")
2. The docket number assigned by the appellate court
3. The case caption — appellant v. appellee or *State of Louisiana v. [Defendant Name]*
4. The trial-court docket number, the parish, and the trial judge
5. The title of the document — "ORIGINAL BRIEF OF APPELLANT," "REPLY BRIEF OF APPELLANT," etc.
6. The name, address, telephone number, email, and Louisiana Bar Roll number of counsel filing the brief
7. Designation of counsel for whom (Counsel for Appellant, Counsel for Appellee, etc.)

### 1.4 Rule 2-12.4 — Brief Structure

A criminal appellant's brief shall contain (in this order):

1. **Cover page** (per Rule 2-12.3)
2. **Table of contents** with page references
3. **Table of authorities** — cases, statutes, rules, secondary, with page references
4. **Jurisdictional statement** — required where appellate jurisdiction is contested or where the court requires
5. **Assignments of error** — numbered, terse restatement
6. **Statement of the case** — procedural history
7. **Statement of facts** — with record cites supporting every factual statement
8. **Summary of argument** — one paragraph per assignment
9. **Argument** — assignment by assignment
10. **Conclusion** — specific relief requested
11. **Certificate of compliance** (if word-count alternative)
12. **Certificate of service**
13. **Signature block**
14. **Appendix** (optional; rare in criminal appeals where record is lodged)

### 1.5 Filing Rules

- Original plus required copies (varies by circuit; commonly an original plus 4 paper copies, plus electronic filing through the Louisiana e-filing portal where required)
- Brief must be filed by the deadline set in the court's scheduling order — typically 30-45 days after lodging of the record [CIRCUIT VERIFY per case]
- Extensions of time may be granted by motion for good cause; serial extensions are disfavored

### 1.6 Service Rules

Service of the brief on opposing counsel must be made on or before the date of filing. Acceptable methods include:
- Personal delivery
- U.S. Mail (with appropriate added time for service)
- Email (where opposing counsel has consented or where local rule permits)
- The Louisiana e-filing portal where applicable

The certificate of service must identify all parties served, the method of service, the date of service, and the address used.

## 7. Louisiana Supreme Court — Writ Applications (La. Sup. Ct. Rule X)

The Louisiana Supreme Court does not hear direct criminal appeals as of right (except in capital cases — La. Const. Art. V, § 5(D), and where a statute or ordinance has been declared unconstitutional). All other criminal cases reach the Louisiana Supreme Court only by **writ application** under La. Sup. Ct. Rule X.

### 7.1 When to seek La. Sup. Ct. review on direct appeal

After the Louisiana court of appeal issues its opinion on direct appeal, the losing party may apply for a writ of certiorari (or review) under Rule X within **30 days** of the court of appeal's judgment. La. Sup. Ct. Rule X(5)(a) [VERIFY CURRENT RULE].

### 7.2 Writ application format (La. Sup. Ct. Rule X)

**Cover page:**
- "Supreme Court of Louisiana"
- Caption: *State of Louisiana v. [Defendant Name]*
- Court of appeal docket number; trial court docket number
- "Application for [Writ of Certiorari / Supervisory Writ]"
- Counsel info

**Required sections [VERIFY CURRENT RULE]:**
1. Cover page
2. Table of contents
3. Table of authorities
4. Statement of jurisdiction (citing Rule X grounds — e.g., conflicting decisions among circuits, important question of law, error of law warranting review)
5. Statement of the case
6. Issues presented for review
7. Reasons for granting the writ (the heart of the application — why this Court should take this case)
8. Argument and authorities
9. Conclusion / prayer
10. Certificate of service
11. Appendix containing:
    - Court of appeal opinion
    - Trial court rulings
    - Pertinent transcript excerpts
    - Other documents required by Rule X

**Length:**
- Writ application: typically 30 pages [CIRCUIT VERIFY current Rule X limit]
- Word-count alternative may be available [CIRCUIT VERIFY]

**Number of copies:**
- Original plus required copies per Rule X — historically 8 copies; verify current rule

### 7.3 Standard for granting the writ

The Louisiana Supreme Court grants writs sparingly. Factors making a writ more likely [VERIFY CURRENT RULE]:
1. Conflict among the circuits on a question of law
2. An important question of state or federal constitutional law not previously decided
3. A decision of a court of appeal that conflicts with a Louisiana Supreme Court decision
4. An issue of significant public interest
5. Manifest error in the court of appeal decision warranting correction

The "Reasons for Granting" section is critical. The Court receives many writ applications and grants few; the reasons section must persuade the Court that the case is important enough to warrant the Court's limited resources.

### 7.4 Pretrial supervisory writs

Note: Pretrial supervisory writs (e.g., review of a motion-to-suppress denial) are interlocutory and follow a different framework. See `dw-appellate-error-monitor-crim`'s WRIT APPLICATION FRAMEWORK section. This skill (`dw-appellate-brief-builder-crim`) covers writs taken FROM a court-of-appeal direct-appeal decision, not interlocutory writs.

## 9. Certificate of Service Template (Louisiana Appellate Brief)

```
                       CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE

I HEREBY CERTIFY that on this _____ day of __________, 20___, a true
and correct copy of the foregoing [Original Brief / Reply Brief] of
Appellant has been served upon counsel for Appellee, the Louisiana
Department of Justice, Criminal Division, [or the elected District
Attorney for the appropriate parish], at [address / email], by
[method of service: U.S. Mail, postage prepaid; email; personal
delivery; e-filing portal].


                                    _______________________________
                                    [counsel name]
                                    Bar Roll No. XXXXX
                                    Counsel for Appellant
```

**Service note:** Where the appellate counsel for the State is the Louisiana Department of Justice (LADOJ), Criminal Division, service is on LADOJ. Where the appellate brief for the State will be filed by the elected District Attorney's appellate division (some parishes), service is on that office. Confirm the proper appellee counsel before serving — the wrong service can void the filing.

## 11. Per-Circuit Compliance Checklist

Before filing in any Louisiana appellate court:

- [ ] Identified the correct circuit per parish-of-origin
- [ ] Cover page conforms to Rule 2-12.3 and any local cover-page rule
- [ ] Table of contents present with accurate page numbers
- [ ] Table of authorities present with all cited cases, statutes, rules, and secondary
- [ ] Body text in 14-point font; double-spaced; 1-inch margins; left-aligned
- [ ] Footnotes in 12-point single-spaced
- [ ] Block quotations of 50+ words single-spaced and indented
- [ ] Page count under 50 (original brief) or 30 (reply) OR word count under 14,000 / 7,000 with certificate of compliance attached
- [ ] Certificate of service identifies all parties served, method, date
- [ ] Certificate of compliance attached if using word-count alternative
- [ ] Signature block per shared protocols (`dw-shared-protocols-crim/references/signature-block.md`)
- [ ] Original plus required copies (typically 4) printed on 8.5"×11" paper
- [ ] E-filing through Louisiana appellate portal where required by circuit
- [ ] Service of the brief on opposing counsel (LADOJ Criminal Division or elected DA appellate division) on or before filing date
- [ ] Calendar deadline for State's response brief (typically 30-45 days from filing) and reply-brief deadline (typically 14-21 days from State's brief)

## 12. Critical Per-Circuit Verification

The page limits, word counts, copy requirements, and e-filing rules vary by circuit and have been amended in recent years. Before filing, the attorney should:

1. **Pull the current Uniform Rules — Courts of Appeal text** from the Louisiana Supreme Court website
2. **Pull the local rules** of the relevant circuit from the circuit's website
3. **Confirm any administrative orders** that affect formatting (some circuits have issued orders requiring electronic filing or modifying page limits)
4. **Confirm the briefing schedule** in the court's scheduling order for the specific case
5. **Confirm the proper appellee counsel** (LADOJ Criminal Division vs. elected DA appellate division)

Items in this reference marked `[CIRCUIT VERIFY]` are particularly susceptible to change and must be confirmed for each filing.

## 13. Appellee (State) Brief — Brief Format Note

This skill primarily drafts appellant briefs. Should the engagement involve drafting an appellee response (rare in criminal defense — the State, not the defense, files appellee briefs in defendant-appeals), the format mirrors the appellant brief with these differences:

1. Title: "ORIGINAL BRIEF OF APPELLEE" (not "Appellant")
2. Designation: "Counsel for Appellee" (not "Appellant")
3. Substantive sections respond to the appellant's assignments of error rather than raise new ones
4. Page limits typically the same as the appellant's brief (50 pages original; word-count alternative)
5. Certificate of service reflects service on appellant's counsel
