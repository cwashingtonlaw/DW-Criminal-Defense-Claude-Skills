# Appellate Brief Section Templates — Louisiana Direct Appeal

This reference provides the boilerplate skeleton for every section of a Louisiana criminal direct-appeal brief, in the order required by Louisiana Uniform Rules — Courts of Appeal, Rule 2-12.4. Use these templates as the structural starting point; populate with case-specific content per the SKILL.md modules.

The standard brief structure is:

1. Cover Page
2. Table of Contents
3. Table of Authorities
4. Jurisdictional Statement (where required)
5. Assignments of Error
6. Statement of the Case
7. Statement of Facts
8. Summary of Argument
9. Argument
10. Conclusion
11. Certificate of Compliance (if word-count alternative)
12. Certificate of Service
13. Signature Block
14. Appendix (optional; rare in criminal)

## 4. Jurisdictional Statement (where required)

A jurisdictional statement is not required in every Louisiana court-of-appeal brief, but is good practice and required in writ applications to the Louisiana Supreme Court. When included:

```
                     JURISDICTIONAL STATEMENT

This Court has appellate jurisdiction over this criminal appeal under
La. Const. Art. V, § 10(A), which vests the Courts of Appeal with
appellate jurisdiction over criminal cases triable by a jury (other than
those in which the death penalty has been imposed).

[DEFENDANT] was convicted by a jury of [offense(s)] in violation of La.
R.S. [statute(s)] and was sentenced on [sentencing date]. Notice of appeal
was timely filed on [date], within the 30-day period set by La. C.Cr.P.
Art. 914. The order granting the appeal was entered on [date]. The
appellate record was lodged in this Court on [date].

This appeal is timely. Jurisdiction is proper.
```

For Louisiana Supreme Court writ applications, the jurisdictional statement cites La. Sup. Ct. Rule X and the criteria warranting review (conflicting decisions, important question of law, etc.).

## 5. Assignments of Error Template

```
                     ASSIGNMENTS OF ERROR

Assignment of Error No. 1:
  The trial court erred in [terse restatement of preserved issue —
  e.g., "denying the motion to suppress the custodial statement obtained
  in violation of Miranda v. Arizona"].

Assignment of Error No. 2:
  The trial court erred in [terse restatement — e.g., "admitting evidence
  of Mr. [Defendant]'s prior arrest under La. C.E. Art. 404(B) without
  proper notice and over defense objection"].

Assignment of Error No. 3:
  The evidence was constitutionally insufficient under Jackson v. Virginia
  to support the conviction for [offense].

Assignment of Error No. 4:
  The trial court erred in imposing a constitutionally excessive sentence
  in violation of La. Const. Art. I, § 20.

Assignment of Error No. 5 (Errors Patent):
  Pursuant to La. C.Cr.P. Art. 920(2), the trial court committed errors
  patent on the face of the record requiring [vacatur of sentence /
  vacatur of conviction] and remand. [Identify specific errors patent —
  e.g., "imposing an illegal sentence by restricting parole eligibility
  where La. R.S. [statute] does not authorize the restriction."]
```

Drafting rule: Each assignment is one or two sentences. Restate the issue, do not argue it. Argument goes in the Argument section.

## 7. Statement of Facts Template

The Statement of Facts is **the single most cite-saturated section of the brief**. Every factual sentence ends with a record cite. Sample paragraph:

```
                     STATEMENT OF FACTS

On [date], [DEFENDANT] was arrested by [law enforcement agency] following
[brief description — traffic stop, search warrant execution, etc.]. (Arrest
Report, R. Vol. ___, p. ___; Trial Tr. Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___.) Officer
[name] testified at trial that [factual content of testimony]. (Trial Tr.
Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___.) Officer [name] also testified that [further
content]. (Trial Tr. Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___.)

[Continue chronologically through trial events relevant to the assignments
of error. Each sentence cites the record.]

The State's case relied on [identify central evidence]. (Trial Tr. Vol. ___,
p. ___, ll. ___.) On cross-examination, Officer [name] acknowledged [fact
helpful to defense]. (Trial Tr. Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___.)

[Cover the trial events that are the subject of the assignments of error.
For each assignment, the Statement of Facts must include sufficient factual
context that the panel can understand the issue without flipping to the
record.]

Defense counsel objected to [trial event] on the ground that [legal basis].
(Trial Tr. Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___.) The trial court overruled the
objection. (Trial Tr. Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___.) The objection was
renewed in the Motion for New Trial. (Defense Mot. for New Trial, R. Vol.
___, p. ___, para. ___.)
```

**Drafting rule:** Every factual sentence has a record cite. No exceptions. If the brief writer cannot cite a sentence, the sentence is wrong — either it is unverifiable, or it is argument (in which case it goes in the Argument section, not here).

## 8. Summary of Argument Template

The Summary of Argument is one paragraph per assignment of error — a plain-English distillation of the argument that follows. The panel reads the Summary first to orient.

```
                     SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT

Assignment of Error No. 1. The trial court erred in denying the motion
to suppress Mr. [Defendant]'s custodial statement. The record establishes
that Mr. [Defendant] unambiguously invoked his right to counsel under
Davis v. United States and Edwards v. Arizona, yet officers continued
questioning. The legal question of unambiguous invocation is reviewed
de novo. The error was preserved by contemporaneous objection at trial
and renewed in the motion for new trial. The error is not harmless under
Chapman v. California: the statement was the centerpiece of the State's
case on the contested element of intent. The conviction must be reversed.

Assignment of Error No. 2. The trial court erred in admitting evidence
of Mr. [Defendant]'s prior [acts] under La. C.E. Art. 404(B). The State
failed to provide adequate notice and the evidence had no permissible
non-character purpose. Reviewed for abuse of discretion, the ruling
exceeded that deferential standard. The error affected substantial rights
under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 921 because the prior-acts evidence invited the
jury to convict on character. The conviction must be reversed.

Assignment of Error No. 3. The evidence was constitutionally insufficient
under Jackson v. Virginia. Viewing the evidence in the light most favorable
to the State, no rational trier could have found the element of [identify]
beyond a reasonable doubt. The conviction must be reversed and judgment
of acquittal entered. Burks v. United States precludes retrial.

Assignment of Error No. 4. The sentence is constitutionally excessive
under La. Const. Art. I, § 20 and reviewed for manifest abuse of discretion.
The sentence is grossly out of proportion to the conduct. The motion to
reconsider sentence under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 881.1 was timely filed and
denied. State v. Mims. The sentence must be vacated and the case remanded
for resentencing.

Assignment of Error No. 5 (Errors Patent). The trial court imposed an
illegal sentence by restricting parole eligibility where La. R.S. [statute]
does not authorize the restriction. The illegal portion of the sentence
must be vacated and the matter remanded for correction. La. C.Cr.P. Arts.
882, 920(2).
```

## 9. Argument Template (per assignment, six-part substructure)

Each Argument section follows the same six-part substructure. This consistency makes the brief easy for the panel to navigate.

```
                          ARGUMENT

ASSIGNMENT OF ERROR NO. 1: THE TRIAL COURT ERRED IN DENYING THE MOTION
TO SUPPRESS [DEFENDANT]'S CUSTODIAL STATEMENT OBTAINED IN VIOLATION OF
MIRANDA V. ARIZONA.

A.  Issue Restated.

    Assignment of Error No. 1 presents whether the trial court reversibly
    erred in denying the motion to suppress Mr. [Defendant]'s custodial
    statement, where the record establishes that Mr. [Defendant]
    unambiguously invoked his right to counsel and officers continued
    questioning. The conviction must be reversed and the case remanded
    for a new trial without the suppressed statement.

B.  Standard of Review.

    The trial court's legal conclusions on a motion to suppress are
    reviewed de novo. State v. Hunt, 2009-1589 (La. 12/1/09), 25 So.3d
    746, 751; Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690, 699 (1996). The
    trial court's subsidiary factual findings, including credibility
    determinations, are entitled to deference and will not be disturbed
    unless clearly contrary to the evidence. State v. Hunt, supra.
    Whether [Defendant]'s statement to officers constituted an
    unambiguous invocation under Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452
    (1994), is a legal question reviewed de novo.

C.  Preservation.

    The issue is preserved. Defense counsel filed a written motion to
    suppress the custodial statement. (Mot. to Suppress, R. Vol. ___, p.
    ___.) The trial court conducted an evidentiary hearing on [date] and
    denied the motion. (Suppression Hr'g Tr., dated ___, p. ___, ll. ___.)
    At trial, when the State offered the statement, defense counsel
    contemporaneously objected on Miranda and Edwards grounds. (Trial Tr.
    Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___.) The trial court overruled. (Id. at p.
    ___, ll. ___.) The objection was renewed in the Motion for New Trial.
    (Defense Mot. for New Trial, R. Vol. ___, p. ___, para. ___.) The
    issue is preserved under La. C.Cr.P. Art. 841.

D.  Statement of the Law.

    The Fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution and Article I,
    Section 13 of the Louisiana Constitution prohibit compelled self-
    incrimination. Custodial interrogation requires Miranda warnings.
    Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966).

    Once a suspect invokes the right to counsel, all interrogation must
    cease, and may not resume until counsel has been provided or the
    suspect initiates further communication. Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S.
    477 (1981).

    The invocation must be unambiguous. Davis v. United States, 512 U.S.
    452 (1994). However, the question of whether a particular statement
    is unambiguous is a legal question, reviewed de novo on appeal,
    informed by the totality of the circumstances. [Authority — verify]

    [Continue building the controlling law: Louisiana cases applying
    Miranda / Edwards / Davis; specific factual analogues.]

E.  Application to Facts.

    The record establishes [Defendant]'s unambiguous invocation. During
    the custodial interrogation on [date], at [timestamp / page in
    transcript], [Defendant] stated: "[exact quote]." (Trial Tr. Vol.
    ___, p. ___, ll. ___.) Under Davis, this statement is unambiguous.
    [Apply the law:]
     - [Distinguish State's likely cited authorities]
     - [Identify the specific record passage demonstrating unambiguity]
     - [Address the trial court's factual findings — defer where
        appropriate, attack legal conclusions de novo]

    Officers continued questioning despite the invocation. (Trial Tr.
    Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___.) Under Edwards, the post-invocation
    interrogation was unlawful and the resulting statement was inadmissible.

F.  Prejudice / Harmless-Error Analysis.

    The error is a federal constitutional error subject to harmless-error
    analysis under Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18, 24 (1967). The
    State must prove the error harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. The
    State cannot meet that burden here.

    [Defendant]'s statement was the centerpiece of the State's case on
    Count [#]. The State's opening argument referenced the statement at
    [Trial Tr. Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___]. The State examined Officer
    [name] about the statement at [Trial Tr. Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___].
    The State's closing argument emphasized the statement at [Trial Tr.
    Vol. ___, p. ___, ll. ___]: "[quote from State's closing]."

    Without the statement, the State's case rests on [identify other
    evidence]. The remaining evidence is [analyze weaknesses]. The State
    cannot prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the verdict would have
    been the same absent the statement. The error is not harmless. The
    conviction must be reversed.
```

Apply the same six-part substructure to every Argument section. For structural errors, replace subsection F with an explicit "no harmless-error analysis applies" section per `harmless-error-framework.md` Section 2.

## 10. Conclusion Template

```
                          CONCLUSION

For the reasons stated, the trial court committed reversible error in
[summarize errors by assignment number]. Each error was preserved.
Assignments 1, 2, and 3 are not harmless under Chapman v. California
or La. C.Cr.P. Art. 921. Assignment 5 (errors patent) requires correction
without harmless-error analysis.

WHEREFORE, [DEFENDANT NAME], appellant, respectfully prays that this
Honorable Court:

   (1) REVERSE the conviction on Count [#] (Assignment of Error No. 1);

   (2) VACATE the sentence on Count [#] and REMAND for a new trial
       without the suppressed statement;

   (3) Alternatively, REVERSE the conviction on Count [#] (Assignment of
       Error No. 3) for insufficiency of the evidence, and ENTER a
       JUDGMENT of acquittal pursuant to Burks v. United States, 437
       U.S. 1 (1978);

   (4) VACATE the constitutionally excessive sentence on Count [#]

   (5) VACATE the illegal sentence on Count [#] (Assignment of Error
       No. 5 — Errors Patent) and REMAND for resentencing within the
       statutory limits; and

   (6) Grant such other and further relief as this Honorable Court
       deems just and proper.
```

## 14. Drafting Workflow

To assemble a complete brief from these templates:

1. Pull the case-specific data from the dw-appellate-error-monitor-crim outputs (Module H ranked-issue list; Module I designated record; Module E post-trial motion package; Module D errors patent; Module F harmless-error pre-assessment).
2. Draft Statement of the Case from Module E (post-trial motions) and minute entries.
3. Draft Statement of Facts using Module I designations and trial transcript — every sentence record-cited.
4. Draft Assignments of Error from Module H rankings.
5. Draft each Argument section using the six-part template, drawing legal-framework content from `standards-of-review-by-issue.md` and `harmless-error-framework.md`.
6. Draft Summary of Argument as the last substantive section.
7. Auto-generate Table of Contents and Table of Authorities.
8. Assemble cover page from case data.
9. Assemble certificates and signature.
10. Run the compliance checklist from `circuit-formatting-rules.md` Section 11.
11. Run the SKILL.md MODULE G compliance checklist.
12. Save to `{{CASE_ROOT}}/05 - Appellate/01 - Direct Appeal/Brief Drafts/[NUM] - Appellant's Brief - [Last Name] - [Date].docx`.
